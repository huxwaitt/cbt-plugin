**Bottom line:** Anchor the cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) formulation in the evidence-based model for the problem that explains the most difficulties or matters most to the client's goals, then individualize it with the client's own thoughts, behaviors and history. Use a transdiagnostic or generic model when no specific model fits. Where a guideline names a manualized model, keep to that model's manual; the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) does this for generalized anxiety and social anxiety. For US guideline choices see `04-treatment-planning.guideline-map`.

## When to use

After the problem list and diagnostic assessment, before the treatment plan. Return to it when a new problem appears or progress stalls.

## Steps

Persons and Tompkins give this sequence [Persons-Tompkins-2022]:
1. **Build the problem list** across all life domains.
2. **Assign diagnoses**, because a diagnosis points to an evidence-based general (nomothetic) model and treatment.
3. **Choose an anchoring diagnosis.** Pick the one that accounts for the most problems on the list, the one that interferes most with functioning. Disorders that drive many problems, such as bipolar disorder, schizophrenia or borderline personality disorder, often anchor. Alternatively, anchor to the client's current goal (for example, panic treatment for someone whose bipolar disorder is stable), while keeping the other condition in view. The authors note this is a pragmatic judgment with little research behind it.
4. **Select the general model** for the anchoring diagnosis.
5. **Individualize it** with the client's specific thoughts, behaviors, emotions, history and goals.
6. **If no model exists,** adapt the nearest one (for example, for subthreshold symptoms), or build from a general theory such as Beck's cognitive theory, conditioning theory or emotion regulation theory.

Dudley, Kuyken and Padesky weigh disorder-specific against transdiagnostic approaches and propose choosing and combining theories by principle for each client, while adding models of resilience [Dudley-etal-2011]. Disorder-specific models are used mainly at the explanatory level, after a descriptive map [Kuyken-etal-2008].

## What to record

- The anchoring problem and why it was chosen (most problems explained, most impairment, or client goal).
- The model selected, with its source.
- Problems the model does not explain, and how they will be handled.
- The date to review the choice.

## Guidance

- If two disorders are present → anchor to the one that explains more of the problem list, and note how the second interacts with it. Because the parsimony principle favors the explanation that accounts for the most problems [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
- If a guideline names a specific model → use that model's manual and stay close to it. Because NICE recommends CBT for GAD based on the manuals used in trials [NICE-CG113], and specifies the Clark and Wells or Heimberg model for social anxiety [NICE-CG159].
- If the presentation is PTSD → anchor to a trauma-focused CBT model. Because NICE recommends individual trauma-focused CBT for adults, listing cognitive processing therapy, cognitive therapy for PTSD, narrative exposure therapy and prolonged exposure, both within the first month after trauma and later [NICE-NG116].
- If nothing fits → build a functional analysis or generic cognitive model and treat it explicitly as a hypothesis. Because general theories underpin most evidence-based treatments [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
- If you are reviewing a trainee's or your own formulation → check that it uses the issue-specific model where a good one exists and the best generic model where none does. Because this is how the collaborative conceptualization competence scale rates the use of theory [Padesky-etal-2011].

## Common problems

- **Choosing by therapist preference.** Fix: state why this model explains this client's problems.
- **Forcing the client into the model.** Fix: keep the client's words and note mismatches as data.
- **Treating comorbid problems in parallel with no anchor.** Fix: choose one anchor and sequence the rest.
- **Using a disorder model for a problem it does not cover.** Fix: check the model's scope in its source paper and condition page before adapting it.
