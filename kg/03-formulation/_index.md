# 03-formulation: Case Formulation

## This stage
Case formulation turns assessment data into a shared, testable explanation of why this client has these problems now and what keeps them going. It sits between intake and assessment (`01-intake`, `10-diagnosis`) and treatment planning (`04-treatment-planning`), and it is revisited throughout treatment as progress data come in. Pages run from simple descriptive maps to disorder-specific and belief-level formulations, and end with turning the formulation into a plan.

## Decisions in order
1. Decide what a formulation must do for this client and how it differs from the diagnosis → `03-formulation.what-is-case-formulation`
2. Map one or two recent episodes with the client as a descriptive formulation → `03-formulation.five-part-model`
3. Identify the loops that keep the problem going → `03-formulation.maintenance-cycles`
4. Choose the anchoring problem and the disorder-specific or generic model → `03-formulation.choosing-a-model`
5. If the problem is a specific behavior, analyze its triggers and consequences → `03-formulation.functional-analysis`
6. If a whole-person or team summary is needed, organize it with the Five Ps → `03-formulation.five-ps-formulation`
7. If problems recur across situations, place thoughts and beliefs at the right level → `03-formulation.cognitive-model-levels`
8. If belief-level work is needed, complete the conceptualization diagram → `03-formulation.cognitive-conceptualization-diagram`
9. Add strengths and protective factors to every level → `03-formulation.strengths-in-formulation`
10. Co-create and share the formulation in the client's words and check its impact → `03-formulation.sharing-the-formulation`
11. Derive targets, interventions and predictions, then monitor and revise → `03-formulation.formulation-to-treatment`
12. Weigh how much confidence the evidence supports → `03-formulation.formulation-evidence`

## Pages
| Page | Summary | Applies when | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| `03-formulation.what-is-case-formulation` | Defines case formulation, contrasts it with diagnosis, explains descriptive to explanatory levels, and summarizes the collaborative (Kuyken, Padesky, Dudley) and hypothesis-testing (Persons) approaches. | A clinician or tool designer needs to know what a CBT case formulation contains, how it differs from a diagnosis, and which approach to follow. | L1-L2 |
| `03-formulation.five-part-model` | How to build the Padesky and Mooney five-part model of one recent episode with a client, how its parts interact, and what treatment targets it suggests. | Early in therapy, or for any presentation, the clinician wants a simple shared map linking situation, thoughts, emotions, body and behavior in a specific episode. | L1-L2 |
| `03-formulation.maintenance-cycles` | How to identify the vicious cycles that keep a problem going, draw them with the client (including Moorey's vicious flower for depression), and turn each cycle into a treatment target. | The clinician needs to show a client how their own coping keeps the problem going and choose which loop to break first. | L2 |
| `03-formulation.choosing-a-model` | Decision steps for picking an anchoring problem and the matching CBT model, with a map from common presentations to model papers and the condition pages in this wiki. | Assessment is done and the clinician must decide which evidence-based model will anchor the formulation, especially with comorbidity or no clear diagnosis. | L2 |
| `03-formulation.functional-analysis` | How to run a functional analysis of a target behavior (antecedents, behavior, short- and long-term consequences), judge which links are causal and changeable, and use it to choose interventions. | The problem is a specific behavior (avoidance, bingeing, drinking, self-injury, outbursts, checking) and the clinician needs to know what triggers and reinforces it. | L2 |
| `03-formulation.five-ps-formulation` | How to organize a case into presenting, predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective factors, what questions fill each, and how to turn the grid into a treatment focus. | The clinician needs a whole-person summary of why a problem arose and persists, for team communication, a complex history, or a referral letter. | L1-L2 |
| `03-formulation.cognitive-model-levels` | Explains automatic thoughts, intermediate beliefs (attitudes, rules, assumptions), core beliefs and coping (compensatory) strategies, how they connect, and which level to target when. | The clinician needs to place a client's thought, rule or belief at the right level of the cognitive model before choosing how to work with it. | L2 |
| `03-formulation.cognitive-conceptualization-diagram` | Step-by-step method for completing the Beck cognitive conceptualization diagram, from three typical situations up to core beliefs and life history, plus the strength-based version. | The clinician has several sessions of data and wants to link a client's recurring automatic thoughts and behaviors to beliefs, coping strategies and history. | L2 |
| `03-formulation.strengths-in-formulation` | How to search for client strengths, record them in the formulation and strength-based diagram, build a personal model of resilience, and use strengths in the treatment plan. | The formulation lists only problems and vulnerabilities, the client feels defined by their difficulties, or therapy is moving toward relapse prevention and resilience. | L2 |
| `03-formulation.sharing-the-formulation` | How to build the formulation with the client in their own language, pace and present it so it helps rather than distresses, check their reaction, and revise it over therapy. | The clinician is about to draw, explain or revise a formulation with a client, or the client reacts to one with doubt, distress or disagreement. | L2 |
| `03-formulation.formulation-to-treatment` | How to turn a formulation into treatment targets, goals, intervention choices and sequence, predict what should change, monitor it, and revise when progress stalls. | A working formulation exists and the clinician must decide what to target first, which intervention to use, and how to tell whether the formulation is right. | L2 |
| `03-formulation.formulation-evidence` | Summarizes research on formulation reliability, quality, training, competence measurement, client experience and treatment outcome, and what it means for practice and tool design. | A clinician, supervisor or tool designer needs to know how far formulation is evidence-based and which practices the evidence supports. | L3 |

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Formulating Core Beliefs and Childhood Too Early | In the first sessions the therapist announces core beliefs and childhood causes the client has not described, and the client looks confused, upset or quietly disagrees. | `03-formulation.pitfall-too-deep-too-soon` |
| Presenting a Finished Formulation to the Client | The therapist hands over a completed, jargon-heavy diagram as fact; the client nods, feels blamed or lectured, does not use it, and sometimes leaves therapy distressed. | `03-formulation.pitfall-imposed-formulation` |
| Never Revising the Formulation | The formulation drawn in session two sits unchanged in the file; therapy continues on the same plan while scores flatline, and new information never changes the targets. | `03-formulation.pitfall-static-formulation` |
| Using the Formulation to Drift from Effective Methods | Sessions become open-ended talk about history and beliefs "because the formulation is complex," while exposure, activation and other behavior change quietly stop. | `03-formulation.pitfall-formulation-drift` |
