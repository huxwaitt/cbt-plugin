**Bottom line:** Each maintaining mechanism in the formulation becomes a treatment target, each target gets an intervention chosen from the evidence-based model, and each intervention carries a prediction you can check with session-by-session measures. Target the problems that drive other problems first. If progress stalls, re-examine the formulation before changing technique at random.

## When to use

At the end of assessment, when presenting the treatment plan, and at every progress review.

## Steps

1. **Sort the problem list into primary and secondary problems.** The formulation suggests which problems cause others. Targeting the primary ones is likely to help most [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
2. **Name the target mechanisms.** In cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) targets are usually cognitions (content such as predictions, or process such as rumination and worry) or behaviors (avoidance, skill deficits, excesses) [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
3. **Set goals that follow the mechanism.** Persons describes a client who wanted certainty that she had chosen the right partner; because the formulation showed that seeking certainty maintained her distress, the goal became spending less time reviewing the decision [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
4. **Match each target to an intervention** from the anchoring model (table below).
5. **Sequence by risk, distress and leverage.** Address safety first: if there is current suicide or self-harm risk, assess it and agree a safety plan before other targets (`02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`, `02-risk.safety-planning`). In Kuyken's example, a client with self-injury, posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and depression agreed to start with PTSD and self-harm because they caused the most distress [Kuyken-etal-2008].
6. **Predict.** Write "If mechanism X maintains problem Y, changing X should reduce Y." Persons gives the example: if lack of pleasant activity maintains depression, increasing pleasant activity should lower depression scores [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
7. **Anticipate obstacles** the formulation predicts. If procrastination is a presenting problem, expect it to affect homework, and plan for that when setting the task [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
8. **Monitor every session and review with the client.** Good progress supports the formulation; poor progress suggests it may be wrong [Persons-Tompkins-2022].

|---|---|
| Biased predictions, catastrophic appraisals | `cognitive.cognitive-restructuring`, `behavioral.behavioral-experiments` |
| Avoidance, escape | `behavioral.exposure-principles`, `behavioral.behavioral-activation` |
| Safety behaviors, checking, reassurance seeking | `behavioral.safety-behavior-reduction`, `behavioral.exposure-and-response-prevention` |
| Withdrawal, inactivity | `behavioral.activity-monitoring-and-scheduling` |
| Rumination, worry | `cognitive.worry-and-rumination-techniques` |
| Rules and assumptions | `cognitive.modifying-intermediate-beliefs` |
| Skill deficits (assertion, problem solving) | `behavioral.skills-training`, `behavioral.problem-solving-therapy` |
| Self-criticism | `cognitive.self-compassion-techniques` |

## What to record

A plan table with one row per problem: client goal, mechanism, intervention, measure, prediction and review date. The Beck Institute case write-up uses this structure, pairing each problem or goal (for example, "social isolation, reconnect with others") with specific interventions (scheduling contact, testing the thought "He won't want to hear from me") [BeckInstitute-Worksheet-Packet-2020].

## Guidance

- If the client wants to work on everything at once → agree one primary target and show on the formulation how it links to the others. Because primary problems drive secondary ones [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
- If scores improve sharply or worsen sharply → ask what happened that week and check it against the formulation. Because large shifts can confirm or disconfirm the hypothesized mechanism [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
- If progress stalls after a fair trial → gather more assessment data and consider a different formulation with different targets. Because a failing plan may reflect a wrong formulation, not a wrong technique [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
- If you feel pulled to drop exposure or behavior change to "explore" → decide with the client, using data, whether to deviate. Because therapists tend to move from methods to process too soon and too often [Kuyken-etal-2008], and drift away from behavior change is a common cause of CBT failure [Waller-2009].

## Common problems

- **Plan not linked to formulation.** Fix: every intervention should point to a box or arrow on the diagram.
- **Measures that do not track the mechanism.** Fix: add an idiographic measure of the target (minutes ruminating, avoided situations).
- **Over-individualizing.** Randomized trials show formulation-guided treatment is generally similar to, and occasionally a little better than, standardized protocols [Persons-Tompkins-2022]; no trial found standardized treatment superior, and the strongest support is for the feedback (monitoring) element [Persons-Hong-2016]. Individualize within the evidence-based model rather than replacing it.
