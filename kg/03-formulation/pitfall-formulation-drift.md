**Bottom line:** In cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), individualizing treatment means adapting the evidence-based model to this client, not replacing its active ingredients. When a formulation is used to justify dropping exposure, activation or other behavior change, outcomes suffer. Deviate from the protocol only on data, and decide it with the client.

## What it looks like

- "She's too complex for the protocol" appears in notes for a client with a common anxiety disorder.
- Exposure is planned, postponed, then not mentioned again.
- Sessions focus on understanding the past while homework disappears.
- The formulation keeps growing; the plan keeps shrinking.

## Why it happens

Schulte and colleagues found that therapists move away from therapy methods (such as exposure) toward therapy process (such as discussing motivation) too soon, too often and sometimes for the wrong reasons [Kuyken-etal-2008]. Waller calls this "therapist drift": shifting from doing therapy to talking therapy, driven by the clinician's own distorted beliefs, emotional reactions and safety behaviors, with poor implementation of behavior change a common reason CBT fails [Waller-2009]. A rich formulation can supply a ready rationale for avoiding the uncomfortable parts of treatment.

## The fix

- If you want to depart from the protocol → write down the data that justify it and agree it with the client. Because deviations should be made empirically and collaboratively, which allows corrective feedback [Kuyken-etal-2008].
- If a guideline names a manualized treatment → keep its core methods and adapt delivery, not content. Because the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), for example, says CBT for generalized anxiety disorder should be based on the treatment manuals used in the trials [NICE-CG113].
- If the formulation is complex → anchor it in the evidence-based model and add individual elements on top. Because formulation-driven CBT is a systematic way to adapt evidence-based models and interventions, not a new treatment [Persons-Tompkins-2022].
- If you notice your own anxiety about a task (exposure, setting homework) → name it in supervision as a possible reason for the drift. Because clinician emotions and safety behaviors drive drift [Waller-2009].
- If you are unsure whether individualizing helps → remember the trial evidence. Because trials show formulation-guided treatment is similar to or occasionally better than standardized treatment, no trial found it worse, and the best-supported element is feedback, not abandoning the protocol [Persons-Hong-2016].

Example phrasing: "Understanding where this came from is useful. To change it, we also need to test it. What step could you try this week?"

## Sources note

Supported by research on therapist deviation from manuals and a widely cited review of therapist drift; a direct trial of drift prevention is lacking.
