**Bottom line:** The Five Ps sort what you know into presenting problems, predisposing factors (vulnerabilities), precipitating factors (triggers of onset), perpetuating factors (what keeps it going) and protective factors (strengths and supports). It answers "why this person, why this problem, why now?" [Macneil-etal-2012]. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) acts mainly on the perpetuating column, so make that column specific, and never leave the protective column empty.

## When to use

- To summarize a complex or long history at the end of assessment, including for a multidisciplinary team, supervisor or referral letter (expert consensus).
- When biological, social and psychological factors all matter and a purely cognitive diagram would leave them out.
- As a longitudinal layer added after a descriptive map, in line with the levels approach [Kuyken-etal-2008].

## Steps

Macneil and colleagues define the five elements as follows [Macneil-etal-2012]:

| P | Definition | Typical content | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presenting | The difficulties the person and clinician identify, and how they affect life; broader than the diagnosis | Symptoms, relationships, work, health | "What are the main difficulties you want help with?" |
| Predisposing | Factors that raise vulnerability | Genetic or family history, birth or brain injury, trauma, attachment history, socioeconomic status, core beliefs, personality factors | "What in your history might have made you more vulnerable to this?" |
| Precipitating | Events just before onset or worsening | Interpersonal, legal, work, physical or financial stressors, substance use | "What was happening in your life when this started or got worse?" |
| Perpetuating | Factors that maintain the difficulties | Avoidance, safety behaviors, withdrawal, ongoing substance use, poor sleep, attention and memory biases | "What keeps this going, even when you try to change it?" |
| Protective | Strengths and supports that reduce impact | Social support, skills, interests, personal qualities | "What helps, even a little? Who is on your side?" |

1. Fill the presenting row with the client's words and the problem list.
2. Fill precipitating and perpetuating rows from recent concrete examples.
3. Fill predisposing factors from history, marking guesses as hypotheses.
4. Fill protective factors deliberately, asking about past coping and current resources.
5. Draw arrows from each perpetuating factor to the problem it maintains.
6. Read the grid back to the client in plain language and ask what is missing or wrong.

Persons and Tompkins build a formulation from four elements: problems, mechanisms, origins and precipitants [Persons-Tompkins-2022]. These line up with the presenting, perpetuating, predisposing and precipitating columns; the Five Ps add a protective column for strengths.

## What to record

- The grid with the date and source of each item (client report, collateral, records, measures).
- Which perpetuating factors are the agreed treatment targets.
- Which predisposing items are confirmed and which are hypotheses.
- Protective factors, including people the client agreed can be involved.

## Guidance

- If the perpetuating column is vague ("stress," "low self-esteem") → rewrite each item as a specific behavior or thought process ("avoids calling friends," "checks body for symptoms several times a day"). Because perpetuating factors are what maintain the current difficulties, so they point to what to target and in what order [Macneil-etal-2012].
- If the protective column is empty → ask about past periods of coping, values, supports and skills before finishing. Because including protective factors builds optimism and supports the relationship, and their absence may make formulation more upsetting [Macneil-etal-2012].
- If predisposing factors include trauma or family difficulties → name them briefly and without blame, and check how the client feels hearing them. Because formulation can be distressing and should be introduced at a reasonable pace [Macneil-etal-2012].
- If the client hears the grid as "my past made me this way" → point to the perpetuating column and say what can change now. Because the maintaining factors, not the history, are the main route to change.

Example phrasing: "Some things made you more vulnerable, something set this off, and some things are keeping it going. The last group is where we can make the biggest difference together."

## Common problems

- **Grid as a list, not an explanation.** Fix: add arrows and one sentence that says how the factors connect.
- **Formulation without strengths.** In a study of formulation in CBT for psychosis that did not include protective factors, some clients found it saddening or upsetting [Macneil-etal-2012; Chadwick-etal-2003].
- **Predisposing factors dominate.** Fix: keep history to the items that explain the current mechanisms.
- **Static grid.** Fix: revise it when new information or progress data arrive [Macneil-etal-2012].
