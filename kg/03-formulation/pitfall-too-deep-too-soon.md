**Bottom line:** Jumping to core beliefs and childhood origins in the first sessions produces the least reliable part of a formulation at the moment the client trusts you least. Start with a descriptive map of recent episodes, add triggers and maintaining factors next, and add longitudinal explanation only if treatment needs it and the client's own data support it.

## What it looks like

- By session two the notes say "core belief: unlovable, from emotionally unavailable mother," but the client has never said this.
- The therapist tells the client their schema in the first formulation conversation.
- A cognitive conceptualization diagram has its top boxes filled before its situation boxes.
- The client goes quiet, cries, argues, or agrees politely and then misses the next session.
- An automated tool turns a few intake answers into a confident statement about the client's deepest beliefs.

## Why it happens

- Belief-level explanations feel insightful and impressive.
- Training often emphasizes the full Beck diagram.
- Therapists overestimate how much they can infer. Agreement between therapists is good for descriptive content and poor for inferred beliefs and maintaining factors [Kuyken-etal-2008]. In a study of 115 practitioners formulating the same case, only 44% produced a formulation rated at least good enough [Kuyken-etal-2005].
- Research designs that give therapists all the information at once encourage the idea that a formulation should be complete from the start [Kuyken-etal-2008].

## The fix

- If it is early in therapy → build a five-part model of one or two recent episodes and stop there. Because formulation usually begins descriptive and adds explanation over time [Kuyken-etal-2008].
- If you have a belief hypothesis → keep it in your notes with a question mark until the client confirms it. Because the conceptualization diagram instructions treat hypotheses as tentative until confirmed by the client [BeckInstitute-Worksheet-Packet-2020].
- If the client spontaneously links the present to the past → reflect it briefly, note it, and return to current maintaining factors. Because history explains vulnerability but current cycles are the main targets.
- If you decide a longitudinal layer is needed → explain why and ask permission before exploring history. Because formulation can upset clients and implicate their sense of identity [Redhead-etal-2015].
- If you review your own recordings or a trainee's → check whether the level of formulation matches the stage of therapy. Because the competence scale rates whether the formulation is as simple as possible given the stage of therapy [Padesky-etal-2011].

Example phrasing: "I have a hunch there might be a bigger theme here, but it's early. Let's keep watching for it together."

## Sources note

Supported by reliability research and expert consensus. No trial has compared early versus later belief-level formulation directly.
