**Bottom line:** Behavioral activation (BA) helps clients with low mood act according to a plan rather than a
mood. You monitor activity, link it to mood, and schedule small, specific activities that bring pleasure,
mastery or meaning. It is supported by randomized trials as a stand-alone treatment for depression [Martell-etal-2010].

## When to use

Use BA when withdrawal, inactivity or avoidance keeps low mood going. It works early in treatment, when
concentration is too poor for detailed cognitive work, and it suits group and individual formats. It also
fits clients who prefer a practical, action-first approach over talking about thoughts.

## How it works

Low mood leads to doing less. Doing less removes sources of reward and adds new problems, which lowers mood
further. Scheduling activity breaks this loop and gives the client fresh evidence about what helps [Martell-etal-2010].

## Steps

1. Explain the withdrawal cycle with the client's own example.
2. Ask the client to record activities and mood each hour for a week.
3. Review the record together and look for links between activity and mood.
4. Choose small activities tied to the client's values and schedule them on specific days.
5. Review what happened, solve problems, and grade activities up over time.

## Guidance

- If the client says they will act when they feel better → explain that action usually comes first and mood
  follows. Because waiting for motivation keeps the cycle going [Martell-etal-2010].
- If a planned activity did not happen → treat it as information and ask what got in the way. Because blame
  feeds self-criticism, while problem solving keeps the plan alive.
- If the client avoids a task because it feels too big → break it into the smallest first step they can do
  this week. Because small successes rebuild a sense of mastery.

## Common problems

Clients often schedule too much at first. Start with two or three activities and build up. Some clients pick
only duties; balance them with pleasant and meaningful activities. Watch for avoidance that looks like
activity, such as cleaning to escape a hard conversation.

## Evidence

BA is supported by randomized trials and is recommended as a psychological treatment option for depression in
current UK guidance [NICE-NG222]. Trials suggest outcomes comparable to full cognitive therapy for many adults.
