**Bottom line:** Ask the client what brings them in now, then narrow the answer into two to five specific problems
in their own words. Agree the list together before you move on, because the problem list drives the
case formulation and the treatment plan [Persons-2008].

## When to use

Use this procedure in the first assessment session of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), after you have
covered consent and confidentiality. Use it again whenever the client raises a new difficulty that does not
fit the agreed list. It suits adults and older adolescents seen in outpatient settings [Beck-2021].

## Steps

1. Ask an open question: "What brings you here now, rather than six months ago?"
2. Listen for situations, thoughts, emotions, body sensations and behaviors, and note each one.
3. Summarize what you heard in the client's words and check that the summary fits.
4. Turn each difficulty into a specific problem: what happens, where, how often, and what it stops the
   client from doing.
5. Ask which problem matters most and number the list in that order.
6. Rate the distress each problem causes from 0 to 100 so you can track change later [Beck-2021].

## What to record

Record the numbered problem list, the client's own words for each problem, the distress ratings, the onset
and course of each problem, and any recent trigger such as a loss, a move or a conflict at work or home.
Note what the client hopes will be different at the end of treatment.

## Guidance

- If the client gives a long story without a clear problem → summarize it in two sentences and ask which part
  they most want to change. Because a summary shows you listened and invites them to choose [Beck-2021].
- If the client names only a diagnosis → ask what the diagnosis stops them from doing day to day. Because
  specific problems are easier to formulate and to measure than labels [Persons-2008].
- If the client becomes distressed while describing the problem → slow down, reflect the emotion and offer a
  short pause. Because feeling heard keeps the alliance strong in the first session.

## Common problems

Clients sometimes list ten or more problems. Group them by theme and agree on a short list for now. Other
clients struggle to name any problem at all; in that case, ask about a recent hard day and work from that
concrete example. Do not skip risk questions because the story is long: screen for risk in every first
session, whatever the presenting problem looks like.
