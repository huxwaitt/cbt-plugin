**Bottom line:** Every first session needs a direct risk screen, even when the client presents with something
that seems low risk. Ask about suicidal thoughts, self-harm and safety in plain words and record the answers.
Asking does not raise risk, and missing it can leave a client without help when they need it [NICE-NG222].

## What it looks like

The note lists the presenting problems, history and goals in detail, but says nothing about suicidal thoughts,
self-harm, harm to others or the client's current safety. Sometimes the screen is planned for "next time" and
then forgotten. Sometimes the therapist judged the client as low risk from their manner, without asking any
direct question at all.

## Why it happens

Therapists run out of time because the story is long. Some worry that asking about suicide will upset the
client or plant the idea. Others assume a client with work stress or mild anxiety cannot be at risk. Clients
rarely raise suicidal thoughts unprompted, so silence is easy to read as safety [Beck-2021].

## The fix

- If the session is running long → move the risk questions earlier and ask them before the history. Because
  the screen is the one part of intake that cannot wait [NICE-NG222].
- If you feel awkward asking → use a calm, direct question: "Have you had thoughts of ending your life?"
  Because direct questions are clearer than hints and clients usually find them a relief.
  Avoid: "You're not thinking of doing anything silly, are you?"
- If the client says yes → ask about plans, intent, past attempts and what keeps them safe, and follow your
  service's escalation steps. In the US, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline takes calls and texts on 988; in an
  emergency call 911. In the UK, call 999, NHS 111, or Samaritans on 116 123.

Plan the screen into the structure of every first session so it never depends on how much time is left.
Write down what you asked, what the client said, and what you did next, including when the answer is no.
Review the screen again whenever the client's mood, circumstances or behavior changes sharply.
