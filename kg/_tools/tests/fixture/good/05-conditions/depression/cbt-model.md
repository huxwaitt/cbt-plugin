**Bottom line:** In the cognitive model, depression is kept going by negative views of the self, the world and
the future. These views shape how clients interpret events and what they do. Withdrawal, rumination and
self-criticism then confirm the views, so the cycle continues until treatment interrupts it [Beck-2021].

## Components

- **Core beliefs:** global views such as "I am a failure" that form early and become active under stress.
- **Rules and assumptions:** conditional beliefs such as "If I am not perfect, people will reject me".
- **Automatic thoughts:** quick, specific thoughts in a situation, such as "I ruined that meeting".
- **Emotions and body:** sadness, guilt, fatigue and poor sleep.
- **Behaviors:** withdrawal, inactivity, rumination and reassurance seeking [Persons-2008].

## How it keeps the problem going

A stressful event activates a core belief. The client interprets the event through that belief and feels low.
They then withdraw or ruminate, which reduces contact with rewarding experiences and creates more evidence that
seems to fit the belief. Each turn of the cycle makes the next one easier to start [Beck-2021].

## Drawing it with the client

Start with a recent, specific low moment. Write the situation in one box, then ask what went through the
client's mind, what they felt in their body and emotions, and what they did next. Draw arrows between the
boxes and ask what happened after they acted. Use the client's own words and keep the drawing simple enough
to fit on one page. Ask the client what they notice about the loop, rather than telling them.

## Treatment targets it implies

The model points to three targets. First, change behavior that keeps the loop going, for example through
behavioral activation. Second, help the client notice and test automatic thoughts. Third, once mood lifts,
work on rules and core beliefs to reduce the risk of relapse [Persons-2008]. Track change with a repeated
measure and review the drawing as treatment goes on, so the client can see which parts have shifted.
