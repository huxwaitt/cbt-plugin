**Bottom line:** For posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), give the free PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5) weekly: 31 to 33 suggests probable PTSD and a 10-point drop suggests response. For obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), the clinician-rated Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) is the reference severity measure, with a 35% reduction marking response; self-report scales such as the Obsessive-Compulsive Inventory-Revised (OCI-R) suit weekly tracking. DSM-5 here is the 2013 Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fifth edition.

## What it measures

- **PCL-5:** the 20 DSM-5 PTSD symptoms, one item each [NCPTSD-PCL-5].
- **Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for DSM-5 (CAPS-5):** a structured diagnostic interview giving diagnosis and total severity [Weathers-etal-2018].
- **International Trauma Questionnaire (ITQ):** the core features of PTSD and complex PTSD as defined in the International Classification of Diseases, 11th revision (ICD-11) [Cloitre-etal-2018].
- **Y-BOCS:** severity of obsessions and compulsions regardless of their content, through a clinician-rated semistructured interview covering the past week [Goodman-etal-1989] [Storch-etal-2015].
- **OCI-R:** obsessive-compulsive symptoms across six subscales [Foa-etal-2002].
- **Dimensional Obsessive-Compulsive Scale (DOCS):** the four most replicated OCD symptom dimensions [Abramowitz-etal-2010].
- **Saving Inventory-Revised (SI-R):** hoarding, with difficulty discarding, clutter and excessive acquisition factors [Frost-etal-2004].

## Format and scoring

| Measure | Format | Range | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| PCL-5 | 20 items, 0 (not at all) to 4 (extremely); past month or past week | 0 to 80 | 5 to 10 minutes [NCPTSD-PCL-5] |
| CAPS-5 | Clinician interview | Severity total | Trained interviewer [Weathers-etal-2018] |
| ITQ | 12 symptom items | Diagnostic algorithm | Brief [ITQ-Site] |
| Y-BOCS | 10 items, 0 to 4; obsession and compulsion subtotals | 0 to 40 | Interview [Goodman-etal-1989] |
| OCI-R | 18 items, 6 subscales | See source | Self-report [Foa-etal-2002] |
| DOCS | 20 items, 4 dimensions | See source | Self-report [Abramowitz-etal-2010] |

PCL-5 cluster sums: B items 1 to 5, C items 6 to 7, D items 8 to 14, E items 15 to 20. For a provisional diagnosis, count items rated 2 or higher and require at least 1 B, 1 C, 2 D and 2 E items [NCPTSD-PCL-5].

## Interpreting scores

- **PCL-5 cutoff:** 31 to 33 was optimally efficient for probable PTSD in veterans [Bovin-etal-2016] [NCPTSD-PCL-5]. Use lower cutoffs for screening and higher ones to cut false positives [NCPTSD-PCL-5].
- **PCL-5 change:** a 10-point decrease suggests response, and a score below 28 may indicate clinically significant change [NCPTSD-PCL-5]. Internal consistency was 0.94 and test-retest reliability 0.82 in the development study [Blevins-etal-2015].
- **ITQ:** use the published diagnostic algorithm; the developers say cutoff scores should not be used [ITQ-Site].
- **Y-BOCS severity (empirical, adults):** 0 to 13 mild, 14 to 25 moderate, 26 to 34 moderate to severe, 35 to 40 severe [Storch-etal-2015]. Older bands in wide use were not empirically based [Storch-etal-2015].
- **Y-BOCS change (expert consensus):**
  - Response: 35% or greater reduction plus a Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I) rating of 1 or 2, lasting at least one week.
  - Partial response: 25% to under 35% reduction plus a CGI-I of 3 (minimally improved), lasting at least one week.
  - Remission: no longer meeting OCD criteria on a structured interview or, if that is not feasible, a score of 12 or less plus a CGI-Severity rating of 1 or 2, for at least one week. Recovery is remission lasting at least one year.
  - Relapse after remission: 13 or more plus a CGI-I of 6 or higher for at least one month. Relapse after response alone: loss of the 35% reduction plus a CGI-I of 6 or higher for at least one month [Mataix-Cols-etal-2016].
- **OCI-R:** the developers derived screening cut scores [Foa-etal-2002], and a clinical replication found it discriminated OCD from other anxiety disorders [Abramowitz-Deacon-2006]. Use the published cut score from the original paper rather than a remembered number.

## Licensing and access

- PCL-5: developed by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs National Center for PTSD; public domain and not copyrighted [NCPTSD-PCL-5].
- ITQ and its child version (ITQ-CA, ages 7 to 17): freely available in the public domain [ITQ-Site].
- Y-BOCS: treat as proprietary; describe scores, never reproduce items. CAPS-5, OCI-R, DOCS and SI-R: obtain from the developers or source papers and confirm terms before electronic use.

## Guidance

- If PCL-5 is 31 or above at intake → record "screen positive for probable PTSD" and confirm with a clinical interview such as the CAPS-5. Because the checklist estimates probability, while the interview establishes diagnosis [Weathers-etal-2018].
- If you monitor PTSD weekly → switch to the past-week PCL-5 and keep the same version throughout. Because mixing past-month and past-week versions muddies the trend [NCPTSD-PCL-5].
- If a client's PCL-5 falls by 10 or more → say so and link it to the work: "Your score has dropped 12 points since we began the memory work." Because 10 points is the suggested response marker [NCPTSD-PCL-5].
- If complex PTSD is a question → give the ITQ and apply its algorithm, not a total-score cutoff. Because the developers advise against cutoffs [ITQ-Site].
- If OCD treatment is planned → rate the Y-BOCS at baseline and end and a self-report weekly. Because consensus outcome rules use the Y-BOCS [Mataix-Cols-etal-2016].
- If an OCD client scores low on symptom counts but reports hours of rituals → trust the interview. Because the Y-BOCS rates time, interference, distress, resistance and control rather than content [Goodman-etal-1989].
