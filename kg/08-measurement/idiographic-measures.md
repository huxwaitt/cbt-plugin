**Bottom line:** Idiographic measures are ratings built around one client's own problem, belief or goal [Haynes-etal-2009]. They make cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) targets concrete: how strongly a thought is believed, how distressing a situation is, how likely a feared outcome seems, how often a behavior happens, and how close a goal is. Use them alongside, not instead of, a standardized measure, and define each anchor in writing so ratings mean the same thing every week.

## What it measures

| Type | What it captures | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Belief rating | How much the client believes a thought or rule right now, 0 to 100% | Thought records, belief change [Beck-2021] |
| Emotion intensity | Strength of an emotion, 0 to 100 | Thought records [Beck-2021] |
| Subjective Units of Distress Scale (SUDS) | Momentary distress or discomfort | Exposure hierarchies; global distress [Tanner-2012] |
| Expectancy rating | Likelihood the feared outcome will happen, 0 to 100 | Before and after exposure or behavioral experiments [Craske-etal-2014] |
| Frequency or duration count | How often or how long a target behavior happens | Rituals, checking, avoidance, binge episodes |
| Goal attainment scaling (GAS) | Progress on individual goals, each on a 5-point scale from -2 to +2 | Planning and review [Turner-Stokes-2009] |
| Goal Based Outcomes (GBO) | Progress toward up to three client goals, 0 to 10 | Services for children, young people and families [CORC-GBO] |

Idiographic measurement is used because people differ in which symptoms and causes matter most for them, so a standard scale can include variance irrelevant to that person [Haynes-etal-2009].

## Format and scoring

- **Belief and emotion ratings:** ask "How much do you believe that right now, from 0 to 100%?" before and after examining the thought [Beck-2021].
- **SUDS:** agree on anchors, for example 0 = completely calm, 50 = clearly anxious but manageable, 100 = the worst you can imagine. SUDS ratings of emotional discomfort correlated with clinician ratings of functioning and fell after three months of therapy [Tanner-2012].
- **Expectancy rating:** before an exposure, the client rates how likely the feared outcome is (0 to 100), then afterward states whether it happened and how they know [Craske-etal-2014].
- **GAS:** write five levels per goal from -2 to +2 and describe every level in detail; one review recommends setting the starting level at -2, even when worsening is possible [Krasny-Pacini-etal-2013]. Scores can be combined into a T-score with a mean of 50 when goals are met exactly and a standard deviation near 10 [Turner-Stokes-2009], a method that originated in community mental health program evaluation [Kiresuk-Sherman-1968]. Because GAS data are ordinal, summarize with medians and use the T-score with caution [Krasny-Pacini-etal-2013].
- **GBO:** agree up to three goals, fixed within the first three sessions; 0 = not met at all, 5 = halfway, 10 = fully met; rate at baseline and the end, ideally every session [CORC-GBO].

## Interpreting scores

- A belief rating that falls from 90% to 40% after a thought record shows partial belief change, which is common and useful. Ask what keeps the remaining 40%.
- In exposure, a falling SUDS within one session is not the sign that learning happened. Within-session fear reduction did not predict later outcome in several studies, and trials should end when the expectancy is violated or the behavioral goal is met, not when fear falls [Craske-etal-2014].
- A GBO rise of 2.45 points or more counts as meaningful change, based on reliable change principles [CORC-GBO].
- Idiographic ratings lack norms, so compare a client only with their own baseline.

## Licensing and access

- Belief, emotion, SUDS, expectancy and frequency ratings are clinical techniques with no license.
- GBO is under a Creative Commons license and free for delivering or improving health and social care; adaptations should reference the original [CORC-GBO].
- GAS is a published method; no license is needed to write your own scales.

## Guidance

- If a client says "I sort of believe it" → anchor a number: "If 0 is not at all and 100 is completely, where would you put it right now?" Because a number makes later change visible [Beck-2021].
- If SUDS stays high during an exposure → continue to the planned end point and ask about the prediction: "What did you expect would happen? What actually happened?" Because expectancy violation, not fear reduction, drives new learning [Craske-etal-2014].
- If goals are vague, such as "feel better" → convert them into observable levels: "What would you be doing that you aren't doing now?" Because goal scales need every level described in detail [Krasny-Pacini-etal-2013].
- If the standard measure improves but the client's own goal does not → give the goal priority at the next review. Because personalized targets capture what the person most cares about [Haynes-etal-2009].
- If you build a digital rating tool → store the exact wording of each anchor with the rating. Because idiographic ratings are only comparable over time when anchors stay the same.
