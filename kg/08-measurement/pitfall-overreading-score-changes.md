**Bottom line:** Every questionnaire score contains measurement error, so small changes are often noise. Judge single changes against the measure's reliable change amount, read trends across several sessions rather than one, and report end-of-treatment results as reliable and clinically significant change, not as raw differences or percentages alone.

## What it looks like

- "Your Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) went from 14 to 11, so the behavioral activation is working," after one week.
- A Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) rise from 9 to 11 leads the therapist to abandon the exposure plan.
- A discharge summary claims a "50% improvement" for a client who started at PHQ-9 of 8 and ended at 4.
- A dashboard colors every one-point drop green.

## Why it happens

- Numbers look precise even when they are not.
- Clinicians and clients both want to see progress, and small falls confirm it.
- The reliable change amount is not printed on most forms, so it has to be looked up or computed with the reliable change index [Jacobson-Truax-1991].
- Percentage rules ignore error: a 50% fall from a low score can be a few points.

## The fix

- If a score changes by less than the reliable amount → call it "within the questionnaire's normal ups and downs", for example under 6 points on the PHQ-9 or under 4 on the GAD-7 [Gyani-etal-2013]. Because only changes beyond the error margin can be trusted for one person [Jacobson-Truax-1991].
- If you want a clinical yardstick → use the PHQ-9 minimal clinically important change of 5 points [Lowe-etal-2004], and say which standard you used.
- If a client started near or below the cutoff → do not expect, or claim, reliable improvement; use a more targeted measure. Because in the first year of the English program 1,024 patients started too low to show reliable improvement [Gyani-etal-2013].
- If you report a final outcome → classify it as recovered, reliably improved, no reliable change or reliably deteriorated. Because a reliable change index is preferable to a 50% rule [McMillan-etal-2010].
- If a client just crosses the cutoff → note that they may sit on the boundary and plan relapse prevention. Because clients near the boundary are at risk of slipping back [PSYCTC-RCSC].
- If a single score jumps → check for a life event or a misread item before changing the plan, and look again next week, unless a risk item rose. Because one reading cannot be separated from error [Jacobson-Truax-1991].
- Say to clients: "Scores bounce around a bit week to week. We'll look at the overall direction over a few weeks."

## Sources note

Thresholds are from the English National Health Service (NHS) Talking Therapies method, which applies the Jacobson and Truax approach to PHQ-9 and GAD-7 [Gyani-etal-2013]. For other measures, compute the threshold from published reliability and standard deviation [PSYCTC-RCSC].
