**Bottom line:** Any nonzero answer on a questionnaire's self-harm or suicide item needs a direct conversation in the same session, before other agenda items. Score forms before the session starts, set up same-day review for forms completed remotely, and tell clients in advance that forms are not monitored in real time. The item opens a risk assessment; it does not replace one.

## What it looks like

- Forms are filed and scored at the end of the day; item 9 of the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) was marked "more than half the days".
- A therapist looks only at the total score, which went down, and misses that the risk item went up.
- A client completes a weekly app check-in on Friday night; the endorsed item is seen on Monday.
- The therapist asks about the item, the client says "I'd never do anything", and nothing is recorded.

## Why it happens

- Totals draw attention; single items do not. The PHQ-9 total sums nine items [Kroenke-etal-2001], so one risk item can rise while the total falls.
- Routines score after the session instead of before it, although measurement-based care expects review before or during the encounter [Lewis-etal-2019].
- Clinicians underrate the item. In a large health system, one-year risk of a suicide attempt was 0.4% for people answering "not at all" and 4% for those answering "nearly every day", and the excess risk grew for months [Simon-etal-2013].
- General distress measures carry risk items too, such as the one risk item in the Clinical Outcomes in Routine Evaluation-10 (CORE-10) [CORE-Site], and these get treated as ordinary items.

## The fix

- If any risk item is above zero → ask in the same session, before the agenda: "On the questionnaire you marked that you've had thoughts of being better off dead or of hurting yourself. Can you tell me about those thoughts this week?" Then follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`. Because item response predicts later attempts independent of overall severity [Simon-etal-2013].
- If the client minimizes → thank them, ask about frequency, intent and plans in plain words, and record the answers. Because the answer, not the reassurance, is the clinical information.
- If risk is current or rising → follow `02-risk.safety-planning` and `02-risk.crisis-response`; in an emergency call 911 (United States) or 999 (United Kingdom). Give crisis contacts: United States, call or text 988 (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline); United Kingdom, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123. Because imminent risk needs same-day escalation, not the next session.
- If forms are completed remotely → set an alert for endorsed risk items, a named reviewer, and a same-day response standard, and tell clients: "These forms aren't watched around the clock. If you're in danger, call or text 988 or call 911." Because the excess risk emerges over several days [Simon-etal-2013].
- If you design a tool → flag risk items separately from totals, never let a falling total hide a rising risk item, and route endorsements to a human. Because one risk item can rise while the total falls [Kroenke-etal-2001].
- Record the item response, what you asked, the client's answers, your risk judgment and the actions taken (see `02-risk.risk-documentation`).

## Sources note

The predictive data come from a cohort of 84,418 outpatients who completed 207,265 PHQ-9 questionnaires [Simon-etal-2013]. Item response raises risk at the group level; it cannot predict an individual's behavior, which is why a full assessment must follow.
