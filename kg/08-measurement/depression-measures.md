**Bottom line:** Use the free Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) every session for most adults: 10 or more suggests probable major depression, and a drop of 6 or more points exceeds measurement error. The Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II) is a licensed alternative, the Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology (QIDS-SR16) a brief one, and the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) suits pregnancy and the postpartum period. Any endorsement of the PHQ-9 self-harm item needs a same-session risk conversation.

## What it measures

- **PHQ-9:** the nine depression symptom criteria of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fourth edition (DSM-IV), over the past two weeks [Kroenke-etal-2001]. The PHQ-2 is its first two items, depressed mood and loss of interest [Kroenke-etal-2003].
- **BDI-II:** depression intensity, ages 13 to 80 [Pearson-BDI-II], over a two-week window [Wang-Gorenstein-2013].
- **QIDS-SR16:** the nine depression symptom domains, built from 16 items [IDS-QIDS-Site]. Total score times about 1.3 predicts the 17-item Hamilton depression score [Rush-etal-2003].
- **EPDS:** postnatal depression in about five minutes [Cox-etal-1987]; accuracy is similar in pregnant and postpartum women [Levis-etal-2020a].

## Format and scoring

| Measure | Items and scale | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| PHQ-9 | 9 items, 0 (not at all) to 3 (nearly every day) | 0 to 27 | Sum items [Kroenke-etal-2001] |
| PHQ-2 | 2 items, 0 to 3 | 0 to 6 | Sum items [Kroenke-etal-2003] |
| BDI-II | 21 items, 0 to 3 | 0 to 63 | About 5 minutes [Pearson-BDI-II] |
| QIDS-SR16 | 16 items, 0 to 3 | 0 to 27 | Take the highest of the 4 sleep items, the highest of the 4 appetite and weight items, the higher of the 2 psychomotor items, then add the 6 single items [IDS-QIDS-Site] |
| EPDS | 10 items | See key | Use the official scoring key |

## Interpreting scores

- **PHQ-9 severity:** 5, 10, 15 and 20 mark mild, moderate, moderately severe and severe depression [Kroenke-etal-2001].
- **PHQ-9 screening:** 10 or more best balances sensitivity (0.88) and specificity (0.85) against semistructured interviews, and can be used regardless of age [Levis-etal-2019]. National Health Service (NHS) Talking Therapies in England uses 10 or more as caseness [Clark-etal-2018].
- **PHQ-9 change:** the minimal clinically important individual change is 5 points [Lowe-etal-2004]. NHS Talking Therapies counts a change greater than 5.20, so 6 or more points, as reliable [Gyani-etal-2013]. The older response rule (final score 9 or less plus a 50% fall) is less preferable than a reliable change index [McMillan-etal-2010].
- **PHQ-2:** 3 or more had sensitivity 83% and specificity 92% for major depression [Kroenke-etal-2003]. Follow a positive PHQ-2 with the full PHQ-9.
- **BDI-II bands (manual):** 0 to 13 minimal, 14 to 19 mild, 20 to 28 moderate, 29 to 63 severe. Cutoffs for major depression varied from 10 to 16 in nonclinical, 7 to 20 in medical and 19 to 31 in psychiatric samples [Wang-Gorenstein-2013].
- **QIDS-SR16 bands:** 0 to 5 none, 6 to 10 mild, 11 to 15 moderate, 16 to 20 severe, 21 to 27 very severe [IDS-QIDS-Site].
- **EPDS:** 11 or more maximized combined sensitivity (0.81) and specificity (0.88); 13 or more is more specific (0.95) but less sensitive (0.66) [Levis-etal-2020a].
- **PHQ-9 item 9 (thoughts of death or self-harm):** cumulative one-year risk of a suicide attempt was about 0.4% for "not at all" and about 4% for "nearly every day". The item stayed a strong predictor after adjusting for total severity [Simon-etal-2013].

## Licensing and access

- PHQ-9, PHQ-2: free; no permission is required to reproduce, translate, display or distribute them [PHQ-Screeners].
- BDI-II: proprietary, sold by Pearson to qualification level B purchasers, with paid digital administrations [Pearson-BDI-II]. Never reproduce its items in notes, handouts or software.
- QIDS-SR16: the official site shows a copyright notice but posts no terms of use [IDS-QIDS-Site]. Confirm permission before building it into software.
- EPDS: confirm current terms with the rights holder before electronic use.

## Guidance

- If PHQ-9 item 9 is scored 1 or higher → ask directly in the same session before other agenda items: "You marked that you've had thoughts that you'd be better off dead or of hurting yourself. Can you tell me more about those thoughts this week?" Then follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`. Because item 9 response predicts suicide attempts over months, independent of total severity [Simon-etal-2013].
- If item 9 is endorsed on a form completed between sessions by app → review it the same day, and tell clients in advance that forms are not monitored in real time and give crisis contacts (United States: call or text 988, or 911; United Kingdom: 999, NHS 111, Samaritans 116 123). Because the excess risk emerges within days [Simon-etal-2013].
- If the PHQ-9 total falls 2 to 4 points → call it "a small change that may be noise" and watch the trend. Because the minimal clinically important individual change is 5 points [Lowe-etal-2004].
- If you use the BDI-II in medical patients → do not apply psychiatric-sample cutoffs. Because BDI-II cutoffs differ widely by sample type [Wang-Gorenstein-2013].
- If the client is perinatal → use the EPDS and record which cutoff you chose and why. Because cutoff choice trades false negatives against false positives [Levis-etal-2020a].
- If you report outcomes in England → use PHQ-9 caseness of 10 and reliable change of 6. Because these match national reporting [Clark-etal-2018] [Gyani-etal-2013].
