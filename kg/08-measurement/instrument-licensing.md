**Bottom line:** "Free to use" covers several different legal positions. Some measures are public domain, some are copyrighted but free with no permission needed, some are free only on paper or only for noncommercial use, and some are sold to qualified purchasers. Check the current terms from the rights holder before copying, translating or building a measure into software, and never reproduce the items of a proprietary instrument.

## What it is

Every questionnaire is someone's written work. Its license says who may copy it, change it, translate it, display it on a screen, charge for it, or score it automatically. Four broad groups cover most measures used in cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT):

| Group | Examples | What the terms say |
|---|---|---|
| Public domain | Posttraumatic stress disorder checklist (PCL-5); International Trauma Questionnaire (ITQ); Depression Anxiety Stress Scales (DASS) | PCL-5 is "in the public domain and not copyrighted" [NCPTSD-PCL-5]. ITQ and its child version are freely available in the public domain [ITQ-Site]. DASS is public domain, with a paid manual [DASS-Site]. |
| Copyrighted, free, no permission needed | Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9), Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) | "No permission is required to reproduce, translate, display or distribute them" [PHQ-Screeners]. |
| Free with conditions | Clinical Outcomes in Routine Evaluation-10 (CORE-10); Eating Disorder Examination Questionnaire (EDE-Q); Outcome and Session Rating Scales (ORS, SRS); Revised Child Anxiety and Depression Scale (RCADS); Goal Based Outcomes (GBO) | CORE measures: free if unchanged, attributed and not the main source of profit [CORE-Site]. EDE-Q: free for noncommercial research use [CREDO-Site]. ORS and SRS: free on paper for individuals, no electronic use [ORS-SRS-Site]. RCADS: free under a limited license that is not perpetual, with no adaptations [RCADS-Site]. GBO: Creative Commons, free for health and social care [CORC-GBO]. |
| Proprietary, purchased | Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II); Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI); Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) | BDI-II is sold by Pearson to level B qualified purchasers, with per-use digital fees [Pearson-BDI-II]. BAI is copyrighted and sold by Pearson; HADS is copyrighted [Julian-2011]. |

Some sites show a copyright notice without posting terms, as with the Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology [IDS-QIDS-Site]. Treat silence as "ask first", not as permission.

## Why it matters in CBT

- Measurement-based care depends on giving the same measure every session, so the license must cover repeated, possibly electronic, use for every client.
- Electronic rights are often separate. A measure free on paper may be barred from apps and electronic health records without a paid license [ORS-SRS-Site].
- Changing a measure can breach its license. The CORE System Trust forbids changes to wording, item order or response options, and calls an unapproved translation a copyright violation [CORE-Site].
- Tools built on artificial intelligence (AI) add risk: a model that reproduces proprietary item text in chat, summaries or generated handouts is copying the instrument.

## Key details

- **Who may buy.** Publishers restrict some measures to purchasers with certain qualifications; Pearson lists the BDI-II at qualification level B [Pearson-BDI-II].
- **Attribution.** Keep the copyright line on every page where required (CORE: "© CORE System Trust") [CORE-Site].
- **Translations.** Use approved translations; the PHQ site distributes translations free [PHQ-Screeners], while CORE requires approved translations [CORE-Site].
- **Commercial use.** "Noncommercial" terms usually exclude products sold mainly because they contain the measure [CORE-Site]. Ask about anything you will charge for.
- **Currency.** Terms change. Record the date you checked and the page you read.
- **United States and United Kingdom.** Copyright rules differ in detail between countries; follow the rights holder's terms and your organization's legal advice.

## In the session

- If you hand a client a proprietary form → use the licensed original, not a retyped copy, and keep completed forms in the clinical record. Because a retyped copy reproduces the items outside the license [Pearson-BDI-II].
- If you design a tool that discusses scores → have it show the score, band and trend, and refer to item content in general terms (for example, "the sleep question") rather than quoting proprietary wording. Because generated text that quotes items copies the instrument.
- If a client wants a copy of their completed questionnaire → treat it as part of their record and follow your records policy (see `14-ethics-law.record-keeping`). Because access to records is governed by records law, not by the instrument license.
- If no free measure fits → use a short idiographic rating (see `08-measurement.idiographic-measures`). Because ratings you write yourself carry no license cost.
