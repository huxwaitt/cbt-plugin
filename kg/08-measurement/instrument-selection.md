**Bottom line:** Pick one brief, validated primary symptom measure matched to the main problem and give it every session. Add a functioning measure, and a longer disorder-specific measure at baseline, midpoint and end if it tracks the target better. Check that each measure is validated for the client's age and setting and that your license covers how you will use it. Screeners suggest possible cases; they do not diagnose.

## When to use

- At the assessment session, once the main problem is named (see `01-intake.presenting-problem`).
- When the focus of treatment changes, for example from depression to an anxiety disorder.
- When a service or digital tool sets its default battery.

## Steps

1. Name the main problem and any major comorbid problem.
2. Choose the every-session measure from the table. Prefer short (under five minutes), free, sensitive to change.
3. Add a functioning measure such as the Work and Social Adjustment Scale (WSAS), which is reliable, valid and sensitive to treatment change across disorders [Mundt-etal-2002].
4. Add a disorder-specific or interview measure for baseline, midpoint and end if the target is narrower than the brief measure.
5. Add one personal rating for the client's own target (see `08-measurement.idiographic-measures`).
6. Check validation for age, language and setting, and check the license (see `08-measurement.instrument-licensing`).
7. Write the decision rules in advance: the reliable change amount and cutoff for each measure.

| Main problem | Every session | Baseline, midpoint, end |
|---|---|---|
| Depression | Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) | Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II, licensed) or Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology (QIDS-SR16) |
| Generalized or mixed anxiety | Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) | Penn State Worry Questionnaire (PSWQ) |
| Panic disorder | Panic Disorder Severity Scale, self-report (PDSS-SR) | Anxiety Sensitivity Index-3 (ASI-3) |
| Social anxiety | Social Phobia Inventory (SPIN) | Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale (LSAS) |
| Health anxiety | Short Health Anxiety Inventory (SHAI) | Same |
| Posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) | PTSD Checklist for the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fifth edition (DSM-5) (PCL-5), past-week version | Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for DSM-5 (CAPS-5) |
| Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) | Obsessive-Compulsive Inventory-Revised (OCI-R) or Dimensional Obsessive-Compulsive Scale (DOCS) | Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) |
| Insomnia | Sleep diary | Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) |
| Alcohol or drug use | Use log | Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test (AUDIT), Drug Abuse Screening Test (DAST-10) |
| Youth anxiety or depression | Revised Child Anxiety and Depression Scale (RCADS) subscale | RCADS full, parent and youth |

The PCL-5 has a past-week version suited to weekly monitoring [NCPTSD-PCL-5]. The RCADS covers ages 8 to 18 with youth and parent forms [RCADS-Site].

## What to record

- Each measure, version and schedule, with one line on why it was chosen.
- Baseline scores and the decision rules (cutoff, reliable change amount).
- Adaptations such as reading items aloud, or use of an approved translation.

## Guidance

- If anxiety is the main problem but the specific disorder is clear → add a disorder-specific measure to the GAD-7. Because the GAD-7 also detects panic, social anxiety and PTSD [Kroenke-etal-2010], so it may not track the narrower target, and England's National Health Service (NHS) Talking Therapies lets services use disorder-specific anxiety measures for this reason [Clark-etal-2018].
- If a screening score is above cutoff → confirm with a clinical interview before recording a diagnosis. Because even the PHQ-9 at 10 or more has specificity of 0.85, so false positives are expected [Levis-etal-2019].
- If the client is pregnant or postpartum → use the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) with a cutoff of 11 or more, or 13 or more when you want fewer false positives. Because those cutoffs maximized combined accuracy and specificity respectively in pregnant and postpartum women [Levis-etal-2020a].
- If the client has limited literacy → read the items aloud and record that you did. Because interviewer and self-report GAD-7 versions agreed well [Spitzer-etal-2006].
- If you are building a digital tool → confirm electronic rights for every measure. Because some measures that are free on paper forbid any electronic use without a separate license [ORS-SRS-Site].
- If an adult client may have attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) → use the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS) screener only at assessment. Because it was built for screening, not for tracking change [Kessler-etal-2005a].
- If the battery takes more than about ten minutes per session → cut it. Because infrequent or burdensome measurement is a known failure mode of measurement-based care [Fortney-etal-2017].

## Common problems

- **Too many measures.** Completion falls and scores stop being discussed. Keep one every-session measure.
- **Measures used outside their validated group.** Adult cutoffs applied to adolescents, or primary care cutoffs applied to inpatients.
- **Using a screener as the outcome.** A screener's yes or no does not show size of change.
- **Picking a proprietary scale without a license.** Check before the first use, not after.
