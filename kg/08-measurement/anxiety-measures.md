**Bottom line:** The free Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) is the default every-session anxiety measure: 10 or more suggests a probable anxiety disorder, and a drop of 4 or more points exceeds measurement error. Because it picks up several anxiety disorders, add a disorder-specific measure when the target is panic, social anxiety or health anxiety. Many specific scales are copyrighted, so check terms before electronic use.

## What it measures

- **GAD-7:** generalized anxiety symptoms; it also detects panic, social anxiety and posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) [Kroenke-etal-2010]. The GAD-2 is its first two items.
- **Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI):** anxiety symptoms over the past week [Julian-2011].
- **Overall Anxiety Severity and Impairment Scale (OASIS):** severity and impairment across any anxiety disorder or several at once [Norman-etal-2006].
- **Panic Disorder Severity Scale (PDSS):** panic disorder severity, clinician-rated [Shear-etal-1997], with a self-report form (PDSS-SR) [Houck-etal-2002].
- **Anxiety Sensitivity Index-3 (ASI-3):** fear of arousal sensations, with physical, cognitive and social concerns subscales [Taylor-etal-2007].
- **Social Phobia Inventory (SPIN):** fear, avoidance and physiological symptoms of social anxiety [Connor-etal-2000]. **Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale (LSAS):** social anxiety across situations, here assessor-administered [Mennin-etal-2002].
- **Health Anxiety Inventory (HAI) and its short form (SHAI):** health anxiety up to hypochondriasis [Salkovskis-etal-2002]; factor studies of the SHAI disagree [Alberts-etal-2013].
- **Penn State Worry Questionnaire (PSWQ):** trait worry [Meyer-etal-1990]. **Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale-12 (IUS-12):** anxious and avoidance components of intolerance of uncertainty [Carleton-etal-2007].
- **Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS):** anxiety and depression in medical settings [Bjelland-etal-2002]. **Depression Anxiety Stress Scales (DASS-21):** depression, anxiety and stress, 7 items each [DASS-Site].
- **Youth:** the Screen for Child Anxiety Related Emotional Disorders (SCARED), child and parent forms [Birmaher-etal-1997], and the Revised Child Anxiety and Depression Scale (RCADS), ages 8 to 18 [RCADS-Site].

## Interpreting scores

- **GAD-7:** 5, 10 and 15 mark mild, moderate and severe; 10 or more is the optimal screening cutpoint, and 3 or more on the GAD-2 [Kroenke-etal-2010]. The development study's cutpoint gave sensitivity 89% and specificity 82% [Spitzer-etal-2006]. England's National Health Service (NHS) Talking Therapies uses 8 or more as caseness [Clark-etal-2018] and a change greater than 3.53, so 4 or more points, as reliable [Gyani-etal-2013].
- **BAI:** one published band set is 0 to 9 normal, 10 to 18 mild to moderate, 19 to 29 moderate to severe, 30 to 63 severe [Julian-2011]. Band sets differ, so use your licensed manual's bands and record which.
- **OASIS:** 8 or more correctly classified 87% of primary care patients as having an anxiety disorder or not [Campbell-Sills-etal-2009].
- **PDSS (clinician-rated):** without agoraphobia, 0 to 1 normal, 2 to 5 borderline, 6 to 9 slightly ill, 10 to 13 moderately ill, 14 or more markedly ill. With agoraphobia, 3 to 7 borderline, 8 to 10 slightly, 11 to 15 moderately, 16 or more markedly ill. Remission is 5 or less; response is a 40% or greater reduction [Furukawa-etal-2009].
- **SPIN:** 19 distinguished social phobia from controls [Connor-etal-2000].
- **LSAS:** 30 for social anxiety disorder and 60 for the generalized subtype [Mennin-etal-2002].
- **SHAI:** no well-established cutoff; cutoff research is still needed [Alberts-etal-2013]. Track change from baseline instead.
- **HADS:** 8 or more on either subscale is the usual caseness threshold, with sensitivity and specificity near 0.80 [Bjelland-etal-2002].

## Licensing and access

- GAD-7, GAD-2: free, no permission needed [PHQ-Screeners].
- DASS: public domain; the manual is sold separately [DASS-Site].
- RCADS: copyrighted, free under a limited license [RCADS-Site].
- BAI: copyrighted, sold by Pearson [Julian-2011]. HADS: copyrighted [Julian-2011]. Do not reproduce their items.
- PDSS, SPIN, LSAS, SHAI, PSWQ, IUS-12, ASI-3, OASIS: confirm terms with the developer or publisher before embedding in software.

## Guidance

- If GAD-7 is high but the formulation points to panic, social or health anxiety → add the PDSS-SR, SPIN or SHAI and track both. Because the GAD-7 is broad [Kroenke-etal-2010].
- If a panic client also has agoraphobia → interpret PDSS bands with the agoraphobia table. Because the same score means less severity when agoraphobia is present [Furukawa-etal-2009].
- If you use the PDSS-SR weekly → treat clinician-rated bands as a rough guide. Because they come from the clinician version [Furukawa-etal-2009], though the self-report form is reliable (test-retest 0.81) and sensitive to cognitive behavioral therapy [Houck-etal-2002].
- If a health-anxious client asks "what is a normal score?" → "There isn't an agreed cutoff for this one, so we'll watch how your own score moves." Because SHAI cutoffs are not established [Alberts-etal-2013].
- If the client is a child → collect youth and parent reports and expect modest agreement. Because SCARED parent and child scores correlated only 0.20 to 0.47 [Birmaher-etal-1997].
