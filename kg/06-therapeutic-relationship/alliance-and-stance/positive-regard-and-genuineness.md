**Bottom line:** Positive regard means valuing and caring about the client as a person, and genuineness (congruence) means being authentic, so that what you say matches what you think and do. Both predict outcome: positive regard g = .28 across 64 studies, and congruence r = .23 across 21 studies [Farber-etal-2018; Kolden-etal-2018]. In cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) they look like steady warmth that does not depend on the client's compliance, plus honest, respectful feedback.

## The principle

**Positive regard** is a consistent message that the therapist values, cares about and believes in the client. It does not need to be felt at every moment, and it is not the same as compliments or shallow praise [Norcross-Karpiak-2025]. It sits naturally beside the CBT stance: the person is accepted, while specific thoughts and behaviors are examined.

**Genuineness** has two sides, as described in its meta-analysis [Kolden-etal-2018].
- Inward: the therapist is aware of their own reactions and is authentic rather than playing a role.
- Outward: the therapist can respectfully and transparently put that experience into words when it helps.

Put simply, the therapist's actions match their words and values [Norcross-Karpiak-2025]. Empathy works best when offered together with positive regard and genuineness, because it is unlikely to help unless grounded in real caring [Elliott-etal-2018].

The opposite of regard is not neutrality but subtle hostility. In one small process study (14 therapy dyads), the number of subtly hostile and controlling therapist statements was strongly correlated with the number of self-blaming statements by patients [Henry-etal-1990].

## Guidance

- If the client discloses something shameful → respond with steady acceptance of the person before any problem-solving. Because positive regard and affirmation predict outcome [Farber-etal-2018; Norcross-Karpiak-2025].
- If homework was not done → respond with curiosity about obstacles, not disappointment or a lecture. Because comments experienced as critical or blaming are among the behaviors that do not work [Norcross-Karpiak-2025].
- If you notice irritation, boredom or discouragement in yourself → name it privately first, treat it as information about the interaction, and take it to supervision; share it only if it serves the client and can be said with respect. Because congruence requires awareness of your own experience and respectful transparency [Kolden-etal-2018].
- If the client asks whether you really think they can get better → answer honestly and specifically. Because congruence means words match beliefs [Kolden-etal-2018], and the client's own expectation of benefit predicts outcome [Constantino-etal-2018a].
- If you are tempted to praise everything → make affirmation specific and earned. Because praise that does not match your real view is not congruent [Kolden-etal-2018].
- If the client's values or culture differ from yours → convey respect and curiosity rather than correcting their worldview. Because imposing the therapist's cultural beliefs ("cultural arrogance") is ineffective [Norcross-Karpiak-2025].
- If you design scripted or automated responses → keep warmth constant regardless of whether the user did the task. Because responses experienced as critical, rejecting or blaming are among the relational behaviors that do not work [Norcross-Karpiak-2025].

## Example phrasings

- "I'm glad you came today, especially since you nearly canceled."
- "Thank you for telling me that. I know it took a lot. It doesn't change how I see you."
- "You did half the plan. Tell me about the half that happened, then what got in the way of the rest."
- "I notice I've been pushing hard today. I want to check whether that's been helpful or too much."
- "Honestly, yes, I think things can change. Here's what makes me think so."
- Avoid: "You didn't do it again?"
- Avoid: "That's amazing!" for every small report.
- Avoid: "I'm not judging you, but..." followed by a judgment.

## Evidence

- Positive regard: 64 studies, g = .28; a multilevel analysis that controlled for repeated samples gave g = .36 [Farber-etal-2018].
- Congruence: 21 studies, 1,192 patients, r = .23 (95% confidence interval .13 to .32), about d = .46 [Kolden-etal-2018].
- The American Psychological Association task force rated positive regard and affirmation "demonstrably effective" and congruence "probably effective" [Norcross-Karpiak-2025].
- Both constructs were developed most fully in the person-centered tradition [Kolden-etal-2018]. The evidence is correlational, and applying it in CBT rests on expert consensus rather than CBT-specific trials.
