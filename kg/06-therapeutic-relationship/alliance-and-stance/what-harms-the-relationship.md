**Bottom line:** The behaviors that damage the relationship are predictable: hostile or critical comments, rigid over-structuring, confrontation, pushing cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) techniques through a strain, assuming you know how the client sees therapy, and imposing your own cultural views [Norcross-Karpiak-2025; Ackerman-Hilsenroth-2001; Castonguay-etal-1996]. About 1 in 5 adult clients drop out of therapy, and weaker alliances predict dropout [Swift-Greenberg-2012; Sharf-etal-2010]. Most of these behaviors are subtle, so watch for them in yourself and in recordings.

## The principle

The American Psychological Association task force identified six relational behaviors that do not work, shown below [Norcross-Karpiak-2025].

| Behavior | What it looks like in CBT |
|---|---|
| Confrontation | Arguing a client out of ambivalence about change |
| Negative processes | Comments heard as hostile, critical, rejecting or blaming |
| Assumptions | Deciding the client is satisfied without asking |
| Therapist-centricity | Trusting your view of the session over the client's |
| Rigidity | Following the agenda or protocol whatever the client needs |
| Cultural arrogance | Treating your cultural beliefs as the standard |

A review of alliance research adds therapist attributes (rigid, uncertain, critical, distant, tense, distracted) and techniques (over-structuring, inappropriate self-disclosure, unyielding interpretation, inappropriate silence) that weaken the alliance [Ackerman-Hilsenroth-2001]. The positive mirror image is a therapist who is flexible, honest, respectful, warm, interested and open [Ackerman-Hilsenroth-2003].

Two findings matter especially for CBT. First, when cognitive therapists responded to alliance strain by focusing harder on distorted cognitions, that focus correlated with worse outcome [Castonguay-etal-1996]. Second, subtly hostile and controlling therapist statements were strongly correlated with patients' self-blaming statements [Henry-etal-1990], so a critical tone can feed the very self-criticism CBT is trying to change.

## Guidance

- If homework was not done → explore obstacles with curiosity instead of restating why homework matters. Because comments experienced as critical or blaming are among the behaviors that do not work [Norcross-Karpiak-2025].
- If the client is distressed and the agenda says "thought record" → adjust the plan with the client. Because over-structuring the therapy weakens the alliance [Ackerman-Hilsenroth-2001].
- If the client pushes back on a technique → stop and explore instead of explaining the rationale more forcefully. Because adherence used to fix strain appeared to worsen it [Castonguay-etal-1996].
- If the client is ambivalent about changing drinking or drug use → use an evoking, motivational style rather than confrontation. Because a review of the addictions field found confrontation ineffective in all 12 trials identified [Norcross-Karpiak-2025].
- If you are tired, distracted or watching the clock → name it to yourself and re-engage, or reschedule if you cannot. Because a distracted or distant therapist weakens the alliance [Ackerman-Hilsenroth-2001].
- If you use silence → make sure it serves the client's reflection, not your uncertainty. Because inappropriate silence harms the alliance [Ackerman-Hilsenroth-2001].
- If the client's values differ from yours → ask how they see it and adapt the work. Because cultural arrogance is ineffective [Norcross-Karpiak-2025].
- If the client is young, has a personality or eating disorder, or you are a trainee → check the alliance early and often. Because these groups have higher dropout rates [Swift-Greenberg-2012].
- If a strain happens and you contributed → acknowledge it. Because ratings of the therapist's contribution to ruptures predicted dropout [Eubanks-etal-2019], and accepting responsibility rather than blaming the client aids repair [Safran-etal-2011].

## Example phrasings

| Avoid | Try instead |
|---|---|
| "You need to do the homework if you want to get better." | "What got in the way this week? Let's see if the task needs changing." |
| "That's a cognitive distortion." | "That thought sounds really convincing right now. Shall we look at it together?" |
| "We have to stick to the agenda." | "I can see today is hard. Do you want to change what we focus on?" |
| "You're in denial about your drinking." | "Part of you wants to cut down and part of you doesn't. Tell me about both." |
| "I'm sure the sessions are going fine." | "What's one thing I could do differently to make these sessions more useful?" |

## Evidence

- Dropout: 669 studies, 83,834 clients, weighted dropout rate 19.7%; higher for younger clients, personality or eating disorder diagnoses, and trainee clinicians [Swift-Greenberg-2012].
- Weaker alliance and dropout: d = .55 across 11 studies [Sharf-etal-2010].
- Confrontation markers (d = .74) and the therapist's contribution to ruptures (d = .61) predicted dropout in early CBT sessions [Eubanks-etal-2019].
- Most of this evidence is correlational or comes from narrative reviews; the "does not work" list is an expert task force consensus.
