**Bottom line:** Brief, relevant self-disclosure that serves the client, followed by a quick return of focus to the client, is usually followed by a stronger relationship and client gains, while inappropriate disclosure, such as disclosure driven by the therapist's own needs, weakens the alliance [Hill-etal-2018; Goldfried-etal-2003; Ackerman-Hilsenroth-2001]. Boundaries are flexible standards judged in context: a crossing (such as accompanying a client on an exposure task) can help, while a violation exploits the client [Gutheil-Gabbard-1993]. Sexual intimacy with a current client is always prohibited [APA-Ethics-2017].

## The principle

**Self-disclosure** is the therapist revealing personal information, feelings or experiences. **Immediacy** is talking about what is happening between therapist and client right now [Hill-etal-2018]. In cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), disclosure is used to give feedback on the client's interpersonal impact, raise positive expectations and motivation, strengthen the bond, normalize reactions, reduce fears, and model effective coping. The therapist should check their own motive before disclosing [Goldfried-etal-2003].

**Boundaries** cover role, time, place and space, money, gifts and services, clothing, language, self-disclosure and physical contact [Gutheil-Gabbard-1993]. A crossing is a departure from usual practice that may be harmless or helpful; a violation is harmful and exploits the client's vulnerable position. Overly rigid, defensive boundaries can also lead to worse care [Gutheil-Gabbard-1998].

For psychologists in the United States, the American Psychological Association (APA) Ethics Code says a multiple relationship should be avoided when it could reasonably impair objectivity or effectiveness or risk exploitation or harm, but multiple relationships without that risk are not unethical (Standard 3.05). Sexual intimacy with current clients is prohibited (10.05), and with former clients for at least two years after termination, and even then only in the most unusual circumstances (10.08) [APA-Ethics-2017]. Other professions and countries have their own codes; check yours.

## Guidance

- If you are considering a disclosure → ask: Whose need does this serve? Is it brief? Will I hand the focus straight back? Because CBT therapists are advised to examine their own motives and keep appropriate boundaries [Goldfried-etal-2003].
- If the client is harshly self-critical about a common struggle → consider a short similarity disclosure. Because disclosures revealing similarity were rated favorably and increased willingness to return [Henretty-etal-2014].
- If the client asks a personal question → give a brief honest answer or a warm explanation of why you will not, then ask what prompted it. Because in one single-case study of adolescent therapy, deflecting personal questions was coded as therapist withdrawal and was often followed by more client withdrawal [Cirasola-etal-2024].
- If the client's behavior in session affects you (interrupting, apologizing constantly) → use immediacy: describe the impact tentatively and link it to their goals. Because immediacy was most often followed by clients opening up [Hill-etal-2018], and feedback on interpersonal impact is a recognized CBT use [Goldfried-etal-2003].
- If the disclosure would involve your current crises, unresolved trauma, or detailed personal history → do not disclose. Because inappropriate self-disclosure contributes to weaker alliances [Ackerman-Hilsenroth-2001].
- If a crossing is clinically indicated, such as exposure outside the office or a longer session in crisis → state the rationale, get consent, document it, and consult. Because the impact of a crossing can only be judged in its clinical context [Gutheil-Gabbard-1993].
- If an unavoidable multiple relationship arises (small community, shared social media) → assess the risk of impaired objectivity or exploitation, take reasonable steps, and document. Because the code requires reasonable steps to resolve potentially harmful multiple relationships [APA-Ethics-2017].
- If you notice romantic or sexual feelings (yours or the client's) → never act on them and seek supervision promptly. Because sexual intimacy with clients is prohibited [APA-Ethics-2017].

## Example phrasings

- "I've had to practice speaking up in meetings too, and the same thing helped: going in without the script. So what happened on Tuesday?"
- "I keep my personal life out of our sessions so the time stays yours. I'm curious what made you wonder about that."
- "Can I share something I notice happening between us right now? When I ask a question, you apologize before answering."
- Avoid: "Let me tell you about my divorce."
- Avoid: "This is about you, not me." said flatly in reply to every personal question.

## Evidence

- Qualitative meta-analysis of 21 studies: self-disclosure and immediacy were most often followed by an enhanced relationship, improved functioning, insight and helpfulness [Hill-etal-2018].
- Meta-analysis of 53 experimental studies: disclosing counselors were perceived more favorably, especially for similarity disclosures [Henretty-etal-2014].
- The 2018 American Psychological Association task force rated 16 relationship elements as demonstrably or probably effective and placed self-disclosure and immediacy in a lower category, promising but with insufficient research to judge [Norcross-Lambert-2018; Norcross-Karpiak-2025]. Treat disclosure as a judicious option, supported by emerging evidence.
