**Bottom line:** Empathy is understanding what the client is experiencing or trying to express, and showing that understanding in a way the client recognizes. Therapist empathy predicts outcome (r = .28 across 82 samples and 6,138 clients), and the link did not differ by theoretical orientation [Elliott-etal-2018]. In cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), show you understand why something is hard before and while you ask the client to do it, and check your understanding instead of assuming it.

## The principle

According to the leading meta-analysis [Elliott-etal-2018], empathy takes three forms that differ in emphasis rather than kind:

| Form | What the therapist does | Where it shows up in CBT |
|---|---|---|
| Empathic rapport and support | Conveys a compassionate attitude and shows understanding, often to set the context for treatment | Opening sessions, socialization to the model, before homework |
| Communicative attunement | Stays tuned, moment to moment, to what the client is communicating and feeling | Eliciting hot thoughts, noticing affect shifts |
| Person empathy | Understands how the client's history led them to see, feel and act as they do | Case formulation, core belief work |

Empathy is judged from three perspectives: the therapist's felt resonance, what an observer sees expressed, and what the client receives. The client's view predicts outcome best [Elliott-etal-2018]. Simply producing more reflections does not count: studies counting reflections found disappointing links with outcome, and empathic therapists respond to the meaning and impact of an experience rather than parroting words [Elliott-etal-2018].

## Guidance

- If the client describes a distressing event → reflect its personal meaning and impact, not only the facts. Because empathy is attunement to the import of experience, not repetition of content [Elliott-etal-2018].
- If you are not sure you have understood → offer your understanding tentatively and invite correction. Because empathic understanding is best offered tentatively and corrected by the client, since therapists cannot read minds [Elliott-etal-2018].
- If you are about to introduce a technique such as a thought record or exposure task → first name why this is hard for this client. Because empathic rapport sets the context for effective treatment [Elliott-etal-2018], and services should build empathic, non-judgmental relationships as an essential part of care [NICE-CG136].
- If a belief seems distorted → first say how it makes sense given the client's history, then invite testing it. Because person empathy means understanding how experiences led the client to think and feel as they do [Elliott-etal-2018].
- If the client is fragile, hostile, or uneasy with talk about feelings → adjust the dose: fewer feeling reflections, more practical understanding and choice. Because fragile clients can find empathy intrusive, hostile clients can find it directive, and some find a feelings focus foreign [Elliott-etal-2018].
- If you want to know whether the client feels understood → ask directly, or use a client-rated measure. Because client-rated empathy is the strongest predictor of outcome [Elliott-etal-2018].
- If you are designing an automated or scripted response → avoid stock phrases repeated across turns; tie each reflection to the client's specific words and situation. Because empathy is individualized and co-created between the two people [Elliott-etal-2018].

## Example phrasings

- "So when he didn't text back, it wasn't just disappointing. It felt like proof that you don't matter. Is that close?"
- "Let me check I've got this right. The meeting itself was fine, but the hour before it was unbearable?"
- "No wonder you've been avoiding the office if every meeting feels like a test you could fail."
- "Before we look at that thought, I want to say: given what happened with your father, it makes sense that criticism hits this hard."
- Avoid: "I know exactly how you feel." (claims an understanding you have not checked)
- Avoid: "I hear you. That must be hard." repeated without specifics.
- Avoid: Jumping straight to "What's the evidence for that thought?" before the client feels understood.

## Evidence

- Meta-analysis: mean weighted r = .28 (95% confidence interval .23 to .33; about d = .58), 82 independent samples, 6,138 clients; effects were heterogeneous [Elliott-etal-2018].
- Theoretical orientation did not significantly moderate the effect; client, observer and therapist perception measures predicted outcome better than empathic accuracy tasks [Elliott-etal-2018].
- The American Psychological Association (APA) task force rated empathy "demonstrably effective" [Norcross-Karpiak-2025].
- The evidence is correlational; a causal role is plausible but not firmly established [Elliott-etal-2018].
- Empathy is trainable: in a randomized trial with 88 community therapists, a deliberate practice workshop produced better observer-rated skill, and four months later interviewees rated those trainees as more empathic [Westra-etal-2021].
