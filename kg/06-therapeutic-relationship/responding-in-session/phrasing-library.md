**Bottom line:** Good phrasing is short, tentative, uses the client's words and leaves room for correction; poor phrasing argues, labels, lectures or rushes past feeling. Use these lines as starting points, then adapt them to the client's language and culture. Any mention of suicide or self-harm moves you to the risk pages, whatever the agenda.

## The principle

Each phrase below applies a rule from another page in this folder: reflect before asking, validate before change, evoke rather than persuade, ask permission before advice, summarize often, and check risk when hopelessness appears [Moyers-etal-2015; Young-Beck-1980; Padesky-1993]. The Avoid versions are the patterns that rating scales score low: debate, persuasion, lecturing, cross-examination and confrontation [Young-Beck-1980; Moyers-etal-2015].

## Guidance

- If a situation is not in the tables → build the response from one reflection plus one open question. Because that pairing keeps the client talking and shows understanding [Moyers-etal-2015].
- If you are writing templates for a tool → include an explicit check ("Did I get that right?") in most reflections and route any risk language to a human. Because tone cannot carry the check in text, and hopelessness or suicidal content calls for a clinician's risk assessment, which a template cannot do [Beck-etal-1985].

## Example phrasings

Opening and focus

| Situation | Helpful | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting the session | "What would make today worth coming in for?" | "So, how was your week?" (open-ended drift) | Collaborative focus [Young-Beck-1980] |
| Client rambling | "Can I pause you? I want to be sure we reach the work stress you mentioned." | Letting 20 minutes pass | Politely interrupt peripheral talk [Young-Beck-1980] |
| Checking understanding | "Let me sum up. ... What did I miss?" | "Does that make sense?" | Summaries reveal mismatches [Padesky-1993] |

Emotion

| Situation | Helpful | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client crying | "Take your time." (pause) "What's coming up?" | "Don't cry, it'll be okay." | Allows feeling, avoids minimizing [Linehan-1997] |
| Mood shift in session | "Something changed just now. What went through your mind?" | Ignoring it to finish the worksheet | Hot thoughts appear at mood shifts [Young-Beck-1980] |
| Angry at service | "You've waited weeks and nobody called. I'd be frustrated too." | "Calm down, that's not my fault." | Agree with the truth, de-escalate [Richmond-etal-2012] |
| Dissociating | "You're here with me in the office. Feel your feet on the floor." | Continuing the trauma story | Ground before content [Hammond-Brown-2025] |

Thoughts and beliefs

| Situation | Helpful | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I'm a failure." | "That word 'failure' feels heavy. What happened that brought it up?" | "No you're not!" | Concrete first, then explore [Padesky-1993] |
| Hypothesis about a belief | "I wonder if there's a rule like 'If I'm not perfect, I'm worthless'. Does that ring true?" | "Your core belief is that you're worthless." | Tentative, open to correction [Young-Beck-1980] |
| Ending guided discovery | "Looking at all of this, how does it fit with 'I always mess up'?" | "So you see, you don't always mess up." | Client draws the conclusion [Padesky-1993] |
| Harsh self-talk | "You're speaking to yourself really harshly. What would you say to a friend?" | "You shouldn't be so hard on yourself." | Shift tone, not just content [Gilbert-2009] |

Motivation and pushback

| Situation | Helpful | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homework not done | "The practice didn't happen. What got in the way?" | "You agreed to do it." | Curiosity over blame [Moyers-etal-2015] |
| "Yes, but..." | "Part of you sees the point, and part of you doubts it'll work for you." | "But if you just tried..." | Double-sided reflection [Moyers-etal-2015] |
| Client asks for advice | "I have some ideas. Would it help to hear them? You'll know what fits." | Giving a list of instructions | Permission and autonomy [Moyers-etal-2015] |
| Client pushes back at you | "I've been pushing too hard. Let's step back. What matters most to you here?" | "I'm just trying to help you." | Respond to discord, not argue [Miller-Rollnick-2023] |
| Therapist mistake | "I got that wrong. Thank you for telling me." | Explaining why you were right | Repair, see `alliance-and-stance.rupture-and-repair` |

Risk and hope

| Situation | Helpful | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Nothing will help." | "When things feel this hopeless, some people think about ending their life. Has that crossed your mind?" | "You have so much to live for." | Hopelessness predicts suicide [Beck-etal-1985] |
| Ending a session after risk talk | "Let's go over your plan and who you can call, including 988." | "See you next week." | Crisis line by call or text [Lifeline-988] |
| Doubting therapy | "It makes sense to doubt it after what you've tried. Can we test it for a few weeks?" | "Trust me, it works." | Prediction to test [Young-Beck-1980] |

Record: phrasings that worked well with this client, so colleagues and future sessions can use the same language.

## Evidence

The helpful and Avoid patterns follow observer rating standards for cognitive therapy and motivational interviewing and consensus de-escalation guidance [Young-Beck-1980; Moyers-etal-2015; Richmond-etal-2012]. The specific wordings are illustrative and have not been tested against each other. Evidence strength: expert consensus.
