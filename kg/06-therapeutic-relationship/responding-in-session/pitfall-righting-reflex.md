**Bottom line:** The righting reflex is the helper's urge to set things right by persuading, warning or fixing. When a client is ambivalent, it pushes them to defend the other side, so they talk themselves out of change [Miller-Rollnick-2023]. Notice the urge, reflect instead, and ask for the client's own reasons.

## What it looks like

- The therapist lists benefits of change, quotes research or says "you need to" while the client listens silently.
- "Yes, but" replies, more reasons not to change, or agreement with no follow-through.
- Warnings ("If you keep avoiding, it will get worse") and moralizing ("Think of your kids").
- High-pressure persuasion such as "Surely you're exaggerating", which the Cognitive Therapy Rating Scale (CTRS) manual gives as a common trainee error [Young-Beck-1980].
- In motivational interviewing (MI) coding, persuasion without permission and confrontation are the two non-adherent behaviors; frequent righting reflex marks the lowest partnership rating [Moyers-etal-2015].

## Why it happens

- Therapists want to help and know what usually works, so pushing feels caring [Miller-Rollnick-2023].
- Time pressure and protocol pressure: the session plan says "start exposure today".
- Mistaking compliance for agreement; depressed clients often agree out of compliance [Young-Beck-1980].
- Reading ambivalence as "resistance" to be overcome rather than a normal state to explore [Miller-Rollnick-2023].

## The fix

- If you notice yourself building an argument → stop and reflect what the client has said, including their doubts. Because confrontation produces resistance that predicts poorer outcomes [Miller-etal-1993].
- If you have important information → ask permission, give it briefly, and ask what the client thinks. Because the motivational interviewing coding manual codes advice given with permission or autonomy support as "persuade with permission" rather than the non-adherent "persuade" [Moyers-etal-2015].
- If the client voices reasons not to change → reflect briefly and ask what they want instead. Because client sustain talk predicts worse outcomes, so it should not be drawn out [Magill-etal-2014].
- If a plan has stalled after you pushed → name it ("I think I pushed too fast"), and return the choice to the client. Because responding to discord means stepping back rather than arguing [Miller-Rollnick-2023].
- If ambivalence is a recurring barrier in cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) → integrate MI skills deliberately. Because MI-integrated CBT for severe generalized anxiety disorder improved long-term worry and diagnostic outcomes [Westra-etal-2016].

Helpful: "You're not sure this is worth the effort. What would need to be true for it to feel worth trying?" Avoid: "Exposure is the most effective treatment. You really have to do it."

## Sources note

The concept comes from MI texts and coding standards [Miller-Rollnick-2023; Moyers-etal-2015], and the CTRS manual describes the same error in cognitive therapy [Young-Beck-1980]. Outcome links come from a small trial (42 problem drinkers) comparing confrontational and client-centered counseling [Miller-etal-1993], a meta-analysis of MI process [Magill-etal-2014] and a randomized trial of MI-integrated CBT [Westra-etal-2016].
