# 06-therapeutic-relationship/responding-in-session: Responding in Session

## This surface
Turn-by-turn guidance on how a therapist, or a clinical tool, should respond to what a client just said: the core listening moves, Socratic dialogue, validation, strong emotion, ambivalence, psychoeducation, pacing, language and tone, and hopelessness and self-criticism, plus a phrasing library. It applies at every stage of the episode of care and sits beside `alliance-and-stance`, which covers the relationship these responses build. Any sign of suicide or self-harm risk routes to `02-risk` pages first.

## Pages in order
1. `responding-in-session.core-response-skills`: start here: the four basic moves every turn is built from
2. `responding-in-session.reflective-listening`: the most-used move, with types and depth
3. `responding-in-session.validation`: how to show a reaction makes sense before asking for change
4. `responding-in-session.language-and-tone`: word choice and delivery that decide how any move lands
5. `responding-in-session.pacing-silence-and-turn-taking`: how much to talk, when to wait and when to interrupt
6. `responding-in-session.socratic-dialogue`: guided discovery, the core CBT questioning method
7. `responding-in-session.psychoeducation-without-lecturing`: giving information without taking over
8. `responding-in-session.responding-to-ambivalence`: when the client argues for staying the same
9. `responding-in-session.responding-to-strong-emotion`: tears, panic, anger and dissociation in the room
10. `responding-in-session.responding-to-hopelessness-and-self-criticism`: hopelessness, shame and harsh self-talk, with the risk check
11. `responding-in-session.phrasing-library`: ready wording for common moments, helpful and Avoid versions

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Interrogation and Question Stacking | The therapist asks question after question, often several at once or leading toward a set answer, and the client gives short, guarded or compliant replies. | `responding-in-session.pitfall-interrogation-and-question-stacking` |
| The Righting Reflex (Arguing, Fixing and Lecturing) | The therapist argues for change, warns, lectures or rushes to fix, and the client answers with more reasons not to change, "yes, but" or quiet disengagement. | `responding-in-session.pitfall-righting-reflex` |
| Premature Reassurance and Rushing Past Emotion | The therapist reassures, cheerleads or jumps to a technique while the client is upset, and the client feels unheard, argues back, withdraws or says "you don't get it". | `responding-in-session.pitfall-premature-reassurance-and-invalidation` |
| Treating Hopelessness Only as a Thought to Challenge | A client says things will never get better, and the therapist moves straight to disputing the thought or reassuring, without asking about suicidal thoughts. | `responding-in-session.pitfall-missing-risk-behind-hopelessness` |
