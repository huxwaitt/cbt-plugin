**Bottom line:** Ambivalence is normal: most clients hold reasons to change and reasons to stay the same at once. Arguing for change pushes the client to argue the other side, so reflect, ask for their own reasons, and respect their choice [Miller-Rollnick-2023]. Treat pushback against you (discord) as a relationship signal and repair it before returning to the task.

## The principle

Motivational interviewing (MI) separates three kinds of client language [Miller-Rollnick-2023]:

| Term | What it sounds like | Therapist aim |
|---|---|---|
| Change talk | "I want to stop avoiding things." "I could try once." | Notice, reflect, ask for more |
| Sustain talk | "Avoiding is the only thing that keeps me sane." | Acknowledge without deepening it |
| Discord | "You don't get it." "You sound like my mother." | Step back and repair the relationship |

Early MI texts called this "resistance" and advised "rolling with resistance"; since the third edition (2013), MI instead speaks of sustain talk (about the behavior) and discord (about the relationship), as the current edition does [Miller-Rollnick-2023]. The righting reflex is the helper's urge to fix, warn and persuade; the MI coding standard lists frequent righting reflex as a mark of low partnership [Moyers-etal-2015].

In cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), ambivalence often shows up as missed homework, "I already know that thought is irrational", delay before exposure, or polite agreement with no action.

## Guidance

- If the client offers sustain talk → give a simple reflection and then turn toward their goals or values, rather than exploring the barriers at length. Because lingering on sustain talk lowers the "softening sustain talk" rating, and sustain talk predicts worse outcome [Moyers-etal-2015; Magill-etal-2014].
- If the client voices both sides → use a double-sided reflection that ends with their reasons for change. Because ending on change talk moves the conversation toward change without arguing [Moyers-etal-2015].
- If change talk appears, even small → reflect it, ask for an example or elaboration, and affirm it. Because MI-consistent therapist skills are associated with more client change talk [Magill-etal-2014].
- If you feel the urge to explain, warn or list benefits → ask a question that lets the client give the reasons instead ("What would be different if this got easier?"). Because supplying reasons for change rather than evoking them is a low-rated pattern [Moyers-etal-2015].
- If the client pushes back at you ("You don't understand") → reflect the frustration, apologize for your part if any, and emphasize their choice. Because confrontation generates resistance that predicts poorer outcomes [Miller-etal-1993].
- If the client is weighing whether to start (exposure, behavioral activation) → emphasize autonomy and ask permission before giving your view. Because statements that leave decisions with the client are the MI-adherent way to share an opinion [Moyers-etal-2015].
- If momentum for change has emerged → move toward planning; do not run a full pros-and-cons exercise. Because giving equal time to both sides after momentum appears misses chances to strengthen change talk [Moyers-etal-2015].
- If ambivalence keeps stalling CBT → consider a few MI-focused sessions before or within the CBT plan. Because MI-integrated CBT improved long-term outcomes in severe generalized anxiety disorder [Westra-etal-2016].

## Example phrasings

- Simple reflection of sustain talk: "Right now avoiding the supermarket feels like the only way to get through the week."
- Double-sided: "Staying home keeps the panic away, and you're also tired of your world getting smaller."
- Evoking: "What would you like to be different six months from now?"
- Autonomy: "It's your call whether we try this step this week. What feels doable?"
- Responding to discord: "I've been pushing, and that's not helpful. Let me slow down. What matters most to you here?"
- Avoid: "If you don't do the exposure, you'll never get better." (warning, confrontation).
- Avoid: "Yes, but you agreed to it last week." (arguing, sounds like blame).

Record: examples of change talk and sustain talk in the client's words, any discord and how it was repaired, and the decision the client made.

## Evidence

In a meta-analysis of the MI causal model (12 primary studies), MI-consistent skills related to more change talk (r = .26) and client sustain talk predicted worse outcome (r = -.24), while change talk alone did not significantly predict outcome (r = .06) [Magill-etal-2014]. In an early trial with 42 problem drinkers, a directive-confrontational style produced more client resistance, and the more a counselor confronted, the more the client drank at one year (r = .65) [Miller-etal-1993]. In a randomized trial for severe generalized anxiety disorder, adding MI to CBT gave no difference at post-treatment but a steeper decline in worry over follow-up and about five times the odds of no longer meeting criteria at 12 months; dropout was 10 percent versus 23 percent (p = .09) [Westra-etal-2016]. Evidence strength: supported by randomized trials for MI-integrated CBT in anxiety; process findings are correlational.
