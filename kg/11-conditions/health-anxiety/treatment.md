**Bottom line:** Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for health anxiety is the best-supported treatment, with moderate to large effects that largely last [Cooper-etal-2017; Axelsson-HedmanLagerlof-2019]. A typical course is about 12 weekly sessions: engage the client in testing a worry explanation, then use behavioral experiments and exposure while dropping checking and reassurance [Axelsson-etal-2020]. Brief versions in medical clinics and therapist-guided internet CBT also work [Tyrer-etal-2017; Axelsson-etal-2020].

## When to use

- Illness anxiety disorder, or somatic symptom disorder where fear of illness drives the problem.
- After medical review has covered current symptoms, and alongside any real medical condition.
- With a client willing to test an alternative explanation for a fixed period, even if not yet convinced.

## How it works

Treatment targets what keeps the fear going, not the disease itself. Clients learn that ordinary sensations are amplified by attention and arousal, and that checking, reassurance and avoidance keep doubt alive. Testing predictions and staying with uncertainty, without safety behaviors, weakens threat beliefs [Warwick-1998; Kikas-etal-2024].

## Steps

An illustrative 12-week outline that combines Warwick's components with the trial format; in the trial the first session lasted 80 minutes and later ones about 50 [Warwick-1998; Axelsson-etal-2020]:

| Sessions | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Assessment, engagement, shared formulation, two competing explanations, goals, baseline measure, start self-monitoring of episodes [Warwick-1998] |
| 3 to 4 | Education on attention, arousal and safety behaviors; experiments such as focusing on a body part for five minutes and noting new sensations |
| 4 to 7 | Re-evaluate the client's evidence for illness; reduce checking, searching and reassurance; family plan for reassurance requests [Warwick-1998] |
| 5 to 10 | Graded exposure to illness cues (reading about the illness, visiting a hospital, doing avoided activity, for example exercising without checking the pulse for a heart fear) with response prevention [Axelsson-etal-2020] |
| 9 to 11 | Assumptions and core beliefs about health, death and vulnerability, tested with experiments [Warwick-1998] |
| 12 | Relapse prevention: early signs, a rule for when to see a doctor, how to handle future real illness |

Give homework every session and rate the health anxiety measure weekly.

## Guidance

- If the client is not convinced the problem is anxiety → do not try to convince them; offer a time-limited test of the worry explanation and keep medical options open afterward. Because this framing helps reluctant clients accept treatment [Warwick-1998].
- If the client asks you for reassurance → link the request to the formulation and explain why answering would undo the work. Because repeated reassurance maintains health anxiety [Warwick-1998].
- If you are planning exposure → target the feared illness itself as well as triggers, and agree response prevention in advance. Because the trial protocol paired exposure to illness cues with refraining from maintaining behaviors [Axelsson-etal-2020].
- If face-to-face therapist time is scarce → offer therapist-guided internet CBT. Because it was noninferior to face-to-face CBT, using about 10 minutes of therapist time per patient per week instead of 46 [Axelsson-etal-2020].
- If the client attends a hospital clinic and declines a full course → offer brief CBT for health anxiety. Because 4 to 10 sessions (mean 6) from trained nurses and other clinicians gave benefits still present at 5 and 8 years [Tyrer-etal-2017; Tyrer-etal-2021].
- If the client also takes or wants medication → coordinate with the prescriber. Because paroxetine beat placebo among completers in one trial and fluoxetine beat placebo on continuous scores in another, though single-treatment responder rates did not differ significantly from placebo there [Greeven-etal-2007; Fallon-etal-2017].

## Common problems

- **Reassurance from the therapist creeps in.** Review recordings or notes for answers to "Is it serious?"; see `health-anxiety.reassurance-seeking`.
- **Safety behaviors go covert.** Clients swap visible checking for mental scanning or asking others; ask about each one by name.
- **A real symptom appears mid-treatment.** Use the agreed rule for seeing a doctor, then resume.
- **Hidden avoidance.** Care-avoidant clients may need graded exposure to medical settings, including overdue screening.

## Evidence

- A Cochrane review of 6 trials (440 participants) found that cognitive therapy, behavior therapy, CBT and behavioral stress management beat waiting list (pooled standardized mean difference -0.86); psychoeducation did not [Thomson-Page-2007].
- A meta-analysis of 14 trials found a large effect of CBT versus controls (d = 1.01), maintained at 6 and 12 months [Cooper-etal-2017].
- Across 19 randomized trials, CBT beat non-CBT controls (g = 0.79; 0.62 after publication bias adjustment), with 66% response and 48% remission and gains largely held at 12 to 18 months [Axelsson-HedmanLagerlof-2019].
- In 204 patients, internet CBT was noninferior to face-to-face CBT, with about three quarters responding in each arm [Axelsson-etal-2020].
- For illness anxiety and somatic symptom disorder as defined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5), guided and unguided internet CBT and bibliotherapy beat waiting list (d = 0.80 to 1.27) [Hedman-etal-2016], and internet CBT beat active psychoeducation (effect size 1.39) [Newby-etal-2018].
- Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy added to usual care reduced health anxiety (d = 0.48) [McManus-etal-2012b].
- **Guideline status.** The UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) has no health anxiety guideline; its common mental health problems guideline (CG123) does not cover it [NIHR-Alert-2017]. No American Psychological Association (APA) clinical practice guideline on health anxiety was found, though this was not confirmed on the APA site. Status: supported by randomized trials and meta-analyses, not guideline-recommended.
