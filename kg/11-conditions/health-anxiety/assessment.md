**Bottom line:** Assess the worry, not the disease. Confirm with the client's doctor what has been investigated, then map the feared illness, its triggers, the belief strength and every checking, reassurance, searching and avoidance behavior. Score a health anxiety measure such as the Short Health Anxiety Inventory (SHAI) and, when symptoms are prominent, a somatic symptom measure. Screen for depression and suicide risk.

## When to use

- At intake when illness fear, body checking or symptom distress is a presenting problem.
- When a medical team refers someone with repeated normal investigations.
- At every session (one brief measure) to track change.

## Steps

1. **Frame the assessment.** Take symptoms seriously, give the client time, and say you have seen similar problems. Many clients expect to be told it is "all in the mind" [Warwick-1998].
2. **Confirm medical status.** With consent, obtain records or speak to the primary care clinician: diagnoses, investigations done, pending tests, and whether anything is new [Warwick-1998].
3. **Map recent episodes.** For two or three specific episodes record the trigger (sensation, news story, illness in others), the thought or image, belief in it (0 to 100), anxiety (0 to 100), and what the client did next [Warwick-1998].
4. **List safety behaviors.** Body checking, reassurance from doctors and family, online searching, avoidance of reminders or of medical settings, and time spent per day [Kikas-etal-2024].
5. **Classify care pattern.** Care-seeking, care-avoidant or fluctuating; most clients fluctuate [Newby-etal-2017].
6. **History.** Onset, past illness in self or family, deaths, medical mishaps, and the client's rules about symptoms ("doctors miss things").
7. **Measures.** Give one health anxiety measure and, if symptoms dominate, one somatic burden measure (table below).
8. **Differential diagnosis and comorbidity.** Use the questions in `health-anxiety.overview`; screen depression, generalized anxiety and panic.
9. **Risk.** Ask directly about suicidal thoughts and follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment` if present. Hypochondriasis carries a raised suicide rate [MataixCols-etal-2024].
10. **Diagnostic interview if needed.** Options include the Anxiety and Related Disorders Interview Schedule and the Structured Clinical Interview, both written for the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5), and the Health Preoccupation Diagnostic Interview [Kikas-etal-2024].

| Measure | Range | Cutoffs and bands |
|---|---|---|
| SHAI-14 (main section) | 0 to 42 | Swedish version: 22 versus other psychiatric patients; 29 maximizes specificity in low-prevalence settings (sensitivity 62%); 18 versus healthy people (remission); 0 to 27 none or mild, 28 to 32 moderate, 33 to 42 substantial [Osterman-etal-2022] |
| SHAI 18-item | 0 to 54 | 27 gave 85.7% sensitivity and 77.9% specificity against other anxiety disorders [Abramowitz-etal-2007a; Osterman-etal-2022; Axelsson-etal-2020] |
| Health Anxiety Inventory, 64 items | 0 to 192 | 67 separated severe health anxiety from obsessive-compulsive disorder and healthy controls [Hedman-etal-2015] |
| Whiteley Index, 14 yes or no items | 0 to 14 | 5, derived against healthy controls only [Hedman-etal-2015] |
| Patient Health Questionnaire-15 (PHQ-15) | 0 to 30 | 5, 10, 15 for low, medium, high somatic severity [Kroenke-etal-2002] |
| Somatic Symptom Scale-8 (SSS-8) | 0 to 32 | 4 to 7 low, 8 to 11 medium, 12 to 15 high, 16 to 32 very high [Gierk-etal-2014] |
| Somatic Symptom Disorder B Criteria Scale (SSD-12) | 0 to 48 | 23 or more, combined with PHQ-15 or SSS-8 of 9 or more, gave about 70% sensitivity and specificity for SSD [Toussaint-etal-2020] |

Cutoffs depend on setting and language version, and `08-measurement.anxiety-measures` treats SHAI cutoffs as unsettled, so use them as prompts for a diagnostic interview, not diagnoses. Do not paste item wording into records or apps; link to the published versions and check permissions.

## What to record

- Feared illness or illnesses, triggers, belief rating and anxiety rating.
- Medical status: diagnoses, investigations, who holds medical responsibility, and any agreed review plan.
- Safety behaviors with frequency and minutes per day, and who provides reassurance.
- Care pattern (seeking, avoidant, fluctuating) and overdue screening if avoidant.
- Scores with date and version, comorbidity, suicide screen result, and the client's own explanation of the problem.

## Guidance

- If the client asks you during assessment whether a symptom is serious → say you cannot judge medical meaning, that their doctor holds that role, and that today you want to understand the worry itself. Because answering would start the reassurance cycle you are about to assess [Warwick-1998].
- If records show adequate investigation and nothing new → do not suggest further tests to settle the worry. Because tests in low-probability presentations do little to reduce illness worry or anxiety [Rolfe-Burton-2013].
- If a symptom is new, changing or worsening → advise medical review before building the formulation. Because health anxiety and real disease can coexist (expert consensus).
- If the SHAI-14 is 22 or more in a mental health service → complete a full diagnostic assessment for health anxiety. Because that cutoff gave 93% sensitivity and 85% specificity there [Osterman-etal-2022].
- If conviction is total and fixed but there are no other psychotic features → record poor or absent insight and continue assessing health anxiety. Because the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) uses an insight qualifier rather than a delusional disorder diagnosis here [Reed-etal-2019].

## Common problems

- **Client feels accused of imagining symptoms.** Name that the symptoms are real and that the question is what keeps the fear going.
- **Physical illness inflates scores.** The Health Anxiety Inventory was built to separate health anxiety from physical illness without excess worry, but interpret scores alongside medical status [Salkovskis-etal-2002].
- **Family member answers for the client.** Interview the client alone first, then gather collateral on how often family give reassurance.
- **Assessment becomes reassurance.** Note each request as data for the formulation.
