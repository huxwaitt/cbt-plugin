**Bottom line:** Health anxiety is persistent, disproportionate fear of having or developing a serious illness, kept going by checking, reassurance seeking, symptom searching or avoidance. The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) splits it into illness anxiety disorder (IAD) and somatic symptom disorder (SSD), which in one clinic sample differed mainly in severity [Newby-etal-2017]. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is the best-supported treatment [Axelsson-HedmanLagerlof-2019]. Suicide risk is raised, so ask about it [MataixCols-etal-2024].

## Diagnostic essentials

The 2013 DSM-5 replaced hypochondriasis with two diagnoses, which DSM-5-TR keeps in its somatic symptom and related disorders chapter [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. Paraphrased essentials:

**Illness anxiety disorder** [APA-DSM5TR-2022; Kikas-etal-2024]
- Preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious illness.
- Somatic symptoms absent or mild. If a medical condition or high risk exists, the preoccupation is clearly excessive.
- High anxiety about health; easily alarmed by health news or sensations.
- Excessive health behaviors (body checking, searching, repeated consultations) or maladaptive avoidance (of doctors, hospitals, screening).
- Present at least 6 months; the feared illness may change.
- Not better explained by another mental disorder.
- Specify care-seeking or care-avoidant type. In one clinic sample 25% were care-seeking, 14% care-avoidant and 61% swung between the two [Newby-etal-2017].

**Somatic symptom disorder** [Lowe-etal-2022; APA-DSM5TR-2022]
- One or more distressing or disruptive somatic symptoms.
- At least one of three psychological features: disproportionate, persistent thoughts about seriousness; persistently high anxiety about health or symptoms; excessive time and energy spent on them.
- Symptomatic state persists, typically more than 6 months, though individual symptoms may change.
- A medical explanation need not be absent; SSD can sit alongside a diagnosed disease [Lowe-etal-2022].
- Specifiers: with predominant pain; persistent; severity mild (one psychological feature), moderate (two or more), severe (two or more plus multiple or very severe somatic complaints) [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

**Coding (United States).** IAD is coded F45.21 and SSD F45.1, the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM) codes titled hypochondriasis and undifferentiated somatoform disorder [APA-DSM5TR-2022; ICD10Data-F45].

**Key ICD-11 differences.** The International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11):
- Places hypochondriasis (health anxiety disorder, 6B23) with obsessive-compulsive or related disorders, cross-listed under anxiety disorders, and lists illness anxiety disorder as an inclusion term [Reed-etal-2019; CISMeF-ICD11-6B23]. It does not split cases by somatic symptom level.
- Uses an insight qualifier ("with poor to absent insight") rather than a delusional disorder diagnosis when conviction is fixed [Reed-etal-2019].
- Replaces somatoform disorders with bodily distress disorder (6C20): distressing symptoms with excessive attention that appropriate examination and reassurance do not relieve, on most days for at least several months (for example 3 or more), graded mild, moderate or severe by impact on functioning [Reed-etal-2019; Hauser-etal-2025]. DSM-5-TR SSD typically needs more than 6 months.

## Course and prevalence

- Adult general population: health anxiety estimates range from 2.1% to 13.1%; in primary and secondary care, 7% to 19.9% [Kikas-etal-2024]. In UK hospital medical clinics, 5,769 of 28,991 screened outpatients (about one in five) scored 20 or more on the Health Anxiety Inventory [Tyrer-etal-2017].
- SSD: self-report studies average 12.9% in the general population and 35% in general medicine; interview-based prevalence studies are lacking [Lowe-etal-2022].
- Onset is usually in early adulthood but can start in childhood. Men and women are affected about equally. Untreated, the course is chronic and episodic over years [Kikas-etal-2024].

## Differential and comorbidity

Questions that separate the main alternatives:
- **Undiagnosed or undertreated medical disease:** has an adequate medical evaluation been done, and is anything new or changing? IAD and SSD can coexist with real illness.
- **Panic disorder:** is the fear of an immediate catastrophe during an attack (heart attack now) rather than a slowly developing disease?
- **Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD):** is health one worry topic among many?
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD):** are there intrusive illness or contamination thoughts with rituals aimed at preventing harm to self or others?
- **Body dysmorphic disorder:** is the concern appearance rather than illness?
- **Depression or psychosis:** are illness ideas mood-congruent ruminations, or bizarre somatic delusions with other psychotic features?

IAD commonly co-occurs with GAD and panic disorder. Compared with IAD, SSD shows more severe health anxiety, more depression, more health service use, and higher rates of major depressive disorder, panic disorder and agoraphobia [Newby-etal-2017; Kikas-etal-2024].

## CBT approach in brief

The cognitive behavioral model holds that bodily sensations and health information are misread as signs of serious illness, and that checking, reassurance seeking, searching, avoidance and body-focused attention keep the belief alive [Kikas-etal-2024]. Treatment builds an alternative, less threatening explanation, then tests it with behavioral experiments and exposure while dropping safety behaviors. Across 19 randomized trials, CBT beat non-CBT controls (g = 0.79), with a 66% response rate and 48% remission [Axelsson-HedmanLagerlof-2019]. See `health-anxiety.treatment`.

## Risk notes

- In a Swedish national cohort, people diagnosed with hypochondriasis had higher all-cause mortality (hazard ratio 1.69) and about four times the suicide rate (hazard ratio 4.14) of matched controls [MataixCols-etal-2024].
- In somatic symptom and related disorders, 24% to 34% of studied participants reported current active suicidal ideation, and the risk appeared independent of co-occurring mental disorders [Torres-etal-2021].
- Ask directly about suicidal thoughts at intake and when hopelessness about health grows. Follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`. For imminent risk in the US call 911 or call or text 988; in the UK call 999, NHS 111 or Samaritans on 116 123.
- Medical risk runs both ways: real disease can be missed once a client is labeled health anxious, and repeated tests carry their own harms. Care-avoidant clients may skip needed screening.
