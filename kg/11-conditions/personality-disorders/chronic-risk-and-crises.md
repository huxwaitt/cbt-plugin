**Bottom line:** In borderline personality disorder (BPD), suicidal thoughts can be a long-standing baseline while risk still rises sharply in a crisis, so assess chronic and acute risk separately and plan for both [NICE-CG78]. Respond to crises calmly and with validation, use the agreed crisis plan, and choose the least restrictive safe setting [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Decide your between-session contact rules before the first crisis.

## The principle

More than 90% of people with BPD self-injure, about 75% attempt suicide, and 3% to 10% die by suicide [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) asks for risk assessment that separates long-term from immediate risk, covers risk to others and to dependent children, is agreed with the client, and is managed by a supervised team [NICE-CG78]. Do not use risk scales or low, medium and high labels to predict suicide or decide who gets care [NICE-NG225]. The American Psychiatric Association (APA) asks clinicians to monitor and document risk, write individualized safety plans, and consider hospitalization for significant acute risk [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Self-harm and proneness to crisis tend to decrease over time, which supports a hopeful stance [Gunderson-etal-2018].

## Guidance

**Telling chronic from acute**
- If suicidal thoughts are long-standing and unchanged from the client's usual pattern → record the baseline, check the safety plan, and continue the planned session. Because NICE separates long-term risk, managed through the treatment plan, from immediate risk [NICE-CG78].
- If there is a new plan or intent, a recent attempt, new access to lethal means, a major loss, new heavy substance use, or the client says "this time is different" → treat it as acute risk and act the same day. Because significant acute risk may need a higher level of care [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].

**Responding in a crisis**
- If the client arrives in crisis → stay calm, look at the crisis from their side, explore their reasons, use open questions and validation, and hold off on solutions until the problem is clear. Because these are NICE crisis principles [NICE-CG78].
- If the client discloses recent self-harm → check for injury or overdose first and arrange same-day medical care if needed (911 or the emergency department in the United States; 999 or accident and emergency in the United Kingdom), then plan a chain analysis for the next session. Because physical safety comes before analysis, and NICE asks that self-harm episodes be managed under its self-harm guideline [NICE-CG78].
- If risk is acute → in the United States call 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, call or text) or 911; in the United Kingdom call 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111, the local crisis team or Samaritans 116 123. Stay with the client or keep contact until handover, and restrict access to lethal means. Because APA calls for an individualized safety plan and, for significant acute risk, actions such as hospitalization [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
- If anger or threats toward others rise → assess risk to others, keep firm limits on acceptable behavior, and consider a higher level of care if violence seems imminent. Because APA advises this [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].

**Admission and medication**
- If admission is being considered → first try crisis resolution or home treatment, admit only for significant risk that cannot be managed elsewhere, and agree the purpose and length in advance. Because NICE sets these conditions [NICE-CG78].
- If the client has been admitted twice or more in 6 months → request a formal review of the whole care plan. Because NICE recommends a formal Care Programme Approach review at this point; use your service's current care-plan review process [NICE-CG78].
- If a crisis prompts a request for new medication → pass the request to the prescriber and share the agreed crisis plan, rather than advising on drugs yourself. Because prescribing is outside most therapists' roles, NICE limits crisis sedation to 1 week or less, and APA calls frequent dose changes in crises problematic and rarely effective [NICE-CG78] [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].

**Contact and team**
- If you will not take after-hours calls → say so at the start and name who the client contacts instead. Because the treatment framework should state after-hours availability and a crisis plan [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024], and volunteer crisis lines cannot take over clinical responsibility [Linehan-2025].
- If you notice rescuing, anger or dread toward a client → take it to supervision or team consultation. Because these reactions are expected and can bend treatment [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
- If a crisis has passed → update the crisis plan with what helped. Because NICE asks for plans to be revised after each crisis [NICE-CG78].

## Example phrasings

- Checking baseline: "You've told me these thoughts are often there. Is anything different about them today: stronger, more of a plan, closer to acting?"
- Validating in crisis: "Of course you're in agony. Being left like that, after everything, would floor anyone. Let's slow it down together."
- Holding the frame: "I care about what happens tonight. I'm not available after 6, so let's use your plan now and check you have the crisis number in your phone."
- Avoid: "You're always suicidal, so this probably isn't serious."

## Evidence

The crisis and admission rules are NICE recommendations (2009, reviewed 2024) [NICE-CG78]; the ban on risk prediction tools comes from NICE's 2022 self-harm guideline [NICE-NG225]. APA assessment statements carry low-certainty (C) ratings and lean on expert opinion [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Record each risk review, the plan agreed and who was told.
