**Bottom line:** Screen when you see repeated self-harm, marked emotional instability or persistent risk-taking [NICE-CG78], then confirm with a structured interview and history across time, contexts and informants. Rate severity with a repeatable measure so you can track change [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Assess chronic and acute risk separately, and share the diagnosis with the client [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].

## When to use

- Signals in the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guideline: repeated self-harm, persistent risk-taking or marked emotional instability [NICE-CG78].
- Lifelong relationship, identity or work problems with no clear episode start.
- Standard cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) has stalled, or homework, alliance and attendance problems recur.
- Clients often present with depression or anxiety and may lack insight into the pattern [Zimmerman-2023a].

## Steps

1. **Cover the full evaluation.** The American Psychiatric Association (APA) recommends: reason for presenting, goals and treatment preferences, psychiatric symptoms including core personality features and common co-occurring disorders, treatment history, physical health, psychosocial and cultural factors, a mental status examination, and risk of suicide, self-injury and aggression [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
2. **Screen.** Use a brief screen (table below), then confirm. A screen is never a diagnosis.
3. **Confirm with an interview.** The Structured Clinical Interview for the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5), Personality Disorders (SCID-5-PD) covers all 10 types; its optional self-report questionnaire (SCID-5-SPQ) lets you skip items answered "no". It is sold by APA Publishing, and some uses need a permission fee [APPI-SCID5-Site].
4. **Map the pattern over time.** Ask when it started, whether it holds across work, home and friendships, and whether it has lasted years. The International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) uses about 2 years as a guide [WHO-CDDR-2024].
5. **Rate severity.** Add a quantitative measure at baseline and repeat it [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
6. **Ask differential questions** (Guidance below).
7. **Gather collateral.** With consent, ask previous clinicians and family [Zimmerman-2023a]. Ask directly whether the client wants family involved [NICE-CG78].
8. **Assess risk.** Separate long-term from immediate risk, include dependent children, and agree the risks with the client [NICE-CG78]. Do not use risk scales or low, medium, high labels to predict suicide or ration care [NICE-NG225].
9. **Feed back.** Discuss the diagnosis and treatment options collaboratively [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Offer support afterward if trauma was discussed [NICE-CG78].

| Measure | What it does | Scores to know |
|---|---|---|
| Standardised Assessment of Personality, Abbreviated Scale (SAPAS) | 8-item clinician interview screen for any personality disorder | 3 or more: sensitivity 0.94, specificity 0.85, preliminary study, 60 patients [Moran-etal-2003] |
| McLean Screening Instrument for Borderline Personality Disorder (MSI-BPD) | 10-item self-report screen for borderline personality disorder (BPD) | 7 or more: sensitivity 0.81, specificity 0.85; 0.90 and 0.93 in those 25 or younger [Zanarini-etal-2003a] |
| Zanarini Rating Scale for BPD (ZAN-BPD) | Clinician rating of the 9 BPD features over 1 week, 0 to 4 each | Total 0 to 36 [Zanarini-etal-2003b]; a 3-point drop is used as the minimal clinically relevant change [Storebo-etal-2020] |
| Borderline Symptom List 23 (BSL-23) | 23-item self-report of BPD symptoms | Sensitive to change in therapy [Bohus-etal-2009] |
| Personality Inventory for DSM-5 (PID-5) | Trait domains; 220 items, brief form 25 items; free for clinicians with their own patients; commercial use needs APA permission | Domain profiles, no single cutoff [APA-Assessment-Measures] |
| Level of Personality Functioning Scale, Brief Form 2.0 | Self and interpersonal functioning, fits ICD-11 severity | Track over time [Bach-First-2018] |

APA also lists the Borderline Evaluation of Severity over Time, Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale and Deliberate Self-Harm Inventory as options [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Check licensing before building any of these into a tool.

## What to record

- Presenting problem, goals, and the features present with examples, onset and contexts.
- Screen and measure scores with dates.
- Differential findings and comorbid diagnoses, and whether either was made during a crisis.
- Collateral sources and consent.
- Long-term and immediate risks, protective factors, dependent children, and the crisis plan.
- What you told the client about the diagnosis and their response.

## Guidance

- If mood changes last hours and follow interpersonal events → record the pattern as consistent with BPD affective instability and screen for bipolar episodes separately. Because bipolar episodes are sustained and change energy and activity [Zimmerman-2023b].
- If a client meets BPD features during an admission or crisis → treat the diagnosis as provisional and review it when settled. Because NICE asks for both diagnoses to be reviewed before a comorbid condition is treated, especially if made in a crisis [NICE-CG78].
- If the client is an adolescent → set a higher threshold for self-harm, moodiness and risk-taking as evidence of personality disorder, and confirm the pattern has lasted at least 1 year. Because these are common in adolescence [WHO-CDDR-2024] [Zimmerman-2023a].
- If the pattern is explained by cultural norms or social conflict → do not diagnose a personality disorder. Because ICD-11 excludes these explanations [WHO-CDDR-2024].
- If you are unsure who may diagnose in your setting → check your license and local law before recording a diagnosis. Because scope of practice varies.

Example questions: "When your mood drops or spikes, how long does it usually last, and what tends to set it off?" "Has your sense of who you are felt steady over the years, or does it shift a lot?" "How do your closest relationships usually go, start to finish?"

## Common problems

- **Screening positive treated as diagnosis.** Confirm with interview and history.
- **Only the presenting episode is assessed.** Ask about the years before it.
- **Diagnosis avoided out of fear of stigma.** Clients prefer disclosure and it does not lower satisfaction [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]; see `personality-disorders.pitfall-withholding-the-diagnosis`.
- **Risk assessed once.** Re-assess at every crisis and at transitions.
