**Bottom line:** People with personality disorders other than borderline usually come to cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for depression, anxiety or the fallout of their behavior, not for the personality pattern itself [Zimmerman-2023a]. Cluster C disorders have randomized trial support for cognitive therapy and for schema therapy [Svartberg-etal-2004] [Bamelis-etal-2014]. Evidence for antisocial personality disorder is weak; use group-based cognitive and behavioral programs and watch risk to others [NICE-CG77] [Gibbon-etal-2020].

## Diagnostic essentials

Paraphrased from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

| Cluster | Disorders and core feature [Zimmerman-2023a] |
|---|---|
| A, odd or eccentric | Paranoid (mistrust), schizoid (disinterest in others), schizotypal (eccentric ideas and behavior) |
| B, dramatic or erratic | Antisocial (disregard for others), histrionic (attention seeking, excess emotion), narcissistic (grandiosity, need for admiration, low empathy) |
| C, anxious or fearful | Avoidant (rejection sensitivity), dependent (submissiveness, need to be cared for), obsessive-compulsive (perfectionism, rigidity) |

- **Avoidant:** a pattern of social avoidance, inadequacy and sensitivity to criticism, shown by at least 4 of 7 features, beginning by early adulthood [Zimmerman-2023d].
- **Antisocial:** disregard for others' rights shown by at least 3 of 7 features, age at least 18, and conduct disorder before age 15 [Zimmerman-2023c].
- **International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11):** these patterns are described with trait specifiers instead of type names. Anankastia covers perfectionism and control; detachment covers social and emotional distance; dissociality covers entitlement, admiration seeking and lack of empathy [WHO-CDDR-2024]. Schizotypal disorder sits with psychotic disorders [Bach-First-2018].

## Course and prevalence

- Global community prevalence: cluster A 3.8%, cluster B 2.8%, cluster C 5.0% [Winsper-etal-2020].
- Avoidant personality disorder: about 2.1% in the United States [Zimmerman-2023d].
- Antisocial personality disorder is about 3 times more common in males [Zimmerman-2023a].
- Antisocial traits tend to lessen with age; obsessive-compulsive and schizotypal patterns are less likely to [Zimmerman-2023a].

## Differential and comorbidity

- **Avoidant personality disorder vs social anxiety disorder:** the difference is subtle; avoidant anxiety and avoidance are more pervasive, while social anxiety is often tied to performance situations [Zimmerman-2023d].
- **Obsessive-compulsive personality disorder vs obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD):** ask about unwanted intrusive thoughts and the rituals that neutralize them; their presence points to OCD (see `ocd.overview`), while rigid perfectionism alone points to the personality pattern.
- **Paranoid and schizotypal vs psychosis:** check for fixed delusions or hallucinations.
- **Antisocial vs substance use disorder:** hard to separate; review the history, including periods without substance use. Both can be diagnosed together [Zimmerman-2023c].
- Depression, anxiety, substance use and eating disorders commonly co-occur and slow treatment [Zimmerman-2023a].

## CBT approach in brief

- **Formulate beliefs.** Each disorder has typical beliefs, confirmed for avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive, narcissistic and paranoid personality disorders [Beck-etal-2001]. Beck and colleagues give disorder-specific CBT chapters for each type [Beck-etal-2015].
- **Cluster C.** In a 50-patient trial, 40 weekly sessions of cognitive therapy did not differ from short-term dynamic therapy; 2 years later 42% of cognitive therapy patients had recovered symptomatically [Svartberg-etal-2004]. About 50 sessions of schema therapy produced more recovery than usual care [Bamelis-etal-2014]. For avoidant personality disorder, group CBT focused on social skills helps [Zimmerman-2023d].
- **Treat the presenting disorder too.** Treating major depression also improved co-occurring personality disorder [Beck-etal-2015].
- **Antisocial.** The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises considering group-based cognitive and behavioral programs for impulsivity, interpersonal problems and offending, contingency management for drug misuse, and no routine medication for aggression or impulsivity [NICE-CG77]. A Cochrane review found very limited, low-certainty evidence for any psychological intervention [Gibbon-etal-2020].
- **Narcissistic, histrionic, paranoid.** Evidence is thin; schema therapy trials included these disorders [Bamelis-etal-2014].

## Risk notes

- People with antisocial personality disorder should not be excluded from services because of their diagnosis or offending history [NICE-CG77].
- If there is current violence, threats suggesting significant risk, or a history of serious violence, including targeting children or vulnerable people → contact or refer to secondary or forensic services [NICE-CG77]. See `02-risk.harm-to-others`; in the United States, check your state's duty-to-warn or duty-to-protect law (`14-ethics-law.duty-to-warn-and-protect`).
- Screen for suicide risk at intake, as for any client with depression or anxiety. United States: call or text 988, or 911. United Kingdom: 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123.
