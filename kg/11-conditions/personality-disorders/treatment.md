**Bottom line:** Treat borderline personality disorder (BPD) with a structured psychotherapy that has research support and targets the core features [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) has the most trials; schema therapy, mentalization-based treatment and other structured models also have trial support, and no type is clearly best [Storebo-etal-2020]. Plan for months, not weeks: most structured programs run 6 to 18 months [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Medication is an adjunct only.

## When to use

- Confirmed BPD, or a personality disorder with borderline pattern in the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11), as the main problem or alongside depression, anxiety or post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).
- The client is willing to commit to a course with an agreed framework.
- Refer elsewhere first for major psychosis, alcohol or Class A drug dependence, or a severe eating disorder, as the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises [NICE-CG78].

## How it works

All effective models share features: a manual or protocol, ongoing supervision, a clear framework, and focus on the core features [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. In cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) terms, treatment breaks the relief cycles that maintain self-harm, builds emotion regulation skills, and then revises the beliefs, schemas and relationship patterns behind them (see `personality-disorders.cbt-model`).

| Approach | Typical dose |
|---|---|
| Standard DBT | Weekly individual therapy, weekly group skills training of about 2.5 hours, phone coaching and a therapist consultation team, usually for 1 year [Linehan-2025]; 6 to 12 months [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024] |
| Structured CBT for BPD | Average of 27 sessions offered over 12 months, 16 attended [Davidson-etal-2006] |
| Schema therapy | Twice weekly for 3 years individually [GiesenBloo-etal-2006]; or individual plus group, twice weekly in year 1, tapering over year 2 [Arntz-etal-2022] |
| Mentalization-based treatment (MBT) | 12 to 18 months [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024] |
| Good psychiatric management (GPM) | About 12 months, generalist, weekly or as needed [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024] |
| Systems Training for Emotional Predictability and Problem Solving (STEPPS) | 20-week group added to other care [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024] |

## Steps

A phased outline for a 12-month weekly CBT-based course. Adjust the pace to the client.

1. **Sessions 1 to 4, engagement.** Finish assessment, discuss the diagnosis, give psychoeducation and hope. Agree the framework: goals, session times, cancellations, fees, your after-hours availability and a crisis plan [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Write the crisis plan with triggers, self-management steps and numbers [NICE-CG78]. Start a daily self-monitoring card.
2. **Sessions 5 to about 20, safety and skills.** Review the card each session. Do a chain analysis of any self-harm or crisis behavior. Teach and rehearse distress tolerance and emotion regulation skills. Address anything interfering with therapy, such as missed sessions.
3. **About sessions 20 to 40, beliefs and patterns.** Work on core beliefs or modes, relationship patterns and ruptures with the therapist. Start exposure-based trauma work only once the alliance is solid and suicide risk is stable [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
4. **Final months, functioning and ending.** Shift to work, study, relationships and routine. Taper session frequency and plan the ending in advance, because endings and transfers can provoke strong reactions [NICE-CG78].

Measure BPD severity, self-harm, depression, substance use and functioning throughout [NICE-CG78].

## Guidance

- If the client wants medication to fix BPD → explain that drugs do not treat the core features and agree, with the prescriber, a time-limited target-symptom trial only. Because the American Psychiatric Association (APA) advises medication be time-limited, symptom-targeted and adjunctive, reviewed at least every 6 months [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
- If reducing recurrent self-harm is the priority for a woman with BPD → consider a comprehensive DBT program. Because NICE recommends it [NICE-CG78].
- If you are a solo CBT therapist without a team → work from one explicit model, share it with the client, and arrange supervision. Because NICE lists an explicit shared model and therapist supervision as required service features [NICE-CG78].
- If you are considering fewer than 3 months of BPD-specific therapy → do not, unless inside a structured service. Because NICE advises against it [NICE-CG78].
- If sessions keep turning into crisis management → return to the agreed framework and crisis plan. Because a stable structure is a shared feature of effective treatments [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].

## Common problems

- **Dropout.** BPD-specific therapy did not reduce attrition compared with usual care [Storebo-etal-2020]. Discuss ambivalence early and agree a plan for missed sessions.
- **Symptom gains without functional gains.** Add vocational and social goals.
- **Therapist strain.** Use supervision or a consultation team.

## Evidence

- **Guidelines.** APA 2024: structured psychotherapy, recommendation strength 1B [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. NICE clinical guideline 78 (CG78), from 2009 and reviewed in 2024: see Guidance; in June 2026 NICE decided to review it in light of ICD-11 [NICE-CG78].
- **Cochrane review, 75 trials, 4,507 people:** psychotherapy beat usual care on BPD severity (standardized mean difference (SMD) minus 0.52, moderate certainty), self-harm (SMD minus 0.32), suicide-related outcomes (minus 0.34) and functioning (minus 0.45), the last three with low certainty. Only severity reached a clinically relevant size, and no therapy type was clearly better [Storebo-etal-2020].
- **Disagreement.** Another meta-analysis found small effects (Hedges g 0.32), inflated by bias and unstable at follow-up; only DBT and psychodynamic approaches beat controls [Cristea-etal-2017].
- **Key trials.** DBT halved suicide attempts compared with expert community therapy [Linehan-etal-2006] but matched GPM [McMain-etal-2009]. Structured CBT reduced suicidal acts over 2 years but not the combined primary outcome [Davidson-etal-2006]. Schema therapy outperformed transference-focused psychotherapy [GiesenBloo-etal-2006], and combined individual and group schema therapy beat usual care (Cohen d 1.14) [Arntz-etal-2022].
