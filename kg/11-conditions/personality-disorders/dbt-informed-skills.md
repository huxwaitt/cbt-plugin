**Bottom line:** Dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) skills give cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) clients concrete alternatives to self-harm and other emotion-driven actions. Use daily monitoring, chain analysis of each target behavior, and skills from four modules: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation and interpersonal effectiveness [Linehan-2025]. Skills training is the component linked to fewer self-injury episodes [Linehan-etal-2015]. Call it DBT-informed CBT, not DBT, and refer for comprehensive DBT when risk is high and persistent.

## When to use

- Emotion dysregulation drives the problem: rapid, intense emotions followed by self-harm, bingeing, substance use, rage or quitting.
- Thought challenging fails because the client is too aroused to use it.
- Alongside or instead of a DBT group when comprehensive DBT is not available.

## How it works

The biosocial theory links these behaviors to impulsivity and emotional sensitivity amplified by an invalidating environment [Crowell-etal-2009]. DBT aims to replace them with skillful responses that get the client through the moment at less cost and lower vulnerability over time [Linehan-2025]. Validation and change are balanced throughout, so the client feels understood while still learning new behavior [Linehan-2025].

| Module | What it is for | Example skills [Linehan-2025] |
|---|---|---|
| Mindfulness | Noticing emotions, urges and thoughts without acting at once | Wise mind; observe, describe, participate; nonjudgmentally, one-mindfully, effectively |
| Distress tolerance | Surviving a crisis without making it worse | STOP (stop, take a step back, observe, proceed mindfully); TIP (temperature, intense exercise, paced breathing, paired muscle relaxation) to bring arousal down fast; pros and cons; mindfulness of current thoughts |
| Emotion regulation | Understanding and changing emotions, lowering vulnerability | Check the facts; opposite action; problem solving; ABC PLEASE; cope ahead |
| Interpersonal effectiveness | Asking, refusing and keeping relationships and self-respect | DEAR MAN (asking or refusing), GIVE (keeping the relationship), FAST (keeping self-respect); mindfulness of others; building and ending relationships |

Before teaching the temperature or intense exercise parts of TIP, check whether the client has a heart condition, an eating disorder or another medical reason to get a doctor's advice first. In DBT groups the core modules take 5 to 7 weeks each, with 2 weeks of mindfulness before each, so one full cycle takes 24 weeks [Linehan-2025].

## Steps

1. **Orient.** Explain the biosocial model and that you will teach skills inside CBT. "Your emotions fire faster and stronger than most people's. The things you do make sense as ways to stop the pain. We'll build other ways that don't cost you so much."
2. **Monitor daily.** Use a diary card: emotion intensity, urges, self-harm, substance use and skills used. Review it at the start of every session.
3. **Chain analysis.** For each target behavior, walk through vulnerabilities, the prompting event, each link (thoughts, sensations, emotions, urges), the behavior and its consequences, short and long term [Linehan-2025].
4. **Find the skill points.** Mark the link where a skill could have changed the outcome.
5. **Teach one skill at a time.** Explain, model, rehearse in session while mildly aroused, and plan when to use it this week.
6. **Assign practice and review it.** Ask what happened, not just whether it was done.
7. **Plan coaching and crises.** Decide in advance whether you take between-session calls. If you do not, write down who the client contacts instead. A crisis line staffed by volunteers is not a substitute for a clinician [Linehan-2025].
8. **Get consultation.** Arrange supervision or peer consultation for yourself [NICE-CG78].

## Guidance

- If the client arrives in crisis with strong urges → start with a distress tolerance skill in the room before any analysis. Because DBT uses crisis survival skills for extreme emotion, past the point where other skills break down [Linehan-2025].
- If the client says "I tried the skill and it didn't work" → do a mini chain analysis of the attempt and adjust timing or choice of skill. Because chain analysis is how DBT finds what prompts a behavior and where a more skillful response fits [Linehan-2025].
- If the client attends a DBT skills group while seeing you for CBT → agree who handles crises and coach skills between groups yourself. Because DBT skills trainers expect the individual therapist to manage suicidal crises and do the coaching [Linehan-2025].
- If self-harm is frequent and suicide attempts recur despite DBT-informed CBT → refer for comprehensive DBT. Because the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) recommends comprehensive DBT when reducing recurrent self-harm is the priority [NICE-CG78].
- If the client is an adolescent with frequent self-harm and emotional dysregulation → consider DBT for adolescents (DBT-A). Because NICE recommends considering it [NICE-NG225].

## Common problems

- **Skills taught as lectures.** Rehearse in session; use real recent situations.
- **Skills used only in crises.** Practice when calm, so they are available when needed.
- **Validation dropped once change starts.** Keep both; see `personality-disorders.pitfall-change-before-validation`.
- **Calling it DBT.** Comprehensive DBT includes individual therapy, group skills training, phone coaching and a consultation team [Linehan-2025].

## Evidence

- In a component trial, all DBT versions reduced suicide attempts, but versions that included skills training reduced nonsuicidal self-injury more than individual DBT without skills [Linehan-etal-2015].
- A 20-week DBT skills group added to usual care reduced suicidal and self-injurious episodes compared with a waitlist [McMain-etal-2017].
- The American Psychiatric Association (APA) notes that DBT skills training alone, with case management in place of individual therapy, showed effects comparable to full DBT and is more accessible [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
- NICE recommends considering comprehensive DBT for women with borderline personality disorder when reducing recurrent self-harm is a priority [NICE-CG78].
