**Bottom line:** Keep one structured plan for borderline personality disorder (BPD) and fit other problems into it, rather than running separate treatments side by side [NICE-CG78]. Refer out for major psychosis, alcohol or Class A drug dependence and severe eating disorders [NICE-CG78]. Raise intensity with severity, weigh development and culture before diagnosing, and treat medication as a time-limited adjunct [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].

## The principle

Three points run through the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guideline, the American Psychiatric Association (APA) guideline and the evidence reviews. Comorbid depression, anxiety and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) are treated inside a well-structured BPD program [NICE-CG78]. No drug treats the core disorder [StoffersWinterling-etal-2022]. Diagnosis must respect development and culture [WHO-CDDR-2024]. Severity guides intensity: severe personality disorder may need multimodal care such as combined group and individual therapy [Bach-First-2018].

## Guidance

**Comorbidity**
- If depression, PTSD or an anxiety disorder co-occurs → treat it within the BPD plan and review both diagnoses first, especially if made in a crisis. Because NICE advises this order [NICE-CG78].
- If PTSD co-occurs → stabilize self-harm and suicide risk and build the alliance before starting exposure-based trauma work. Because APA describes a phased approach for BPD with PTSD [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
- If a substance use disorder co-occurs → begin with motivational interviewing or a brief intervention, stabilize or detoxify if needed, and add evidence-based medication for the substance problem. Because outcomes and safety are worse when both are present [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
- If major psychosis, alcohol or Class A drug dependence, or a severe eating disorder is present → refer to the specialist service and keep in contact so BPD work can resume. Because NICE advises referral for these [NICE-CG78].
- If panic or social anxiety co-occurs → add anxiety-focused elements to the plan and avoid benzodiazepines. Because benzodiazepines can increase impulsivity and carry misuse risk [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].

**Severity**
- If personality problems are mild or subthreshold and a mood or anxiety disorder is the main problem → deliver the disorder-specific protocol with personality-aware pacing and alliance work. Because in one study cited by Beck and colleagues, treating major depression also improved co-occurring personality disorder [Beck-etal-2015].
- If the disorder is severe, with frequent harm to self or others → offer more intensive, multimodal care and involve a specialist team. Because severity predicts risk, dropout and the intensity needed [Bach-First-2018].

**Age**
- If the client is under 18 → offer the full range of treatments through child and adolescent services, and confirm the pattern has lasted at least 1 year before diagnosing. Because NICE gives young people the same access [NICE-CG78] and the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) requires 1 year under 18 [Zimmerman-2023a].
- If an adolescent has frequent self-harm and emotional dysregulation → consider dialectical behavior therapy for adolescents (DBT-A). Because NICE recommends considering it [NICE-NG225] and it reduced suicide attempts and self-harm in 12- to 18-year-olds by the end of treatment [McCauley-etal-2018].
- If an older adult shows new personality change → screen for medical causes, cognitive decline and substance use. Because the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) asks clinicians to consider a medical condition or substance use when personality changes in middle or later life [WHO-CDDR-2024].

**Culture and language**
- If the behavior fits cultural norms or results from social or political conflict → do not diagnose a personality disorder. Because ICD-11 excludes these explanations [WHO-CDDR-2024].
- If language is a barrier → provide therapy in the client's preferred language. Because NICE recommends it [NICE-CG78].

**Learning disability**
- If a client with mild learning disability has BPD → offer the same services, with specialist advice. Because NICE says so; moderate or severe learning disability should not normally receive a BPD diagnosis [NICE-CG78].

**Medication**
- If a client is taking several drugs for BPD alone → ask the prescriber to review and reduce them. Because NICE advises reducing unnecessary drugs, and APA recommends review at least every 6 months [NICE-CG78] [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
- If a prescriber adds sedation in a crisis → expect it to be short and ask that the end date is in the crisis plan. Because NICE limits crisis sedation to no longer than 1 week [NICE-CG78].

**Step up or refer**
- If self-harm or crises continue despite a structured course → step up to comprehensive dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) or a specialist personality disorder service. Because specialist teams serve people with complex needs or high risk [NICE-CG78].

## Example phrasings

- "We'll keep working on the panic, inside the same plan, so we're not pulling in two directions."
- "Medication may help with the depression. It won't change the core patterns, which is what therapy is for."
- "Before we name this a personality pattern, I want to understand how things are seen in your family and community."

## Evidence

The comorbidity and service rules are NICE recommendations from 2009, reviewed in 2024 and under review since June 2026 [NICE-CG78]. APA statements on medication carry low-certainty ratings (1C or 2C) [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. A Cochrane review of 46 drug trials found mostly very low-certainty evidence and no medication that specifically treats BPD [StoffersWinterling-etal-2022]. DBT-A is supported by randomized trials [McCauley-etal-2018].
