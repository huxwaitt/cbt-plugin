**Bottom line:** A personality disorder is an enduring, inflexible pattern of experience and behavior that begins by adolescence or early adulthood and causes distress or impairment across situations [Zimmerman-2023a]. Borderline personality disorder (BPD), the most researched type in cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) [Beck-etal-2015], affects 1.4% to 2.7% of United States adults over a lifetime and about 10% to 25% of psychiatric outpatients and inpatients [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Most people with BPD remit over time [Zanarini-etal-2010]. Suicide risk is high, so assess it from the first contact.

## Diagnostic essentials

Paraphrased from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

**Any personality disorder**
- A persistent, inflexible, pervasive pattern in at least 2 of 4 areas: cognition, affectivity, interpersonal functioning and impulse control [Zimmerman-2023a].
- Clinically significant distress or impairment; stable, with onset traceable to adolescence or early adulthood [Zimmerman-2023a].
- Exclusions: not better explained by another mental disorder, a substance, or a medical condition such as head injury [Zimmerman-2023a].
- Under age 18 the pattern must have lasted at least 1 year; antisocial personality disorder is never diagnosed before 18 [Zimmerman-2023a].
- Ten types in three clusters: A, odd or eccentric (paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal); B, dramatic or erratic (antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic); C, anxious or fearful (avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive) [Zimmerman-2023a]. The criteria are unchanged from the fourth edition (DSM-IV) [APPI-SCID5-Site].
- Section III adds the Alternative Model for Personality Disorders: level of personality functioning plus five pathological trait domains [APPI-SCID5-Site].

**Borderline personality disorder**
- Long-standing instability in relationships, self-image and emotions, plus marked impulsivity, shown by 5 or more of 9 features [Zimmerman-2023b].
- The nine, in plain words: desperate attempts not to be left; relationships that swing between idealizing and devaluing the other person; a shifting sense of who one is; risky impulsive acts in 2 or more areas (such as spending, sex, substances or binge eating); repeated suicidal acts or threats, or self-injury; mood swings set off by events, usually over within hours to days; persistent emptiness; anger that is intense or hard to control; short-lived suspiciousness or feeling cut off from oneself under stress [Zimmerman-2023b].
- Onset by early adulthood, sometimes in adolescence [Zimmerman-2023b].

**Key differences in the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11)**

| Feature | DSM-5-TR | ICD-11 |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Ten categorical types | One diagnosis rated mild, moderate or severe [WHO-CDDR-2024] |
| Duration | Stable pattern from adolescence or early adulthood | Extended period, for example 2 years or more [WHO-CDDR-2024] |
| Style | Type names | Optional trait specifiers: negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition, anankastia [WHO-CDDR-2024] |
| Borderline | A specific disorder | Optional borderline pattern specifier, 5 or more of 9 features [WHO-CDDR-2024] |
| Subthreshold | None | Personality difficulty, coded as a factor influencing health, not a disorder [WHO-CDDR-2024] |
| Schizotypal | Personality disorder | Classified with primary psychotic disorders [Bach-First-2018] |

ICD-11 severity also signals risk: mild is typically not associated with substantial harm to self or others, moderate sometimes is, and severe often is [WHO-CDDR-2024].

## Course and prevalence

- **Any personality disorder:** pooled global community prevalence 7.8%; 9.6% in high-income and 4.3% in low- and middle-income countries; cluster A 3.8%, B 2.8%, C 5.0% [Winsper-etal-2020].
- **BPD:** 1.4% to 2.7% lifetime in the United States; 10% to 18% of outpatients and 9% to 25% of inpatients [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Other estimates run from about 1.7% [Gunderson-etal-2018] to 5.9% [Zimmerman-2023b]; figures vary with sampling and interview method.
- **Sex:** about 75% of those diagnosed are female; the community ratio is about 1:1 [Zimmerman-2023b].
- **Course:** among 290 inpatients followed for 10 years, 93% had a symptomatic remission lasting 2 years and 86% one lasting 4 years, but only 50% reached recovery with good social and vocational functioning [Zanarini-etal-2010].

## Differential and comorbidity

- **Bipolar disorder:** BPD mood shifts are fast and triggered by interpersonal stress; bipolar episodes are sustained and change energy and activity [Zimmerman-2023b]. About 1 in 5 people with bipolar disorder have BPD [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
- **Major depressive disorder:** about 15% of people with major depression have BPD, and almost everyone with BPD has a depressive episode. Neurovegetative symptoms point to depression; abandonment fears, emptiness and self-injury point to BPD [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].
- **Complex post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD):** a stable negative self-view, avoidance and disconnection rather than abandonment fears, and suicidal behavior that is less frequent and less impulsive but can be more severe favor complex PTSD [WHO-CDDR-2024].
- **Comorbidity:** PTSD, anxiety, eating and substance use disorders are also common; panic disorder and social anxiety occur in one-fifth to almost half of some samples [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024].

## CBT approach in brief

The American Psychiatric Association (APA) recommends (1B) a structured psychotherapy with research support that targets the core features; dialectical behavior therapy (DBT), schema-focused therapy, mentalization-based treatment and good psychiatric management each beat comparison treatments on some outcomes [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. In the United Kingdom, the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises against BPD-specific therapy shorter than 3 months outside a structured service, and against drugs for BPD itself; in June 2026 it decided to review this 2009 guideline for ICD-11 [NICE-CG78]. ## Risk notes

- More than 90% of people with BPD self-injure, about 75% attempt suicide, and 3% to 10% die by suicide [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]; another source gives 8% to 10% [Zimmerman-2023b].
- Separate long-standing (chronic) risk from a new rise (acute risk); see `personality-disorders.chronic-risk-and-crises`.
- If a client has a plan, intent or recent attempt, act the same day. United States: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) or 911. United Kingdom: 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123. Agree a safety plan that restricts access to lethal means.
