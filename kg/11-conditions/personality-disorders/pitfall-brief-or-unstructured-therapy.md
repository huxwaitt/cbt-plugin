**Bottom line:** Borderline personality disorder (BPD) needs a structured therapy with an explicit model, a framework and enough time; most structured programs run 6 to 18 months [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Short courses of standard cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for BPD itself, or open-ended sessions that follow each week's crisis, are not what the evidence supports [NICE-CG78]. Choose a structured approach at the start and share it with the client.

## What it looks like

- A client with BPD is offered 6 to 12 sessions of the protocol for their depression or anxiety, with no adjustment for the personality pattern.
- Each session starts from whatever happened that week. There is no written model, crisis plan, target list or end date.
- The therapist switches technique after each setback: thought records, then mindfulness, then "just support."
- The client drops out or the course ends mid-crisis, and the notes say "not suitable for CBT" or "treatment-resistant."

## Why it happens

- Service limits push toward short courses designed for anxiety and depression.
- The personality pattern was not assessed, so the plan was built for the presenting disorder only.
- Crises feel urgent, and following them feels responsive.
- Therapists may not know that structured BPD treatments exist outside specialist services, or that structured CBT itself has been tested [Davidson-etal-2006].

## The fix

1. **Assess for personality disorder** when self-harm, emotional instability or repeated ruptures appear (see `personality-disorders.assessment`).
2. **Pick one explicit model** and share it with the client in writing. The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) requires an explicit, integrated approach shared by therapist and client, structured care, and therapist supervision [NICE-CG78].
3. **Plan the dose.** Do not use psychological interventions of under 3 months specifically for BPD outside such a service [NICE-CG78]. Structured CBT for BPD offered an average of 27 sessions over a year, of which clients attended 16 on average [Davidson-etal-2006].
4. **Agree a framework:** goals, session rules, crisis plan, and how missed sessions are handled.
5. **Monitor broad outcomes,** such as functioning, substance use, self-harm, depression and BPD symptoms, rather than a single symptom scale [NICE-CG78].
6. **If resources are limited,** a time-limited skills group added to other care is a tested option: a 20-week dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) skills group reduced self-harm compared with a waitlist [McMain-etal-2017].

Example: "What you're dealing with is long-standing, so a few sessions on the low mood won't be enough. I'd like to offer a structured plan over about a year. Here's how it works."

## Sources note

NICE clinical guideline 78 (CG78), from 2009 and reviewed in 2024, is the source of the 3-month rule and service features [NICE-CG78]; the American Psychiatric Association (APA) recommends a structured psychotherapy with research support, rated 1B [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024]. Brief work is not always wrong: NICE recommends 4 to 10 sessions of CBT-informed therapy tailored for adults who self-harm in general [NICE-NG225], and brief skills groups help as an add-on [McMain-etal-2017]. The pitfall is brief or unstructured work offered as the treatment for BPD itself. Across structured therapies, no single type is clearly better [Storebo-etal-2020].
