# 11-conditions/personality-disorders: Personality Disorders

## This surface
Personality disorders as they affect cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), with a focus on borderline personality disorder (BPD): recognition, assessment, formulation, structured treatment, dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) skills, schema therapy, crises and adaptations. These pages span the intake, assessment, formulation, planning and treatment stages. Start with the overview and assessment, use the model and treatment pages to plan, and check the pitfalls when a course stalls or keeps lurching from crisis to crisis.

## Pages in order
1. `personality-disorders.overview`: the diagnostic picture, International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) differences, differentials and suicide risk facts come first.
2. `personality-disorders.assessment`: screening cutoffs, structured interview, severity measures, collateral and how to share the diagnosis.
3. `personality-disorders.cbt-model`: Beck, biosocial and schema mode models turn the assessment into a shared formulation and targets.
4. `personality-disorders.treatment`: guideline status, choice among structured therapies, dose and a phased session outline.
5. `personality-disorders.dbt-informed-skills`: chain analysis, diary cards and the four skills modules used inside CBT.
6. `personality-disorders.chronic-risk-and-crises`: chronic versus acute risk, crisis response, admission and between-session contact.
7. `personality-disorders.schema-therapy-link`: when to refer for or integrate schema therapy, with dose and evidence.
8. `personality-disorders.adaptations`: changes for comorbidity, severity, age, culture, learning disability and medication, and when to step up.
9. `personality-disorders.other-personality-disorders`: clusters A and C and antisocial or narcissistic patterns, with their CBT evidence.

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Brief or Unstructured Therapy for Borderline Personality Disorder | A client with borderline personality disorder gets a short standard CBT course or a plan that changes each crisis, then drops out or relapses and is called treatment-resistant. | `personality-disorders.pitfall-brief-or-unstructured-therapy` |
| Pushing Change Before Validation | The therapist challenges thoughts or sets behavior goals while the client is flooded, the client feels dismissed, escalates or shuts down, and the session ends in rupture or missed homework. | `personality-disorders.pitfall-change-before-validation` |
| Crisis-Driven Treatment Drift | Each crisis changes the plan, adds a medication, extends contact or triggers admission, so the agreed treatment never runs and the therapist ends up exhausted, resentful or rescuing. | `personality-disorders.pitfall-crisis-driven-drift` |
| Withholding or Misusing the Diagnosis | The clinician avoids naming borderline personality disorder, or uses it as a pejorative or reason to exclude, so the client lacks an explanation, a matched treatment and hope. | `personality-disorders.pitfall-withholding-the-diagnosis` |
