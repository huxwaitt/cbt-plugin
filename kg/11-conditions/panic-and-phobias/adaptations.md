**Bottom line:** Keep the core of exposure and testing catastrophic predictions, and change the delivery: who joins sessions, where exposure happens, how meaning is framed, and how medication is handled. The biggest risks are as-needed benzodiazepines working as safety behaviors, untreated depression or alcohol use, and suicide risk that goes unasked. Step up or refer when the formulation, not the session count, says the treatment is not reaching the problem.

## The principle

Adapt delivery, not mechanism. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for panic and phobias works by letting the client find out that feared sensations and situations do not lead to the predicted disaster. Every adaptation should keep that test intact and remove barriers to it.

## Guidance

**Comorbidity**
- If major depression is also present → assess which problem drives the other; if depression blocks attendance or practice, treat it first or in parallel. Because comorbidity is the rule: 83.1% of people with panic disorder had another lifetime disorder [Kessler-etal-2006].
- If the client uses alcohol or cannabis to get through attacks → name it as a safety behavior, assess for a use disorder, and plan reduction alongside exposure. Because safety behaviors block the disconfirmation that exposure depends on [Salkovskis-etal-1999], and a use disorder may need its own treatment (see `substance-use.assessment`).
- If panic attacks occur only at trauma reminders or with intrusive thoughts → treat the primary disorder (posttraumatic stress disorder or obsessive-compulsive disorder) rather than running a panic protocol. Because attacks better explained by another disorder do not count toward panic disorder [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

**Severity and format**
- If the presentation is mild to moderate → offer guided self-help or internet CBT first. Because the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) offers self-help at step 2 [NICE-CG113], and internet CBT showed a mean effect of g = 0.80 across panic, social anxiety, generalized anxiety and depression [Andrews-etal-2018].
- If the client is housebound → start with telehealth or home visits, and do the first exposure steps together at the door, then the street. Because therapist-guided exposure in situ improved agoraphobic avoidance more than prescribed exposure [Gloster-etal-2011].
- If there is no gain after a fair trial with exposure done well → review the formulation, then discuss medication or referral to specialist services. Because NICE offers referral to specialist mental health services when 2 interventions (therapy, medication or self-help, in any combination) leave significant symptoms [NICE-CG113].

**Age**
- If the client is a child or adolescent → involve parents in planning and in reducing accommodation, use the youth Panic Disorder Severity Scale (PDSS-C) for ages 11 to 17 [Elkins-etal-2014], and consider brief intensive formats. Because the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry guideline finds considerable support for CBT in youth anxiety [Walter-etal-2020], and brief intensive treatments matched standard CBT [Ost-Ollendick-2017].
- If an older adult fears falling or collapsing outside → check physical risk with their doctor, separate realistic precautions from safety behaviors, and grade exposure to real capacity. Because late-onset agoraphobia often centers on personal safety and physical limits [Barnhill-2026b].

**Culture**
- If the client explains attacks through a cultural syndrome (khyâl, ataque de nervios, "weak heart") → ask what they fear the symptoms will do, and test that specific prediction. Because cultural syndromes shape the catastrophic cognitions that drive panic [Lewis-Fernandez-etal-2010; Hinton-etal-2012].
- If a woman's limited travel reflects family or cultural norms → do not label it agoraphobia without fear-driven avoidance. Because culturally sanctioned restrictions must be distinguished from the disorder [Lewis-Fernandez-etal-2010].

**Medication**
- If the client takes a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) → continue CBT. Because combined treatment beat either alone in the short term, and after treatment ended it matched psychotherapy alone [Furukawa-etal-2007]. Antidepressants beat placebo with a number needed to treat of 7, on low-quality evidence [Bighelli-etal-2018].
- If the client carries a benzodiazepine "just in case" → explore its role in the formulation, and plan any reduction with the prescriber, never by advising the client to stop. Because benzodiazepines help short term [Breilmann-etal-2019], but evidence that they add to psychotherapy is inadequate [Watanabe-etal-2009], and NICE advises against them for panic disorder [NICE-CG113]. Scope of practice: only prescribers change medication.

**Risk**
- If the client reports hopelessness or suicidal thoughts → assess risk the same day and follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`. Because panic disorder independently raises the odds of suicide attempts [Nepon-etal-2010]. Crisis contacts: US call or text 988 [Lifeline-988] or 911; UK 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111, Samaritans 116 123 [Samaritans-Site].

## Example phrasings

- Benzodiazepine: "Many people keep a tablet in their bag as insurance. Part of what we're testing is whether you need it. Would you be willing to talk with your doctor about a plan for that?" Avoid: "You should stop taking those."
- Culture: "In your family, what do people say happens when the wind rises in the body? What do you fear it might do to you?"
- Parent: "When you drive him everywhere, the fear never gets tested. Let's plan small steps you can step back from."
- Step-up: "We've done the exposure carefully and you're still stuck. Let's look again at what's keeping this going, and talk about whether medication or a specialist service would add something."

## Evidence

Combination and medication findings come from Cochrane reviews with low to moderate quality evidence [Furukawa-etal-2007; Bighelli-etal-2018; Breilmann-etal-2019; Watanabe-etal-2009]. Format adaptations rest on randomized trials and meta-analyses [Andrews-etal-2018; Gloster-etal-2011; Ost-Ollendick-2017]. Cultural adaptations rest on reviews and small trials [Lewis-Fernandez-etal-2010; Hinton-etal-2012], and substance use guidance on expert consensus.
