**Bottom line:** Map one recent attack or phobic encounter in detail, then inventory avoidance and safety behaviors, screen for medical and substance causes, ask what the client fears will happen, and measure severity at baseline. The Panic Disorder Severity Scale (PDSS) and the free severity measures from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) track change; in panic disorder, a Mobility Inventory avoidance-alone score of 1.61 or more suggests agoraphobia.

## When to use

At intake and in early sessions whenever panic attacks, fear-driven avoidance or a phobia is on the problem list. Repeat measures every session or every few sessions.

## Steps

1. **Screen.** The Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) and its two-item core performed well for panic as well as generalized anxiety (area under the curve 0.80 to 0.91 across four anxiety disorders) [Kroenke-etal-2007]. UK guidance relies on interview, judging no panic screen well enough validated [NICE-CG113]. Ask: "Have you had sudden rushes of fear or discomfort that peaked within minutes?"
2. **Map a recent attack.** Where were you, and what did you notice first? At the worst moment, what did you think would happen? How much did you believe it then, and now (0 to 100)? What did you do to stop it or stay safe? How did it end? Count attacks per week and note which came "out of the blue".
3. **Check worry and change.** Has worry about more attacks, or a change in behavior, lasted 1 month or more [APA-DSM5TR-2022]?
4. **Inventory avoidance.** Situations (the five agoraphobia groups), activities that cause sensations (exercise, caffeine, heat, sex, arguments), and objects or places for phobias. Then safety behaviors: carrying pills or water, sitting near exits, needing a companion, holding on, slow breathing to "stop a heart attack", distraction.
5. **Screen medical and substance causes.** Date of last physical exam and tests; heart, thyroid, lung, vestibular and seizure history; caffeine, stimulants, cannabis, alcohol, and benzodiazepine use or withdrawal [Barnhill-2026a]. Onset after 45 or atypical symptoms need medical review [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. Pair any work-up with psychoeducation, since reassurance alone rarely settles worry [Tunnell-etal-2024].
6. **Ask differential questions.** "What were you most afraid would happen?" Imminent death or collapse fits panic; being judged fits social anxiety; illness over months fits health anxiety; harm from the object fits phobia; reminders of trauma fit posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD).
7. **Ask about culture and meaning.** "What do you, or people in your family, call these attacks? What do you think causes them?" Cultural syndromes such as khyâl attacks or ataque de nervios shape which sensations are feared [Lewis-Fernandez-etal-2010].
8. **Measure** (below) and **screen risk**: panic disorder raises the odds of suicide attempts [Nepon-etal-2010]; see `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`.
9. **Collect collateral** with consent: what family members do to help (driving, shopping, staying home).

| Measure | Format | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| PDSS, clinician-rated | 7 items, total 0 to 28 [Shear-etal-1997] | Without agoraphobia 2 to 5 borderline, 6 to 9 slightly, 10 to 13 moderately, 14 or more markedly ill; with agoraphobia 3 to 7, 8 to 10, 11 to 15, 16 or more. Remission 5 or less; response a 40% or greater drop [Furukawa-etal-2009] |
| DSM-5-TR severity measures for panic, agoraphobia, specific phobia (adult; child 11 to 17) | 10 items, 0 to 4, past 7 days, total 0 to 40 | Average 0 none, 1 mild, 2 moderate, 3 severe, 4 extreme; free for clinical use [APA-DSM5TR-Severity-Measures] |
| Mobility Inventory | Avoidance alone and accompanied subscales | In 129 people with panic disorder, avoidance alone at 1.61: sensitivity .87, specificity .73 for agoraphobia [Chambless-etal-2011] |
| Body Sensations Questionnaire and Agoraphobic Cognitions Questionnaire | Fear of sensations; frequency of catastrophic thoughts [Chambless-etal-1984] | Use to pick interoceptive and cognitive targets |
| Anxiety Sensitivity Index-3 (ASI-3) | 18 items; physical, cognitive, social concerns [Taylor-etal-2007] | Track physical concerns over treatment |
| PDSS for children (PDSS-C) | Youth version, alpha .82 [Elkins-etal-2014] | Ages 11 to 17 |

## What to record

- Attack map: sensations, catastrophic thought with belief rating, safety behaviors, outcome.
- Frequency of full and limited-symptom attacks; expected or unexpected.
- Avoidance and safety behavior lists, ranked by fear (0 to 100), as the seed of an exposure hierarchy.
- Medical and substance screen, with any referral and its result.
- Baseline scores, differential reasoning, risk screen, and the client's own explanation.

## Guidance

- If the client is sure the attacks are a heart problem and has never been checked → arrange a medical review before exposure, and frame it as a one-time check, not ongoing reassurance. Because medical causes must be excluded first [APA-DSM5TR-2022], but repeated checking maintains the fear [Tunnell-etal-2024].
- If the client says attacks "just happen" → ask about the seconds before the attack and the first body change. Because many attacks labeled unexpected follow an internal cue, and cued attacks were more common than uncued ones even in panic disorder [Kessler-etal-2006].
- If panic attacks occur only in one feared situation → assess the disorder that owns that situation and add the panic attack specifier. Because panic disorder needs recurrent unexpected attacks [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- If a score is high but the client describes cultural attack symptoms → record them and ask what they fear the symptoms mean. Because culture-specific symptoms do not count toward the four attack symptoms [APA-DSM5TR-2022] but can carry catastrophic meanings [Lewis-Fernandez-etal-2010].

## Common problems

- Accepting "I avoid nothing" without asking about companions and subtle avoidance.
- Skipping the substance screen, especially caffeine and withdrawal.
- Applying clinician-rated PDSS bands to self-report scores unnoticed.
- Embedding copyrighted scales such as the PDSS in software without checking terms; see `08-measurement.instrument-licensing`.
