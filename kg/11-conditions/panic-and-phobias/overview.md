**Bottom line:** Panic disorder is recurrent unexpected panic attacks plus at least 1 month of worry about more attacks or a maladaptive change in behavior. Agoraphobia and specific phobia are separate diagnoses of out-of-proportion fear and avoidance of situations or objects, typically lasting 6 months or more. All three are common, start early, persist without treatment, and respond to exposure-based cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT).

## Diagnostic essentials

Paraphrased from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. Who may diagnose depends on license.

- **Panic attack:** an abrupt surge of intense fear or discomfort that peaks within minutes, with 4 or more of 13 physical and cognitive symptoms (for example palpitations, breathlessness, dizziness, derealization, fear of dying or losing control). It can start from a calm or an anxious state. Culture-specific symptoms such as tinnitus, neck soreness, headache or uncontrollable screaming do not count toward the four. A "with panic attacks" specifier can be added to other mental disorders, such as posttraumatic stress disorder.
- **Panic disorder:** recurrent unexpected attacks; at least one is followed by 1 month or more of persistent concern about further attacks or their consequences, a significant maladaptive behavior change (for example avoiding exercise), or both. Exclude substances, medical conditions, and attacks better explained by another disorder.
- **Agoraphobia:** marked fear of 2 or more of 5 situation groups (public transport, open spaces, enclosed places, lines or crowds, being outside home alone), feared because escape or help might be unavailable if panic-like or embarrassing symptoms occur; typically 6 months or more [APA-DSM5TR-2022; Barnhill-2026b]. It is diagnosed whether or not panic disorder is present.
- **Specific phobia:** marked, out-of-proportion fear of a specific object or situation that almost always provokes immediate fear and is avoided or endured, typically 6 months or more [Barnhill-2026c]. Specifiers: animal, natural environment, blood-injection-injury, situational, other.

US codes in the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM): panic disorder F41.0; agoraphobia F40.00; specific phobia F40.218 animal, F40.228 natural environment, F40.230 to F40.233 blood-injection-injury subtypes, F40.248 situational, F40.298 other [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

**International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) differences.** ICD-11 groups these under anxiety or fear-related disorders defined by the "focus of apprehension" (codes 6B01 panic disorder, 6B02 agoraphobia, 6B03 specific phobia) [Kogan-etal-2016; WHO-ICD11-2022]. Agoraphobia needs fear of multiple situations, focused on incapacitating or embarrassing outcomes, without a fixed count of situation groups. Attacks that occur only with another disorder's feared stimulus take a "with panic attacks" qualifier rather than a panic disorder diagnosis. ICD-11 generally avoids fixed counts and durations [Reed-etal-2019].

## Course and prevalence

| | Prevalence | Onset and sex |
|---|---|---|
| Panic disorder | 12-month 2% to 3% [Barnhill-2026a]; 2.8% in the National Comorbidity Survey Replication (NCS-R); isolated panic attacks lifetime 22.7% [Kessler-etal-2006] | Late adolescence or early adulthood [Barnhill-2026a], average about 22 to 23 [Tunnell-etal-2024]; women about twice men [Barnhill-2026a] |
| Agoraphobia | About 1% to 2% per year; 30% to 50% also have panic disorder [Barnhill-2026b] | Adolescence or young adulthood; can begin late in life [Barnhill-2026b] |
| Specific phobia | Lifetime median 7.2% across 25 adult surveys [Eaton-etal-2018]; 12-month about 8% of women and 3% of men [Barnhill-2026c] | Childhood; median onset 8 years in World Mental Health surveys [Eaton-etal-2018] |

Untreated panic disorder usually waxes and wanes [Barnhill-2026a]. In the NCS-R, 57.4% of lifetime panic disorder cases without agoraphobia and 62.6% with agoraphobia still had it in the past year [Kessler-etal-2006]. Treatment delays of 9 to 15 years are reported [Tunnell-etal-2024]. Specific phobias persist for years in 10% to 30% of cases and predict later anxiety, mood and substance disorders [Eaton-etal-2018].

## Differential and comorbidity

- **Medical:** hyperthyroidism, acute coronary syndrome, arrhythmias, asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and substance use or withdrawal can mimic or coexist with panic [Barnhill-2026a]. Up to 12% of all emergency department patients, and 20% to 35% of those with chest pain, meet panic disorder criteria [Tunnell-etal-2024]. Onset after 45 or atypical symptoms (vertigo, loss of consciousness, loss of bladder or bowel control, slurred speech) point toward a medical or substance cause [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- **Psychiatric:** ask what the client fears will happen. Imminent catastrophe during attacks suggests panic; long-term disease worry suggests health anxiety; embarrassment or judgment suggests social anxiety; attacks only at trauma reminders suggest posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD); attacks only at obsessions suggest obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- **Comorbidity:** other anxiety disorders, major depression, bipolar disorder and alcohol use are common [Barnhill-2026a]. In the NCS-R, 83.1% of people with panic disorder alone, and all with agoraphobia, had another lifetime disorder [Kessler-etal-2006].

## CBT approach in brief

For panic disorder, the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) offers self-help for mild to moderate cases, then CBT of 7 to 14 hours completed within 4 months, or a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) antidepressant if the disorder is long-standing or therapy has not helped or is declined; benzodiazepines should not be prescribed [NICE-CG113]. Exposure, in vivo where possible, is the core of phobia treatment; see `panic-and-phobias.treatment` and `panic-and-phobias.specific-phobia`.

## Risk notes

- Panic disorder is independently associated with suicide attempts (adjusted odds ratio 1.31), rising to 5.76 with a comorbid personality disorder [Nepon-etal-2010]. Ask directly about suicidal thoughts and follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`; if risk is imminent, do not leave the client alone and call emergency services.
- Crisis contacts: US 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) [Lifeline-988] or 911; UK 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111, or Samaritans on 116 123 [Samaritans-Site].
- Never attribute new or changed chest pain, fainting or breathlessness to panic without a medical assessment.
