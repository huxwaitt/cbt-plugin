**Bottom line:** Three adult attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) programs dominate. Safren's individual cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) has the strongest trial support [Safren-etal-2010]; Solanto's group meta-cognitive therapy (MCT) targets time management, organization and planning [Solanto-etal-2010]; Ramsay and Rostain's integrative approach combines CBT with medication and stresses implementation over a longer course [Ramsay-Rostain-2015]. All share a core: an external organizing system, task breakdown, distraction management, and work on thoughts that block action [Knouse-Safren-2010].

## When to use

| Program | Format and dose | Best fit |
|---|---|---|
| Safren CBT | 12 individual sessions of about 50 minutes [Safren-etal-2010] | Medicated adults with residual symptoms; comorbid mood or anxiety |
| Solanto MCT | 12 weekly 2-hour groups of 6 to 8 [Solanto-etal-2010] | Mainly inattentive problems; services that run groups |
| Ramsay and Rostain | 16 individual 50-minute sessions plus stimulant medication over 6 months in an open study [Knouse-Safren-2010] | Complex cases; integrated psychiatric care |

## How it works

All three treat ADHD as a problem of performance: medication may reduce symptoms but does not build compensatory habits, so skills are taught, rehearsed and repeated until automatic [Knouse-Safren-2010] [Solanto-etal-2010].

## Steps

**Safren individual CBT** (manual: *Mastering Your Adult ADHD*, 2nd edition [Safren-etal-2017]) [Safren-etal-2010]:
1. **Psychoeducation, organizing and planning (4 sessions):** ADHD education; a single calendar and task list, reviewed daily; prioritizing tasks; problem solving (list options, choose, break overwhelming tasks into steps).
2. **Distractibility (2 sessions):** measure attention span with a timer, set task chunks to match, and write distractions down to deal with later instead of switching.
3. **Adaptive thinking (3 sessions):** thought records, thinking errors and rational responses for self-critical, anxious and avoidant thoughts.
4. **Optional:** procrastination (1 session, used by 40 of 43 trial participants); family member session (1, used by 27 of 43).
5. **Relapse prevention (1 session).**
Each session opens with symptom and medication adherence review and homework review [Knouse-Safren-2010].

**Solanto group MCT** [Solanto-etal-2010]:
1. **Session 1:** orientation and expectations (regular, punctual attendance; confidentiality).
2. **Sessions 2 to 6:** time awareness; starting and finishing tasks by breaking them down; rewarding oneself after aversive tasks; scheduling and prioritizing; picturing long-term rewards; standard CBT for depressive and anxious thoughts.
3. **Sessions 7 to 9:** setting up and keeping organizational systems.
4. **Sessions 10 to 11:** planning, using flow charts of goals and subgoals.
5. **Session 12:** review and plan continued practice.
Each session spends the first hour reviewing home exercises and troubleshooting obstacles, then teaches and models a new skill. Short cue phrases link a problem to its solution, for example "Out of sight, out of mind" for clearing distractions [Solanto-etal-2010].

**Ramsay and Rostain integrative CBT** (outline from published descriptions, not the manual itself) [Ramsay-Rostain-2015] [Knouse-Safren-2010]:
1. Psychoeducation and a shared case formulation.
2. Coping strategies for time, organization and procrastination, with emphasis on implementation in daily life.
3. Modifying unhelpful thoughts and treatment-interfering behavior.
4. Building on strengths.
5. Medication management coordinated with therapy, then maintenance and follow-up.

## Guidance

- If the client knows the strategies but does not use them → shift time to implementation: if-then plans, cues and rehearsal of the first step. Because implementation is the stated focus of the integrative approach [Ramsay-Rostain-2015] and of work on avoidance [Knouse-Mitchell-2015].
- If the client is mainly inattentive and a group is available → consider MCT. Because it was designed around inattention, the more prevalent adult symptom domain, and improved blind-rated inattention [Solanto-etal-2010].
- If depression or anxiety is prominent → favor the Safren protocol with its adaptive thinking module. Because it reduced anxiety and depression ratings alongside ADHD symptoms [Safren-etal-2005], while MCT showed no advantage over support on mood, anxiety or self-esteem [Solanto-etal-2010].
- If an optional module is not relevant → use the session as a booster on earlier skills. Because that is how the trial protocol handled it [Safren-etal-2010].

## Common problems

- Mixing protocols into a broad, unfocused course; narrower skill-focused programs performed best [Knouse-Safren-2010].
- Group dropout; plan reminders and a catch-up handout for missed sessions.
- Treating the integrative course as open-ended; set goals and review progress at fixed points (expert consensus).

## Evidence

- Safren CBT: randomized trials against relaxation with educational support (86 adults; response 53% versus 23%) [Safren-etal-2010] and medication alone (31 adults; 56% versus 13%) [Safren-etal-2005].
- Solanto MCT: randomized trial of 88 adults versus supportive group; blind-rated response 42% versus 12% (adjusted odds ratio 5.41), with inattention gains on self, informant and blinded evaluator ratings [Solanto-etal-2010].
- Ramsay and Rostain: open study only (43 patients; clinician-rated clinical global impression severity fell from 5.28 to 3.40), so effects of CBT and medication cannot be separated [Knouse-Safren-2010].
- Overall, CBT for adult ADHD rests on low to moderate certainty evidence [Lopez-etal-2018].
