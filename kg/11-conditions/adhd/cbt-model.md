**Bottom line:** In the leading cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) model of adult attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), neurobiological deficits in attention, inhibition, working memory and motivation make it hard to learn and use compensatory skills; the resulting failures breed negative beliefs, low mood and avoidance, which further reduce skill use [Safren-etal-2004] [Knouse-Safren-2010]. A second loop runs through overly optimistic, permission-giving thoughts ("I work better at the last minute") that relieve discomfort now and feed procrastination [Knouse-Mitchell-2015]. Treatment therefore teaches skills and targets whatever stops the client using them.

## Components

**Safren model** [Safren-etal-2004] [Knouse-Safren-2010]:
1. **Core neuropsychological deficits:** sustained attention, inhibitory control, working memory and motivation, which produce the core symptoms.
2. **Failure to use compensatory strategies:** organizing, planning and task breakdown are hard to learn and keep using.
3. **Failure and underachievement:** years of missed deadlines, unfinished work and negative feedback from parents, teachers and peers.
4. **Negative thoughts and beliefs:** "I'm lazy," "I always mess things up," "There's no point trying."
5. **Mood and avoidance:** low mood, anxiety and avoidance of demanding tasks.
6. **Feedback:** avoidance and low mood make consistent skill practice less likely, so impairment grows.

**Positively valenced avoidance loop** [Knouse-Mitchell-2015]:
- **Trigger:** a task that brings discomfort (a looming deadline, a room to organize).
- **Aversive response:** overwhelm, anxiety, annoyance, feeling trapped.
- **Overly optimistic thought:** "It'll only take 30 minutes, so I can start later," "I'll sort my email first, then start."
- **Short-term relief:** the thought makes the task feel less urgent, so delay is negatively reinforced.
- **Long-term cost:** less active coping, more behavioral avoidance and less use of CBT skills.

In two online community samples, such thoughts correlated with functional impairment, time-management problems and avoidant coping, and positively (about r = .40) with negative automatic thoughts, suggesting a shared avoidance function [Knouse-etal-2019].

**Implementation.** Ramsay and Rostain stress that many adults know what to do but struggle to act on it, so treatment emphasizes implementation of coping skills in daily life [Ramsay-Rostain-2015]. ADHD is described as a disorder of performance rather than knowledge [Knouse-Safren-2010].

## How it keeps the problem going

- **Skill deficit loop:** no reliable calendar or task list, so commitments are missed, which confirms "I can't manage" and lowers the effort put into a system [Knouse-Safren-2010].
- **Negative belief loop:** expected failure leads to avoidance of hard tasks, which produces the failure that was predicted [Safren-etal-2004].
- **Optimism loop:** permission-giving thoughts relieve discomfort, so delay is rewarded each time, and the deadline crisis is later read as proof that "I work best under pressure" [Knouse-Mitchell-2015].
- **Delay aversion:** immediate relief outweighs the delayed payoff of active coping [Knouse-Mitchell-2015].

## Drawing it with the client

1. Pick one recent, specific episode: "Last Tuesday, the expense report."
2. Map it as Trigger, Response, Avoidance Pattern (TRAP): what happened, what you felt, what you told yourself, what you did, and what happened next. Then add Alternative Coping (TRAC) [Knouse-Mitchell-2015].
3. Draw the longer cycle across the page: core symptoms, missed strategy, outcome, thought about self, mood, next avoidance [Knouse-Safren-2010].
4. Look for "red flag thoughts" that recur across episodes [Knouse-Mitchell-2015].
5. Ask about function, not accuracy: "What did that thought do for you in the moment? And what did it cost by Friday?"
6. Mark where skills will break the cycle and where thoughts will be worked on. Keep the diagram on one page, in the client's words.

Example phrasings:
- "It sounds like 'I'll do it later' brought quick relief. Shall we look at what it cost you later in the week?"
- "Your brain makes starting harder than it is for others. That's the ADHD part. The 'I'm lazy' part is a conclusion we can test."
- Avoid: "That's just an excuse." It shames the client and misses the thought's relief function.

## Treatment targets it implies

- **Compensatory skills, overlearned:** one calendar, one task list, daily review, breaking tasks into steps, managing distractions [Knouse-Safren-2010] [Solanto-etal-2010].
- **Implementation:** cues, reminders and if-then plans ("If I open my laptop at 9, then I open the task list first") so skills happen at the point of performance [Knouse-Mitchell-2015] [Ramsay-Rostain-2015].
- **Negative thoughts and beliefs:** cognitive restructuring for self-critical, hopeless and anxious thoughts [Knouse-Safren-2010].
- **Optimistic avoidance thoughts:** spot them, examine their function and replace delay with a planned active step; progress is counted in active-coping episodes, not in thoughts eliminated [Knouse-Mitchell-2015].
- **Mood and anxiety:** treat comorbid depression or anxiety that drives avoidance (see `adhd.adaptations`).
