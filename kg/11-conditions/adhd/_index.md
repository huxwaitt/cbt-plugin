# 11-conditions/adhd: Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)

## This surface
Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) in children, adolescents and adults: diagnosis, assessment with informants and rating scales, the cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) model of adult ADHD, skills-based adult CBT protocols, behavioral parent, classroom and organization programs for children, how therapy fits with medication, under-recognition in women and late-diagnosed adults, and associated features such as emotional dysregulation, sleep problems and ADHD in later life. These pages span assessment, formulation, planning and treatment, and link to the intake, measurement, formulation and pharmacology domains.

## Pages in order
1. `adhd.overview`: Start here: what ADHD is, how DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 define it, and what commonly co-occurs.
2. `adhd.assessment`: Confirm the picture with informants, records and rating scales, and rule out look-alikes.
3. `adhd.underdiagnosis-in-women-and-adults`: When ADHD may have been missed in a girl, woman or adult, or a late diagnosis needs working through.
4. `adhd.cbt-model`: Formulate why skills are not used and procrastination persists before choosing techniques.
5. `adhd.treatment`: Plan the adult course: the 12-session skills protocol, evidence and guideline status.
6. `adhd.adult-cbt-modules`: Choose between and deliver the Safren, Solanto, or Ramsay and Rostain protocols.
7. `adhd.children-and-parents`: For children and teenagers: parent training, classroom management and organization skills.
8. `adhd.medication-and-cbt`: When the client is on, starting or questioning medication, and how CBT complements it.
9. `adhd.adaptations`: Adjust structure, homework and pacing, handle comorbidity, age and culture, and decide when to refer.
10. `adhd.emotional-dysregulation-and-associated-features`: When emotional outbursts, rejection sensitivity, late sleep or older age shape the presentation.

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosing ADHD from a Screener or Self-Report Alone | An ADHD label is given or accepted on the strength of a positive questionnaire, a client's self-diagnosis or current symptoms, without childhood onset, informants, other settings or ruling out look-alikes. | `adhd.pitfall-diagnosis-from-self-report-alone` |
| Missing or Arguing with Overly Optimistic Thoughts | The therapist hunts only for negative thoughts and misses "I'll do it later" style permission-giving thoughts, or debates whether the optimism is accurate, while procrastination continues unchanged. | `adhd.pitfall-disputing-optimistic-thoughts` |
| Teaching ADHD Skills Without Building Them into Daily Life | Sessions explain planners, task lists and timers well, the client agrees they make sense, but between sessions nothing is used, and therapy drifts into talking about ADHD. | `adhd.pitfall-skills-without-implementation` |
| Offering Individual Talk or Cognitive Therapy to Young Children with ADHD | A young child with ADHD gets individual talk therapy or a social skills group, while parents and teachers get little help and behavior at home and school stays unchanged. | `adhd.pitfall-child-cognitive-therapy-first` |
