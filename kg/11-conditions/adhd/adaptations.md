**Bottom line:** Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) affects the very behaviors cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) relies on: arriving on time, remembering homework and holding a thread through the session. Build external structure into therapy itself (reminders, written summaries, short focused homework, one skill at a time) and adjust for comorbidity, age and context. Step up or refer when risk, substance misuse, severe mood problems or lack of progress call for it.

## The principle

ADHD is a problem of doing what one knows, not of knowing what to do [Knouse-Safren-2010]. Adaptations therefore move help to the point of performance: the place and moment the client must act [Ramsay-Rostain-2015]. Effective adult programs keep a predictable structure (homework review, new skill, in-session practice, next assignment with troubleshooting) [Solanto-etal-2010] and keep content narrow [Knouse-Safren-2010].

## Guidance

**Session structure and homework**
- If the client misses or forgets sessions → agree a reminder system (text, calendar invite) and, with consent, a support person. Because a coach who reminded participants and called weekly was built into an effective program [Knouse-Safren-2010].
- If the client loses track mid-session → keep a written agenda in view, summarize every 10 to 15 minutes, and end with a written summary the client photographs. Because external cues stand in for working memory (expert consensus).
- If homework is not done → set one specific task, rehearse its first step in session, and plan obstacles before leaving. Because home-exercise completion predicted symptom improvement [Solanto-etal-2010].
- If the client wants to cover everything → keep to one new skill per session and revisit earlier skills. Because short, skill-focused programs had the largest effects [Knouse-Safren-2010].

**Comorbidity**
- If depression or anxiety is present → include the adaptive thinking module and track mood each session. Because CBT for adult ADHD also reduced anxiety and depression ratings in a randomized trial [Safren-etal-2005].
- If the client misuses alcohol or drugs → assess use, coordinate with substance use care, and share history with the prescriber. Because ADHD raises the odds of substance use disorders [Faraone-etal-2021] and stimulants carry misuse warnings [FDA-Stimulant-Warning-2023].
- If the client is also autistic → use autism adaptations (concrete language, visual supports, predictable sessions). Because both diagnoses can be made together since the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5) [Epstein-Loren-2013]; see `13-context-culture-development.autism-adaptations`.
- If suicidal thoughts or self-harm appear → assess risk the same day and follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`. Because suicide attempt and death rates are raised in ADHD, especially with another psychiatric diagnosis [Faraone-etal-2021]. United States (US): 988 (call or text) or 911; United Kingdom (UK): Samaritans 116 123, National Health Service (NHS) 111, or 999 [Lifeline-988] [Samaritans-Site].

**Age and development**
- If the client is an adolescent on medication with residual problems → consider adolescent-adapted CBT with parent involvement. Because in a randomized trial of 46 adolescents aged 14 to 18, response was 50% after CBT versus 18% on waitlist by parent report [Sprich-etal-2016].
- If the client is an older adult → assess carefully for other causes of new attention problems. Because ADHD persists in later life (about 2.2% of older adults by rating scales, far fewer by clinical diagnosis) [Faraone-etal-2021], but problems that begin late suggest another cause (expert consensus).

**Culture and context**
- If the family or community sees ADHD as laziness, poor parenting or a label to avoid → explore their explanation first, then offer the model as one way to understand the difficulties. Because acceptance of the model shapes engagement (expert consensus); see `13-context-culture-development.cultural-humility`.
- If the client belongs to a group with low diagnosis rates → consider that under-recognition may be at work. Because US parent-reported diagnosis rates differ widely by race and ethnicity, for example 11% of Black and White children versus 3% of Asian children in 2022 to 2023 [CDC-ADHD-Data].

**Medication**
- If the client takes medication → schedule sessions and homework when it is working and ask about adherence each session. Because medication adherence review is part of each session in the main protocol [Knouse-Safren-2010].

**Stepping up or referring**
- If there is no functional change by mid-course → review skill use, comorbidity, sleep and medication with the client and prescriber (expert consensus); see `04-treatment-planning.managing-non-response`. Because comorbid conditions should be screened for and treated [Wolraich-etal-2019].
- If severe mood, psychotic or bipolar symptoms appear → refer for psychiatric assessment. Because a clinician not trained to diagnose or treat a comorbid condition should refer to a specialist [Wolraich-etal-2019].

## Example phrasings

- "Let's put the next appointment in your phone now, with a reminder the day before."
- "Of everything today, what's the one thing you'll try this week? Let's write when and where."
- "Before you go, what might get in the way, and what's your backup?"
- Avoid: "You just need to try harder." It repeats the criticism clients have heard for years.

## Evidence

Adaptations to session format are drawn from protocols tested in randomized trials [Safren-etal-2010] [Solanto-etal-2010] and expert sources [Ramsay-Rostain-2015]; few have been tested on their own. Comorbidity and risk guidance rests on epidemiology [Faraone-etal-2021] and pediatric guideline recommendations [Wolraich-etal-2019].
