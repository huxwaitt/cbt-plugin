**Bottom line:** Medication is the most effective treatment for core attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) symptoms: stimulants have the largest effects and non-stimulants moderate ones [Cortese-etal-2018] [Faraone-etal-2021]. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) adds what pills do not: organizing habits, coping with avoidance, and help with residual impairment and comorbid mood problems [Knouse-Safren-2010]. Most adult CBT trials were run in people already on medication [Safren-etal-2010]. Therapists inform and support; prescribing decisions belong to licensed prescribers.

## The principle

**Medicines, in brief** (details in `12-medical-pharmacology.adhd-medications`):
- **Stimulants:** methylphenidate and amphetamines. In a network meta-analysis of 133 trials, amphetamines (clinician-rated standardized mean difference, SMD, -1.02) and methylphenidate (-0.78) had the largest effects in children; in adults, amphetamines -0.79 and methylphenidate -0.49 [Cortese-etal-2018]. The authors favor methylphenidate first for children and adolescents and amphetamines for adults [Cortese-etal-2018]. All stimulants carry a United States (US) Food and Drug Administration (FDA) boxed warning on misuse, abuse, addiction and overdose [FDA-Stimulant-Warning-2023].
- **Atomoxetine:** a non-stimulant with moderate effects at all ages [Faraone-etal-2021]; boxed warning for suicidal thoughts in children and adolescents [FDA-Atomoxetine-Label].
- **Viloxazine:** a non-stimulant thought to act by blocking norepinephrine reuptake, approved from age 6 and in adults; boxed warning for suicidal thoughts and behaviors [FDA-Qelbree-Label].
- **Extended-release guanfacine:** an alpha-2A agonist, not a stimulant. It is approved alone or added to stimulants, studied in ages 6 to 17; stopping abruptly can cause rebound high blood pressure [FDA-Guanfacine-Label].

**Where CBT fits:**
- **Children:** in the Multimodal Treatment Study of ADHD (MTA), managed medication beat intensive behavioral treatment for core symptoms, and combined treatment added gains in oppositional behavior, social skills, parent-child relations and reading [MTA-Group-1999]. Behavioral gains tend to persist after treatment, while medication effects stop when the medicine stops [Wolraich-etal-2019].
- **Adults:** CBT plus medication beat medication alone (very low quality evidence) [Lopez-etal-2018]. In a large trial, structured group psychotherapy was no better than individual clinical management, while methylphenidate beat placebo [Philipsen-etal-2015]. Only stimulants and atomoxetine improved both self-rated and clinician-rated symptoms; psychological treatments improved clinician ratings only [Ostinelli-etal-2025].
- **Sequence:** the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) offers adults medication when impairment persists after environmental changes. It advises considering non-drug treatment if medication is declined, hard to adhere to, ineffective or not tolerated, and alongside medication when impairment remains [NICE-NG87]. For school-age children the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends medication together with behavioral treatment [Wolraich-etal-2019].

## Guidance

- If a client asks "Should I take medication?" → give balanced information, explore their goals and concerns, and refer to a prescriber for the decision. Because diagnosis and prescribing are restricted by license (see `10-diagnosis.scope-of-practice-diagnosis`).
- If the client is on medication → ask each session about adherence, timing and side effects, and plan demanding homework for when it is working. Because medication review is part of each session in the main protocol [Knouse-Safren-2010].
- If the client says "The pills fixed my focus, but I still miss deadlines" → explain that medication improves attention but does not build planning habits, and start organizing and planning skills. Because responders to medication often remain impaired [Knouse-Safren-2010].
- If the client stops or changes medication mid-course → record it, inform the prescriber with consent, and reinterpret progress. Because trial benefits were shown in clients with stable medication [Safren-etal-2010].
- If the client shares, sells or is pressured to give away stimulants, or takes more than prescribed → discuss safety and storage, and with consent alert the prescriber. Because the FDA advises against sharing and asks prescribers to monitor for misuse and diversion [FDA-Stimulant-Warning-2023].
- If a client fears stimulants cause addiction → share that prescribed stimulants were not linked to more substance misuse and were linked to over 30% fewer substance problems in registry data, while misuse and sharing remain real risks to discuss with the prescriber. Because accurate information supports informed choice [Faraone-etal-2021] [FDA-Stimulant-Warning-2023].
- If a child or adolescent on atomoxetine or viloxazine shows new low mood, agitation or suicidal talk → assess risk the same day and contact the prescriber and parents. Because both carry boxed suicidality warnings [FDA-Atomoxetine-Label] [FDA-Qelbree-Label]. US: 988 (call or text), or 911 for immediate danger; United Kingdom (UK): Samaritans 116 123, National Health Service (NHS) 111, or 999 [Lifeline-988] [Samaritans-Site].
- If a stimulant prescription cannot be filled → plan coping skills for the gap and contact the prescriber. Because 71.5% of US adults on stimulants reported shortage-related difficulty filling them in 2023 [Staley-etal-2024].

## Example phrasings

- "Medication can turn the volume down on distraction. What we do here is build the systems that get things done."
- "That's a question for your prescriber. Shall we write down what you'd like to ask?"
- Avoid: "You don't really need pills if you do the therapy." It overrides medical advice and the evidence.

## Evidence

Medication efficacy rests on large meta-analyses of randomized trials [Cortese-etal-2018] [Ostinelli-etal-2025]. Combined treatment benefits are supported by randomized trials in children [MTA-Group-1999] and smaller trials in adults [Lopez-etal-2018]. Long-term comparative evidence is limited [Ostinelli-etal-2025].
