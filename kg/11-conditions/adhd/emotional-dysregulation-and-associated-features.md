**Bottom line:** Emotional dysregulation (fast, intense, short-lived frustration, anger and hurt) is common in attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and drives much of its impairment, but it is an associated feature, not a diagnostic criterion [Kooij-etal-2019] [Beheshti-etal-2020]. "Rejection sensitive dysphoria" is a popular label, not a diagnosis [VanAsselt-2026]. Delayed sleep timing is estimated to affect most people with ADHD [Bijlenga-etal-2019], and ADHD persists into later life [Dobrosavljevic-etal-2020]. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) can add emotion skills borrowed from dialectical behavior therapy (DBT), sleep work and age adaptations; evidence for these additions is emerging.

## What it is

- **Emotional dysregulation:** low frustration tolerance, irritability, temper outbursts, emotional impulsivity and mood lability. Feelings rise sharply in response to daily events and return to baseline within a few hours, unlike sustained depressive or manic episodes [Kooij-etal-2019]. In a meta-analysis of 13 studies (2,535 participants), adults with ADHD showed far more emotional dysregulation than controls (Hedges' g = 1.17), and it correlated with ADHD severity (r = 0.54) [Beheshti-etal-2020].
- **Rejection sensitivity:** intense distress at real or perceived criticism or rejection. In written accounts from 162 adults with ADHD or high ADHD traits, many described frequent criticism and reactions more intense than expected [Beaton-etal-2022]. The term "rejection sensitive dysphoria" (RSD) came from one psychiatrist's clinical experience and spread through social media; empirical evidence is limited [VanAsselt-2026]. It is not a diagnosis in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) or the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) [APA-DSM5TR-2022] [WHO-ICD11-2022].
- **Sleep and circadian problems:** a delayed sleep phase is estimated in 73% to 78% of children and adults with ADHD [Bijlenga-etal-2019]. In a meta-analysis, adults with ADHD reported worse sleep on 7 of 9 self-report measures and, on actigraphy, took longer to fall asleep and slept less efficiently; polysomnography showed no differences [DiazRoman-etal-2018].
- **ADHD in older adults:** pooled prevalence is 2.18% by validated rating scales but 0.23% by clinical diagnosis [Dobrosavljevic-etal-2020]. It comes with more mood and anxiety symptoms, health problems, divorce and loneliness [Kooij-etal-2019].

## Why it matters in CBT

Outbursts and hurt at feedback strain relationships, jobs and the therapeutic alliance. They overlap with borderline personality disorder (BPD), depression and bipolar disorder, so misreading them changes both diagnosis and plan [Kooij-etal-2019]. Late sleep leaves clients tired, inattentive and late for morning appointments. In older adults, memory complaints raise the question of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or dementia [Torgersen-etal-2016].

## Key details

- **DBT-informed skills (emerging evidence).** In a randomized trial of 51 adults, a DBT-based skills group reduced self-rated ADHD symptoms in completers on stable medication, while a discussion group did not; neither reduced comorbidity [Hirvikoski-etal-2011]. In a pilot trial of 33 college students, response rates were 59% to 65% with DBT group skills versus 19% to 25% with handouts [Fleming-etal-2015]. In the largest trial (433 adults), a DBT-based group did not beat individual clinical management on the blinded primary outcome [Philipsen-etal-2015] or on emotional symptoms [LopezPinar-etal-2023].
- **Telling ADHD from BPD.** In ADHD, anger is usually brief and thoughtless, and self-harm, identity disturbance and abandonment fears are less intense; both are trait-like, so the line can blur [Kooij-etal-2019].
- **Sleep management.** Start with sleep hygiene education and optimizing ADHD medication. Melatonin has trial support in children, and evening melatonin or morning light rests on clinical experience in adults. Stimulants can lengthen time to fall asleep [Kooij-etal-2019]. In the United Kingdom (UK), the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) asks prescribers to monitor sleep, for example with a sleep diary [NICE-NG87].
- **Older adults.** In a secondary analysis of a group CBT trial, 26 adults aged 50 or over improved on attention as much as 55 younger adults, though they also did well with a support group [Solanto-etal-2018]. Polypharmacy, sleep problems, pain and impaired vision or hearing complicate assessment [Torgersen-etal-2016].

## In the session

- If the client flares in session or reports blow-ups → map one episode as a chain (trigger, urge, action, aftermath) and practice a pause: name the feeling, delay the reply. Because ADHD emotions rise fast and settle within hours, so the first minutes are the target [Kooij-etal-2019].
- If the client says "I have RSD" → validate the hurt, keep their language, and formulate rejection sensitivity as a pattern (triggers, interpretations, withdrawal or anger). Because treating it as innate can hide changeable social and cognitive factors [VanAsselt-2026].
- If feedback in session leads to shutdown → ask before giving feedback, pair it with what went well, and check meaning: "What did you hear me say?" Because adults with ADHD report strong reactions to criticism [Beaton-etal-2022].
- If chronic self-harm, intense abandonment fears or unstable identity appear → assess for BPD and consider DBT (`personality-disorders.dbt-informed-skills`). Because these features are more intense in BPD [Kooij-etal-2019].
- If the client sleeps late and wakes exhausted → start a sleep diary, anchor the wake time, and ask the prescriber about dose timing before adapting CBT for insomnia (CBT-I). Because delayed sleep phase is common and stimulants can delay sleep [Bijlenga-etal-2019] [Kooij-etal-2019].
- If an older adult reports new attention or memory problems → take a lifelong history and arrange medical and cognitive assessment. Because MCI, dementia, medicines and sensory loss can mimic ADHD [Torgersen-etal-2016].
- If anger or distress includes suicidal thoughts or self-harm → assess risk the same day with `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`. United States (US): call or text 988, or 911 [Lifeline-988]; UK: Samaritans 116 123 [Samaritans-Site], National Health Service (NHS) 111 or 999 [NHS-Urgent-Help].
