**Bottom line:** A positive attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) screener or a convincing self-description shows that assessment is worth doing, not that ADHD is present. The diagnosis needs childhood onset, symptoms in more than one setting, impairment, and other causes ruled out [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. Skipping these steps risks missing depression, anxiety, sleep or substance problems and building treatment on the wrong formulation.

## What it looks like

- The intake note reads "ADHD, positive on the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS)" with no history of childhood symptoms.
- A client who learned about ADHD online is treated as diagnosed, and therapy starts on planners and timers.
- Concentration problems that began with a depressive episode, a new baby or night shifts are labeled ADHD.
- No parent, partner, school report or other informant has been consulted.

## Why it happens

- Screeners are quick and feel objective. But in a specialty clinic the 6-item ASRS screener for the fifth edition of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5) had specificity of only 74.0%, against 96.0% in the general population [Ustun-etal-2017].
- Many adults now seek assessment for the first time: in a 2023 United States (US) survey, 55.9% of adults with a current ADHD diagnosis were diagnosed in adulthood [Staley-etal-2024]. Adults often recall childhood onset poorly [Epstein-Loren-2013], and informants take effort to reach.
- Inattention is non-specific. Mood episodes, anxiety, poor sleep and substance use all impair concentration [Ramsay-2017] [Wolraich-etal-2019].
- Clinicians want to validate a client who feels finally understood.

## The fix

1. Treat a positive screen as a referral question. Say: "This suggests a full assessment is worthwhile. It isn't a diagnosis yet."
2. Establish onset before age 12 and symptoms in two or more settings [APA-DSM5TR-2022], using a parent or sibling and old school reports where possible [Ramsay-2017].
3. Ask timing questions: "Was this there before the low mood, the worry, the drinking or the new job?"
4. Screen for comorbid and alternative conditions: anxiety, depression, substance use, learning disorders and sleep apnea [Wolraich-etal-2019].
5. If medication is the main goal, or there is current substance misuse, share the full history with the prescriber, because the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) asks prescribers to assess misuse risk before prescribing stimulants [FDA-Stimulant-Warning-2023].
6. Do not overcorrect: masking and missing records are common in women and late-diagnosed adults, so weigh the whole history (see `adhd.underdiagnosis-in-women-and-adults`).
7. Record what is confirmed, what is provisional, and what evidence is missing. A working formulation of attention and organization problems can guide cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) while assessment continues.

## Sources note

Screening accuracy comes from the scale validation studies [Kessler-etal-2005a] [Ustun-etal-2017]; the criteria are paraphrased from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. The value of informants and records is expert guidance [Ramsay-2017] and a pediatric guideline requirement [Wolraich-etal-2019].
