**Bottom line:** Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) works with comorbid psychiatric and medical conditions (remission 36.0% versus 16.9% for controls) [Wu-etal-2015], so treat insomnia alongside the other problem. Adapt or delay when sleep loss is dangerous: bipolar disorder, uncontrolled seizures, excessive sleepiness, active substance use disorder, medical instability, acute mental health crises, or during exposure-based trauma therapy [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].

## The principle

Keep the active ingredients (fixed rise time, sleep window, stimulus control, cognitive work) and change the dose, order or safety margins. Delay only for reasons the US Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense (VA/DoD) lists [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].

## Guidance

**Comorbidity**
- If the client has depression → treat insomnia concurrently with depression care. Because insomnia predicts later depression (odds ratio 2.60) [Baglioni-etal-2011], and adding CBT-I to an antidepressant gave 61.5% versus 33.3% depression remission in a 30-person pilot, a promising but nonsignificant difference [Manber-etal-2008].
- If depression, pain or older age leaves the extra hours out of bed empty → plan valued activities for them. Because the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) notes these clients struggle to fill that time [Edinger-etal-2021a].
- If the client reports suicidal thoughts → complete a suicide risk assessment (`02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`) before sleep restriction, and give crisis contacts (US: call or text 988, or 911; UK: 999, NHS 111, Samaritans 116 123). Because sleep disturbance is a risk factor for suicidal thoughts and behavior [Pigeon-etal-2012], and the VA/DoD notes CBT-I may need delaying with current suicidal ideation [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- If the client has bipolar disorder → never set the window below 6.5 hours, monitor mood weekly, regularize sleep and daily routines, add a dim-light wind-down and morning light, and if mania symptoms appear, stop restriction and contact the prescriber. Because sleep loss can trigger mania, and this adapted protocol cut hypomania or mania relapse to 4.6% versus 31.6% in a pilot trial [Harvey-etal-2015].
- If the client is in exposure-based trauma therapy → delay CBT-I until that course ends unless the team agrees otherwise. Because the VA/DoD lists this as a reason to delay [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- If the client has persistent psychotic symptoms but is not acutely unwell → offer CBT-I with the care team's agreement; delay during acute episodes. Because a 50-person pilot trial in schizophrenia-spectrum disorders found large insomnia gains (d = 1.9) [Freeman-etal-2015], and acute mental health symptoms are a reason to delay [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- If there is an active alcohol or drug use disorder, an unstable medical condition or uncontrolled seizures → delay CBT-I and stabilize first. Because the VA/DoD lists each as a reason to delay [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- If sleep apnea is suspected or diagnosed → refer for testing and run CBT-I alongside once sleepiness is managed. Because testing is indicated for suspected breathing disorders [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008], and excessive sleepiness calls for adapting or delaying [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].

**Severity and sleepiness**
- If the client is very sleepy at baseline, or perceived and measured sleep differ widely → use a more generous window. Because restriction impaired vigilance early on, and the authors advise a liberal window for these groups [Kyle-etal-2014].
- If the client drives for work or operates machinery → use a gentler schedule such as sleep compression and agree a no-driving-when-sleepy plan. Because sleep restriction may be contraindicated in high-risk occupations [Edinger-etal-2021a], and compression is gentler though slower [Jernelov-etal-2025].

**Age and life stage**
- If the client is an older adult → consider a brief behavioral program and adapt stimulus control for falls. Because brief behavioral treatment gave 67% versus 25% response [Buysse-etal-2011], and the AASM advises adapting stimulus control for fall risk, mobility problems or sedative use [Edinger-etal-2021a].
- If the client is an adolescent → use CBT-I and involve parents in schedule changes. Because a meta-analysis of 8 trials found a large effect on insomnia (standardized mean difference -1.06) [Mei-etal-2024].
- If the client is pregnant or postpartum → adapt and coordinate with obstetric care. Because the VA/DoD flags this group [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025], and a major sleep restriction trial excluded pregnancy [Kyle-etal-2020].
- If the client works nights or rotating shifts → do not apply a standard window; assess circadian problems first. Because shift workers were excluded from that trial [Kyle-etal-2020].

**Culture and context**
- If the program was not designed for the client's group → ask how sleep is organized at home (shared rooms, co-sleeping, caregiving, prayer times, noise) and tailor examples and schedule. Because a culturally tailored internet CBT-I for Black women matched standard outcomes and raised completion from 64.8% to 78.2% [Zhou-etal-2022].

**Medication**
- If the client takes a hypnotic → keep it unchanged at first; the prescriber leads any taper. Because CBT-I plus supervised taper left 85% drug-free versus 48% with taper alone [Morin-etal-2004].
- If the client wants medication added → explain that CBT-I plus medication is suggested over medication alone, not over CBT-I alone. Because that is the 2026 AASM position (conditional, low certainty) [Buysse-etal-2026].

**Step up or refer**
- If a brief course has not worked → step up to full CBT-I. Because the VA/DoD advises stepping up nonresponders [VA-DoD-BehaviorChange-2025].
- If CBT-I fails or the diagnosis is uncertain → refer to sleep medicine. Because treatment failure is an indication for polysomnography [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].

## Example phrasings

- Bipolar disorder: "We'll keep at least six and a half hours in bed, and if your mood ratings rise we'll pause the schedule changes."
- Hypnotics: "You don't need to stop your tablet to start. Once sleep is steadier, your doctor can plan a slow taper."

## Evidence

Randomized trials support CBT-I for comorbid insomnia [Wu-etal-2015], older adults [Buysse-etal-2011] and adolescents [Mei-etal-2024]; bipolar and psychosis evidence comes from pilot trials [Harvey-etal-2015] [Freeman-etal-2015]. Delay criteria are guideline consensus [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
