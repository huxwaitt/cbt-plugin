**Bottom line:** Sleep restriction and stimulus control are the behavioral core of cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I); cognitive therapy, relaxation and education are added around them [Edinger-etal-2021a]. Set time in bed close to average sleep time, never below 5 hours, and adjust weekly by sleep efficiency [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008] [Kyle-etal-2014]. Sleep hygiene advice can sit inside CBT-I but should not be offered as the treatment on its own [Edinger-etal-2021a].

## When to use

After assessment and at least 1 week of baseline diary, once screening has ruled out reasons to delay or adapt (sleep apnea untreated, mania risk, uncontrolled seizures, excessive sleepiness, high-risk driving) [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].

## How it works

Restricting time in bed raises sleep pressure and consolidates sleep; stimulus control re-pairs bed with sleep; cognitive therapy reduces worry, monitoring and unhelpful beliefs [Edinger-etal-2021a] [Harvey-etal-2007]. See `insomnia.cbt-model`.

## Steps

**1. Sleep restriction (the sleep window)**

1. From 1 to 2 weeks of diary, calculate mean total sleep time (TST) [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
2. Set time in bed (TIB) equal to that average, not below 5 hours [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008]. Fix the rise time first, then count back to the earliest bedtime.
3. Each week, compute mean sleep efficiency (SE = TST divided by TIB, times 100) and adjust. A common rule: 90% or more, add 15 minutes; 85% to 89%, no change; under 85%, remove 15 minutes [Kyle-etal-2014]. The 2008 American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) guideline gives 15 to 20 minute steps, increasing above 85% to 90% and decreasing below 80% [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
4. Continue until sleep is consolidated and daytime function is satisfactory.

Protocols differ: the brief behavioral protocol used average sleep plus 30 minutes, minimum 6 hours [Buysse-etal-2011]; the bipolar adaptation never went below 6.5 hours [Harvey-etal-2015]. Only 7% of trials reported all their parameters and 39% reported none [Kyle-etal-2015]. A gentler option, sleep compression, shortens time in bed gradually over several weeks; in a 2025 trial it had better adherence and somewhat fewer side effects but slower, smaller gains than sleep restriction, so keep it for when restriction cannot be used [Jernelov-etal-2025].

**2. Stimulus control** [Edinger-etal-2021a] [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008]

1. Go to bed only when sleepy.
2. If unable to fall asleep, or back to sleep, within about 20 minutes, get up and go somewhere calm and dim; return only when sleepy. Repeat as needed.
3. Use the bed only for sleep and sex.
4. Get up at the same time every day.
5. Avoid daytime naps.

Judge the 20 minutes by feel, not by the clock [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008], because clock watching raises arousal [Harvey-2002]. The 2021 AASM guideline gives no fixed time limit [Edinger-etal-2021a].

**3. Cognitive therapy** [Harvey-etal-2007]

- Target worry and rumination, attention to sleep-threat cues, unhelpful beliefs, misperception of sleep and daytime deficits, and daytime safety behaviors.
- Use Socratic questioning on beliefs such as "I must get 8 hours or I can't function."
- Run behavioral experiments: keep a planned activity after a poor night and rate how the day actually went against the prediction.
- Reduce monitoring: turn the clock away and drop checking of body sensations.
- Reduce sleep effort. Paradoxical intention (staying passively awake without trying to sleep) was rated guideline level in 2008 [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008], but the 2021 AASM guideline made no recommendation because too few trials qualified [Edinger-etal-2021a].

**4. Arousal reduction:** a calming wind-down routine and relaxation such as diaphragmatic breathing or a body scan [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025]; see `behavioral.relaxation-and-arousal-reduction`.

**5. Sleep hygiene (optional, within CBT-I):** avoid stimulants for several hours before bed and alcohol near bedtime, exercise regularly, allow about an hour to unwind, keep the bedroom quiet, dark and comfortable, keep a regular schedule [VA-DoD-BehaviorChange-2025].

## Guidance

- If the client is sleepy in the first 2 weeks → reassure that this is expected and usually temporary, and repeat the no-driving-when-sleepy agreement. Because objective sleep fell by up to 91 minutes, sleepiness rose for 3 weeks and attention lapses increased during treatment, all returning to baseline by 3 months [Kyle-etal-2014].
- If the client has fall risk, mobility problems or takes sedatives → adapt stimulus control, for example sitting up in bed in dim light rather than walking about. Because the AASM advises adapting stimulus control for these groups [Edinger-etal-2021a].
- If the client says "I can't just lie there doing nothing" when they get up → plan a boring, low-light activity in advance. Because the client returns to bed only when sleepy again, so the time up should be calm rather than stimulating [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
- If efficiency is high but the client still feels unrefreshed → extend the window in steps and work on beliefs about needed sleep. Because the goal is satisfactory sleep and function, not a fixed number of hours [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].

Example phrasing: "Your diary shows about 5 hours 45 minutes of sleep across 8 hours in bed. Let's set a window close to the sleep you're already getting, then grow it."

Avoid: "Just relax and it'll come." It adds effort and pressure.

## Common problems

- Shifting the rise time later at weekends, which undoes the window.
- Letting the window drop below 5 hours.
- Delivering stimulus control without sleep restriction for clients whose main problem is long time awake in bed.

## Evidence

Single components carry conditional AASM recommendations: stimulus control, sleep restriction and relaxation for; sleep hygiene alone against [Edinger-etal-2021a]. Sleep restriction alone had large effects on insomnia severity (g = -0.93) in 8 trials [Maurer-etal-2021]; nurse-delivered sleep restriction beat a sleep hygiene booklet at 6 months (Cohen's d -0.74) [Kyle-etal-2023]. Behavior therapy works faster and cognitive therapy more durably [Harvey-etal-2014].
