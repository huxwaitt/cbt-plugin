**Bottom line:** Offer cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) as the first treatment for chronic insomnia in adults, including older adults and people with psychiatric or medical comorbidity [Qaseem-etal-2016] [Riemann-etal-2023]. It typically runs 4 to 8 weekly sessions guided by a sleep diary, built around sleep restriction and stimulus control, with cognitive therapy and education [Edinger-etal-2021a]. Medication is a second step when CBT-I alone has not worked [Qaseem-etal-2016].

## When to use

For insomnia lasting 3 months or more, with or without other conditions. Screen first for sleep apnea, bipolar disorder, seizures, high-risk driving or work, and other reasons to adapt or delay (see `insomnia.adaptations`) [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].

## How it works

CBT-I reverses the maintaining processes in `insomnia.cbt-model`: sleep restriction builds sleep drive, stimulus control re-pairs bed with sleep, cognitive therapy reduces worry, monitoring and unhelpful beliefs, and education corrects expectations [Edinger-etal-2021a]. Behavior therapy acts faster, cognitive therapy slower but durably, and the full combination does best [Harvey-etal-2014].

## Steps

A composite outline from manualized trial protocols [Manber-etal-2008] [Harvey-etal-2015] [Edinger-etal-2021a]:

| Session | Content |
|---|---|
| Assessment | History, screens, Insomnia Severity Index (ISI); start a 1 to 2 week diary (`insomnia.sleep-diary`) |
| 1 | Shared formulation; education on sleep drive, body clock and normal sleep; goals; continue diary |
| 2 | Calculate sleep efficiency; set the sleep window (sleep restriction) and fixed rise time; stimulus control rules; sleepiness and driving safety |
| 3 | Review diary; adjust window; troubleshoot adherence; arousal management and wind-down |
| 4 to 6 | Weekly window adjustments; cognitive therapy for worry, monitoring, beliefs and daytime safety behaviors, using behavioral experiments; relevant sleep hygiene points |
| Final | Relapse prevention plan: what helped, warning signs, what to do after a bad run of nights |

Trials used 7 sessions [Manber-etal-2008] or 8 weekly sessions [Harvey-etal-2014]; the US Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense (VA/DoD) describe 4 to 10 weekly hour-long sessions [VA-DoD-BehaviorChange-2025].

## Guidance

- If the client is anxious about sleeping less → frame sleep restriction as building sleep pressure, a temporary window that grows as sleep consolidates. Because time in bed is extended across treatment as efficiency rises [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- If the client drives or does safety-critical work → agree in writing not to drive when sleepy during the first weeks, and consider a gentler window. Because sleep restriction slowed reactions and increased attention lapses during the first 3 weeks [Kyle-etal-2014] and may be contraindicated in high-risk occupations [Edinger-etal-2021a].
- If the client asks for sleep hygiene tips only → explain that hygiene advice alone is not an effective treatment for chronic insomnia. Because both the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) and VA/DoD advise against it as a stand-alone therapy [Edinger-etal-2021a] [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- If the client takes a hypnotic → start CBT-I without stopping it, never advise stopping a benzodiazepine or Z-drug abruptly, and leave any taper to the prescriber. Because combining CBT-I with medication is preferred over medication alone [Buysse-etal-2026], tapering after combined treatment gave the best long-term remission [Morin-etal-2009b], and supervised tapers work best with CBT-I [Morin-etal-2004].
- If ISI has not fallen by session 4 → check diary adherence to the window and rise time before adding techniques. Because the window works only if kept, so trial protocols check diaries against prescribed bed and rise times [Kyle-etal-2020].

Example phrasing: "We're going to make your nights shorter for a little while, so the sleep you get is deeper and more solid, and then we'll stretch it back out."

## Common problems

- Giving rules without a formulation or diary data, so they feel arbitrary and adherence drops.
- Stopping after the behavioral sessions, losing the durability cognitive therapy adds [Harvey-etal-2014].
- No written relapse plan; insomnia often recurs.

## Evidence

Guideline-recommended and supported by randomized trials.

- **Effect sizes:** against inactive controls, CBT-I shortened sleep onset by 19 minutes, cut wake after sleep onset by 26 minutes and raised sleep efficiency by 9.9 percentage points; total sleep time changed little (7.6 minutes, not significant) [Trauer-etal-2015]. In the AASM review, remission rates were 33 percentage points higher and response rates 45 points higher than control, with an ISI effect size of 0.95 [Edinger-etal-2021b].
- **Comorbid insomnia:** in 37 trials, remission was 36.0% with CBT-I versus 16.9% with control [Wu-etal-2015].
- **Medication comparison:** CBT-I alone and CBT-I plus zolpidem had similar 6-week response (60% and 61%); combined treatment followed by CBT-I alone gave 68% remission at 6 months [Morin-etal-2009b].

**Guideline status**

- **AASM:** strong recommendation for CBT-I; conditional for brief therapies, stimulus control, sleep restriction and relaxation alone; conditional against sleep hygiene alone [Edinger-etal-2021a]. Its 2026 guideline suggests CBT-I plus medication over medication alone, but not over CBT-I alone [Buysse-etal-2026].
- **American College of Physicians (ACP):** CBT-I as initial treatment for all adults (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence) [Qaseem-etal-2016].
- **VA/DoD (Version 3.0, 2025):** CBT-I recommended (strong for); brief behavioral therapy suggested (weak for); sleep hygiene alone suggested against; CBT-I suggested over medication as first-line (weak for) [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- **European Insomnia Guideline 2023:** CBT-I first-line for adults of any age, in person or digital (grade A) [Riemann-etal-2023].
- **UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE):** no full insomnia guideline. Its 2023 appraisal of daridorexant calls CBT-I the standard first treatment for long-term insomnia and reserves the drug for when CBT-I has not worked or is unavailable or unsuitable [NICE-TA922]. Its 2022 guidance HTG624 (formerly MTG70) recommends the digital CBT-I program Sleepio as a cost-saving option in primary care for people who would otherwise get sleep hygiene advice or sleeping pills [NICE-HTG624] [Atenstaedt-2023].
- **American Psychiatric Association (APA):** no insomnia guideline of its own; it lists the AASM insomnia guidelines under other practice guidelines [APA-Psychiatry-Guidelines-Site].
