**Bottom line:** Insomnia is diagnosed from a clinical sleep history plus at least 2 weeks of sleep diary; overnight testing is not routine [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008]. Measure severity with the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) and screen every client for sleep apnea, restless legs, circadian timing problems, mood disorder, substances and suicide risk, because each changes the plan [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025] [Pigeon-etal-2012].

## When to use

At intake when sleep is a complaint, when a client with another condition reports poor sleep, and before cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I). Reassess every few weeks until sleep is stable, then about every 6 months [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].

## Steps

1. **Screen.** The US Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense (VA/DoD) guideline (Version 3.0, 2025) suggests a validated insomnia screen (ISI or Athens Insomnia Scale) plus an obstructive sleep apnea screen [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
2. **Take the sleep history.** Cover onset, course, frequency, severity, perpetuating factors, past treatments, the hours before bed, the schedule on work and free days, night-time symptoms and daytime function [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008]. Typical complaint thresholds are sleep onset over 30 minutes, wake after sleep onset over 30 minutes, sleep efficiency under 85% or total sleep under 6.5 hours [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
3. **Confirm adequate opportunity.** If the client simply has too little time in bed, this is insufficient sleep, not insomnia [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
4. **Rule out other sleep disorders.** Ask about snoring, witnessed pauses in breathing, restless legs, sleepwalking and nightmares [Grima-etal-2019].
5. **Screen for sleep apnea.** The STOP-Bang questionnaire has 8 yes or no items scored 0 to 8: 0 to 2 is low risk, 5 to 8 high risk, and 3 or more has 93% sensitivity for moderate to severe apnea [Chung-etal-2016].
6. **Medical, psychiatric, medication and substance history.** Include caffeine, alcohol, cannabis, nicotine, prescribed and over-the-counter sleep aids [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
7. **Risk.** Ask directly about suicidal thoughts [Pigeon-etal-2012], past hypomania or mania, seizures, driving, safety-critical work and falls.
8. **Measures.** Start a sleep diary (`insomnia.sleep-diary`) and give the ISI, plus the Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) if daytime sleepiness is reported [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
9. **Refer for sleep testing** when sleep apnea or a movement disorder is suspected, the diagnosis is uncertain, treatment fails, or arousals are violent [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008]. Actigraphy is not routine but can help with differential diagnosis [Riemann-etal-2023].

**Measures and cutoffs**

| Measure | Format | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| ISI | 7 items, 0 to 4 each, total 0 to 28, past month | 0 to 7 none; 8 to 14 subthreshold; 15 to 21 moderate; 22 to 28 severe. Case cutoff 10 in community samples (sensitivity 86.1%, specificity 87.7%), 11 in clinical samples [Morin-etal-2011] |
| ISI change | Repeat measure | A drop of more than 7 points marks moderate improvement [Morin-etal-2011]. An American Academy of Sleep Medicine review counted remission as ISI under 8 and response as a drop of 8 or more [Edinger-etal-2021b] |
| Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) | 19 items, 7 components, global 0 to 21, past month | Global score over 5 separates poor from good sleepers (sensitivity 89.6%, specificity 86.5%) [Buysse-etal-1989] |
| ESS | 8 items, 0 to 24 | 11 to 12 mild, 13 to 15 moderate, 16 to 24 severe excessive sleepiness [ESS-Site] |
| Dysfunctional Beliefs and Attitudes about Sleep (DBAS-16) | 16 items rated 0 to 10, mean score | Four factors: consequences, worry and helplessness, sleep expectations, medication. No validated cutoff in the source paper [Morin-etal-2007] |

The ISI, PSQI and ESS are copyrighted; the ESS needs a license whether or not fees apply [ESS-Site]. Check current terms (`08-measurement.instrument-licensing`) before building any of them into a tool, and do not reproduce items.

**Scope of practice.** Therapists can assess insomnia disorder within their license, but sleep apnea and other sleep disorders are diagnosed by a physician or sleep specialist, and only a prescriber changes sleep medication. Screen and refer.

## What to record

- Sleep pattern, frequency, duration, course, triggers and daytime impact in the client's words.
- Perpetuating behaviors: early bedtimes, lying in, napping, screens or work in bed, alcohol as a sleep aid.
- Screen results for apnea, restless legs, circadian delay, nightmares and parasomnias.
- Comorbidity, suicide risk, bipolar history, substances and medicines.
- Dated ISI and ESS scores; diary averages once available.
- Driving or safety-critical work, fall risk, pregnancy, shift work.

## Guidance

- If the client snores loudly, has witnessed breathing pauses or scores high on STOP-Bang → refer for sleep apnea testing and treat insomnia alongside, not instead. Because the two often coexist and the VA/DoD suggests screening for both [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- If the ESS is 11 or higher → explore sleepiness before any sleep restriction and consider medical referral. Because people with insomnia often score in the normal range (mean 4.95 at baseline in one sample) [Kyle-etal-2014], and excessive daytime sleepiness is a reason to adapt or delay CBT-I [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- If the client says "I didn't sleep at all" → record their estimate without arguing and gather diary data. Because misperception of sleep is common in insomnia and becomes a later treatment target [Harvey-Tang-2012].
- If the client sleeps well only from about 3 a.m. to 11 a.m. → assess delayed sleep-wake phase before diagnosing insomnia. Because circadian disorders are a key differential [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
- If a bed partner is available → ask, with consent, about snoring, breathing pauses and leg movements. Because the client cannot observe these [Grima-etal-2019].

Example phrasing: "Walk me through last night, from an hour before bed until you got up."

## Common problems

- Diagnosing insomnia without asking about snoring or leg discomfort.
- Relying on a retrospective estimate instead of a prospective diary [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
- Skipping the suicide question because sleep seems minor.
