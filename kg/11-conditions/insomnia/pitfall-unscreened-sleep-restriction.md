**Bottom line:** Sleep restriction, a core part of cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), works by making people mildly sleep-deprived at first, so it carries real risks: daytime sleepiness and slowed reactions in the first weeks [Kyle-etal-2014], and possible harm for drivers, people prone to mania and people with poorly controlled seizures [Edinger-etal-2021a]. Screen before you prescribe a window, adapt or delay when a risk is present, and agree a no-driving-when-sleepy plan.

## What it looks like

- A window of 5 hours is set at the first session for a long-distance driver who was not asked about work.
- A client with loud snoring and witnessed breathing pauses is restricted further, and daytime sleepiness worsens.
- A client with bipolar disorder sleeps less on the program and becomes elevated and irritable.
- An app sets the window automatically; no clinician checks sleepiness or mood.
- The client stops after one exhausting week because nobody warned them it would be hard.

## Why it happens

- The protocol looks simple, and the risks are not on the handout.
- Assessment focused on the insomnia complaint, not on other sleep disorders or mood history.
- Protocols vary widely, and 39% of CBT-I trials did not report their sleep restriction parameters, so clinicians improvise [Kyle-etal-2015].
- Clinicians underestimate the early effects. In one study, objectively measured sleep fell by 91 minutes on the first night, sleepiness scores rose for the first 3 weeks, and attention lapses increased from the second week to the end of the 4-week course [Kyle-etal-2014].

## The fix

Before setting any window, check each of these and record the answer:

| Check | Action if present |
|---|---|
| Snoring, breathing pauses, high STOP-Bang (sleep apnea screen) score | Refer for sleep testing; manage sleepiness before restriction [Chung-etal-2016] [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008] |
| Excessive daytime sleepiness | Adapt or delay [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025] |
| Driving or safety-critical work | Gentler window; written no-driving-when-sleepy plan [Edinger-etal-2021a] |
| Bipolar disorder or past mania | Window never below 6.5 hours; weekly mood monitoring [Harvey-etal-2015] |
| Uncontrolled seizure disorder | Delay [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025] |
| Current suicidal ideation | Risk assessment first; consider delay [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025] |
| Night-time fall risk, sedative use | Adapt stimulus control [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025] [Edinger-etal-2021a] |
| Pregnancy, shift work | Adapt; these groups were excluded from a major primary care trial [Kyle-etal-2020] |

- If no risk is present → set the window at average diary sleep, never below 5 hours [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008]. Because this floor limits sleep loss while still building sleep drive.
- If the client is starting restriction → warn that the first 2 to 3 weeks are the hardest and that sleepiness usually settles. Because sleepiness was no longer significantly raised by week 4 in one study [Kyle-etal-2014], and harms typically fade as time in bed increases [Edinger-etal-2021a].
- If mood rises, sleepiness becomes unsafe, or the client nods off at the wheel → widen the window at once, tell the client not to drive, and contact the prescriber or care team about rising mood. Because sleep restriction may harm people prone to mania and those in high-risk jobs [Edinger-etal-2021a].

Example phrasing: "For the first couple of weeks you'll probably feel more tired. If you feel sleepy, please don't drive. Let's agree what you'll do instead."

## Sources note

The contraindications are guideline consensus and expert remarks rather than trial findings [Edinger-etal-2021a] [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025]. The sleepiness data come from an uncontrolled study of 15 completers [Kyle-etal-2014]. The 6.5-hour bipolar floor comes from one pilot trial [Harvey-etal-2015].
