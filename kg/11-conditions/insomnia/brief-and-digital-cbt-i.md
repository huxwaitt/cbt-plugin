**Bottom line:** Brief behavioral therapy for insomnia (BBTI, 1 to 4 sessions) and digital cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) are effective, guideline-supported ways to widen access [Edinger-etal-2021a] [Riemann-etal-2023]. In-person CBT-I by a trained clinician remains the best-studied format, and head-to-head trials show small differences in its favor [Knutzen-etal-2024] [Edinger-etal-2021b]. Step up to full CBT-I when a brief course does not work [VA-DoD-BehaviorChange-2025].

## When to use

- Primary care or settings with little therapist time.
- Waiting lists for in-person CBT-I.
- Clients who prefer self-paced or remote treatment.
- Not as a substitute for screening: rule out sleep apnea, bipolar disorder and other reasons to delay first (see `insomnia.adaptations`).

## How it works

Brief formats keep the behavioral core: a fixed rise time, a sleep window from diary data, and stimulus control, with little or no cognitive therapy [Edinger-etal-2021a]. Digital programs deliver the full CBT-I content through automated sessions, often with diary-driven sleep window calculations.

## Steps

**Brief behavioral therapy (BBTI)** [Buysse-etal-2011]

1. Session 1 (45 to 60 minutes): education on sleep regulation; diary review; four rules: reduce time in bed, get up at the same time every day, do not go to bed unless sleepy, do not stay in bed unless asleep. Discourage naps.
2. Set time in bed at average sleep time plus 30 minutes, minimum 6 hours.
3. Phone call at week 1 (about 20 minutes); review and adjust.
4. Session 2 at week 2 (30 minutes); adjust the window.
5. Phone call at week 3; plan for continuing on their own.

A mental health nurse practitioner new to behavioral sleep treatment delivered it [Buysse-etal-2011]. The US Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense (VA/DoD) describe brief behavioral therapy as 4 sessions over 4 weeks [VA-DoD-BehaviorChange-2025]. In a primary care trial, nurses delivered sleep restriction in 4 brief sessions (2 in person, 2 by phone), about 1 hour 5 minutes of contact in total [Kyle-etal-2020].

**Digital CBT-I**

1. Screen and assess as for in-person care.
2. Choose a program with trial evidence; check data privacy terms. Programs with some therapist support ranked highest for insomnia severity in one review cited by the VA/DoD [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
3. Agree a start date and an early check-in (session, call or message) in the first weeks, when sleepiness and window problems are most likely.
4. Re-measure with the Insomnia Severity Index at the end; step up if improvement is small.

**Group CBT-I:** the same content in a group. Evidence on group versus individual delivery is limited and mixed [Edinger-etal-2021b].

## Guidance

- If full CBT-I is not available in a timely way → offer brief behavioral therapy. Because the VA/DoD suggest it as an alternative when CBT-I is not readily available [VA-DoD-BehaviorChange-2025] [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025].
- If a client does not respond to a brief or digital course → step up to full CBT-I with a clinician. Because the VA/DoD advises stepping up brief-treatment nonresponders to CBT-I [VA-DoD-BehaviorChange-2025], and the same logic applies after digital care.
- If the client is in UK primary care and would otherwise get sleep hygiene advice or sleeping pills → digital CBT-I is a recommended option, after a medical check if another sleep disorder is likely. Because the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) recommends Sleepio for this group as a cost-saving option (HTG624, formerly MTG70) [NICE-HTG624] [Atenstaedt-2023].
- If the client is likely to drop out of self-guided material → add brief human contact or a culturally tailored version. Because a tailored internet program raised completion from 64.8% to 78.2%, and completers improved more [Zhou-etal-2022].
- If the client is sleepy, drives for work or has bipolar disorder → do not leave sleep restriction to an unsupervised app. Because these groups need adapted windows and monitoring (see `insomnia.pitfall-unscreened-sleep-restriction`) [Edinger-etal-2021a].

Example phrasing: "This program walks you through the same steps we'd do in person. I'll check in at week 2 to see how the sleep window is going."

## Common problems

- Prescribing an app with no follow-up, so nonresponse goes unnoticed.
- Using brief formats for clients with complex comorbidity who need cognitive work.
- Treating sleep hygiene leaflets as a brief treatment; they are not.

## Evidence

- **Brief behavioral:** response 67% versus 25% for control in older adults, number needed to treat 2.4 [Buysse-etal-2011]. Nurse-delivered sleep restriction beat sleep hygiene at 6 months (insomnia severity difference -3.05 points, Cohen's d -0.74) and was cost-effective [Kyle-etal-2023]. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine gives brief therapies a conditional recommendation [Edinger-etal-2021a].
- **Digital:** 15 head-to-head trials (1,083 participants) found digital and in-person CBT-I statistically equivalent on most outcomes, with small differences favoring in-person [Knutzen-etal-2024]. Digital CBT-I improved insomnia (d = 1.11) in 3,755 students [Freeman-etal-2017]. The European guideline accepts digital delivery as first-line [Riemann-etal-2023].
- **Formats in general:** individual, group, telephone, web and self-help delivery all appear effective, though most studies tested in-person CBT-I [Qaseem-etal-2016]. In the UK, most people with insomnia lack access to face-to-face CBT-I [Atenstaedt-2023].
