**Bottom line:** A prospective sleep diary, kept each morning for at least 2 weeks before treatment, is the core insomnia measure and the basis for every sleep window decision [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008] [Carney-etal-2012]. Sleep efficiency is total sleep time divided by time in bed, times 100; under 85% is a common complaint threshold and the main titration signal [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008] [Kyle-etal-2014].

## When to use

- Baseline: at least 2 weeks before treatment [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008]; 1 week of baseline was used to set the window in one trial protocol [Kyle-etal-2014].
- Throughout cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), every night, reviewed every session.
- At relapse and at long-term follow-up [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].

## Steps

1. **Choose a format.** The Consensus Sleep Diary (CSD) core version has 9 items: time into bed, time tried to sleep, time to fall asleep, number of awakenings, total duration of awakenings, final waking time, time out of bed, sleep quality rating and comments. One page holds a week [Carney-etal-2012]. Extended versions add early waking, naps, caffeine, alcohol and medication [Carney-etal-2012]. Paper, app or spreadsheet all work.
2. **Explain the rules.** Complete it each morning within 1 hour of getting up [Carney-etal-2012]. Give best estimates; do not watch the clock at night. Rough is fine.
3. **Calculate each night.**
   - Time in bed (TIB) = time out of bed minus time into bed.
   - Total sleep time (TST) = TIB minus time to fall asleep (sleep onset latency, SOL) minus wake after sleep onset (WASO), including any early-morning time awake before rising [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
   - Sleep efficiency (SE) = TST divided by TIB, times 100 [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
4. **Average the week.** Use 7-day means; single nights vary widely.
5. **Act on the averages.** Set the sleep window from mean TST and adjust weekly by mean SE (see the table below).
6. **Show the client.** Plot weekly SE and TST so the client sees the trend.

**Worked example**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Into bed 22:30, out of bed 07:00 | TIB = 510 minutes |
| Took 60 minutes to fall asleep | SOL = 60 |
| Awake 40 minutes in the night plus 20 minutes before rising | WASO = 60 |
| TST | 510 - 60 - 60 = 390 minutes (6 hours 30 minutes) |
| SE | 390 / 510 x 100 = 76.5%, below 85%, so the window would be cut toward 6.5 hours |

**Weekly titration rule (one common protocol)** [Kyle-etal-2014]

| Mean SE for the week | Change to the window |
|---|---|
| 90% or more | Add 15 minutes (earlier bedtime) |
| 85% to 89% | No change |
| Under 85% | Remove 15 minutes, never below 5 hours |

The 2008 American Academy of Sleep Medicine guideline allows 15 to 20 minute steps and a decrease only below 80% [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008]; agree one rule and use it consistently.

## What to record

- The diary pages or export, and weekly means of TIB, TST, SOL, WASO, SE and naps.
- The prescribed window (bedtime and rise time) for each week and whether it was kept; trial protocols compare diaries with the prescribed times to measure adherence [Kyle-etal-2020].
- Insomnia Severity Index score at the same points.
- Notable nights (illness, travel, alcohol) so outliers are explained.

## Guidance

- If the client cannot recall the night → ask for a rough estimate each morning rather than a missed entry. Because the diary measures perceived sleep, which is the complaint being treated [Carney-etal-2012].
- If the client brings a wearable's sleep score instead → keep the diary as the primary measure and treat the device as optional extra data. Because diagnosis rests on self-report and actigraphy is not recommended for routine evaluation [Riemann-etal-2023].
- If diary and objective data disagree → discuss misperception gently as a finding, not an accusation. Because people with insomnia often perceive light sleep as wakefulness [Harvey-Tang-2012].
- If the client stops filling it in → simplify to the five core times and review it first in every session. Because every window decision depends on diary data [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
- If the client is checking the clock to fill the diary precisely → tell them to estimate in the morning instead. Because monitoring sleep-related cues keeps arousal high [Harvey-2002].

Example phrasing: "This isn't a test. A rough guess each morning is exactly what we need."

## Common problems

- Calculating SE from time asleep over 8 hours rather than over actual time in bed.
- Changing the window after one bad night instead of on the weekly mean.
- Counting time lying in bed after the final waking as sleep.
- Collecting diaries but never scoring them in session.
