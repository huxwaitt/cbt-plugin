**Bottom line:** Insomnia disorder is a persistent complaint of trouble starting sleep, staying asleep or waking too early, despite adequate opportunity to sleep, with daytime distress or impairment, on at least 3 nights a week for at least 3 months [Grima-etal-2019]. About 6% to 10% of adults meet diagnostic criteria [Qaseem-etal-2016]. It is a disorder in its own right even when depression, pain or another sleep disorder is present, and cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the first-line treatment [Qaseem-etal-2016] [Riemann-etal-2023].

## Diagnostic essentials

Paraphrased from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR); do not copy the criteria text [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

- **Core complaint:** the person is unhappy with how much or how well they sleep, with trouble getting to sleep, trouble staying asleep, or waking too early and not getting back to sleep (one or more) [Grima-etal-2019].
- **Impact:** clinically significant distress or impairment in daytime functioning [Grima-etal-2019].
- **Frequency and duration:** at least 3 nights per week, for at least 3 months [Grima-etal-2019].
- **Opportunity:** the difficulty occurs despite adequate opportunity for sleep. This separates insomnia from insufficient sleep caused by a restricted schedule [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
- **Exclusions:** not better explained by, and not only during, another sleep-wake disorder; not attributable to a substance or medication; coexisting mental and medical conditions do not adequately explain it [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- **Specifiers:** with a non-sleep mental disorder (including substance use), with another medical condition, or with another sleep disorder. Course: episodic (at least 1 month but under 3 months), persistent (3 months or longer), or recurrent (two or more episodes within 1 year) [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- **Under 3 months:** DSM-5-TR uses other specified insomnia disorder [Grima-etal-2019].
- **Coding:** DSM-5-TR insomnia disorder carries the ICD-10-CM code F51.01 [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- **No primary versus secondary split:** DSM-5 dropped this distinction, so insomnia alongside depression or sleep apnea is diagnosed and treated in its own right [Khurshid-2015].

**Key differences in the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11)** [WHO-ICD11-2022]:

| Feature | DSM-5-TR | ICD-11 |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | At least 3 nights a week | Several times a week |
| Short duration | Other specified insomnia disorder | Its own code, 7A01 short-term insomnia (under 3 months) |
| Chapter | Sleep-wake disorders within the DSM | Separate sleep-wake disorders chapter, outside mental disorders |
| Severity | No severity specifier listed | Optional severity extension code (mild, moderate, severe) |
| With another condition | Comorbidity specifiers | Diagnose only when insomnia is an independent focus of clinical attention |

ICD-11 also notes that some people have an episodic course, with episodes lasting several weeks recurring over years [WHO-ICD11-2022].

## Course and prevalence

- **Tiers:** about one third of adults report at least one insomnia symptom, 9% to 15% when daytime consequences are required, and about 6% meet full diagnostic criteria [Ohayon-2002]. The American College of Physicians (ACP) cites 6% to 10% [Qaseem-etal-2016]; a 2026 review gives 10% to 16% [Spiegelhalder-etal-2026]. Estimates differ with the definition used.
- **Sex and age:** women have about 1.41 times the risk of men, and the gap widens with age [Zhang-Wing-2006]. Insomnia is more common in older adults [Qaseem-etal-2016].
- **Onset:** acute insomnia after a stressor is common and usually resolves; perpetuating habits and beliefs turn some cases chronic [Cox-Olatunji-2021].
- **Course:** in a 3-year cohort of 388 adults with insomnia, 74% had it for at least 1 year and 46% for all 3 years; 54% remitted, but 27% of those relapsed [Morin-etal-2009a].

## Differential and comorbidity

- **Insufficient sleep opportunity** (work, caregiving, schedule): not insomnia [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
- **Other sleep disorders:** sleep apnea, restless legs and periodic limb movements, circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders, parasomnias and nightmares can cause or coexist with insomnia [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008] [Grima-etal-2019].
- **Medical, medication and substance causes** [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008]; see `12-medical-pharmacology.medical-mimics`.
- **Mental disorders:** depression, bipolar disorder, anxiety disorders, posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), obsessive-compulsive disorder, substance use and psychosis commonly co-occur [Grima-etal-2019]. Insomnia about doubles the risk of later depression (pooled odds ratio 2.60 across 21 studies) [Baglioni-etal-2011].
- **Sleep misperception:** many people with insomnia overestimate time to fall asleep and underestimate sleep time; this is a treatment target, not a reason to dismiss the complaint [Harvey-Tang-2012].

## CBT approach in brief

CBT-I combines sleep restriction, stimulus control, cognitive therapy and education, usually over 4 to 8 sessions guided by a sleep diary [Edinger-etal-2021a]. It is first-line for adults of any age, including people with comorbid conditions [Riemann-etal-2023], and a strong recommendation of the ACP [Qaseem-etal-2016]. Medication is considered when CBT-I alone has not worked [Qaseem-etal-2016]. Read `insomnia.cbt-model`, then `insomnia.treatment` and `insomnia.cbt-i-components`.

## Risk notes

- Sleep disturbance is associated with suicidal ideation, attempts and suicide death (unadjusted relative risks 1.95 to 2.95) [Pigeon-etal-2012]. Ask about suicidal thoughts at every insomnia assessment and follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`. With current suicidal ideation, assess risk before sleep restriction: the US Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense (VA/DoD) guideline notes CBT-I may need to be delayed because restriction can worsen it [VA-DoD-CPG-Insomnia-2025]. If risk is imminent: US, call or text 988 or call 911; UK, 999, NHS 111 or Samaritans 116 123.
- Sleep restriction raises sleepiness and slows reaction time in the first weeks [Kyle-etal-2014]; it may be contraindicated for drivers, people in high-risk jobs and people with poorly controlled seizures [Edinger-etal-2021a].
- Sleep loss can trigger hypomania or mania in bipolar disorder, so sleep restriction is modified [Harvey-etal-2015].
