**Bottom line:** Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) mainly shortens time to fall asleep and time awake at night; total sleep time changes little at the end of treatment (about 8 minutes, not significant, against control) [Trauer-etal-2015]. Judge progress by sleep efficiency, time awake, the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) and daytime function, not hours alone, or both of you will wrongly conclude it is failing.

## What it looks like

- The client says "I'm still only getting 6 hours" in week 3 and asks to go back to 9 hours in bed.
- The therapist widens the window early because the client is unhappy, and efficiency falls back.
- Session time is spent debating the "8 hours" target.
- Treatment ends as "unsuccessful" although the client now falls asleep in 15 minutes and rarely wakes.

## Why it happens

- "Eight hours" is a strong cultural belief and a common unhelpful belief in insomnia.
- Sleep restriction cuts measured sleep at first: objectively recorded sleep fell by 69 to 91 minutes during a 4-week course in one study [Kyle-etal-2014].
- Single-component sleep restriction improved severity, efficiency and wakefulness but not total sleep time (g = 0.02) [Maurer-etal-2021].
- People with insomnia tend to underestimate how much they sleep [Harvey-Tang-2012].

## The fix

- If the client focuses on hours → show weekly sleep efficiency, time to fall asleep and time awake from the diary alongside hours. Because these are what CBT-I changes most (sleep onset about 19 minutes faster, night waking about 26 minutes shorter, efficiency about 10 points higher) [Trauer-etal-2015].
- If the client asks to extend the window before efficiency is high → keep the titration rule: extend only when weekly efficiency passes the agreed threshold. Because time in bed is lengthened step by step as efficiency rises [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].
- If you need one outcome to judge response → use ISI change. Because a drop of more than 7 points marks moderate improvement [Morin-etal-2011], and the American Academy of Sleep Medicine review counted remission as ISI under 8 [Edinger-etal-2021b].
- If hours stay low but the client functions well → work on beliefs about sleep need with cognitive therapy and behavioral experiments. Because cognitive therapy adds durable gains [Harvey-etal-2014].
- If the client feels unrefreshed despite high efficiency → extend the window gradually. Because the goal is satisfactory sleep and daytime function, not a set number of hours [Schutte-Rodin-etal-2008].

Example phrasing: "Your hours look similar, but look here: you used to lie awake for two hours a night and now it's about thirty minutes. That's the change we usually see first. As your sleep gets more solid, we'll extend your window."

Avoid: "Don't worry about the hours." It dismisses a real concern without showing the evidence.

## Sources note

Total sleep time effects are small at post-treatment in meta-analyses [Trauer-etal-2015] [Maurer-etal-2021]; the Trauer review suggested gains persist at follow-up but estimates there are less certain [Trauer-etal-2015].
