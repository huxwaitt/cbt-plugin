**Bottom line:** Eating disorders can kill through cardiac, electrolyte and refeeding complications at any body weight [RCPsych-CR233] [Vo-Golden-2022]. Most therapists do not take vital signs or bloods, so their job is to ask about warning symptoms each session, make sure a named medical clinician is monitoring, and escalate the same day when red flags appear. Use the Royal College of Psychiatrists' Medical Emergencies in Eating Disorders (MEED) framework or the American Psychiatric Association (APA) admission factors to judge urgency; no single number decides it [RCPsych-CR233].

## When to use

At assessment, every session with an underweight or purging client, after rapid weight loss, and when eating increases after severe restriction (refeeding risk).

## Steps

1. **Confirm medical monitoring.** Name who checks weight, vital signs, blood tests and electrocardiogram (ECG), and how often. APA recommends orthostatic vital signs, a blood count and metabolic panel, and an ECG for restriction, severe purging or QT-prolonging drugs [APA-ED-Guideline-2023].
2. **Ask each session** about fainting, dizziness on standing, palpitations, chest pain, breathlessness, confusion, weakness climbing stairs or rising from a chair, vomiting blood, fluid intake, vomiting and laxative frequency, and exercise hours.
3. **Check weight trend** from in-session weighing.
4. **Grade urgency** with the thresholds below.

| Finding | MEED amber (high concern) | MEED red (high risk) | APA hospitalization factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult body mass index (BMI) | 13 to 14.9 | under 13 | under 15 |
| Under 18 (APA column: ages 12 to 19) | 70% to 80% of median BMI | under 70% | under 75% of median BMI |
| Weight loss | 500 to 999 g a week for 2 weeks, if undernourished | 1 kg a week for 2 weeks if undernourished, or rapid loss at any weight | over 10% in 6 months or 20% in 1 year |
| Heart rate (awake) | 40 to 50 | under 40 | under 50 |
| Temperature | under 36 °C | under 35.5 °C (ear) or 35.0 °C (armpit) | under 36 °C |
| Rising from squat or lying | noticeable difficulty | unable without using arms | not listed |
| Purging | 3 or more times a week | several times a day | frequent, and causing serious physical harm or not responding to lower-level care |
| Exercise while malnourished | over 1 hour a day | over 2 hours a day | not listed |

From MEED Table 1 [RCPsych-CR233] and APA Table 6 [APA-ED-Guideline-2023]. APA also lists blood pressure under 90/60 mmHg (90/45 in adolescents), a sustained pulse rise over 30 (over 40 in adolescents) or systolic drop over 20 mmHg on standing, glucose under 60 mg/dL, corrected QT interval (QTc) over 450 ms, low potassium, sodium, phosphate or magnesium, and acute complications such as seizures, syncope, cardiac failure or pancreatitis [APA-ED-Guideline-2023]. MEED adds acute food refusal or intake under 500 kcal a day for 2 or more days as red [RCPsych-CR233].

**Escalation tiers**
- **Emergency now** (911 in the US; 999 or emergency department in the UK): collapse or fainting, chest pain, seizure, confusion, vomiting blood, severe weakness, or suicidal intent with a plan. The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) calls for acute care for severe electrolyte imbalance, malnutrition, dehydration or signs of organ failure [NICE-NG69].
- **Same-day medical review:** one or more MEED red ratings or two or more amber, which MEED treats as probably high risk; fluid refusal, a red rating [RCPsych-CR233]; weight loss over 1 kg a week, which NICE links to needing inpatient care [NICE-NG69]. Same-day timing is expert consensus; if no medical clinician can be reached that day, use urgent care or the emergency department.
- **Within days, sooner if worsening:** a single new amber finding, rising purging, or a new medication that can affect the heart, which may need ECG monitoring [NICE-NG69].

## What to record

- Symptoms asked about and answers, weight trend, purging and exercise frequency, and who monitors physical health, with the date of the last vital signs, bloods and ECG.
- Any red or amber findings, who you contacted, when, and what was agreed.
- The client's view and any refusal, with the explanation you gave.

## Guidance

- If weight is normal or high but loss was rapid → request vital signs and bloods (see `eating-disorders.pitfall-normal-weight-assumed-safe`). Because rate and amount of loss, not presenting weight, predict complications [Vo-Golden-2022].
- If a client with bulimia nervosa reports frequent vomiting → ask for electrolytes. Because people who are not underweight can present in life-threatening emergencies from electrolyte disturbance [RCPsych-CR233].
- If intake increases quickly after severe restriction → alert the medical team before a meal plan rises. Because refeeding can cause dangerous drops in phosphate, potassium and magnesium; close monitoring prevents it [APA-ED-Guideline-2023].
- If the client misuses laxatives or diuretics → explain they do not reduce calorie absorption and plan gradual reduction with the physician. Because NICE advises gradual stopping [NICE-NG69].
- If acute suicide risk appears → follow crisis procedures (see `02-risk.crisis-response`). Because one in five deaths in anorexia nervosa is by suicide [Arcelus-etal-2011].

Example phrasing: "Your heart and blood chemistry can be affected before you feel very ill. I'd like the doctor to check you today. Shall we call now together?"

Avoid: "Your weight looks okay, so the dizziness is probably nothing."

Crisis contacts: US 988 (call or text) [Lifeline-988] or 911; UK Samaritans 116 123 [Samaritans-Site], National Health Service (NHS) 111, or 999.

## Common problems

- **Thresholds disagree** (for example heart rate): follow your service's protocol and record which you used. NICE advises against any absolute BMI threshold for admission [NICE-NG69].
- **Adult cutoffs used for children.** Youth need age- and sex-adjusted values [RCPsych-CR233].
- **Scope:** therapists flag and refer; physicians interpret results and decide admission.
