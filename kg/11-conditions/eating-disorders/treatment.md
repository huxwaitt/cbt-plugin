**Bottom line:** For adults with bulimia nervosa or binge-eating disorder, the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) starts with cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) based guided self-help and steps up to eating-disorder-focused CBT (CBT-ED, most often the enhanced form, CBT-E); for adults with anorexia nervosa, CBT-ED is one of three options [NICE-NG69]. CBT-E runs about 20 sessions over 20 weeks for clients with a body mass index (BMI) above 17.5 and 40 sessions over 40 weeks below that [Murphy-etal-2010]. Young people with anorexia or bulimia nervosa start with family therapy, with CBT-ED as an alternative [NICE-NG69].

## When to use

| Presentation | First step | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Binge-eating disorder (all ages) | Guided self-help with CBT materials, for example 4 to 9 brief 20-minute support sessions over 16 weeks [NICE-NG69] | Group CBT-ED (16 weekly 90-minute sessions over 4 months); individual CBT-ED (typically 16 to 20 sessions) if group is unavailable or declined [NICE-NG69] |
| Adult bulimia nervosa | Consider guided self-help [NICE-NG69] | Individual CBT-ED, up to 20 sessions over 20 weeks [NICE-NG69] |
| Adult anorexia nervosa | CBT-ED (up to 40 sessions over 40 weeks), Maudsley Anorexia Nervosa Treatment for Adults (MANTRA), or specialist supportive clinical management (SSCM), by client choice [NICE-NG69] | Another of the three, or focal psychodynamic therapy |
| Under 18, anorexia nervosa | Anorexia-focused family therapy, 18 to 20 sessions over 1 year [NICE-NG69] | Individual CBT-ED (up to 40 sessions plus 8 to 12 family sessions) or adolescent-focused psychotherapy |
| Under 18, bulimia nervosa | Bulimia-focused family therapy, 18 to 20 sessions over 6 months [NICE-NG69] | Individual CBT-ED, 18 sessions over 6 months plus up to 4 sessions with parents |
| Other specified eating disorder | Treatment for the disorder it most resembles [NICE-NG69] | |

Do not offer medication as the sole treatment for anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa or binge-eating disorder [NICE-NG69].

## How it works

CBT-E targets the processes that maintain the eating disorder rather than the diagnosis: dieting, binge eating, compensatory behavior, overevaluation of shape and weight, and, where relevant, low weight [Murphy-etal-2010].

## Steps

Session outline for 20-session CBT-E (focused form) [Murphy-etal-2010]:

| Stage | Sessions | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation | 1 preparatory session (90 minutes) [Fairburn-etal-2015] | Engagement, explain treatment, check medical safety |
| One | About 8, twice weekly over 4 weeks | Joint formulation, real-time self-monitoring, weekly in-session weighing, education, regular eating by about session 3 |
| Two | 2, one week apart | Review progress, revise formulation, decide whether broad modules are needed |
| Three | About 8, weekly | Overevaluation of shape and weight, body checking and avoidance, feeling fat, dietary rules and avoided foods, event- and mood-related eating |
| Four | 3, about 2 weeks apart | Maintenance plan, lapse versus relapse, stop monitoring, weigh weekly at home |
| Review | 1, about 20 weeks later | Check maintenance, address setbacks |

For a BMI of 17.5 or below, the 40-session version adds motivation work and weight regain, which the client decides on rather than having it imposed [Fairburn-etal-2013] [APA-ED-Guideline-2023].

A 10-session version for clients who are not underweight (CBT-T), delivered by supervised clinical assistants, matched effectiveness benchmarks for longer CBT-ED in a case series of 106 referrals [Waller-etal-2018]. In a randomized trial of 98 adults, CBT-T and a motivational 10-session variant both gave large gains, with no difference [Wade-etal-2021]. No trial has compared CBT-T head to head with CBT-E, so its evidence is emerging.

## Guidance

- If guided self-help is unacceptable, contraindicated or not reducing binges after 4 weeks → step up to CBT-ED. Because NICE sets that review point [NICE-NG69].
- If the client is not underweight → offer the 20-session version and front-load sessions twice weekly. Because the intensive start builds momentum and early change predicts outcome [Murphy-etal-2010].
- If perfectionism, low self-esteem or interpersonal problems clearly maintain the disorder → add the broad modules after the stage two review. Because complex clients did better with the broad form in one trial [Fairburn-etal-2009].
- If tempted to improvise → follow an eating-disorder manual for content, structure and length. Because NICE asks for manual-based treatment [NICE-NG69].

Example phrasing: "We'll meet twice a week for the first month. The early weeks matter most, because the changes you make then predict how well treatment goes."

## Common problems

- Dropout: in one trial 22% of participants across CBT-E and interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT) did not complete, rising to 32% in bulimia nervosa [Fairburn-etal-2015]. Address ambivalence early.
- Drift into general talk (see `eating-disorders.pitfall-therapist-drift`).
- Setting weight-loss targets in binge-eating disorder (see `eating-disorders.pitfall-weight-loss-goals-in-binge-eating`).

## Evidence

- **Guideline status.** The American Psychiatric Association (APA) recommends eating-disorder-focused CBT for adults with bulimia nervosa, with a serotonin reuptake inhibitor (for example fluoxetine 60 mg daily) at the start or if response is minimal by 6 weeks (1C). It recommends CBT or IPT, individual or group, for binge-eating disorder (1C), eating-disorder-focused psychotherapy for adult anorexia nervosa (1B), and family-based treatment for adolescents and emerging adults with anorexia nervosa who have an involved caregiver (1B) [APA-ED-Guideline-2023].
- **Meta-analysis.** Across 79 randomized trials, therapist-led CBT beat waiting list and other psychotherapies for bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder; many trials were of poor quality [Linardon-etal-2017].
- **CBT-E trials.** In 130 adults (BMI over 17.5 and under 40), 65.5% were in remission after CBT-E versus 33.3% after IPT, and 69.4% versus 49.0% at 60 weeks [Fairburn-etal-2015]. In 99 adults with BMI 17.5 or below, 64% completed 40 sessions and completers gained a mean 7.47 kg [Fairburn-etal-2013]. A review of 20 studies supported CBT-E across diagnoses, though most trials did not show it beating comparison treatments long term [Atwood-Friedman-2020].
- **Binge-eating disorder.** Across 81 trials, CBT had large effects on binge eating and CBT-based self-help medium-to-large effects, but evidence quality was very low [Hilbert-etal-2019].
