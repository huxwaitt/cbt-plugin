**Bottom line:** Keep the eating disorder focus and adapt around it. Young people start with family-based treatment; very low weight lengthens treatment and needs medical monitoring; diabetes, pregnancy and severe comorbidity need joint care; and medication is an add-on, never the sole treatment, according to the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) [NICE-NG69]. Step up care when outpatient work cannot stop the decline, judged on the whole picture rather than one weight threshold [NICE-NG69].

## The principle

Adapt the format, length, people involved and medical support, not the core targets: regular eating, weight restoration where needed, and the processes in the client's formulation. Decide the order of treating comorbid problems by severity, functioning and the client's preference [NICE-NG69].

## Guidance

**Comorbidity**
- If severe depression, significant substance misuse or a crisis would stop the client using cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) → treat or stabilize that first, then start. Because these are contraindications to starting enhanced CBT (CBT-E) at once [Murphy-etal-2010].
- If substance misuse is present but not blocking progress → treat the eating disorder anyway. Because NICE advises this [NICE-NG69].
- If self-harm or suicidal thinking appears → assess risk that session and build a safety plan; for acute risk use crisis services (US 988 or 911; UK 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123) [Lifeline-988] [Samaritans-Site]. Because NICE advises psychiatric crisis or inpatient care for significant suicide risk [NICE-NG69].

**Severity and level of care**
- If weight keeps falling, vital signs are unstable, or the client cannot reduce restriction or purging without meal support → discuss day patient or inpatient care with the team. Because these are among the factors the American Psychiatric Association (APA) guideline uses to choose level of care [APA-ED-Guideline-2023].
- If someone suggests admission only for psychological therapy, or discharge only because weight is restored → challenge it. Because NICE advises against both [NICE-NG69].

**Age**
- If the client is under 18 with anorexia nervosa → offer family therapy first, with parents taking temporary charge of eating. Because it is the first-line option [NICE-NG69] [APA-ED-Guideline-2023].
- If family therapy is unacceptable or ineffective → offer individual eating-disorder-focused CBT (CBT-ED) with 8 to 12 brief family sessions. Because NICE lists it as the alternative [NICE-NG69].
- If an adult client has a partner or parent who comments on eating or body → invite them to a session, with consent. Because others can help or hinder change [Murphy-etal-2010].

**Culture and identity**
- If the client is male, from a racial or ethnic minority, transgender or nonbinary, or in a larger body → screen as you would anyone. Because these groups are often overlooked, and eating disorders may be more frequent in transgender and nonbinary people [APA-ED-Guideline-2023].
- If eating practices are culturally or religiously sanctioned (feasts, fasts, avoided foods) → judge harm to health, distress and impairment, not the practice itself. Because the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) does not count culturally sanctioned feasting as binge eating, or religious food avoidance as avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder (ARFID), unless health or functioning suffers [WHO-CDDR-2024].
- If presentation centers on muscularity in men → ask about it directly. Because body ideals differ by gender and region [WHO-CDDR-2024].

**Diabetes, pregnancy and ARFID**
- If a client with diabetes omits insulin → address it within CBT and agree with the diabetes team who monitors glucose and ketones. Because insulin misuse is part of the eating disorder [NICE-NG69].
- If the client is pregnant or postpartum → offer standard treatment and a named monitoring professional. Because weight concern and perinatal risk rise [NICE-NG69].
- If restriction stems from sensory sensitivity, fear of choking or low interest → consider CBT for ARFID (CBT-AR), typically 20 to 30 sessions. Because in an open pilot with 10- to 17-year-olds, 70% no longer met ARFID criteria afterward [Thomas-etal-2020]. Evidence is emerging, and NICE does not cover ARFID [NICE-NG69].
- If a child aged 6 to 12 with ARFID is underweight → involve parents and consider family-based treatment for ARFID. Because in a randomized trial it produced more weight gain than individual therapy [Lock-etal-2026].

**Medication**
- If an adult with bulimia nervosa responds minimally by 6 weeks → refer to a prescriber about fluoxetine 60 mg daily. Because the APA recommends adding a serotonin reuptake inhibitor (1C) [APA-ED-Guideline-2023].
- If an adult with binge-eating disorder prefers medication or has not responded → discuss an antidepressant or lisdexamfetamine with a prescriber. Because APA suggests this (2C) [APA-ED-Guideline-2023]; lisdexamfetamine 50 and 70 mg beat placebo [McElroy-etal-2015].
- If a medication question comes up → refer to the prescriber and share your progress data. Because prescribing belongs to licensed prescribers, and scope varies by license and country.

**Stepping up or referring**
- If the client lacks capacity, refuses care and is at serious physical risk → involve psychiatry about legal frameworks. Because compulsory treatment follows local law [NICE-NG69].

## Example phrasings

- To parents: "For now, you are in charge of meals, the way you would be if your child had any illness that stopped them eating. This is temporary."
- To an adult with diabetes: "Skipping insulin seems to be one way you control your weight. It carries serious risks, so can we put it on the formulation?"

## Evidence

Family-based treatment produced more full remission than adolescent-focused therapy at 6 and 12 months in 121 adolescents [Lock-etal-2010]. APA rates family-based treatment for adolescent anorexia nervosa 1B and for adolescent bulimia nervosa 2C [APA-ED-Guideline-2023]. CBT-AR and adolescent CBT-E rest on uncontrolled studies [Thomas-etal-2020] [DalleGrave-etal-2013]; family-based treatment for low-weight children with ARFID has one randomized trial [Lock-etal-2026]. Comorbidity and culture rules are guideline-based or expert consensus.
