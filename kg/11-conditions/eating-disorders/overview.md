**Bottom line:** Anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder share one core: self-worth judged largely by shape, weight and control of eating, which drives dieting, binge eating and compensatory behavior [Murphy-etal-2010]. Avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder (ARFID) restricts food without that weight concern [Reed-etal-2019]. Anorexia nervosa has the highest standardized mortality of the group, so every assessment checks medical risk [Arcelus-etal-2011].

## Diagnostic essentials

From the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) [APA-DSM5TR-2022]:

- **Anorexia nervosa (AN):** restriction leading to significantly low weight for age, sex, development and health; intense fear of weight gain or persistent behavior that blocks gain; and disturbed body experience, undue influence of weight or shape on self-worth, or not recognizing how serious the low weight is. No amenorrhea requirement [Udo-Grilo-2018]. Subtypes (last 3 months): restricting or binge-eating/purging. Adult severity uses body mass index (BMI): mild 17 or more, moderate 16 to 16.99, severe 15 to 15.99, extreme under 15 [Smith-etal-2017]; youth use BMI percentiles [APA-DSM5TR-Code-Updates].
- **Bulimia nervosa (BN):** recurrent binge eating (an unusually large amount in a discrete period, with loss of control) and recurrent compensatory behavior (vomiting, laxatives, diuretics, fasting, excessive exercise), both at least weekly for 3 months; self-worth unduly tied to shape and weight; not only during AN [Udo-Grilo-2018]. Severity counts compensatory episodes a week: 1 to 3 mild, 4 to 7 moderate, 8 to 13 severe, 14 or more extreme [Smith-etal-2017].
- **Binge-eating disorder (BED):** binge eating at least weekly for 3 months, with at least 3 of 5 features (eating fast, until uncomfortably full, when not hungry, alone from embarrassment, disgust or guilt afterward), marked distress, and no regular compensatory behavior [Udo-Grilo-2018]. Severity counts binge episodes a week, same bands [Smith-etal-2017].
- **ARFID:** intake too limited in amount or variety (low interest, sensory sensitivity, or fear such as choking), causing weight loss or faltering growth, nutritional deficiency, supplement or tube-feeding dependence, or impairment, without weight or shape concern [Reed-etal-2019].
- **Other specified feeding or eating disorder (OSFED):** for example atypical anorexia nervosa (all AN features, weight still in or above the normal range after marked loss), purging disorder and night eating syndrome. Since September 2025 the low-frequency examples read "bulimia nervosa-like" and "binge-eating-like" disorder [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025].
- **Codes:** since October 1, 2024, US codes in the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM) carry severity or remission for AN, BN and BED, for example F50.010 (restricting AN, mild) [APA-DSM5TR-Code-Updates].

**ICD-11 differences** (International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision) [WHO-CDDR-2024]:
- AN: commonly BMI under 18.5 in adults (under the 5th percentile in youth), or loss of over 20% of body weight within 6 months. Specifiers: significantly low (BMI 18.5 to 14.0) and dangerously low (under 14.0). It is kept until healthy weight without weight-loss behavior has lasted about 1 year after treatment.
- BN: binges and compensation at least weekly over at least 1 month, not 3. Subjective binges (loss of control over an ordinary amount) count.
- BED: weekly over 3 months (about 1 month if binges are frequent and distressing).

## Course and prevalence

- US adults (n = 36,306): lifetime AN 0.80%, BN 0.28%, BED 0.85%; mean onset 19.3, 20.0 and 24.5 years; past-year persistence of lifetime cases 9.4%, 54.7% and 63.5% [Udo-Grilo-2018].
- Across 94 studies, weighted lifetime prevalence was 8.4% in women and 2.2% in men; point prevalence rose from 3.5% (2000 to 2006) to 7.8% (2013 to 2018) [Galmiche-etal-2019].
- The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) notes risk is highest between 13 and 17 years [NICE-NG69]. In a US survey, only a minority of people with BN or BED had ever sought treatment [Hudson-etal-2007].
- Standardized mortality ratios: 5.86 (AN), 1.93 (BN), 1.92 (other eating disorders); one in five deaths in AN was by suicide [Arcelus-etal-2011].

## Differential and comorbidity

- **Medical causes** of weight loss or abnormal blood results (for example celiac disease, infection, blood cancers) need exclusion, without delaying nutrition [RCPsych-CR233].
- **Depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder and body dysmorphic disorder:** appetite loss, rituals or appearance worries without fear of weight gain; muscle-focused concern without a drive for low weight suggests body dysmorphic disorder [WHO-CDDR-2024].
- **ARFID versus AN:** fear of fatness points to AN.
- **BED versus overeating:** large meals without loss of control are not binges.
- **Comorbidity:** all three are highly comorbid with other mental disorders [Hudson-etal-2007]; suicidality is raised in each [Smith-etal-2018a].

## CBT approach in brief

In the transdiagnostic cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) model, overvaluing shape and weight drives strict dieting, broken dietary rules trigger binges, and purging and low weight keep the cycle going. Treatment builds a formulation, establishes regular eating and collaborative weighing, then targets shape concern, dietary rules and mood-related eating [Murphy-etal-2010]. Young people with AN or BN usually start with eating-disorder-focused family therapy, with individual CBT as an alternative [NICE-NG69] [APA-ED-Guideline-2023].

## Risk notes

- If weight loss or physical symptoms raise concern, at any BMI → arrange medical assessment before or alongside therapy (see `eating-disorders.medical-risk`). Because NICE advises against judging need from single measures such as BMI [NICE-NG69].
- If an eating disorder is suspected in the UK → refer at once to a community, age-appropriate eating disorder service. Because NICE asks for immediate referral [NICE-NG69].
- If the client voices hopelessness or suicidal thoughts → assess suicide risk that session (see `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`). Because suicide deaths run about 18 times the expected rate in AN and 7 times in BN [Smith-etal-2018a].
- Crisis contacts: US 988 (call or text) [Lifeline-988] or 911; UK Samaritans 116 123 [Samaritans-Site], National Health Service (NHS) 111, or 999.
