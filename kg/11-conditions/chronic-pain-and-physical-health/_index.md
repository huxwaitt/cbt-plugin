# 11-conditions/chronic-pain-and-physical-health: Chronic Pain and Long-Term Physical Health Conditions

## This surface
Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for people living with chronic pain or a long-term physical condition such as diabetes, coronary heart disease or cancer, when distress, depression, fear or disability is the focus. These pages cover definitions and differentials (including overlap with health anxiety), assessment and measures, the fear-avoidance model and pain catastrophizing, CBT and acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT), guidance from the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) and US bodies, and collaboration with medical teams. Any sign of suicide risk routes to `02-risk` pages first, and new or changing physical symptoms route to medical review.

## Pages in order
1. `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.overview`: start here for definitions, prevalence, differentials including health anxiety, and suicide risk.
2. `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.assessment`: what to ask, which measures to use, and how to involve the medical team.
3. `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.cbt-model`: the fear-avoidance model and its extension to long-term conditions, to draw with the client.
4. `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.treatment`: CBT and ACT for pain, CBT for depression with diabetes, heart disease or cancer, and the evidence.
5. `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.adaptations`: decision rules for specific conditions, opioids, health anxiety overlap and collaboration with medical teams.

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Making Pain Relief the Goal | Therapy is judged by pain scores, sessions stall when pain stays the same, client and therapist feel they are failing, and activity waits until the pain improves. | `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.pitfall-pain-relief-as-the-goal` |
| Implying the Pain or Illness Is Psychological | The client hears that their pain or symptoms are "in their head", feels blamed or dismissed, argues for the physical cause, and disengages or seeks more tests. | `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.pitfall-implying-it-is-psychological` |
| Working Apart From the Medical Team | The therapist sets activity targets, discusses medicines or interprets new symptoms without the treating clinicians, and the client gets mixed messages, flare-ups or missed medical change. | `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.pitfall-working-apart-from-the-medical-team` |
