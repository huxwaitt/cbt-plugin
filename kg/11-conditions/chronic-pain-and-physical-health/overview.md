**Bottom line:** Chronic pain is pain that persists or recurs for more than 3 months [Treede-etal-2019], and about one US adult in four has it [Lucas-Sohi-2024]. Long-term physical conditions such as angina, arthritis, asthma and diabetes raise the rate of depression, and the combination gives the worst health [Moussavi-etal-2007]. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) targets distress, disability and coping, not the disease itself. Suicide risk is raised in chronic pain, so ask directly [Tang-Crane-2006].

## Diagnostic essentials

Chronic pain and long-term conditions are medical presentations, not mental disorders. The therapist's diagnostic task is to name the psychological problem that sits alongside them.

**Chronic pain classification** (International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision, ICD-11)
- Chronic primary pain: pain for more than 3 months with significant emotional distress or functional disability, not better accounted for by another condition. Fibromyalgia and nonspecific low back pain are examples [Nicholas-etal-2019].
- Chronic secondary pain: pain that is at least initially a symptom of another disease, in six groups such as cancer-related, neuropathic, postsurgical and secondary musculoskeletal pain [Treede-etal-2019].
- The two can coexist, and an initial diagnosis of chronic primary pain may change with time, so re-evaluate when the presentation changes [NICE-NG193].

**Diagnoses a CBT therapist may consider** (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision, DSM-5-TR)
- Depressive or anxiety disorders, assessed as usual.
- Somatic symptom disorder, which has a "with predominant pain" specifier and no longer requires symptoms to be medically unexplained [Lowe-etal-2022].
- Psychological factors affecting other medical conditions: a psychological or behavioral factor (for example poor adherence or denial of severity) adversely affects a medical condition. An abnormal reaction that does not affect the medical outcome is an adjustment disorder instead [Dimsdale-2026].

## Course and prevalence

- In the 2023 US National Health Interview Survey, 24.3% of adults had chronic pain and 8.5% had high-impact chronic pain that frequently limited life or work [Lucas-Sohi-2024].
- In World Health Organization surveys across 60 countries, 9.3% to 23.0% of people with angina, arthritis, asthma or diabetes had comorbid depression, significantly more than people without a chronic physical disease; depression with a chronic disease gave the worst health scores [Moussavi-etal-2007].
- Pain often fluctuates with flare-ups, a cause may not be found, and quality of life can improve even when pain does not [NICE-NG193].

## Differential and comorbidity

Questions that separate the main alternatives:
- **Health anxiety:** is the fear about having an undiagnosed serious disease, with checking and reassurance seeking? Or is it fear that movement will cause harm or more pain? The first points to `health-anxiety.overview`; the second to the fear-avoidance model in `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.cbt-model`. Both can coexist.
- **Somatic symptom disorder:** are thoughts, anxiety or time spent on the symptoms disproportionate and persistent? It can be diagnosed alongside a confirmed disease [Lowe-etal-2022].
- **Depression:** fatigue, poor sleep and low appetite may come from the illness or its treatment. Consider the role of the physical problem and medication in the low mood [NICE-CG91].
- **Substance and medication problems:** ask about opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, alcohol and cannabis.
- **Medical change:** new, different or worsening symptoms need medical review before being formulated as psychological.

Insomnia, posttraumatic stress (after injury, surgery or cardiac events) and fear of recurrence are common companions (expert consensus); sleep problems also predict suicidality in chronic pain [Racine-2018].

## CBT approach in brief

Treatment builds a shared biopsychosocial formulation, sets goals for function and valued activity rather than cure, and targets catastrophizing, avoidance, overactivity cycles, low mood and illness-management behavior. For chronic pain, CBT has small benefits on pain, disability and distress [Williams-etal-2020], and the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) suggests considering acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) or CBT for chronic primary pain [NICE-NG193]. For depression with a long-term condition, NICE recommends stepped care with CBT [NICE-CG91]. See `chronic-pain-and-physical-health.treatment`.

## Risk notes

- Risk of death by suicide appears at least doubled in chronic pain; lifetime suicide attempts were 5% to 14% and suicidal ideation about 20% [Tang-Crane-2006].
- Risk factors include depression, sleep problems, unemployment or disability, substance misuse, hopelessness, mental defeat, pain catastrophizing and perceived burdensomeness [Racine-2018].
- Rapid opioid tapering or sudden discontinuation has been linked to mental health crises, overdose, and suicidal ideation and behavior [Dowell-etal-2022].
- Always ask people with depression and a chronic physical health problem directly about suicidal ideation and intent [NICE-CG91]. Follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`. For imminent risk in the US call 911 or call or text 988; in the UK call 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans on 116 123.
