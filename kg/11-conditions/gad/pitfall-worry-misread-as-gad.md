**Bottom line:** Worry appears in nearly every anxiety and mood disorder. Diagnose generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) only when worry spans several everyday areas and is not better explained by another disorder, a substance or a medical condition, as the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) requires [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. Comorbidity is the rule (81.9% lifetime) [Ruscio-etal-2017], so look for what else is present, not just whether worry exists.

## What it looks like

- Worry about one theme, such as illness, contamination or others' judgment, is diagnosed as GAD.
- Imaginal exposure or worry postponement is used for intrusive, unwanted images that the client neutralizes, which suggests obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD).
- Low mood, loss of interest and brooding about the past are filed under "worry".
- Shakiness, palpitations and poor sleep are attributed to GAD without asking about caffeine, stimulants, alcohol withdrawal or thyroid problems.
- A high Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) score is taken as a diagnosis.
- The protocol stalls after several sessions, and nobody revisits the diagnosis.

## Why it happens

- "Worry" is a lay word for many processes: obsessions, rumination, health fears and trauma-related anxiety.
- The GAD-7 screens well for panic, social anxiety and posttraumatic stress disorder, not GAD alone [Kroenke-etal-2007].
- DSM-5-TR excludes worry better explained by another disorder, which requires active checking [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- Substance use and medical problems are not asked about when the presentation looks psychological.
- Somatic presentations in some cultural groups can hide worry, or be misread [Lewis-Fernandez-etal-2010].

## The fix

- If worry centers on one theme → ask whether it is all about illness, contamination, evaluation, separation or trauma, and assess that disorder. Because DSM-5-TR excludes worry better explained by another disorder [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- If thoughts feel unwanted and senseless and the client neutralizes them → assess for OCD and plan exposure and response prevention (see `ocd.overview`). Because obsessions differ from everyday worry [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- If low mood dominates → rate depression with the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) and treat the more severe disorder first. Because the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises treating the primary disorder first [Kendall-etal-2011].
- If alcohol, drugs, caffeine or medication could explain symptoms → record use, and plan substance treatment first if use is harmful. Because NICE asks for this assessment and order [Kendall-etal-2011], and substance use cut the chance of GAD recovery [Bruce-etal-2005].
- If symptoms are new in later life, sudden or mainly physical → ask the physician to rule out medical causes. Because DSM-5-TR excludes anxiety caused by a medical condition [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- If a protocol stalls → re-run the differential before adding sessions.

Example phrasings:
- "When the worry comes, is it mostly about one thing, or does it jump between lots of things?"
- "Do any of these thoughts feel unwanted or senseless, as if they aren't the kind of thing you'd normally think?"
- Avoid: "You're a worrier, so it's GAD."
