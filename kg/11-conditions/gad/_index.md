# 11-conditions/gad: Generalized Anxiety Disorder

## This surface
Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) and pathological worry, from recognizing the disorder to delivering and adapting cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT). The pages run through the episode of care: diagnosis and assessment at intake, a worry model for formulation, the protocol for treatment, and adaptations for comorbidity, age, culture and medication. For worry as a process across disorders, see `15-transdiagnostic-processes.worry`.

## Pages in order
1. `gad.overview`: confirms what GAD is, how common it is and what it is not.
2. `gad.assessment`: what to ask, which measures and cutoffs, and the differential questions.
3. `gad.cbt-model`: the shared maintenance formulation that sets treatment targets.
4. `gad.worry-models-compared`: when the first model does not fit, or you must choose between models.
5. `gad.treatment`: the session-by-session protocol, doses and evidence.
6. `gad.adaptations`: changes for comorbidity, age, culture, medication and non-response.

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Chasing the Content of Each Worry | Each session tackles this week's new worry topic with evidence-gathering or reassurance, the client feels briefly better, and next week a different worry has taken its place. | `gad.pitfall-chasing-worry-content` |
| Calling Every Worrier GAD | A worrier is labeled with generalized anxiety disorder and a worry protocol stalls, because the real driver is obsessions, health anxiety, depression, trauma, substances or a medical problem. | `gad.pitfall-worry-misread-as-gad` |
| Generic Anxiety Management Instead of a GAD Protocol | Therapy for generalized anxiety disorder samples breathing, thought records, sleep tips and relaxation week by week without a worry model, and worry scores barely move after many sessions. | `gad.pitfall-eclectic-anxiety-management` |
