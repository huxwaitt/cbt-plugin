**Bottom line:** Confirm generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) by mapping a few real worry episodes, then checking duration, controllability, associated symptoms and impairment. Screen with the Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7), where 10 or more suggests GAD [Spitzer-etal-2006], and track worry with the Penn State Worry Questionnaire (PSWQ). Rule out other disorders, substances and medical causes, and screen for suicide risk before formulating [Kendall-etal-2011].

## When to use

- At intake when worry, tension or poor sleep is the main complaint, or a GAD-2 (the first two GAD-7 items) score is 3 or more [Plummer-etal-2016].
- With frequent attenders who have chronic physical illness or seek repeated reassurance about symptoms [Kendall-etal-2011].

## Steps

1. **Map worry episodes.** Ask for the last two or three times worry took hold: trigger, the "what if" chain, how long it lasted, what the client did, and how it ended. "Talk me through the last time you couldn't switch the worry off."
2. **Check the diagnostic frame** [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. More days than not for 6 months or more? Several topics? Hard to control? How many of the six associated symptoms (restless, tired, poor concentration, irritable, tense, poor sleep)? Three are needed in adults, one in children. What has it cost in work, relationships and rest?
3. **Measure.** Use a screening and severity measure, a worry measure and, if useful, process measures (table below).
4. **Ask differential questions.**
   - "Are these thoughts about real-life matters, or do unwanted thoughts or images pop in that feel senseless, with an urge to undo them?" (obsessive-compulsive disorder, OCD)
   - "Is the fear mostly about having a serious illness?" (health anxiety)
   - "Do you get sudden surges of fear, and worry about the next one?" (panic)
   - "Is the worry mostly about what others think of you?" (social anxiety)
   - "Does it go back to something frightening that happened?" (posttraumatic stress disorder)
   - "Is it more going over the past and feeling low?" (depression)
5. **Screen medical and substance factors.** Ask about caffeine, stimulants, alcohol, sedatives and withdrawal, thyroid problems and new physical symptoms. The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) asks for assessment of substance misuse and medical conditions [Kendall-etal-2011]. Refer to the physician when onset is late, sudden or atypical (see `12-medical-pharmacology.medical-mimics`).
6. **Screen comorbidity and risk.** Ask about depression, substance use and suicidal thoughts (see `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`).
7. **Gather formulation data.** Ask "What are the advantages of worrying?" and "What would happen if you couldn't stop?" Note reassurance-seeking, checking, over-preparing, procrastination and avoidance.
8. **Collateral, with consent.** A partner or parent can describe reassurance-seeking and family accommodation. For children, parent report is essential.
9. **Baseline monitoring.** Start a daily worry log (time spent worrying, topics, distress 0 to 100).

| Measure | What it gives | Cutoffs and change |
|---|---|---|
| GAD-7 (7 items, 0 to 21, free) | Screening and severity | 5, 10, 15 = mild, moderate, severe; 10 or more gave sensitivity 89% and specificity 82% [Spitzer-etal-2006]. Pooled data: 8 gave 0.83 and 0.84, and 7 to 10 performed similarly [Plummer-etal-2016]. England's National Health Service (NHS) Talking Therapies: caseness 8 or more; reliable change over 3.53, so 4 points [Gyani-etal-2013] |
| PSWQ (16 items, 16 to 80) | Trait worry, the core target [Meyer-etal-1990] | 65 separated GAD from social anxiety [Fresco-etal-2003]. Trials used recovery cutoffs of 47 or 53 and a 7-point reliable change [AfWinklerfeltHammarberg-etal-2023] |
| Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAM-A), clinician | Severity in drug trials | 0 to 7 minimal, 8 to 14 mild, 15 to 23 moderate, 24 or more severe; validate before relying on it [Matza-etal-2010] |
| Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale-12 (IUS-12) | Intolerance of uncertainty | Short form of the 27-item scale with two stable factors; no agreed cutoff [Carleton-etal-2007] |
| Metacognitions Questionnaire-30 (MCQ-30) | Positive and negative beliefs about worry | Five subscales; no agreed cutoff [Wells-CartwrightHatton-2004] |

Get official GAD-7 copies from the publisher site [PHQ-Screeners]. A structured interview such as the Anxiety Disorders Interview Schedule (ADIS) gives a clinician severity rating from 0 to 8 [Dugas-etal-2010] (see `01-intake.structured-interviews`).

## What to record

- Worry topics, daily time spent worrying, onset and duration, controllability.
- Associated symptoms counted against criteria, and impairment by domain.
- Scores with dates: GAD-7, PSWQ, Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) for depression.
- Differentials considered and why ruled in or out; medical and substance findings.
- Beliefs about worry, safety behaviors, avoidance.
- Risk screen result and actions taken.

## Guidance

- If worry topics are all about one theme, such as illness or evaluation → assess the specific disorder first. Because DSM-5-TR excludes worry better explained by another disorder [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- If the client presents mainly with dizziness, stomach upset or palpitations → ask about worry directly and do not discount somatic expression. Because somatic presentations of pathological worry are common in many cultural groups [Lewis-Fernandez-etal-2010].
- If the GAD-7 is high but the PSWQ is low → look for panic, social anxiety or trauma. Because the GAD-7 also screens well for panic, social anxiety and posttraumatic stress disorder [Kroenke-etal-2007].
- If the client says "Everyone in my family worries" → ask about control, time and cost rather than debating normality. Because difficulty controlling worry and impairment define the disorder [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- If substance misuse is harmful or dependent → plan to treat it first. Because NICE advises treating harmful or dependent substance misuse before GAD [Kendall-etal-2011].

## Common problems

- Accepting "I worry about everything" without episodes: ask for one recent, specific episode.
- Missing comorbid depression because anxiety dominates the interview.
- Treating realistic, current problems as excessive worry: separate them, since current problems call for problem solving.
- Forgetting that clients see worry as useful, so they rarely call it "excessive".
