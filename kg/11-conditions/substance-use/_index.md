# 11-conditions/substance-use: Substance Use Disorders

## This surface
Alcohol and drug use disorders in a cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) context, from screening and diagnosis to motivational interviewing, skills-based CBT, medication alongside therapy, and relapse prevention. The pages follow the episode of care: assessment and risk checks at intake, motivation and a functional analysis for formulation, the skills protocol and Marlatt's relapse prevention model for treatment and ending, and adaptations throughout. For ambivalence in any disorder, see `responding-in-session.responding-to-ambivalence`.

## Pages in order
1. `substance-use.overview`: confirms what a substance use disorder is in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11, how common it is, and the risks.
2. `substance-use.assessment`: screening, severity measures with cutoffs, withdrawal and overdose checks, and what to record.
3. `substance-use.motivational-interviewing`: building commitment before and during skills work.
4. `substance-use.cbt-model`: the shared functional analysis that sets treatment targets.
5. `substance-use.treatment`: the 12-session skills protocol, doses, evidence and guideline status.
6. `substance-use.addiction-medications`: how medication fits with CBT, and when to suggest a medical review.
7. `substance-use.relapse-prevention-model`: anticipating and managing lapses once change has started.
8. `substance-use.adaptations`: changes for comorbidity, severity, age, culture, medication and non-response.

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Confronting Denial and Using Labels | The therapist argues the client has a problem, calls it denial or uses labels like addict, the client defends use harder, and sessions become debates or end in dropout. | `substance-use.pitfall-confrontation-and-labels` |
| Missing Withdrawal and Overdose Risk | A therapist encourages a heavy daily drinker or sedative user to stop abruptly, or celebrates opioid abstinence without warning about lost tolerance, and the client has withdrawal seizures or overdoses. | `substance-use.pitfall-missed-withdrawal-and-overdose-risk` |
| Treating a Lapse as Failure | After a slip, the client hides it or gives up, the therapist shows disappointment, resets the count or ends treatment, and one episode of use grows into full relapse. | `substance-use.pitfall-lapse-treated-as-failure` |
| Waiting for Sobriety Before Treating Comorbidity | A client with trauma, depression or anxiety plus substance use is told to get sober first, bounces between mental health and addiction services, and neither problem gets treated. | `substance-use.pitfall-waiting-for-sobriety` |
