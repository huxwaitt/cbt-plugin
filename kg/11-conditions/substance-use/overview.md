**Bottom line:** A substance use disorder (SUD) in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) is a problem pattern of use shown by at least 2 of 11 criteria within 12 months, graded mild (2 to 3), moderate (4 to 5) or severe (6 or more) [Hasin-etal-2013; APA-DSM5TR-2022]. In 2024, 48.4 million people aged 12 or older in the United States (US), 16.8 percent, had one [SAMHSA-NSDUH-2024]. Recovery is common, and cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is one of several guideline-supported psychosocial treatments [Kelly-etal-2017b; VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].

## Diagnostic essentials

DSM-5-TR, paraphrased [APA-DSM5TR-2022; Hasin-etal-2013]:

- **One diagnosis per substance class**, such as alcohol, cannabis, opioid or stimulant use disorder. DSM-5 merged "abuse" and "dependence", dropped legal problems and added craving.
- **Eleven criteria in four groups:** impaired control (more or longer than intended, failed efforts to cut down, much time spent, craving); social impairment (role failure, use despite relationship problems, activities given up); risky use (use in physically hazardous situations, use despite known physical or psychological harm); and pharmacological signs (tolerance, withdrawal).
- **Threshold:** 2 or more criteria in the same 12 months. Severity is the criterion count.
- **Medication exception:** tolerance and withdrawal that occur only with medication taken as prescribed under medical supervision do not count.
- **Specifiers:** early remission (at least 3 but under 12 months with no criteria met except craving); sustained remission (12 months or more, craving allowed); in a controlled environment; on maintenance therapy (opioid and tobacco use disorders only).
- Intoxication, withdrawal and substance-induced disorders are separate diagnoses.

**International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11)** splits the field differently [WHO-CDDR-2024]:
- **Harmful pattern of use:** use that has damaged physical or mental health, or behavior while intoxicated that harmed others. The pattern lasts at least 12 months if episodic or 1 month if continuous.
- **Dependence:** impaired regulation of use shown by 2 or more of 3 features (impaired control; increasing precedence over other parts of life; physiological features such as tolerance or withdrawal). Usually evident over 12 months, or 3 months if use is daily or almost daily.
- **Alcohol remission:** early full (abstinent 1 to 12 months), sustained partial (reduced use beyond 12 months) and sustained full (abstinent 12 months or more).
- ICD-11 has no "mild" grade. Clients with harm but no dependence get "harmful pattern". In the United Kingdom (UK), the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) uses "harmful drinking" and "dependence" in the same sense [NICE-CG115].

## Course and prevalence

- **US, 2024 survey:** 27.9 million had alcohol use disorder and 28.2 million a drug use disorder. Only 3.5 percent of people aged 12 or older received substance use treatment; 18.2 percent needed it [SAMHSA-NSDUH-2024].
- **US, 2012 to 2013 interview survey:** alcohol use disorder 13.9 percent past year and 29.1 percent lifetime, with only 19.8 percent ever treated; drug use disorder 3.9 and 9.9 percent [Grant-etal-2015; Grant-etal-2016]. Surveys differ in method, so name the source when quoting a rate.
- **Onset:** median age 20 for DSM-IV substance use disorders [Kessler-etal-2005c].
- **Recovery:** 9.1 percent of US adults say they once had an alcohol or drug problem and no longer do; 53.9 percent of them used treatment, mutual help or recovery services, and the rest recovered without them [Kelly-etal-2017b].

## Differential and comorbidity

Of 46.3 million US adults with a past-year SUD, 21.2 million also had a mental illness [SAMHSA-NSDUH-2024]. Alcohol use disorder is linked with other SUDs, depression, bipolar I disorder, and antisocial and borderline personality disorders [Grant-etal-2015]; drug use disorder also with posttraumatic stress disorder [Grant-etal-2016].

| Question | What it decides |
|---|---|
| Did mood or anxiety symptoms start before regular use, or persist after weeks of abstinence? | Independent disorder versus substance-induced; NICE reassesses after 3 to 4 weeks without alcohol [NICE-CG115] |
| Are symptoms intoxication or withdrawal? | Anxiety, insomnia and agitation in withdrawal are not a separate disorder |
| Is the drug prescribed and taken as directed? | Tolerance and withdrawal alone do not make an SUD [Hasin-etal-2013] |
| Psychosis, mania or cognitive decline? | Medical and psychiatric review first |

## CBT approach in brief

- Engage with motivational interviewing, then build a shared functional analysis of triggers, beliefs, cravings and consequences (`substance-use.cbt-model`).
- NICE offers CBT among psychological therapies for harmful drinking and mild dependence [NICE-CG115]. The US Veterans Affairs and Defense (VA/DoD) guideline lists CBT for alcohol, cannabis and cocaine use disorders, and contingency management for methamphetamine [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021]. See `substance-use.treatment`.

## Risk notes

- In cohort studies, suicide was about 10 times expected with alcohol use disorder and 13 to 17 times expected with opioid, injection and mixed drug use [Wilcox-etal-2004].
- Overdose risk spikes after a period of abstinence, because tolerance is lost. In one cohort, all three overdose deaths in the 4 months after inpatient opioid detoxification were in people who had completed it [Strang-etal-2003].
- Alcohol and sedative withdrawal can cause seizures and delirium [ASAM-Alcohol-Withdrawal-2020].

- If a client describes suicidal thoughts or an overdose that "might not have been an accident" → assess suicide risk that session (`02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`). Because SUDs multiply suicide risk [Wilcox-etal-2004].
- If risk is imminent or an overdose is happening → call 911 (UK: 999), stay with the client and give naloxone if available; for crisis support use 988 (call or text) in the US, or NHS 111 (National Health Service) and Samaritans 116 123 in the UK. Because these need emergency care, not routine CBT.
- If a client plans to stop alcohol or sedatives suddenly after heavy daily use → arrange medical assessment before they stop. Because withdrawal seizures and delirium can be fatal [ASAM-Alcohol-Withdrawal-2020].
