**Bottom line:** Keep the core of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for substance use and change the order, intensity and partners. Treat co-occurring disorders alongside the substance use rather than waiting for sobriety, except that the United Kingdom's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises treating alcohol misuse first when depression or anxiety may be alcohol-related [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021; NICE-CG115]. Step up the setting when dependence is severe, support is thin or treatment is not working [NICE-CG115].

## The principle

Adapt in three directions: who else is involved (family, prescriber, psychiatrist, mutual help), how intensive care is (outpatient, intensive community, inpatient), and what is treated first. Keep the functional analysis and skills core in all versions.

## Guidance

**Comorbidity**
- If depression or anxiety co-occurs with alcohol misuse → treat the alcohol use first, and reassess the mood or anxiety after 3 to 4 weeks of abstinence. Because these often improve with abstinence [NICE-CG115].
- If the client cannot achieve abstinence, or mood symptoms are severe → treat both at once with combined CBT and motivational interviewing. Because combined treatment reduced drinking (g = 0.17) and depression (g = 0.27) versus usual care [Riper-etal-2014], and the United States (US) Veterans Affairs and Defense (VA/DoD) guideline says substance use should not block treatment of a co-occurring condition [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].
- If posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) co-occurs → offer integrated, exposure-based trauma treatment rather than postponing it. Because in veterans, 12 sessions of integrated prolonged exposure cut PTSD symptoms far more than relapse prevention alone (d = 1.4) with similar substance use gains [Back-etal-2019].
- If there is psychosis, bipolar disorder, another significant disorder or high suicide risk → involve a psychiatrist. Because NICE asks for psychiatric assessment and risk planning [NICE-CG115].
- If alcohol and opioids are both used → treat both actively and warn about combined overdose risk. Because NICE notes the higher mortality of the combination [NICE-CG115].

**Severity and setting**
- If dependence is moderate to severe with poor support, complex comorbidity or no response to community care → move to an intensive structured community program. Because NICE recommends this step [NICE-CG115].
- If the client is homeless and alcohol-dependent → consider residential rehabilitation for up to 3 months. Because NICE recommends it [NICE-CG115].
- If outcome measures show deterioration or no improvement → stop and review the care plan. Because NICE requires outcome monitoring to guide continuation [NICE-CG115].

**Age**
- If the client is aged 10 to 17 → usually aim for abstinence first; offer individual CBT if comorbidity is limited and support is good, or a family-based multicomponent program if not. Because NICE recommends this split [NICE-CG115].
- If a child aged 10 to 15 has alcohol-related problems → refer to specialist child and adolescent mental health services. Because NICE recommends comprehensive specialist assessment [NICE-CG115].
- If the client is an older adult → lower the Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test (AUDIT) cutoff to 7 and the threshold for supervised withdrawal. Because a cutoff of 7 raises sensitivity for women and men over 65 [Babor-etal-2001] and NICE lowers the inpatient threshold for older people [NICE-CG115].

**Culture, identity and stigma**
- If the client is from a group with poorer access to care → ask directly about barriers and past experiences of services. Because racial and ethnic minorities face more consequences and less access to evidence-based treatment at similar prevalence [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].
- If community norms or rates differ (lifetime alcohol use disorder was 43.4 percent among Native American respondents in one US survey [Grant-etal-2015]) → explore what use means to the client and which community resources fit, without assuming. Because group rates do not describe the individual.
- If writing notes or letters → use person-first terms ("person with alcohol use disorder"). Because clinicians rated a "substance abuser" as more blameworthy and deserving punishment than a person with an identical "substance use disorder" [Kelly-Westerhoff-2010].

**Medication**
- If the client takes opioid agonist or alcohol medication and uses another substance → keep the medication, and tell the prescriber. Because VA/DoD advises that such use should not automatically end medication treatment [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].
- If a client wants to stop prescribed benzodiazepines → route it through the prescriber as a gradual taper. Because VA/DoD strongly recommends gradual tapering [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].

**Relationships and support**
- If there is a willing partner → offer behavioral couples therapy, unless there is current domestic abuse. Because NICE recommends it with that exclusion [NICE-CG115].
- If the client is in early recovery or has relapsed → actively link to mutual help (peer linkage, network support or twelve-step facilitation). Because VA/DoD recommends this [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].

**Cognitive limits and literacy**
- If the client has memory or literacy problems → simplify forms, use phone or verbal check-ins for homework, and repeat skills across sessions. Because written homework may need these alternatives [McHugh-etal-2010].

## Example phrasings

- "Your mood and your drinking are tangled together. We'll work on both, and in a few weeks we'll see what the low mood looks like without alcohol."
- "Would it help to bring your partner to one session, so they know what you're working on?"
- Avoid: "Come back when you've been sober for a month." It sends away the people at highest risk.

## Evidence

NICE and VA/DoD disagree on sequencing [NICE-CG115; VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021]: NICE treats alcohol misuse first for depression and anxiety, while VA/DoD favors concurrent care. Trial support for integrated care comes from a meta-analysis of 12 studies with 1,721 patients [Riper-etal-2014] and one trial in 81 veterans [Back-etal-2019]. Other rules here are guideline consensus.
