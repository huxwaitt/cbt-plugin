**Bottom line:** Co-occurrence is common: 21.2 million adults in the United States (US) had both a mental illness and a substance use disorder in 2024 [SAMHSA-NSDUH-2024]. Making sobriety a condition of mental health care, or the reverse, leaves both untreated. The US Veterans Affairs and Defense (VA/DoD) guideline says substance use should not block treatment of co-occurring conditions [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021], and integrated trauma treatment works during active substance use disorder [Back-etal-2019]. The one sequencing rule with support is narrower: reassess alcohol-related depression and anxiety after 3 to 4 weeks of abstinence [NICE-CG115].

## What it looks like

- A referral letter says "Treat the drinking first, then refer back for trauma therapy."
- The addiction service says the client's posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) "is a mental health issue", and the mental health service says the client "must be abstinent".
- Exposure therapy is never offered because the client still drinks.
- The client uses substances to cope with symptoms nobody is treating, so the substance use does not improve either.

## Why it happens

- Fear that trauma work will trigger relapse.
- Separate services, funding and training for addiction and mental health.
- Over-reading a real rule: the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) says alcohol-dependent people need abstinence or much-reduced drinking to benefit from psychological treatment for comorbid disorders [NICE-CG115], which is often taken to mean "no treatment until sober".

## The fix

- If PTSD co-occurs with substance use → offer integrated, exposure-based treatment now. Because in veterans, integrated prolonged exposure reduced PTSD far more than relapse prevention (d = 1.4) with no worse substance outcomes and fewer drinks per drinking day at 6 months [Back-etal-2019].
- If depression co-occurs with drinking → combine cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) and motivational interviewing. Because combined treatment reduced both drinking and depression versus usual care [Riper-etal-2014].
- If mood or anxiety symptoms may be alcohol-induced → monitor them, and reassess after 3 to 4 weeks without alcohol. Because many improve with abstinence, and persistent symptoms then need their own treatment [NICE-CG115].
- If the client uses cannabis or stimulants and has depression or anxiety → offer CBT for the depression or anxiety. Because NICE drug guidance says to consider evidence-based therapy, in particular CBT, for these comorbid disorders [NICE-CG51].
- If the client uses more than one substance → treat each, and keep medication for one going despite use of another. Because VA/DoD advises against stopping effective treatment for that reason [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].
- If you cannot provide both → coordinate actively with the other service and agree who does what, in writing.

Example phrasings:
- "Your drinking and your nightmares feed each other. We'll work on both, starting now."
- Avoid: "Come back when you've been sober for 3 months." Many never come back.

## Sources note

The guidelines disagree on emphasis: NICE treats alcohol misuse first for depression and anxiety and reassesses [NICE-CG115], while VA/DoD favors concurrent care [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021]. Trial evidence for integration comes from one randomized trial of 81 veterans [Back-etal-2019] and a meta-analysis of 12 studies [Riper-etal-2014].
