**Bottom line:** Screen briefly, then measure consumption, count the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) criteria per substance, and check withdrawal and overdose risk before anything else [Hasin-etal-2013; NICE-CG115]. The Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test (AUDIT) gives risk zones and guides referral [Babor-etal-2001]. A history of withdrawal seizures or delirium, or heavy daily alcohol or sedative use, means medical assessment before the client stops [ASAM-Alcohol-Withdrawal-2020].

## When to use

At intake for every adult, when use seems to interfere with therapy, and before treating depression, anxiety, trauma or sleep problems, which use can mimic or maintain.

## Steps

1. **Screen.** Alcohol: the 3-item AUDIT-Consumption (AUDIT-C), positive at 4 or more for men and 3 or more for women [Bradley-etal-2007]; the United States (US) Veterans Affairs and Defense (VA/DoD) health systems use 5 or more [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021]. A single question also works: any day in the past year with 5 or more drinks (men) or 4 or more (women) [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021]. Drugs: ask how many times in the past year the client used an illegal drug or a prescription medicine for non-medical reasons; 1 or more is positive, and this was 100 percent sensitive for a drug use disorder [Smith-etal-2010].
2. **Adolescents (12 to 21).** Use the CRAFFT 2.1; 2 or more "yes" answers in Part B is positive, and the chance of a DSM-5 substance use disorder (SUD) was 64 percent at 2 and 92 percent at 4 [CRAFFT-Site].
3. **Full AUDIT** if alcohol screens positive. Zones: 0 to 7 education, 8 to 15 simple advice, 16 to 19 brief counseling and monitoring, 20 to 40 specialist referral. The manual suggests a cutoff of 7 for women and men over 65 [Babor-etal-2001].
4. **Consumption.** For each substance: amount, frequency, route, time of last use, pattern over the past 30 to 90 days on a calendar, and use of more than one depressant at once.
5. **Criteria count.** Ask about each DSM-5-TR criterion per substance; 2 or more in 12 months is a disorder, and the count sets severity [Hasin-etal-2013].
6. **Dependence and withdrawal.** The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) uses the Severity of Alcohol Dependence Questionnaire (SADQ) or Leeds Dependence Questionnaire (LDQ) for dependence and the Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment for Alcohol, revised (CIWA-Ar) for withdrawal [NICE-CG115]. Ask about past seizures, delirium, many prior withdrawals, and sedative dependence [ASAM-Alcohol-Withdrawal-2020].
7. **Risk.** Suicide (see `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`), overdose, driving, children in the home, domestic abuse, and neglect; NICE lists unplanned withdrawal as a risk to self [NICE-CG115].
8. **Timeline.** Which came first, the use or the mood, anxiety or trauma symptoms, and what happened during past periods of abstinence.
9. **Goals and readiness.** Abstinence, reduction or undecided; confidence and importance on 0 to 10 scales.
10. **Level of care.** Summarize needs across the six American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM) Criteria dimensions to judge outpatient versus more intensive care [ASAM-Criteria-Site].

## What to record

- Screen and AUDIT scores with date, and the cutoff used.
- Per substance: quantity, frequency, route, last use, criteria met and severity.
- Withdrawal history and current withdrawal signs; whether medical assessment was arranged.
- Risk findings and actions, including naloxone access for anyone using opioids.
- Agreed goal, readiness ratings and who else is involved.
- Consent for any contact with family, prescribers or programs.

## Guidance

- If the client drinks over 15 United Kingdom (UK) units a day or scores 20 or more on the AUDIT → arrange assessment for assisted withdrawal. Because NICE sets this threshold [NICE-CG115].
- If the client drinks over 30 units a day, scores over 30 on the SADQ, or has had withdrawal seizures or delirium → refer for inpatient or residential withdrawal. Because NICE lists these as criteria for that setting [NICE-CG115].
- If the client is shaky or sweating in session after cutting down → arrange same-day medical assessment; if confused, hallucinating or seizing → call 911 (UK: 999). Because a CIWA-Ar of 10 or more is at least moderate withdrawal, and seizures, delirium or new hallucinations mark complicated withdrawal [ASAM-Alcohol-Withdrawal-2020].
- If suicide risk is imminent or an overdose is under way → stay with the client and call 911 (UK: 999); crisis line 988, call or text (UK: Samaritans 116 123). Because this needs emergency care, not cognitive behavioral therapy.
- If the client's goal is moderation but dependence is severe or there is serious comorbidity → advise abstinence, but do not refuse treatment. Because NICE says exactly this [NICE-CG115].
- If a young person aged 10 to 16 screens positive → use a lower AUDIT threshold. Because a given amount of alcohol does more harm at that age [NICE-CG115].
- If you want family input or records → get written consent; records from a federally assisted US addiction program fall under 42 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Part 2. Because that rule restricts their use and disclosure [CFR-42-2-12]; its 2024 revision applies from February 16, 2026 [HHS-Part2-Final-Rule-2024].
- If the client minimizes use → reflect, and ask permission to share screening results. Because NICE builds a motivational approach into assessment [NICE-CG115].

Example phrasings:
- "Walk me through a typical week. When is the first drink, and how many after that?"
- Avoid: "Are you an alcoholic?" Labels invite defense and give you no usable data.

## Common problems

- **Asking only about alcohol.** Ask about each class, including prescribed opioids, sedatives and stimulants.
- **Accepting "socially".** Turn it into numbers on a calendar.
- **Missing withdrawal.** Morning anxiety, insomnia and shakes may be withdrawal, not an anxiety disorder.
