**Bottom line:** Motivational interviewing (MI) is a collaborative way of talking that draws out the client's own reasons to change. In substance use care it opens assessment, stands alone as motivational enhancement therapy (MET), and serves as the front end of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) [NICE-CG115; VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021]. It reduces use compared with no treatment, mainly in the short term, and is no better than usual care or other active treatments [Schwenker-etal-2023]. The skill that matters most is evoking change talk and not provoking sustain talk [Magill-etal-2018].

## When to use

- At every first assessment for alcohol misuse: the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) asks for a motivational intervention that helps the client see problems, resolves ambivalence, and is supportive rather than argumentative [NICE-CG115].
- When the client is unsure, has been sent by someone else, or talks mostly about reasons not to change.
- During CBT, whenever homework, attendance or goals slip.

## How it works

MI has a relational part (empathy and a partnership spirit) and a technical part: the therapist selectively evokes and reflects the client's change talk [Miller-Rose-2009]. In a meta-analysis of 36 studies, MI-consistent therapist behavior went with more client change talk (r = .55) but also more sustain talk (r = .40), MI-inconsistent behavior went with more sustain talk (r = .16), and more sustain talk predicted worse outcome (r = .19) [Magill-etal-2018].

## Steps

The fourth edition of the MI text names four tasks [Miller-Rollnick-2023]:

1. **Engaging.** Build the working relationship with open questions, affirmations, reflections and summaries.
2. **Focusing.** Agree what to talk about: the drinking, the cocaine, or the problems the client links to them.
3. **Evoking.** Draw out the client's own desire, ability, reasons and need to change. Ask the importance and confidence rulers: "On 0 to 10, how important is cutting down? Why a 5 and not a 2?"
4. **Planning.** When change talk outweighs sustain talk, build a specific plan and commitment.

**MET format.** Usually four 60-minute sessions built around personalized feedback from assessment, with a significant other invited to at least one [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].

**Joining MI to CBT.**
- Sessions 1 and 2: MET-style feedback and goal setting. Adding motivational work early in CBT can raise motivation and retention [Carroll-Kiluk-2017].
- Sessions 3 onward: CBT skills, with MI style throughout.
- Whenever sustain talk rises: pause the skill, return to evoking.

## Guidance

- If the client says "I don't drink more than my friends" → reflect, then ask permission before offering information. Because arguing raises resistance [Miller-etal-1993].
- If the client gives change talk ("I hate how I feel on Mondays") → reflect it and ask for more. Because change talk is the mechanism MI targets [Miller-Rose-2009].
- If the client is not ready to stop → agree to monitoring or a reduction trial. Because NICE says not to refuse treatment to people who will not accept abstinence [NICE-CG115].
- If depression co-occurs with drinking → combine CBT and MI. Because the combination reduced both drinking (g = 0.17) and depression (g = 0.27) [Riper-etal-2014].
- If you notice yourself listing reasons the client should quit → stop and ask the client for theirs. Because confrontation predicted more drinking a year later [Miller-etal-1993].

Example phrasings:
- "Part of you enjoys the weekend drinking, and part of you is worried about what it's doing to your job."
- "What would need to happen for this to move from a 4 to a 6?"
- Avoid: "You're in denial." The label provokes sustain talk, which predicts worse outcomes.

## Common problems

- **Questions without reflections.** Aim for more reflections than questions.
- **Rushing to planning.** A plan made before change talk dominates rarely sticks.
- **MI only in session 1.** Ambivalence returns; the style continues throughout.
- **Reflecting sustain talk alone.** Use a double-sided reflection that ends with the change side.

## Evidence

- **Cochrane review update, 93 trials, 22,776 participants:** compared with no intervention, MI reduced substance use right after treatment (standardized mean difference 0.48) and less at short and medium follow-up (0.20 and 0.12), with no clear effect at long follow-up. It made little or no difference compared with usual care or other active treatments, and did slightly better than assessment and feedback alone at medium and long follow-up (0.24). Certainty was mostly low or very low [Schwenker-etal-2023].
- **Brief format:** MET over 3 to 4 sessions gave benefits comparable to 8 to 12 sessions of CBT or twelve-step facilitation for alcohol use disorder [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].
- **Counselor style:** in a trial of 42 problem drinkers, a confrontational style produced more resistance, and the more the therapist confronted, the more the client drank at one year (r = .65) [Miller-etal-1993].
- Guideline status: NICE recommends a motivational intervention at assessment for all alcohol misuse [NICE-CG115]; the United States Veterans Affairs and Defense (VA/DoD) guideline lists MET among options for alcohol and cannabis use disorders [VA-DoD-SUD-CPG-2021].
