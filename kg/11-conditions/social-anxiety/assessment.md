**Bottom line:** Screen with the three-item Mini Social Phobia Inventory (Mini-SPIN, positive at 6 or more) or two plain questions about avoidance and embarrassment, then assess fear, avoidance, distress, impairment and comorbidity in a full interview [NICE-CG159] [Connor-etal-2001]. Measure severity every session with the Social Phobia Inventory (SPIN, clinical cutoff 19) or the Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale (LSAS, cutoff 30) [Connor-etal-2000] [Mennin-etal-2002]. The core question for diagnosis is what the person fears others will think.

## When to use

At intake when a client describes shyness, avoidance of people, dread of speaking, eating or being watched, or screens positive for anxiety. People with social anxiety disorder (SAD) may come asking for help with low mood, drinking or work problems, so ask about social fears directly (expert consensus).

## Steps

1. **Make the first contact easy.** The UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises flexible times, a private place to wait, written information beforehand, few changes of clinician, and a first contact by phone or internet if attending is too hard [NICE-CG159]. Contact anyone who does not return after a first assessment to ask why [NICE-CG159].
2. **Screen.** Use the Mini-SPIN or two questions: does the person avoid social situations or activities, and do they feel fearful or embarrassed in them? A Mini-SPIN of 6 or more, or yes to either question, means a full assessment [NICE-CG159]. In a US managed care sample, a Mini-SPIN of 6 had 88.7% sensitivity and 90.0% specificity, but a positive predictive value of only 52.5%, so a positive screen is not a diagnosis [Connor-etal-2001].
3. **Interview.** Cover fear, avoidance, distress and functional impairment across situations: conversations, groups, strangers, authority figures, eating or writing while watched, speaking up and performing [NICE-CG159]. Trials confirm diagnosis with a structured interview such as the Anxiety Disorders Interview Schedule (ADIS) [Clark-etal-2023].
4. **Take a recent example in detail.** Ask what went through their mind, what image they had of how they looked, where their attention went, what they did to stop the feared outcome, and how they felt before and after (see `social-anxiety.cbt-model`).
5. **Check comorbidity and risk.** Look for depression, other anxiety disorders, avoidant personality disorder, alcohol or drug misuse, psychosis and autism [NICE-CG159]. Screen for suicide risk.
6. **Measure severity.** Choose one primary measure and repeat it every session [NICE-CG159]:
   - **SPIN:** 17 self-rated items covering fear, avoidance and physical symptoms; a score of 19 separated people with SAD from controls [Connor-etal-2000]. Items are rated 0 to 4, so totals run 0 to 68 [Wiltink-etal-2017]. England's National Health Service (NHS) Talking Therapies uses 19 as its caseness threshold [Clark-etal-2023].
   - **LSAS:** 24 situations, each rated for fear and for avoidance on 0 to 3 scales, total 0 to 144 [Caballo-etal-2019]. A total of 30 best separated SAD from no SAD and 60 marked the broad (generalized) form, in clinician and self-report versions [Mennin-etal-2002] [Rytwinski-etal-2009].
   - **Social Interaction Anxiety Scale and Social Phobia Scale:** companion scales for interaction fears and fears of being watched while eating, drinking or writing [Mattick-Clarke-1998].
7. **Add process measures if you will deliver cognitive therapy for SAD.** The Social Cognitions Questionnaire and Social Phobia Weekly Summary Scale are given each session; the Social Behaviour Questionnaire (safety behaviors) and Social Attitudes Questionnaire (beliefs) at the start, middle and end [Warnock-Parkes-etal-2020].

## What to record

- Feared situations ranked by fear and avoidance, and goals in the client's words.
- Feared outcomes, the self-image, safety behaviors, attention focus, and anticipatory and post-event thinking from one recent episode.
- Measure scores with dates, onset age, course, past treatment and medication.
- Comorbid conditions, which came first if depression is present, substance use, and the risk screen.
- Cultural context that shapes what counts as excessive [Hofmann-etal-2010].

## Guidance

- If the client avoids eye contact, speaks briefly or seems "uncooperative" → slow down, normalize nerves about meeting a stranger, and use questionnaires to let them disclose in writing. Because the clinician is initially a feared stranger (expert consensus).
- If the main fear is of panic sensations anywhere, not of judgment → assess panic disorder and agoraphobia. Because the focus of fear decides the diagnosis (expert consensus).
- If social difficulty has been lifelong and pervasive, with few friendships since childhood → also assess autism and avoidant personality disorder. Because NICE lists both as comorbidities to look for [NICE-CG159].
- If depression is present → ask which came first and whether the person would still be depressed if the social anxiety were gone. Because the answer decides which to treat first [NICE-CG159].
- If the client drinks before social events → assess whether the drinking mainly serves to manage anxiety, keep SAD treatment going, and refer harmful or dependent use to specialist services. Because NICE treats such use as often an attempt to manage social anxiety [NICE-CG159].
- If the client is a child or young person → interview them alone at some point, involve a parent or carer, and use the child LSAS or Social Phobia and Anxiety Inventory for Children (children) or the SPIN or LSAS (young people). Because NICE recommends these [NICE-CG159].

Example phrasing: "When you walked into that meeting, what were you afraid people would notice or think about you?"

## Common problems

- Treating shyness or introversion as disorder. Require distress or impairment and disproportionate fear.
- Accepting "I'm just not a people person" without asking what the client fears would happen.
- Measuring once at intake only. Session-by-session scores show whether treatment is working [NICE-CG159].
- Embedding copyrighted scales in software without checking terms. See `08-measurement.instrument-licensing`.
