**Bottom line:** Social anxiety disorder (SAD) is one of the few common disorders for which the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) recommends going straight to high-intensity individual therapy rather than stepped care [Warnock-Parkes-etal-2020]. NICE advises against routinely preferring group cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) and reserves supported self-help for people who decline CBT [NICE-CG159]. Offer the full individual protocol, or a proven internet version, unless the client chooses otherwise.

## What it looks like

- Every anxiety referral receives six 50-minute sessions or a psychoeducation class first.
- The client is placed in a mixed anxiety group, does not attend, and is discharged as "not engaging."
- Sessions are too short to run and review a behavioral experiment, so therapy drifts into talking about the week.
- Unsupported self-help books are offered as the whole treatment.

## Why it happens

Services are built around stepped care and standard session lengths. Cognitive therapy for SAD (CT-SAD) asks for up to 14 sessions of about 90 minutes, some out of the office, while routine services in England averaged 7.5 sessions [Clark-etal-2023]. Groups look efficient. Avoidance of group settings and of phone contact can be misread as low motivation.

## The fix

- If a pathway sends SAD to a low-intensity first step → offer individual CBT based on the Clark and Wells or Heimberg model. Because NICE recommends individual CBT as the first treatment for adults [NICE-CG159].
- If group CBT is the only option → tell the client about the difference in evidence and let them choose. Because individual CBT had a larger effect than group CBT against waitlist (standardized mean difference -1.19 vs -0.92) [Mayo-Wilson-etal-2014], and individual cognitive therapy outperformed intensive group treatment in a trial [Mortberg-etal-2007]. Meta-analyses differ on this, so present it honestly [Katzman-etal-2014].
- If therapist time is the constraint → consider internet CT-SAD with therapist support. Because it matched face-to-face CT-SAD on the primary outcome with 6.45 rather than 15.8 therapist hours [Clark-etal-2023].
- If sessions are capped at 60 minutes → book double sessions for the experiment, video feedback and out-of-office work. Because 90 minutes allows experiments to be set up, run and reviewed [Warnock-Parkes-etal-2020].
- If the client declines CBT → offer CBT-based supported self-help (up to 9 sessions over 3 to 4 months, about 3 hours of support). Because NICE recommends it for people who decline CBT [NICE-CG159].

Example phrasing: "The treatment with the best results for social anxiety is one-to-one and a bit longer than usual. It includes trying things out together, sometimes outside the office. How does that sound?"

## Sources note

Format recommendations are NICE guideline recommendations [NICE-CG159]. The comparison of individual and group formats rests on a network meta-analysis and one direct trial [Mayo-Wilson-etal-2014] [Mortberg-etal-2007]; the Canadian guideline reports that pairwise meta-analyses found no significant difference between formats [Katzman-etal-2014].
