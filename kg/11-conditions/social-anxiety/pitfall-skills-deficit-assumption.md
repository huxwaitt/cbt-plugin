**Bottom line:** Most apparent social skill problems in social anxiety disorder (SAD) are the visible side of safety behaviors and self-focus, not a lack of ability: treated adults do not show ongoing skills deficits, and performance problems tend to appear only when the person is anxious [Leigh-Clark-2018]. Teaching scripts gives the client more things to monitor and rehearse. Test what happens when the client drops the safety behaviors before deciding a skill is missing.

## What it looks like

- Sessions spent on "conversation openers", eye contact drills or prepared small-talk lists.
- The client memorizes topics, then reports feeling even more self-conscious, checking whether they used the techniques.
- The formulation reads "lacks social skills" with no mention of fear, images or safety behaviors.
- The client concludes, "I'm fundamentally bad with people," and belief in being inadequate rises.

## Why it happens

Safety behaviors look like deficits from the outside. Saying little, giving short answers, avoiding eye contact and mentally comparing each sentence with the last can make a person seem distant or uninterested [Warnock-Parkes-etal-2020]. Clients themselves read their own safety behaviors on video as proof of inadequacy [Warnock-Parkes-etal-2017]. Some models also include the client's belief that their skills are poor, which therapists can mistake for an actual deficit [Hofmann-2007]. Skills training feels concrete and safe for both therapist and client.

## The fix

- If the client seems socially awkward → ask what they were doing internally at the time ("Were you planning what to say? Watching how you came across?"). Because withdrawn behavior is often a deliberate strategy the client can drop [Warnock-Parkes-etal-2017].
- If you suspect a skill gap → first run a conversation with and without safety behaviors and compare. Because performance problems in SAD are largely tied to anxiety, not to missing knowledge [Leigh-Clark-2018].
- If the client asks for scripts → explore what they fear would happen without one and turn that into a prediction to test. Because a script is a safety behavior that blocks disconfirmation [Warnock-Parkes-etal-2020].
- If the client is autistic or has a language or developmental condition → assess communication differences directly and agree goals with the client. Because autism-specific factors can contribute alongside the Clark and Wells processes [Lei-etal-2024].
- If a genuine skill gap remains after safety behaviors are dropped (for example, no experience of job interviews) → teach the skill briefly as information, then test predictions with it. Because a missing skill and a fear can coexist (expert consensus).
- If the client is a child → include social skills training and rehearsal within treatment for social anxiety. Because the child protocols of the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) include it [NICE-CG159], and in pre-adolescents a review linked social anxiety disorder with real skills deficits [Leigh-Clark-2018].

Example phrasing: "On the video you were quieter than you'd like. Were you holding back on purpose? What were you worried would happen if you just said what came to mind?"

Avoid: "Let's work on your social skills so people respond better to you."

## Sources note

The claim that deficits reflect anxiety rather than ability rests on adult evidence reviewed by Leigh and Clark; in pre-adolescents a review found an association with skills deficits, and in adolescents the direction of cause is unclear [Leigh-Clark-2018]. The adult advice here does not override NICE's child protocols, which include skills training [NICE-CG159]. Remaining guidance is expert consensus based on the cognitive model.
