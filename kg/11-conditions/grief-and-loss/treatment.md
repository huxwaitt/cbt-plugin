**Bottom line:** For prolonged grief disorder (PGD), offer grief-focused cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) that includes exposure to the reality of the loss, work on beliefs, and re-engagement with life; prolonged grief disorder therapy (PGDT) has the largest trials, with 95, 151 and 395 adults [Shear-etal-2005; Shear-etal-2014; Shear-etal-2016]. Across 22 randomized trials, grief-focused CBT had a medium effect on grief (Hedges' g 0.65) that was larger at follow-up [Komischke-Konnerup-etal-2024]. Treat people with the disorder, not everyone bereaved: preventive interventions for unselected mourners were not effective [Wittouck-etal-2011].

## When to use

- PGD, or persistent impairing grief that exceeds the client's cultural norms.
- Comorbid depression or posttraumatic stress symptoms do not rule it out; grief-focused CBT also reduced them (g 0.53 and 0.74) [Komischke-Konnerup-etal-2024].
- Not for acute or culturally expected grief (`grief-and-loss.pitfall-treating-normal-grief`).

## How it works

Treatment targets the processes in `grief-and-loss.cbt-model`. Exposure to the story of the death and to avoided reminders helps the loss become real and tests catastrophic beliefs about grief; restructuring addresses beliefs about self, life, future and blame; activation and goal work rebuild a life [Boelen-etal-2006]. PGDT, formerly complicated grief treatment (CGT) [NSSF-PGDT-Course], pairs loss-focused and restoration-focused work in every session, following the dual process model of coping with bereavement [Shear-etal-2014; Stroebe-Schut-1999].

## Steps

PGDT runs 16 sessions over up to 20 weeks [Shear-etal-2016]:

1. **Sessions 1 to 3:** history, psychoeducation about grief and PGD, daily grief monitoring, starting work on personal (aspirational) goals, and one joint session with a significant other.
2. **Sessions 4 to 9:** imaginal revisiting (retelling the story of the death) and situational revisiting (graded return to avoided places, people and activities), plus work with memories and pictures.
3. **Session 10:** midcourse review.
4. **Sessions 11 to 16:** an imaginal conversation with the deceased, consolidating goals, and ending [Shear-etal-2016; Shear-etal-2014].

Boelen-style CBT used 12 sessions: 6 of exposure and 6 of cognitive restructuring, with exposure first working best [Boelen-etal-2007].

## Guidance

- If the client avoids the story of the death → make exposure central and start it early. Because exposure outperformed restructuring alone and exposure-first beat restructuring-first [Boelen-etal-2007]; adding exposure to group CBT cut PGD at 6 months from 37.9% to 14.8% of completers [Bryant-etal-2014].
- If the client fears that feeling grief fully will make them lose control → set up revisiting as a behavioral experiment that tests the prediction. Because catastrophic misinterpretation of grief reactions maintains avoidance [Boelen-etal-2006].
- If the client fears that recovery means forgetting the person → frame goals as carrying the relationship forward while rebuilding a life. Because PGDT pairs loss work with restoration work rather than ending the bond [Shear-etal-2014].
- If the imaginal conversation or revisiting feels too hard → prepare, pace and review the rationale. Because clients interviewed after PGDT described imaginal conversations as challenging but powerful, and revisiting and home practice as the hardest parts to engage with [King-etal-2025].
- If the client asks about medication → refer the question to the prescriber and do not present medication as a grief treatment. Because citalopram did not improve grief over placebo or add to CGT, though it reduced depressive symptoms when added [Shear-etal-2016], and no medication treats grief itself [APA-PGD-Site]. Only licensed prescribers decide on medication.

## Common problems

- **Sessions drift into support only.** See `grief-and-loss.pitfall-colluding-with-avoidance`.
- **Violent death with PTSD symptoms.** Screen for posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and integrate trauma-focused work (`ptsd.treatment`).
- **Suicidal thoughts emerge during revisiting.** Pause, assess and update the safety plan, including restricting access to lethal means, before continuing; for imminent danger, in the United States call or text 988 or call 911 [Lifeline-988], and in the United Kingdom (UK) call 999 or National Health Service (NHS) 111 [NHS-Urgent-Help] (`grief-and-loss.pitfall-missing-suicide-risk-in-grief`).

## Evidence

- CGT versus interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT): response 51% versus 28%, number needed to treat (NNT) 4.3, in 95 adults [Shear-etal-2005]; in 151 adults aged 50 or older, 70.5% versus 32.0%, NNT 2.56 [Shear-etal-2014].
- In 395 adults, CGT with placebo beat placebo alone (82.5% versus 54.8% response), and suicidal ideation fell more with CGT [Shear-etal-2016].
- In 54 mourners, both CBT sequences beat supportive counseling [Boelen-etal-2007].
- Meta-analysis: 22 trials, 2,602 bereaved adults; g 0.65 after treatment and 0.90 at follow-up (7 trials) [Komischke-Konnerup-etal-2024].
- UK: an NHS Talking Therapies pilot in Devon, England, audited PGDT in 80 clients and reported large pre-post effects (Cohen's d above 1.05) and 63% recovery on the Brief Grief Questionnaire, without a control group [Goff-etal-2025]; 11 clients interviewed afterwards found it acceptable [King-etal-2025].

Evidence strength: supported by randomized trials and meta-analysis.
