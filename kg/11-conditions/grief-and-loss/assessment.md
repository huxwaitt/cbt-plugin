**Bottom line:** Assess the loss and how it happened, the time since the death, what mourning is expected in the client's culture and faith, the prolonged grief disorder (PGD) symptom pattern, comorbid depression and posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), and suicide risk. Use the Prolonged Grief Disorder scale, revised (PG-13-R), or the Inventory of Complicated Grief (ICG) for a baseline and repeat it, but diagnose by interview, because a score cannot show whether grief exceeds the client's norms [Prigerson-etal-2021; WHO-ICD11-2022].

## When to use

- Grief is a presenting problem, or a death emerges during history-taking.
- Low mood, loneliness or avoidance began after a death.
- Before grief-focused treatment starts, and at regular intervals during it.

## Steps

1. **The loss.** Who died, when and how (expected, sudden, violent, by suicide), the relationship, and what went with them (roles, income, home). Unnatural losses carry much higher PGD rates [Djelantik-etal-2020].
2. **Time and norms.** Count the months since the death. The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) needs 12 months for adults and 6 for children and adolescents; the International Classification of Diseases, 11th revision (ICD-11) needs more than 6 months [Prigerson-etal-2021; WHO-ICD11-2022]. Ask what mourning looks like, and for how long, in the client's family, faith and community [Stelzer-etal-2020].
3. **Symptoms.** Ask about yearning and preoccupation, then the 8 DSM-5-TR symptoms (`grief-and-loss.overview`), rating how often each occurred in the last month. Ask what the client avoids and what they do to feel close to the person.
4. **Differentials.** Generalized loss of pleasure and withdrawal not tied to the deceased suggest depression; fear and reliving of the death suggest PTSD [Szuhany-etal-2021]. Ask about alcohol and drug use.
5. **Risk.** Ask directly about suicidal thoughts, including wishing to die to be with the person. If present, complete a full assessment (`02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`).
6. **Measure.** Give a grief scale and a depression measure.
7. **Share** a summary and agree whether this is grief to support, PGD to treat, or another disorder to treat first.

| Measure | Content | Scoring | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| PG-13-R | 13 items: loss and time-since-death questions, 10 symptom items rated 1 to 5, 1 impairment item | Symptom total 10 to 50; 30 or more indicates syndromal PGD, which with impairment marks a probable case; internal consistency 0.83 to 0.93 [Prigerson-etal-2021] | Free for clinical diagnosis and bereavement research; copyrighted, request from the developers [PG-13-R-Site] |
| ICG | 19 items rated 0 to 4, total 0 to 76 [Szuhany-etal-2021] | Above 25 marked greater impairment in the original study [Prigerson-etal-1995]; 30 or more is the usual research threshold [Szuhany-etal-2021] and was a trial entry criterion [Shear-etal-2014] | Obtain from the developers; item wording not reproduced here |
| Brief Grief Questionnaire (BGQ) | 5 items rated 0 to 2, total 0 to 10 | 8 or more probable PGD, 5 to 7 subthreshold [Szuhany-etal-2021] | Screening only |
| Structured Clinical Interview for Complicated Grief (SCI-CG) | 31-item clinician interview, total 31 to 93 | No cutoff; supports clinical judgment [Szuhany-etal-2021] | Clinician-administered |

## What to record

Date and circumstances of the death; the relationship; months since the loss; the client's cultural and religious mourning norms; symptom pattern and frequency; avoided reminders and ways of staying close; comorbid conditions; suicide questions asked, answers and actions; scale scores with dates; and the shared conclusion.

## Guidance

- If the death was less than 6 months ago, or less than 12 months for an adult under DSM-5-TR → do not diagnose PGD; offer support, monitor and screen again later. Because both systems set a minimum time since the death [Prigerson-etal-2021; WHO-ICD11-2022].
- If scores are high but the mourning matches the client's cultural or religious norms → do not diagnose. Because ICD-11 treats grief within a normative mourning period as normal bereavement [WHO-ICD11-2022].
- If the client describes bodily distress, emptiness, or seeing or hearing the deceased → ask what this means in their culture before scoring it. Because standard items can miss bodily distress after loss, and grief hallucinations are particularly common in Chinese samples [Stelzer-etal-2020].
- If the client says "I just want to be with him" → ask whether that includes thoughts of dying or ending their life, and if it does, assess fully and safety plan. Because PGD was linked to current and later suicidal ideation in the validation samples [Prigerson-etal-2021].
- If the client reports intent, a plan or imminent danger → do not leave them alone and contact emergency help: in the United States, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or call 911; in the United Kingdom, call 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans on 116 123. Because these are the 24-hour crisis routes in each country [Lifeline-988; NHS-Urgent-Help; Samaritans-Site].

Example phrasing: "In your family and faith, how would people expect you to be grieving at this point?"

## Common problems

- **Score taken as diagnosis.** Fix: confirm by interview, including timing and norms.
- **Only depression assessed.** Yearning is never asked about, so PGD is missed. Fix: ask about longing for the person specifically.
- **The death story floods the session.** Fix: take the outline now and pace detail, since treatment revisits it.
