**Bottom line:** Boelen and colleagues propose that grief becomes prolonged through three interacting processes: the loss is not integrated into autobiographical memory, global negative beliefs and catastrophic readings of grief reactions take hold, and anxious and depressive avoidance block both facing the loss and rebuilding life [Boelen-etal-2006]. Background factors such as the type of death or attachment style are thought to act through these processes, so the processes are the treatment targets [Boelen-etal-2006].

## Components

1. **Poor integration of the loss.** The new fact that the person is permanently gone is not joined to what the client knows about themselves, the deceased and the relationship. A sense of unrealness ("it feels as if she will walk in any minute") marks it. In 728 bereaved Turkish adults, unrealness was related to prolonged grief disorder (PGD) symptoms but not to posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) or depression [Keser-Boelen-2025].
2. **Negative global beliefs and misinterpretations.** Beliefs about self, life and future ("I am worthless without him", "my life is meaningless", "the future is hopeless"), and catastrophic readings of grief itself ("if I let myself feel this, I will lose control or go mad") [Keser-Boelen-2025].
3. **Anxious avoidance.** Staying away from places, people, objects and thoughts that confront the reality of the death, driven by the belief that facing it would be unbearable [Keser-Boelen-2025].
4. **Depressive avoidance.** Inactivity and withdrawal from social and other activities, driven by the belief that they no longer hold meaning or pleasure [Keser-Boelen-2025].

The two kinds of avoidance are separate, and each uniquely explained grief severity beyond depression and PTSD symptoms in 161 mourners [Boelen-vandenBout-2010].

## How it keeps the problem going

- Anxious avoidance keeps the client from processing the reality of the loss, so it stays unintegrated and feels unreal [Boelen-etal-2006].
- Catastrophic beliefs about grief ("feeling it fully will break me") drive more avoidance, so the belief is never tested.
- Depressive avoidance strips out reward, contact and new roles, which seems to confirm that life is meaningless.
- Rumination can act as avoidance. In 282 bereaved adults, experiential avoidance mediated the link between grief rumination and later complicated grief symptoms [Eisma-etal-2013].
- In the dual process model, healthy coping moves back and forth between loss-oriented work and restoration-oriented work, with breaks from both [Stroebe-Schut-1999]. Anxious avoidance blocks the first; depressive avoidance blocks the second.

## Drawing it with the client

Start from one recent hard moment and map it onto three boxes, each with an arrow back to "grief stays stuck":

| Box | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Unreal sense of the loss | "Are there moments it still does not feel real that she has died?" |
| Beliefs | "What does losing him say about you, your life and your future? What do you fear would happen if you let the grief in fully?" |
| Avoidance | "What do you stay away from because it hurts too much? What have you stopped doing since the death?" |

Rate key beliefs from 0 to 100 for later comparison. Separate grief from being stuck: "Missing him is not the problem we are treating. What we are looking at is what keeps the grief locked in the same place." Avoid: "You need to let go of her." It implies ending the bond and invites shame and dropout.

## Treatment targets it implies

| Process | Target | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Poor integration | Facing the reality of the death | Repeated exposure to the story of the death; exposure alone outperformed cognitive restructuring alone [Boelen-etal-2007] |
| Negative beliefs | Beliefs about self, life, future, blame, and grief itself | Cognitive restructuring and behavioral experiments |
| Anxious avoidance | Approach to avoided reminders, places and people | Graded exposure in real life |
| Depressive avoidance | Re-engagement in valued activity and relationships | Behavioral activation and goal work; in a small online trial, activation reduced grief versus a waiting list but 59% dropped out, and online exposure was better supported [Eisma-etal-2015] |

Use the formulation to choose the order: exposure first worked better than restructuring first [Boelen-etal-2007]. See `grief-and-loss.treatment` for the protocols.
