**Bottom line:** To assess posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), ask about traumatic events directly, using concrete examples, but take only the headline of each event. Screen briefly, confirm the diagnosis by interview, and rate severity with a validated checklist at baseline and every session. Record the index (worst) event, current danger, suicide risk, dissociation, substance use and complex PTSD features, because each changes the plan.

## When to use

- Intrusive memories, nightmares, avoidance, startle or hypervigilance, or unexplained physical symptoms with repeated health care use [NICE-NG116].
- After a major disaster (screen at 1 month) and for refugees and asylum seekers at high risk [NICE-NG116].
- Before trauma-focused treatment, then repeatedly (often every session) to track change [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023].

## Steps

1. **Set expectations.** "I'll ask what happened in a sentence or two. You don't need to give details today, and you can say 'pass'."
2. **Screen.** DSM-5 here is the 2013 Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fifth edition. The Primary Care PTSD Screen for DSM-5 (PC-PTSD-5) asks about lifetime trauma exposure, then 5 yes or no items about the past month. A cut-point of 4 balances false positives and false negatives; 3 maximized sensitivity in the validation study [PC-PTSD-5-Site] [Prins-etal-2016]. The United States (US) Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense (VA/DoD) guideline suggests it for screening [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023].
3. **Take a trauma history.** Ask about each type with examples: serious accidents, physical and sexual assault, childhood or domestic abuse, work-related exposure, serious illness, intensive care or childbirth, war, torture [NICE-NG116]. For several traumas, sketch a timeline. The Life Events Checklist for DSM-5 (LEC-5) is available paired with the PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5) [NCPTSD-PCL-5].
4. **Identify the index event.** Ask which event intrudes most now; treatment usually starts there [VA-CSP591-2018]. Check that it meets Criterion A (see `ptsd.overview`).
5. **Ask symptom questions** about re-experiencing, avoidance, arousal, dissociation, negative mood and thinking, and impairment [NICE-NG116]. "When the memory comes, does it feel like it is happening now?"
6. **Confirm the diagnosis** when it matters for care with a structured interview such as the Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for DSM-5 (CAPS-5) [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023]. CAPS-5 diagnosis showed strong interrater reliability (kappa .78 to 1.00) [Weathers-etal-2018].
7. **Rate severity.** The PCL-5 has 20 items scored 0 to 4, total 0 to 80. Scores of 31 to 33 indicate probable PTSD [Bovin-etal-2016] [NCPTSD-PCL-5]. A provisional diagnosis counts items rated 2 or more: at least 1 intrusion (items 1 to 5), 1 avoidance (6 to 7), 2 cognition and mood (8 to 14) and 2 arousal (15 to 20) [NCPTSD-PCL-5]. Use the past-month version at baseline and the past-week version weekly. A 10-point drop suggests response, and a score below 28 suggests clinically significant change [NCPTSD-PCL-5].
8. **Check for complex PTSD** with the International Trauma Questionnaire (ITQ): 12 symptom items plus impairment items, free, scored by its diagnostic algorithm, not by cutoffs [ITQ-Site] [Cloitre-etal-2018].
9. **Assess risk and complicating factors:** suicide and self-harm, harm to others, ongoing threat (for example, an abusive partner), substance use, depression, psychosis, head injury and medical problems [NICE-NG116].

## What to record

- Index event in one line (type, date, age), the Criterion A basis, and the timeline of other traumas.
- Symptom profile, impairment, and scores with version and time frame (PCL-5 past month or past week; CAPS-5; ITQ).
- Dissociation, current danger, suicide and violence risk, substance use, comorbid diagnoses.
- Legal proceedings or compensation claims, and the client's preferences, such as therapist gender [NICE-NG116].
- Avoided situations, main appraisals (blame, shame, current danger) and safety behaviors, for the formulation.

## Guidance

- If a screen is positive → do a full assessment before diagnosing. Because no screening measure or cutoff should be the sole basis for diagnosis [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023].
- If a woman scores 3 on the PC-PTSD-5 and you have capacity → follow up anyway. Because a cut-point of 4 produced many false negatives for women [PC-PTSD-5-Site].
- If the client starts giving graphic detail and becomes overwhelmed → slow down and ground: "Let's pause. You've told me enough for today. Look around the room and tell me three things you can see." Because full processing belongs inside a structured treatment (expert consensus).
- If the client says "nothing really bad happened" but has symptoms → ask about each event type with examples, including events years ago. Because people may not label events as trauma, and onset can be delayed [NICE-NG116].
- If there is a court case or compensation claim → do not delay treatment for that reason alone; discuss how the timing of treatment might interact with the case. Because the United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises against withholding treatment solely for legal proceedings [NICE-NG116].
- If the client is still in danger → make a safety and risk plan first and follow `02-risk.safeguarding`. Because significant risk calls for a risk management and safety plan in initial treatment planning [NICE-NG116].
- If the client reports dissociation → record it and plan grounding, but do not treat it as a reason to exclude. Because pre-treatment dissociation did not predict psychotherapy outcome across 21 trials [Hoeboer-etal-2020].

## Common problems

- **Screening score treated as a diagnosis.** Confirm by interview.
- **Wrong target event.** The trauma that prompted referral is not always the one that intrudes most. Ask.
- **Assessment as avoidance.** Clients may minimize. Normalize: "Many people skip over the worst part. Is there a part you'd rather not say yet?"
- **Measuring once.** Without repeated scores you cannot tell response from drift; VA/DoD suggests a validated measure to track change [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023].
