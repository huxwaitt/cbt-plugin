**Bottom line:** Single-session psychological debriefing, in which people recount the event and their emotions soon after it, does not prevent posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and may increase it. Treating everyone exposed has no proven benefit either. Offer practical support, information and active monitoring to all, and trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) to those with acute stress disorder or marked symptoms.

## What it looks like

- A workplace books a group session the day after an incident where staff must describe what they saw and felt.
- A clinician tells a newly bereaved or injured person they "need to talk it all out now or it will get buried".
- Everyone exposed is booked into therapy, and nobody checks a month later on who is still struggling.

## Why it happens

- Organizations want to be seen to help quickly, and debriefing is familiar.
- It feels intuitive that talking early prevents problems.
- Early distress is mistaken for early disorder, although many people recover without treatment.

## The fix

- If asked to run a debriefing → decline the emotional-processing format and offer information about common reactions, practical support, a point of contact, and screening or follow-up at about 1 month. Because the United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) says not to offer psychologically focused debriefing for prevention or treatment of PTSD [NICE-NG116], and the Cochrane review found no prevention benefit, with one trial showing higher PTSD risk at 1 year (odds ratio 2.88) [Rose-etal-2002].
- If a person has few symptoms in the first month → use active monitoring with follow-up within 1 month. Because NICE recommends considering active monitoring for subthreshold symptoms [NICE-NG116].
- If a person has acute stress disorder or clinically important symptoms → offer individual trauma-focused CBT. Because early multi-session interventions helped people with symptoms, especially those with acute stress disorder, but not unscreened groups [Roberts-etal-2019].
- If someone without acute stress disorder still has rising symptoms → keep them on follow-up. Because acute stress disorder misses most people who go on to develop PTSD [Bryant-etal-2011].
- If an organization still wants a meeting → keep it to information and practical arrangements, make attendance voluntary, and invite no one to recount the event. Because the review authors concluded compulsory debriefing should cease [Rose-etal-2002].

## Sources note

Evidence against single-session debriefing comes from a Cochrane review of 11 trials of generally poor quality [Rose-etal-2002] and from NICE [NICE-NG116]. Evidence for targeted early treatment comes from a meta-analysis of 61 studies [Roberts-etal-2019]. The United States (US) Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense (VA/DoD) guideline found insufficient evidence for or against any psychotherapy or medication for everyone exposed in the immediate post-trauma period [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023].
