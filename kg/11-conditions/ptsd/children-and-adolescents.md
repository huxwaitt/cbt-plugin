**Bottom line:** Individual trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is the treatment of choice for posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in children and young people, delivered with a non-offending caregiver involved. The United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) says to offer it to ages 7 to 17 presenting more than 3 months after a trauma, typically over 6 to 12 sessions, and not to offer drug treatment under 18 [NICE-NG116]. Check for ongoing abuse before anything else.

## When to use

NICE timing and age rules [NICE-NG116]:

| Age | Time since trauma | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 5 to 6 | More than 1 month | Consider individual trauma-focused CBT |
| 7 to 17 | 1 to 3 months | Consider individual trauma-focused CBT |
| 7 to 17 | More than 3 months | Offer individual trauma-focused CBT |
| 7 to 17 | More than 3 months, no response to or engagement with trauma-focused CBT | Consider eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR) |
| Under 18 | Any | Do not offer drugs to prevent or treat PTSD |

Children 6 and under are diagnosed with a separate Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) criteria set needing fewer symptoms (1 intrusion, 1 avoidance or negative cognition, 2 arousal) [Mikolajewski-etal-2017]. The International Trauma Questionnaire has a child and adolescent version for ages 7 to 17 and a caregiver-report version [ITQ-Site].

## How it works

The same mechanisms as adult treatment: processing the memory, correcting trauma-related meanings and ending avoidance, scaffolded for development and with caregivers trained to support the child. NICE lists these components: a validated manual; psychoeducation; managing arousal and flashbacks; safety planning; elaborating the memory; processing shame, guilt, loss and anger; restructuring meanings; overcoming avoidance; preparing for the ending; and boosters around anniversaries [NICE-NG116].

## Steps

**Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (TF-CBT)** by Cohen, Mannarino and Deblinger is designed for ages 3 to 18 with at least some memory of the trauma, usually over 8 to 20 sessions. It runs in three phases: stabilization and skill building, trauma narration and processing, and integration and consolidation [Pollio-Deblinger-2017]. Its components spell PRACTICE [Pollio-Deblinger-2017]:

1. **P**sychoeducation and **P**arenting skills
2. **R**elaxation
3. **A**ffect expression and modulation
4. **C**ognitive coping
5. **T**rauma narration and processing
6. **I**n vivo mastery of reminders
7. **C**onjoint child and caregiver sessions, including sharing the narrative
8. **E**nhancing safety and future development

For young children, child time is about 20 to 30 minutes per session, worked in short bursts of 5 to 15 minutes alternated with positive activities, with the rest of the session spent with caregivers [Pollio-Deblinger-2017].

**Cognitive therapy for PTSD** adapted for youth is an alternative. In a trial of 24 young people aged 8 to 18 after single-incident trauma, 10 weeks of individual trauma-focused CBT left 92% free of PTSD, against 42% on a waitlist [Smith-etal-2007].

## Guidance

- If a child discloses ongoing abuse → follow safeguarding procedures before trauma processing: in the US, your state's mandatory reporting law; in the UK, local safeguarding procedures. Because treatment cannot proceed safely while the child is still exposed (expert consensus); see `02-risk.safeguarding`.
- If the young person is actively suicidal or showing dangerous acting-out behavior → address safety first (`02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`) and start TF-CBT once stable. Because its developers list these as exclusion criteria [Pollio-Deblinger-2017].
- If the caregiver is highly distressed by the trauma → offer the caregiver support and parent sessions alongside the child's work. Because caregivers' abuse-related distress predicted children's response to TF-CBT [Pollio-Deblinger-2017].
- If the child avoids the narrative → build it gradually through drawings, books or play, a chapter at a time. Because the TF-CBT developers describe developmentally creative ways to engage young children in each component [Pollio-Deblinger-2017].
- If the family asks for medication → explain that NICE advises against drug treatment for PTSD under 18 and refer for a medical opinion if another condition needs treatment. Because no drug is recommended for PTSD in this age group [NICE-NG116].
- If the child has had multiple traumas → plan more than 12 sessions. Because NICE allows more sessions when clinically indicated, for example after multiple traumas [NICE-NG116].
- If the trauma anniversary approaches after treatment → offer a booster. Because NICE includes booster planning around significant dates [NICE-NG116].

## Common problems

- **Skills phase that never ends.** Stabilization is a phase in TF-CBT, not the treatment; move to the narrative.
- **Caregiver left out.** Without caregiver work, avoidance at home undoes session gains.
- **Treating behavior problems only.** Trauma symptoms in children can look like defiance, inattention or regression. Assess for PTSD directly.

## Evidence

- A network meta-analysis of 32 trials (2,260 participants) found individual forms of trauma-focused CBT most effective. Cognitive therapy for PTSD showed the largest effect against waitlist; EMDR was effective but less so; results for supportive counseling, parent training and family therapy were inconclusive [Mavranezouli-etal-2020].
- Its developers report support for TF-CBT from more than 50 studies, including 20 randomized trials, with gains maintained at 1 to 2 years [Pollio-Deblinger-2017].
- The evidence overall was of moderate to low quality [Mavranezouli-etal-2020].
