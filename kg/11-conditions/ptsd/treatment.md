**Bottom line:** Offer adults with posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) more than 1 month after the trauma an individual, manualized trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), such as cognitive processing therapy (CPT), prolonged exposure (PE) or cognitive therapy for PTSD (CT-PTSD). Plan for about 8 to 12 weekly sessions, more for multiple traumas. Every major guideline rates these above medication, and none has been shown reliably better than the others, so let client preference and therapist training decide.

## When to use

- PTSD or clinically important symptoms more than 1 month after a trauma [NICE-NG116].
- Comorbid depression, substance use or other disorders do not by themselves rule it out (see `ptsd.adaptations`).
- Pause or adapt for acute suicide risk, current serious danger, or acute psychosis or mania.

## How it works

All trauma-focused CBT works on the memory and its meaning. The United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) lists shared components: psychoeducation, managing arousal and flashbacks, safety planning, elaborating and processing the trauma memory, processing shame, guilt, loss and anger, restructuring trauma-related meanings, overcoming avoidance, restoring work and relationships, preparing for the ending, and booster sessions around anniversaries [NICE-NG116]. The protocols differ in emphasis. PE leans on repeated imaginal and in vivo exposure. CPT leans on written worksheets and Socratic questioning about stuck points. CT-PTSD updates hot spots in memory and changes appraisals.

**Choosing a protocol (shared decision):**

| Client situation | Reasonable first choice |
|---|---|
| Strong guilt or self-blame | CPT; it produced better scores than PE on 2 of 4 guilt subscales in one trial [Resick-etal-2002] |
| Prominent fear and avoidance of reminders | PE |
| Frequent intrusions with strong "now" quality, shame or many appraisals | CT-PTSD |
| Cannot attend 12 sessions, or high dropout risk | Written exposure therapy, 5 sessions, was noninferior to CPT with fewer dropouts (6.4% versus 39.7%) [Sloan-etal-2018] |
| Needs a quick course | Massed PE: 10 sessions over 2 weeks was noninferior to 10 over 8 weeks [Foa-etal-2018] |

Rows without a citation are expert consensus; matching protocol to presentation has not been tested directly.

## Steps

A generic outline for 12 weekly sessions of 60 to 90 minutes. Follow your chosen manual for the exact content.

1. **Session 1:** feedback on the assessment, a shared formulation, the rationale for approaching the memory, psychoeducation on common reactions, goals, baseline measures and a safety plan if needed.
2. **Sessions 2 to 3:** start approach work early. Build the in vivo list (PE), write the impact statement (CPT) or begin reclaiming-life assignments (CT-PTSD). Start memory work by session 3.
3. **Sessions 3 to 9:** process the memory (imaginal reliving, a written account or narrative), work on hot spots or stuck points, keep up between-session practice, and discriminate triggers.
4. **Sessions 8 to 12:** broader themes (safety, trust, power, esteem, intimacy), rebuilding activities and relationships, and a relapse prevention blueprint.
5. **Ending:** review scores, plan for setbacks and anniversaries, and offer boosters [NICE-NG116].

## Guidance

- If symptoms rise early in memory work → normalize and continue. "A temporary rise is common and usually means you are facing the memory." Because exacerbations affected a minority, were not linked to dropout, and those affected still improved by the end [Larsen-etal-2016].
- If the client prefers medication to psychological therapy → refer to a prescriber for venlafaxine or a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) and review regularly. Because NICE suggests this when the person prefers drug treatment [NICE-NG116]. Prescribing is outside a psychotherapist's scope in most jurisdictions.
- If the client asks for a benzodiazepine for PTSD → raise it with the prescriber. Because the United States (US) Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense (VA/DoD) guideline recommends against benzodiazepines for PTSD [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023].
- If scores are not falling by mid-treatment → review adherence and avoidance, and add sessions if needed. Because NICE allows more than 12 sessions when clinically indicated, for example after multiple traumas [NICE-NG116]. See `04-treatment-planning.managing-non-response`.
- If the client cannot attend in person → deliver by secure video. Because VA/DoD strongly recommends video delivery when the therapy has been validated for it or no other option exists [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023].

## Common problems

- **Drift into supportive talk.** See `ptsd.pitfall-therapist-avoidance-of-trauma-work`.
- **Indefinite "stabilization first."** See `ptsd.pitfall-indefinite-stabilization`.
- **Homework not done.** Treat non-completion as avoidance to formulate, not as failure.
- **Stopping at the first improvement.** Finish the course and the blueprint unless scores show stable remission.

## Evidence

| Guideline | Recommended psychological treatments | Medication |
|---|---|---|
| NICE NG116 (2018) | Offer individual trauma-focused CBT (CPT, CT-PTSD, narrative exposure therapy, PE) after 1 month; offer eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR) after 3 months for non-combat trauma; consider supported computerized trauma-focused CBT for milder cases [NICE-NG116] | Consider venlafaxine or an SSRI if preferred [NICE-NG116] |
| American Psychological Association (APA) 2025 | Strong: CPT, PE, trauma-focused CBT. Conditional: cognitive therapy, EMDR, narrative exposure therapy [APA-PTSD-Guideline-2025] | Conditional: fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline, venlafaxine. Conditional against: topiramate, alprazolam [APA-PTSD-Guideline-2025] |
| VA/DoD 2023 | Strong: CPT, EMDR, PE, and psychotherapy over medication. Weak for: CT-PTSD, present-centered therapy, written exposure therapy [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023] | Strong: paroxetine, sertraline, venlafaxine. Strong against: benzodiazepines [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023] |

- A meta-analysis of 114 randomized trials (8,171 participants) found the strongest evidence for CPT, CT-PTSD and PE among trauma-focused CBTs [Lewis-etal-2020].
- In 916 US veterans, PE and CPT both produced substantial improvements; PE was statistically but not clinically better (standardized mean difference 0.17) [Schnurr-etal-2022].
- APA found insufficient evidence that any trauma-focused treatment beats another active trauma-focused treatment [APA-PTSD-Guideline-2025].
- Typical dose: 8 to 12 sessions [NICE-NG116]; 10 to 12 weekly sessions of 60 or 90 minutes for CPT, EMDR and PE [VA-DoD-PTSD-CPG-2023].
