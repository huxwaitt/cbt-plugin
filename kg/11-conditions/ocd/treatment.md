**Bottom line:** Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) built around exposure and response prevention (ERP) is the first-line psychological treatment for obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), with large effects in many randomized controlled trials (RCTs) [Ost-etal-2015]. Match intensity to impairment: up to 10 therapist hours of guided work for mild OCD, more than 10 therapist hours or a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) for moderate, and both for severe [NICE-CG31]. Courses usually run 6 to 20 sessions depending on severity and chronicity, with daily homework [Veale-2007].

## When to use

- Any adult with OCD, as the first offer or alongside medication.
- Guideline status: the United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guideline CG31 (2005) recommends CBT including ERP at every step [NICE-CG31]. NICE is partially updating CG31 for OCD and body dysmorphic disorder: draft consultation ran 17 September to 21 October 2026 and publication is expected 16 February 2027, so check for the final guideline [NICE-GID-NG10435].
- The American Psychiatric Association practice guideline (2007, with a 2013 guideline watch) is now listed as a legacy guideline [Maintainer/2026-11-06]. It and the association's current patient guidance name ERP and SSRIs as first-line, with both together for severe OCD [Koran-etal-2007] [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site]. The American Psychological Association has no OCD guideline (see `04-treatment-planning.guideline-map`).

| Impairment (NICE) | Offer [NICE-CG31] |
|---|---|
| Mild | Low-intensity CBT including ERP, up to 10 therapist hours: guided self-help, telephone, or group |
| Moderate, or no response to low intensity | More intensive CBT including ERP (over 10 therapist hours) or an SSRI |
| Severe | SSRI plus CBT including ERP |

## How it works

Rituals, avoidance and reassurance stop the client from learning that feared outcomes do not follow and that distress and doubt can be tolerated. ERP removes them while the client faces triggers on purpose [Hezel-Simpson-2019]. Cognitive work targets the meaning given to intrusions, mainly inflated responsibility (see `ocd.cbt-model`).

## Steps

A typical twice-weekly or weekly outline. The trial protocol of Simpson and colleagues used 17 sessions of 90 minutes, twice weekly over 8 weeks: 2 introductory sessions, then 15 exposure sessions, with at least 1 hour of daily self-directed exposure and at least 2 sessions outside the clinic [Simpson-etal-2013].

| Sessions | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Assessment and Y-BOCS (Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale), shared formulation, rationale, goals linked to values, ritual self-monitoring |
| 2 to 3 | Exposure hierarchy, written response prevention rules, family session with consent |
| 3 onward | Therapist-guided exposure in every session, imaginal exposure for feared consequences, post-exposure processing, daily homework |
| Middle | Cognitive work on responsibility and thought-action fusion, top hierarchy items, exposures at home and in real settings |
| Last 2 | Relapse prevention plan, "lifestyle exposure," booster schedule |

Veale adds that longer sessions (about 90 minutes) several times a week give larger effects [Veale-2007]. The National Health Service (NHS) advises about 8 to 20 sessions for fairly mild OCD [NHS-OCD-Treatment]. The treatment algorithm of Stein and colleagues suggests monthly booster sessions for 3 to 6 months after acute treatment [Stein-etal-2019].

## Guidance

- If impairment is moderate, or low-intensity work has not helped → offer more intensive CBT including ERP (over 10 therapist hours) or an SSRI, by client choice. Because NICE reserves low-intensity work for mild impairment and judges the two options comparably effective [NICE-CG31].
- If impairment is severe → offer CBT including ERP together with an SSRI, with referral to a prescriber. Because both NICE and the American Psychiatric Association recommend combined treatment for severe OCD [NICE-CG31] [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site].
- If the client is on a stable SSRI with partial response → add ERP rather than wait for another medication change. Because ERP added to serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SRIs) beat risperidone and placebo (80% versus 23% and 15% with a 25% or greater Y-BOCS drop) [Simpson-etal-2013].
- If homework is not done → make it smaller and more specific, and review it first each session. Because low adherence to between-session exposures predicts worse symptoms, an association the review calls strong [Hezel-Simpson-2019].
- If the client refuses ERP → offer cognitive therapy for OCD focused on appraisals, with behavioral experiments. Because ERP and cognitive therapy showed no meaningful difference (effect 0.07) [Ost-etal-2015].
- If there is no response after an adequate course → check fidelity first (see `ocd.pitfall-missed-mental-compulsions`), then step up (see `ocd.adaptations`). Because going too easy on exposures and missing mental compulsions are common, fixable therapist errors [Gillihan-etal-2012].
- If there is risk to life, severe self-neglect, or extreme distress or impairment → arrange urgent psychiatric review and consider specialist or inpatient care. Because NICE names these as reasons to consider inpatient services [NICE-CG31].

## Common problems

- **Dropout:** about 20% to 30% leave ERP early [Hezel-Simpson-2019]. Build the rationale and start with achievable steps.
- **Partial gains:** only about half reach full remission [Hezel-Simpson-2019].
- **Therapist drift** into reassurance or general talk (see `ocd.pitfall-therapist-reassurance`).

## Evidence

- Meta-analysis of 37 RCTs: CBT effects of 1.31 against waitlist and 1.33 against placebo, and 0.55 over antidepressant medication alone [Ost-etal-2015]. Evidence strength: guideline-recommended, supported by many RCTs.
- Network meta-analysis of 54 trials (6,652 participants): behavioral therapy reduced Y-BOCS by about 14.5 points more than drug placebo, SSRIs by about 3.5. Most psychotherapy trials allowed stable antidepressants and waitlist controls inflate effects [Skapinakis-etal-2016].
- In an RCT of intensive ERP, clomipramine and the combination, ERP alone gave 62% response (intent to treat); adding clomipramine did not improve on ERP [Foa-etal-2005].
- Recovery depends on the definition: 50% to 60% by clinical significance criteria, about 25% fully asymptomatic [Fisher-Wells-2005]. Number needed to treat is about 3 for CBT and 5 for SSRIs [Stein-etal-2019].
