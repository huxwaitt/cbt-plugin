# 11-conditions/ocd: Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders

## This surface
Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) and its relatives, body dysmorphic disorder, hoarding disorder, hair pulling and skin picking, from first recognition through formulation, exposure and response prevention (ERP) and adaptation. These pages span the assessment, formulation and treatment stages of an episode of care. Start with the overview and assessment, then use the model and treatment pages to plan, and the pitfalls to check fidelity when progress stalls.

## Pages in order
1. `ocd.overview`: the diagnostic picture, differentials and suicide risk come first.
2. `ocd.assessment`: screening, mapping rituals including mental ones, and a Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) baseline.
3. `ocd.cbt-model`: the responsibility model turns the assessment into a shared formulation.
4. `ocd.treatment`: guideline status, stepped care by impairment and the session outline.
5. `ocd.erp-protocol`: the in-session exposure procedure and cognitive work on responsibility.
6. `ocd.family-accommodation`: bringing relatives into the plan when they answer questions or join rituals.
7. `ocd.adaptations`: changes for severity, insight, comorbidity, age, faith, medication and non-response.
8. `ocd.related-disorders`: body dysmorphic disorder, hoarding, hair pulling and skin picking.

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Therapist Reassurance and Debating Obsessional Content | The client asks the therapist whether the feared outcome could happen, the therapist explains why it is unlikely, relief lasts minutes, and the same question returns in a new form. | `ocd.pitfall-therapist-reassurance` |
| Treating Taboo Obsessions as Dangerousness | A client discloses unwanted harm, sexual or violent thoughts, and the clinician responds with alarm, a risk report or a paraphilia diagnosis instead of assessing OCD. | `ocd.pitfall-misreading-intrusions-as-risk` |
| Hidden Rituals That Undermine ERP | Exposures are completed and overt rituals stop, but symptoms barely change because the client is neutralizing in their head, distracting, or substituting quieter rituals. | `ocd.pitfall-missed-mental-compulsions` |
