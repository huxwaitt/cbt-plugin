**Bottom line:** Body dysmorphic disorder (BDD), hoarding disorder and the body-focused repetitive behavior disorders (hair pulling and skin picking) sit with obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) in both diagnostic systems, but each needs its own cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) focus [Reed-etal-2019]. BDD responds to CBT with exposure, ritual prevention and attention retraining; hoarding to skills, motivation and graded discarding at home; hair pulling to habit reversal [Harrison-etal-2016] [Steketee-etal-2010] [McGuire-etal-2014]. Skin picking uses the same behavioral methods, on thinner controlled evidence [Selles-etal-2016].

## Diagnostic essentials

Paraphrased from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fifth edition, text revision (DSM-5-TR) [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

- **BDD:** preoccupation with perceived flaws in appearance that others cannot see or see as slight, plus repetitive behaviors (mirror checking, grooming, skin picking, reassurance seeking) or mental acts (comparing), causing distress or impairment, and not better explained by weight or shape concerns in an eating disorder. Specifiers: muscle dysmorphia; insight level.
- **Hoarding disorder:** persistent difficulty discarding possessions regardless of value, from a felt need to save them and distress at discarding, so clutter compromises living areas (unless others have cleared it). Specifiers: excessive acquisition; insight level. Exclude brain injury and other mental disorders as causes.
- **Trichotillomania:** recurrent hair pulling with hair loss and repeated attempts to stop, causing distress or impairment. **Excoriation disorder** is the same pattern with skin picking and lesions.
- The International Classification of Diseases, 11th revision (ICD-11) adds olfactory reference disorder and hypochondriasis to this grouping and notes that body-focused behaviors are often preceded by sensations rather than thoughts [Reed-etal-2019].

## Course and prevalence

| Disorder | Prevalence | Onset and course |
|---|---|---|
| BDD | 1.9% community adults; 13.2% in general cosmetic surgery, 20.1% in rhinoplasty, 11.3% in dermatology clinics [Veale-etal-2016] | Usually before age 18 [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site] |
| Hoarding | Pooled 2.5%, similar in men and women [Postlethwaite-etal-2019] | Starts early and worsens each decade, especially after age 30 [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site] |
| Trichotillomania | 1% to 3% of adults report clinically significant pulling [McGuire-etal-2014] | Usually begins around puberty [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site] |
| Excoriation | About 2% of adults [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site] | Most often begins in adolescence and is usually chronic if untreated [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site] |

## Differential and comorbidity

- **BDD versus eating disorders:** if the concern is mainly weight or shape with eating behavior, assess for an eating disorder first.
- **BDD versus OCD:** BDD concerns are about appearance, often with poorer insight [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site].
- **Hoarding versus OCD:** saving driven by contamination or harm fears, or a need for completeness, may be OCD; hoarding disorder centers on attachment to possessions and difficulty deciding [Frost-Hartl-1996].
- **Hoarding versus dementia or brain injury:** new-onset late-life accumulation needs medical assessment [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- **Pulling or picking versus self-harm:** in trichotillomania and excoriation disorder the aim is not to injure oneself. If the client describes hurting themselves to cope with emotional pain or to punish themselves, assess self-harm and risk (see `02-risk.self-harm`).

## CBT approach in brief

**BDD.** The Veale model centers on self-focused attention to a distorted body image, negative appraisal, rumination and safety behaviors such as camouflage [Veale-2004]. In a two-site trial of 120 adults, CBT for BDD (CBT-BDD) ran 22 sessions of 60 minutes over 24 weeks: psychoeducation, formulation, motivation, cognitive restructuring, exposure and ritual prevention, mindfulness and attentional retraining, and work on self-acceptance. It beat supportive psychotherapy clearly at one site but not the other [Wilhelm-etal-2019]. Across 7 trials, CBT reduced BDD symptoms (delta -1.22), depression and poor insight [Harrison-etal-2016]. Severity: the BDD version of the Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale runs 0 to 48, and the trial required 24 or more [Wilhelm-etal-2019].

**Hoarding.** The Frost and Hartl model links information-processing deficits, emotional attachment to possessions, beliefs about possessions and avoidance [Frost-Hartl-1996]. The tested program ran 26 weekly sessions over an average of about 45 weeks, every fourth session (2 hours) at home or an acquiring site, with motivational interviewing, organizing, problem-solving and decision skills, graded discarding and non-acquiring, and cognitive work [Steketee-etal-2010]. Measure with the Saving Inventory-Revised (SI-R; 23 items, three subscales) [Frost-etal-2004] or the Hoarding Rating Scale-Interview (5 items rated 0 to 8; total of 14 best separated hoarding from OCD) [Tolin-etal-2010]. In the trial 41% of completers improved to a clinically significant degree [Steketee-etal-2010]; across 12 samples, clinically significant change ranged from only 24% to 43% by outcome domain [Tolin-etal-2015b].

**Hair pulling and skin picking.** Habit reversal training combines awareness training and competing response training [McGuire-etal-2014]: the client learns to spot cues and early movements, then uses a physically incompatible action until the urge passes (standard practice description). Across 11 randomized trials for hair pulling, behavior therapy had a large pooled effect (1.41) and serotonin reuptake inhibitors a moderate one (0.41) [McGuire-etal-2014]. For skin picking, behavioral treatments showed large pre-to-post effects (g = 1.19), but only 3 of 9 studies had a control group [Selles-etal-2016].

## Risk notes

- BDD carries high rates of suicidality and hospitalization [Wilhelm-etal-2019]. Ask about suicidal thinking at every review (see `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`). United States (US) crisis line: call or text 988, or 911; United Kingdom (UK): 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111, Samaritans 116 123.
- Cosmetic procedures almost never help BDD and can worsen it [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site].
- Severe hoarding can create fire, fall and sanitation hazards and may affect children or dependent adults; follow `02-risk.safeguarding` (expert consensus).
