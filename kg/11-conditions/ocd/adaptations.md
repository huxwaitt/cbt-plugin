**Bottom line:** Keep exposure and response prevention (ERP) at the core of every adaptation and change the format, intensity or supports around it. Step up to combined treatment or intensive formats when impairment is severe or response stalls, bring in families for children, and treat poor insight as obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), not psychosis [NICE-CG31] [Reed-etal-2019].

## The principle

Adaptations fit the delivery to the person; they do not replace ERP with talk, relaxation or reassurance. Decide on objective markers: Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) severity bands (26 to 34 moderate to severe, 35 to 40 severe) [Storch-etal-2015] and consensus response (35% or greater drop) [Mataix-Cols-etal-2016].

## Guidance

**Severity and stepping up**
- If impairment is severe (for example, Y-BOCS 26 or more with major role loss) → offer cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) with ERP plus a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI), with referral to a prescriber. Because guidelines recommend combined treatment for severe OCD [NICE-CG31] [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site].
- If Y-BOCS has dropped less than 25% after an adequate ERP course (non-response under consensus definitions) → check fidelity, then consider intensive formats such as daily sessions or the Bergen 4-day treatment, or a specialist service [Mataix-Cols-etal-2016]. Because concentrated formats show high response (91.1% after the 4-day format and 84.4% at 3 months, in an uncontrolled evaluation of 90 patients) [Kvale-etal-2018].
- If the client is housebound, not eating or drinking because of contamination fears, or neglecting self-care → arrange same-week medical and psychiatric review and consider home-based, specialist or inpatient care; if life is at immediate risk, use emergency services (United States (US) 911, United Kingdom (UK) 999). Because the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) names risk to life, severe self-neglect and extreme distress or impairment as reasons to consider inpatient care [NICE-CG31], and England has national specialist services for severe OCD not helped locally [NHS-OCD-Treatment].

**Insight**
- If the client is certain the feared outcome is real → keep the OCD diagnosis with a poor insight specifier, use Theory A versus Theory B, and run small experiments. Because the International Classification of Diseases, 11th revision (ICD-11) advises against diagnosing delusional disorder when the belief fits OCD, and poor insight predicts poorer response [Reed-etal-2019] [Stein-etal-2019].

**Comorbidity**
- If depression is severe enough to block homework → treat it first or in parallel, and monitor suicide risk (see `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`). Because comorbidity may weaken outcomes partly through adherence [Hezel-Simpson-2019], and in OCD a previous suicide attempt, comorbid substance use and personality disorders raise the risk of suicide death [FernandezDeLaCruz-etal-2017].
- If tics are present → record the tic-related specifier and separate "just right" rituals from tics. Because tic-related OCD is a distinct specifier [Stein-etal-2019].
- If the client is autistic → separate preferred routines from distressing compulsions, use visual hierarchies and concrete language, and involve family. Because in a trial of adolescents and adults with autism, CBT for OCD gave more responders than anxiety management (45% versus 20%), though the groups did not differ significantly at end of treatment, and family accommodation predicted worse outcomes [Russell-etal-2013a].

**Children and adolescents**
- If the child is 7 to 17 with moderate OCD → offer CBT with ERP that involves parents, alone or with an SSRI. Because in the Pediatric OCD Treatment Study (POTS) remission was 53.6% combined, 39.3% CBT, 21.4% sertraline, 3.6% placebo [POTS-2004].
- If the child is 5 to 8 → use family-based CBT with parent coaching. Because it gave 72% response versus 41% for family-based relaxation [Freeman-etal-2014].
- If you need a national standard for a young person → in the US follow the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry practice parameter; in the UK, NICE recommends family-involved CBT including ERP for moderate to severe OCD, and adding an SSRI at ages 12 to 18 (considered at 8 to 11) when CBT alone is not enough, and refer any medication question to a child and adolescent psychiatrist. Because these are the current national standards, and NICE reserves starting an SSRI in youth for a child and adolescent psychiatrist after assessment [AACAP-OCD-2012] [NICE-CG31].

**Culture and religion**
- If obsessions concern sin, purity or prayer → ask what the client's own tradition requires, and with consent consult a trusted faith leader to separate observance from compulsion. Because ERP targets the excess beyond normative practice, not faith itself (expert consensus).

**Medication**
- If the client takes an SSRI → coordinate with the prescriber and do not advise on changes. Because SSRI trials for OCD need 8 to 12 weeks [Stein-etal-2019] or at least 12 weeks at an adequate dose [APA-Psychiatry-OCRD-Site]; the sources differ slightly.
- If the client is on a stable serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SRI) with partial response → add ERP. Because ERP beat risperidone augmentation [Simpson-etal-2013].

**Remote delivery**
- If access is limited → offer therapist-supported internet or video ERP, with home exposures done live on camera. Because therapist-supported internet ERP showed efficacy with maintained gains [Hezel-Simpson-2019].

## Example phrasings

- To a parent: "When she asks if her hands are clean, try 'I can see this is hard. You know what we're practicing.' Then step back."
- Poor insight: "You're very sure. Rather than argue, let's test it and see what happens."
- Faith: "What would your faith leader say a sincere prayer requires? Let's practice only that much."
- Avoid: "Let's leave the exposures until you feel less anxious."

## Evidence

Combined treatment for severe OCD and family-involved CBT for youth are guideline-recommended [NICE-CG31]. Pediatric findings rest on randomized controlled trials [POTS-2004] [Freeman-etal-2014]. Intensive and remote formats show emerging evidence [Kvale-etal-2018] [Hezel-Simpson-2019]. Cultural and religious adaptations are expert consensus.
