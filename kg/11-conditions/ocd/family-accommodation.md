**Bottom line:** Family accommodation means relatives helping the obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) run: answering reassurance questions, joining rituals, helping avoid triggers, or reorganizing the household. It tracks severity (r = .42 across 41 studies) [Wu-etal-2016] and is listed among factors linked to poorer treatment outcome [Stein-etal-2019]. With the client's consent, bring relatives into the plan, teach them to give comfort without reassurance, and withdraw accommodation step by step in line with the exposure hierarchy.

## The principle

Accommodation usually starts from love and exhaustion. It lowers distress in the moment, so it works like a ritual done by someone else. Treatment aims for consistent, warm support that does not feed the OCD [Veale-2007]. Relatives may cope differently from each other, be overprotective, aggressive or sarcastic, or minimize the problem, so plan with all key adults [Veale-2007]. The United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) asks clinicians to assess how far carers support or carry out OCD behaviors, and to help them reduce that involvement sensitively and supportively [NICE-CG31].

## Guidance

- If you suspect accommodation → ask each person, with consent, what they do when the client is distressed: "When he asks if the door is locked, what happens next? What do you avoid at home because of the OCD?" Because families often accommodate without anyone naming it as part of the OCD (expert consensus).
- If a relative answers reassurance questions → teach the difference between comfort (warmth, encouragement to use skills) and reassurance (answers about the feared outcome). Because comfort helps and reassurance maintains the cycle [Gillihan-etal-2012].
- If a relative is doing rituals for the client, such as extra laundry or checking → list these "OCD by proxy" tasks and withdraw them gradually, matched to the hierarchy. Because they extend the ritual system [Gillihan-etal-2012].
- If the client keeps pressing after a supportive refusal → agree in advance that the relative may leave the room or reply with an exposure statement. Because arguments raise distress and slip back into reassurance [Gillihan-etal-2012].
- If relatives say "that's just your OCD," criticize or get angry → coach a neutral supportive script instead. Because criticism, anger and labeling increase distress [Gillihan-etal-2012].
- If the client is a child aged 5 to 8 → make parents the main agents of change, coaching them to lead exposures and reduce accommodation. Because family-based cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) outperformed family-based relaxation (72% versus 41% response) [Freeman-etal-2014].
- If the client is autistic → assess accommodation early and plan its reduction. Because in a trial of adolescents and adults with autism and OCD, greater family accommodation was linked to poorer outcomes in both arms [Russell-etal-2013a].
- If an adult client declines family involvement → respect it, work on the client's own reassurance seeking, and revisit later. Because consent governs disclosure to relatives (see `01-intake.collateral-information`).
- If a relative is burned out or the home is in conflict → offer them a separate session or signpost carer support. Because relatives of people with OCD have lower quality of life [Stein-etal-2019].

**Stepwise plan**
1. Map accommodation with each relative.
2. Explain the cycle using the client's formulation.
3. Choose one or two accommodations to drop first, matched to current exposure steps.
4. Script what the relative will say, and rehearse it in session.
5. Review weekly, praise effort, and adjust.

## Example phrasings

- Relative to client: "I can see this is really hard. I'm not going to answer that one. What can you do instead?"
- Relative to child: "I know your worry wants me to check again. I believe you can handle not knowing. Let's do something else together."
- Therapist to relatives: "Your help has been an act of care. We're going to change the kind of help, not the care."
- Avoid (relative): "For the hundredth time, the door is locked!"
- Avoid (therapist): "You need to stop enabling her."

## Evidence

The association between accommodation and severity comes from a meta-analysis of 41 studies [Wu-etal-2016]. Family-based CBT for young children is supported by a randomized controlled trial [Freeman-etal-2014]. The specific scripts and the stepwise plan are expert consensus drawn from published guidance on exposure and response prevention (ERP) pitfalls [Gillihan-etal-2012].
