**Bottom line:** Sleep loss can trigger hypomania or mania [Harvey-etal-2015], and sleep disturbance is the most common early sign of mania, reported by a median 77% of people [Jackson-etal-2003]. Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) and activation still help in bipolar disorder, but they need guardrails: time in bed no less than 6.5 hours, regular rhythms, and weekly mood ratings [Harvey-etal-2015]. With these, the adapted CBT-I protocol was followed by 4.6% hypomania or mania relapse versus 31.6% with psychoeducation over 6 months in a pilot trial [Harvey-etal-2015].

## What it looks like

- Standard sleep restriction sets a 5-hour sleep window for a client with bipolar disorder.
- Behavioral activation adds evening goals and new projects as mood lifts, with no check on sleep or energy.
- The client reports "loads more done, barely tired," and it is praised as progress.
- Homework includes late-night thought records or screen work.
- Two weeks later the client is spending, irritable and sleeping three hours.

## Why it happens

- Protocols for unipolar depression and primary insomnia are applied unchanged.
- The bipolar diagnosis is unknown or not weighed (see `bipolar-and-psychosis.pitfall-missed-bipolarity`).
- Rising energy looks like recovery, and clients often value it. Goal pursuit and staying up are ascent behaviors that can push activation higher [Kelly-etal-2017a].

## The fix

- If using CBT-I with a bipolar client → keep time in bed at 6.5 hours or more, start with regular bed and rise times, add a dim-light wind-down and a bright-light wake-up routine. Because these were the bipolar adaptations in the trial protocol [Harvey-etal-2015].
- If using stimulus control or sleep restriction → rate mania, depression and sleepiness weekly. Because in a case series of 15, 2 of the 5 who had sleep restriction reported mild hypomania, and the authors judged the methods safe only with careful monitoring of mood and sleepiness [Kaplan-Harvey-2013].
- If scheduling activities → keep them in the daytime, pace increases, and include regular meals, exercise and wind-down. Because daily rhythm regulation is one of the components most linked to lower depression [Miklowitz-etal-2021].
- If sleep drops for two nights or energy surges → pause activation goals, protect sleep, and follow the client's amber plan. Because sleep disturbance is the most common early sign of mania [Jackson-etal-2003].
- If the client celebrates feeling "wired" → explore the appraisal kindly: "What happened the last time you felt this way?" Because positive appraisals of activation can drive ascent behaviors [Kelly-etal-2017a].

Example phrasings:
- "Let's aim for steady rather than more. What time will you be winding down tonight?"
- "Your energy has jumped and sleep has dropped. That's on your amber list. What did we agree you'd do?"
- Avoid: "Great, keep pushing while you've got the energy."

## Sources note

The guardrails come from the bipolar-adapted CBT-I trial, a pilot with 58 participants [Harvey-etal-2015], and an earlier case series [Kaplan-Harvey-2013]. No trial has tested behavioral activation pacing rules specifically in bipolar disorder; that advice is expert consensus drawn from the prodrome and rhythm literature.
