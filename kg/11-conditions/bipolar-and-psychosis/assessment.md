**Bottom line:** Ask every depressed client about past periods of high energy and reduced need for sleep, because bipolar depression is often misdiagnosed as unipolar depression [Yatham-etal-2018]. Use a validated screener only when suspicion is raised, then confirm by interview and collateral [VADoD-Bipolar-2023]. For psychosis, map each experience, its distress and its timeline, rate severity on a simple dimensional scale, and check risk and medical causes the same day.

## When to use

- A client with depression or anxiety, especially if onset was before 25, there is a family history of bipolar disorder, or an antidepressant was followed by agitation or high mood [Yatham-etal-2018].
- New insomnia without fatigue, spending, risky sex, job or housing churn, or legal trouble outside the person's usual pattern [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
- Any report of voices, visions, special messages, being watched or targeted, or thoughts being interfered with.
- A young person in a help-seeking service with unusual experiences short of full psychosis (see `bipolar-and-psychosis.at-risk-mental-state`).

## Steps

1. **Engage first.** Use the client's words ("the voices," "the surveillance").
2. **Mood history timeline.** Plot every high and low period: start, length, sleep, energy, activity, triggers, treatment, hospital stays. Ask: "Has there been a time you needed much less sleep but still felt full of energy?" [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
3. **Psychotic experiences map.** For each experience record content, frequency, conviction (0 to 100%), distress, what the person thinks causes it, what they do in response, and onset relative to drugs, sleep loss, stress or childbirth.
4. **Screen when suspicion is raised.** Use a validated instrument for possible bipolarity, not routine screening of everyone [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
5. **Rate severity** at baseline with the measures below and repeat regularly.
6. **Differential questions.** "Did this happen only when using drugs or alcohol?" "Any head injury, seizures, new medication or steroids?" "Do the mood changes last hours or days, and do they follow arguments?" [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
7. **Collateral.** With consent, ask a relative or partner about changes in speech pace, spending, sleep and irritability that the client may not notice [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
8. **Risk the same day.** Suicide, self-neglect, harm to or from others, command hallucinations, driving and finances (see `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`).
9. **Decide the route.** Suspected mania, first-episode psychosis or high risk means same-day contact with psychiatry or crisis services. First-episode cases do better in coordinated specialty care, especially when untreated psychosis is short [Kane-etal-2016].

| Measure | Use | Scoring and cutoffs |
|---|---|---|
| Mood Disorder Questionnaire (MDQ) | Lifetime bipolar screen, self-report, free | Positive at 7 or more of 13 symptom items, plus co-occurrence and moderate or serious problems; sensitivity 0.73, specificity 0.90 in mood-clinic outpatients [Hirschfeld-etal-2000] |
| Hypomania Checklist (HCL-32) | Lifetime hypomania screen | Sensitivity 80%, specificity 51% for bipolar versus major depression [Angst-etal-2005] |
| Altman Self-Rating Mania Scale (ASRM) | Current mania, 5 items, past week | 0 to 20; 6 or more suggests mania or hypomania [APA-Level2-Mania-ASRM]; sensitivity 85.5%, specificity 87.3% [Altman-etal-1997] |
| Young Mania Rating Scale (YMRS) | Clinician-rated mania severity, 11 items | Inter-rater reliability 0.93 [Young-etal-1978] |
| Clinician-Rated Dimensions of Psychosis Symptom Severity | 8 domains, each 0 to 4, past 7 days, read item by item | Free for clinical use from the American Psychiatric Association [APA-Psychosis-Dimensions-Measure] |
| Prodromal Questionnaire, 16-item (PQ-16) | Psychosis-risk screen in help-seekers | 6 or more items positive; sensitivity and specificity 87% [Ising-etal-2012] |
| Psychotic Symptom Rating Scales (PSYRATS) | Voices and delusions: conviction, distress, frequency | Good inter-rater reliability [Haddock-etal-1999] |
| Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) | 30-item research standard | Licensed; needs training [Kay-etal-1987] |

## What to record

- The mood timeline and episode counts, with dates of hospital stays and medication changes.
- Each psychotic experience with conviction, distress and response, in the client's words.
- Screening and severity scores with date and version.
- Substance use, medical history, family history of bipolar disorder, psychosis or suicide.
- Collateral sources, consent given, and what they reported.
- Risk findings, actions and who was contacted.

## Guidance

- If a depressed client describes a few days of little sleep with high energy → ask about duration, others noticing, and consequences before planning activation-based work. Because a missed bipolar diagnosis changes medication and therapy choices [Yatham-etal-2018].
- If the client denies hypomania but a partner describes it → record both accounts and seek specialist review. Because people often do not see their own elevated states as illness [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
- If voices command harm, or the client feels they must act to protect themselves → complete risk assessment that session and involve the psychiatric team; if danger is imminent, call 911 or 988 in the United States, or 999 in the United Kingdom. Because acting on commands or threat beliefs is the main danger (expert consensus).
- If psychotic symptoms began with heavy drug use or a new medication → arrange medical review before diagnosing. Because substance and medical causes are exclusions.
- If the client is guarded → ask about distress and goals before content: "What would you like to be different?" Because engagement precedes detailed mapping.

## Common problems

- Screening everyone in general medical settings; a 2023 United States veterans' guideline suggests against it [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
- Relying on self-report alone: insight into highs is often limited.
- Asking about "hallucinations" in technical words; ask "Do you ever hear or see things other people do not seem to?"
