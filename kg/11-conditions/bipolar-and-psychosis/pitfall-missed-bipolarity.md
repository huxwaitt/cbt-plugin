**Bottom line:** Depression in bipolar disorder is frequently misdiagnosed as major depressive disorder, so a diagnosis of unipolar depression should come only after bipolar disorder has been excluded [Yatham-etal-2018]. The fix takes five minutes: ask every depressed client about past hypomania, family history and antidepressant reactions, and use a validated screener when suspicion is raised [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].

## What it looks like

- Intake records "depression, recurrent" without any question about high periods.
- The client describes "my good weeks" when they slept four hours, started businesses or spent heavily, and this is noted as recovery.
- An antidepressant was followed by agitation, irritability or racing thoughts, and nobody connected it.
- Treatment cycles between partial response and sudden crashes.
- A relative's comment ("she was flying last spring") never reaches the formulation.

## Why it happens

- Clients seek help when low, not when high; hypomania feels good and is rarely reported unprompted.
- Hypomania needs only 4 days and causes no marked impairment, so it is easy to miss.
- Bipolar II and subthreshold forms are common: lifetime 1.1% and 2.4% in the United States (US) [Merikangas-etal-2007].
- Diagnostic delays of up to 14.5 years have been reported [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].

## The fix

- If a client presents with depression → ask in every intake: "Have you ever had a few days or more when you needed much less sleep but felt full of energy, or others said you were not yourself?" Because guidelines advise routinely asking about past manic or hypomanic symptoms in every patient with a major depressive episode [Yatham-etal-2018].
- If onset was before 25, or a first-degree relative has bipolar disorder → raise your index of suspicion and seek collateral. Because both raise the likelihood of bipolar disorder [Yatham-etal-2018].
- If suspicion is raised → use the Mood Disorder Questionnaire (MDQ) or Hypomania Checklist, then confirm by interview. Because validated instruments support the diagnostic decision [VADoD-Bipolar-2023]; the MDQ had sensitivity 0.73 and specificity 0.90 in outpatients [Hirschfeld-etal-2000].
- If hypomania is likely and the client is on an antidepressant alone → tell the prescriber promptly. Because antidepressant monotherapy is not recommended in bipolar I depression and carries switch concerns [Yatham-etal-2018].
- If you are planning behavioral activation → add weekly sleep and energy ratings. Because activation can be paced down if early signs of elevation appear (see `bipolar-and-psychosis.pitfall-sleep-loss-and-activation`).

Example phrasings:
- "Some people with low mood also have times when they feel unusually high or wired. Has anything like that happened to you?"
- "Could I speak with someone who knows you well about how you've been over the years?"
- Avoid: "So you've never been manic, right?"

## Sources note

The routine-question rule, risk markers and antidepressant caution come from the Canadian Network for Mood and Anxiety Treatments and International Society for Bipolar Disorders guideline [Yatham-etal-2018]. Targeted rather than universal screening follows the 2023 Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense guideline [VADoD-Bipolar-2023]. MDQ accuracy comes from a psychiatric outpatient sample [Hirschfeld-etal-2000]; screeners are most useful where pretest probability is reasonable [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
