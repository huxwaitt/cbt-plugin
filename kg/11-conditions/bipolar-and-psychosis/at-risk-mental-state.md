**Bottom line:** People at clinical high risk (also called an at-risk mental state) have attenuated or brief psychotic experiences with distress or decline; about 15% develop psychosis within 1 year and 25% within 3 years in the latest meta-analysis [SalazarDePablo-etal-2021]. The United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises individual cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), with or without family intervention, and advises against antipsychotics to prevent psychosis [NICE-CG178]. Preventive interventions roughly halved transition at 12 months in pooled trials [VanDerGaag-etal-2013].

## When to use

- Help-seeking adolescents and young adults with unusual perceptions, suspiciousness or odd beliefs held with some doubt, or brief psychotic symptoms that resolve on their own.
- Distress and functional decline plus a first-degree relative with psychosis; NICE advises referral without delay for any of these patterns [NICE-CG178].
- In the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fifth edition, text revision (DSM-5-TR), attenuated psychosis syndrome sits in the section for further study, not as an official diagnosis [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- Screen with the 16-item Prodromal Questionnaire (PQ-16): 6 or more positive items triggers a specialist interview; sensitivity and specificity were 87% in help-seekers [Ising-etal-2012]. Confirm with a structured risk interview in a specialist team.

## How it works

The same appraisal model applies as in psychosis: unusual experiences become distressing and entrenched when read as external threat, under stress, low mood and safety behaviors (see `bipolar-and-psychosis.cbt-model`). Early CBT targets distress, appraisals and coping before beliefs become fixed.

## Steps

1. **Refer for specialist assessment** to an early intervention or coordinated specialty care team where available.
2. **Engage around distress and goals**, not labels: school, work, friends, sleep.
3. **Normalize:** many people have occasional odd experiences; stress, sleep loss and cannabis make them more likely (expert consensus).
4. **Formulate** one recent episode as a cycle: experience, meaning, feeling, response.
5. **Treat what is there:** anxiety, depression, trauma, sleep and substance use often drive the distress; NICE advises the standard recommended treatments for these alongside CBT [NICE-CG178].
6. **Test appraisals** with behavioral experiments and alternative explanations; build emotion regulation and social coping. One adolescent trial protocol uses up to 12 individual and 4 family sessions [Jolley-etal-2017].
7. **Monitor** symptoms at set intervals, and agree with the client what would prompt faster review. If distress or symptoms persist without a clear psychosis diagnosis, NICE advises monitoring with a structured, validated tool for up to 3 years [NICE-CG178].
8. **Plan the ending:** early signs, who to contact, and a route back into care.

## Guidance

- If the client asks "Am I going to become psychotic?" → give honest numbers: most people in this group do not, and treatment may lower the risk further. Because about 1 in 4 transition by 3 years [SalazarDePablo-etal-2021] and pooled prevention trials show reduced transition [VanDerGaag-etal-2013].
- If a prescriber proposes antipsychotics only to prevent psychosis → share the NICE position and discuss. Because NICE advises against antipsychotics for this purpose [NICE-CG178].
- If symptoms become frequent, fixed and impairing for days → refer urgently to the psychosis team. Because this may be transition to first-episode psychosis, where earlier care improves outcomes [Kane-etal-2016].
- If the client reports suicidal thoughts or self-harm → assess risk that session and follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`, with crisis escalation if danger is imminent. Because depression and anxiety are common in this group and are what most clients seek help for (expert consensus).
- If cannabis use is regular → make reduction a shared goal with motivational work. Because substance use is a modifiable stressor (expert consensus).
- If the young person is under 18 → include family sessions with consent. Because adolescent protocols add family support meetings [Jolley-etal-2017].

Example phrasings:
- "Lots of people have experiences like this when they're stressed or not sleeping. Let's understand yours."
- "Most people with experiences like these do not go on to develop psychosis, and the work we do may lower the chance further."
- Avoid: "You're in the early stages of schizophrenia."

## Common problems

- Labeling the client as "prodromal," which implies certainty and increases stigma.
- Focusing on transition and missing the depression or anxiety that brought the client in.
- Discharging without a re-entry route.

## Evidence

- Transition estimates differ by era and sample: 18% at 6 months, 22% at 1 year and 36% at 3 years in a 2012 meta-analysis [FusarPoli-etal-2012], versus 9%, 15% and 25% in a 2021 update [SalazarDePablo-etal-2021]. Falling rates may reflect broader referral and earlier treatment.
- Pooled prevention trials (CBT, integrated psychological, antipsychotic and omega-3 trials together): risk ratio 0.463 at 12 months, number needed to treat 9; risk ratio 0.635 at 24 to 48 months. The CBT finding looked robust but with a wide confidence interval [VanDerGaag-etal-2013].
- Evidence strength: guideline-recommended, supported by randomized trials of moderate size; which preventive approach works best is still uncertain.
