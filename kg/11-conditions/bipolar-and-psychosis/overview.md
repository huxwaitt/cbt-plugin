**Bottom line:** Bipolar I disorder needs one manic episode; bipolar II needs a hypomanic and a major depressive episode; schizophrenia needs at least a month of active psychotic symptoms within six months of disturbance under the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fifth edition, text revision (DSM-5-TR) [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. Medication is the base of treatment, and cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is a recommended add-on for both [Yatham-etal-2018] [Serra-etal-2023]. Suicide risk is high in both, so screen at every contact [Palmer-etal-2005] [Yatham-etal-2018].

## Diagnostic essentials

Paraphrased; read the manual for criteria [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

**Bipolar and related disorders**

| Episode or disorder | Core requirements (DSM-5-TR) |
|---|---|
| Manic episode | Elevated, expansive or irritable mood plus increased activity or energy, most of the day, nearly every day, for at least 1 week (any length if hospitalized); at least 3 further symptoms (4 if mood is only irritable); marked impairment, hospitalization or psychotic features [APA-DSM5TR-2022] [Yatham-etal-2018] |
| Hypomanic episode | Same symptom picture for at least 4 consecutive days, observable by others, but no marked impairment, no hospitalization and no psychosis [APA-Psychiatry-Bipolar-Site] |
| Major depressive episode | At least 2 weeks, low mood or loss of interest plus other symptoms, five in total [APA-Psychiatry-Bipolar-Site] |
| Bipolar I | At least one manic episode; depression is common but not required |
| Bipolar II | At least one hypomanic and one major depressive episode, never a manic episode |
| Cyclothymic disorder | Hypomanic and depressive symptoms below episode threshold for at least 2 years (1 year in youth), present at least half the time, never absent more than 2 months [APA-Psychiatry-Bipolar-Site] |

Specifiers include mixed features, rapid cycling (4 or more episodes in 12 months), psychotic features, peripartum onset and seasonal pattern [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. A full manic episode that emerges on an antidepressant and persists beyond the drug's physiological effect counts toward bipolar I.

**Schizophrenia spectrum**

| Disorder | Duration and core requirements (DSM-5-TR) |
|---|---|
| Schizophrenia | Two or more of delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior, negative symptoms (one of the first three) for much of 1 month; signs for at least 6 months; functional decline [APA-DSM5TR-2022] |
| Schizophreniform disorder | Same symptoms, 1 to 6 months [APA-Psychiatry-Schizophrenia-Site] |
| Brief psychotic disorder | 1 day to under 1 month, then full return to baseline [APA-Psychiatry-Schizophrenia-Site] |
| Schizoaffective disorder | Major mood episode alongside schizophrenia symptoms, at least 2 weeks of delusions or hallucinations without a mood episode, and mood episodes present for most of the illness [APA-DSM5TR-2022] |
| Delusional disorder | One or more delusions for at least 1 month without other marked psychotic features [APA-Psychiatry-Schizophrenia-Site] |

Exclusion for all: a substance, medication or medical cause.

**Key differences in the International Classification of Diseases, 11th revision (ICD-11)**

- Schizophrenia needs only 1 month of symptoms, not 6; subtypes are replaced by symptom and course qualifiers; catatonia is a separate category [NicklJockschat-etal-2025] [Reed-etal-2019].
- Acute and transient psychotic disorder lasts no longer than 3 months; schizoaffective disorder is judged on the current episode [Reed-etal-2019].
- Mania needs increased activity or energy, and under 1 week counts if treatment cuts it short; hypomania needs "several days"; the mixed episode is kept and indicates bipolar type I [Severus-Bauer-2020] [Reed-etal-2019].

## Course and prevalence

- **Bipolar:** United States (US) lifetime prevalence 1.0% for bipolar I, 1.1% for bipolar II and 2.4% for subthreshold forms [Merikangas-etal-2007]. Average onset is about 25 [APA-Psychiatry-Bipolar-Site]. Most people present first with depression; diagnostic delays of up to 14.5 years are reported [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
- **Schizophrenia:** median lifetime morbid risk 7.2 per 1,000, male to female incidence ratio 1.4 to 1, all-cause mortality 2.6 times expected [McGrath-etal-2008]. Onset is usually late teens to early 20s in men and early 20s to early 30s in women [APA-Psychiatry-Schizophrenia-Site].

## Differential and comorbidity

- **Unipolar depression:** ask about past hypomania in every depressed client; early onset (before 25) and family history raise suspicion [Yatham-etal-2018].
- **Substance or medical causes:** stimulants, cannabis, steroids, thyroid and neurological disease (see `12-medical-pharmacology.medical-mimics`).
- **Trauma:** post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) can cause irritability and voice-like intrusions [VADoD-Bipolar-2023].
- **Obsessive-compulsive and related disorders with absent insight** stay in that category if no other psychotic features are present [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- **Borderline personality disorder:** mood shifts last hours and are reactive, not sustained episodes with reduced need for sleep (expert consensus).

## CBT approach in brief

CBT for bipolar disorder adds mood monitoring, relapse signatures, routine and sleep regulation and work on extreme appraisals of mood states to medication (see `bipolar-and-psychosis.bipolar-cbt`). CBT for psychosis (CBTp) builds a shared formulation of distressing experiences and tests threat beliefs (see `bipolar-and-psychosis.cbtp`).

## Risk notes

- About 4.9% of people with schizophrenia die by suicide, mostly near illness onset [Palmer-etal-2005]. About 6% to 7% of people with bipolar disorder die by suicide, and over 70% of suicide deaths and attempts happen during depression [Yatham-etal-2018].
- Violence risk in schizophrenia and other psychoses is modestly raised without substance misuse (odds ratio 2.1) and much higher with it (8.9), similar to substance misuse alone [Fazel-etal-2009]. Assess both directions (see `02-risk.harm-to-others`).
- Imminent danger, commands to harm, or mania with risky behavior: contact the psychiatric team or crisis service the same day.
- In crisis: US, call or text 988 (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or 911; United Kingdom (UK), 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123.
