**Bottom line:** Confirm the diagnosis by interview, not by questionnaire. Cover symptoms, episode history, suicide risk, past highs, medical and substance causes, and life context. Take a baseline Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) and repeat it every session, because scores guide treatment choice and progress review [NICE-NG222; VA-DoD-MDD-2022].

## When to use

- First assessment of anyone with low mood, loss of interest or a positive screen.
- Reassessment when treatment stalls, risk changes or elevated mood appears.
- Before choosing cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) alone, combination treatment or referral.

## Steps

1. **Screen.** A two-item PHQ (PHQ-2) score of 3 or more had 83% sensitivity and 92% specificity for major depression; follow a positive screen with the full PHQ-9 and an interview [Kroenke-etal-2003].
2. **Review symptoms.** Ask about each of the nine symptom domains over the past 2 weeks, plus hopelessness. Get onset, duration, past episodes, gaps between them, and past treatments with dose, length and response. Count a PHQ-9 symptom as present if endorsed on more than half the days, except suicidal ideation, which counts at "several days" [VA-DoD-MDD-2022].
3. **Assess risk.** Ask directly about suicidal thoughts, intent, plans, past attempts, self-harm and access to means. Check social support and whether the client knows where to get help [NICE-NG222]. Any positive answer on PHQ-9 item 9 needs a full risk assessment (`02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`). If there is intent, a plan or preparation, act that day: do not leave the client alone and contact crisis or emergency services (United States (US): 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, call or text 988, or 911; United Kingdom (UK): 999, NHS 111 (National Health Service) or Samaritans 116 123), following `02-risk.crisis-response`.
4. **Screen for bipolarity.** Ask about past days of unusually high or irritable mood with more energy and less need for sleep [NICE-NG222]. A positive Mood Disorder Questionnaire (MDQ) needs 7 or more of 13 items, several at the same time, causing moderate or serious problems; it had 0.73 sensitivity and 0.90 specificity in psychiatric outpatients [Hirschfeld-etal-2000].
5. **Screen medical and substance factors.** Ask about medical conditions, medications, alcohol and drugs. Ask the primary care clinician for labs such as blood count, thyroid function, electrolytes, vitamin B12 and folate [Coryell-2026].
6. **Ask differential questions** (table below).
7. **Map context.** Losses, trauma, relationships, housing, debt, employment and loneliness shape treatment [NICE-NG222].
8. **Gather collateral**, with consent (`01-intake.collateral-information`), when insight, memory or risk is a concern, and for children, who often need parent reports [WHO-CDDR-2024].
9. **Feed back** the provisional diagnosis, severity and a first sketch of the maintenance cycle.

| Differential | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Bipolar disorder | "Have you ever had days when you felt so good or wired that others worried?" |
| Grief | "Do the painful feelings come in waves, mostly about the person you lost?" [WHO-CDDR-2024] |
| Substance or medication | "Did the low mood start or worsen with a new medicine, drinking or drug use?" |
| Anxiety disorder | "Which came first, the worry or the low mood?" |
| Psychotic features | "Have you heard or believed things others found hard to accept?" |

**Measures** (`08-measurement.depression-measures` also covers the Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology, QIDS-SR16):

| Measure | Content | Cutoffs and change | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| PHQ-9 | 9 items, 0 to 3, past 2 weeks, total 0 to 27 | 5, 10, 15, 20 mark mild, moderate, moderately severe, severe [Kroenke-etal-2001]; 10 or more is the best screening cutoff (sensitivity 0.88, specificity 0.85) [Levis-etal-2019]; individual change of 5 points is clinically important [Lowe-etal-2004] | Free [PHQ-Screeners] |
| Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II) | 21 items, ages 13 to 80 [Pearson-BDI-II] | 0 to 13 minimal, 14 to 19 mild, 20 to 28 moderate, 29 to 63 severe [Wang-Gorenstein-2013] | Licensed, Pearson |

PHQ-9 bands differ by source: the US Departments of Veterans Affairs and Defense (VA/DoD) also use 0 to 5 none, 6 to 9 mild, 10 to 15 moderate, 16 or more severe [VA-DoD-MDD-2022]; the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) splits less from more severe at 16 [NICE-NG222]. Say which you use.

## What to record

- Each symptom domain present or absent, onset, duration, episode count and best inter-episode level.
- PHQ-9 total and item 9 at baseline, then every session.
- Risk: ideation, intent, plan, past attempts, access to means, protective factors, actions taken.
- Bipolar screen; medical, medication and substance findings; labs requested.
- Context factors, collateral sources and consent, provisional diagnosis with specifiers, and the client's own words for the problem.

## Guidance

- If PHQ-9 item 9 is above 0 → ask about suicidal thoughts in the session, that day, and record the answer. Because a questionnaire item is a prompt, not a risk assessment [NICE-NG222].
- If the client says "nothing will ever change" → treat it as a risk signal and ask about suicide before working on the thought. Because hopelessness predicted eventual suicide [Beck-etal-1985].
- If any past episode of elevated mood is described → pause CBT planning and arrange psychiatric assessment (`bipolar-and-psychosis.assessment`). Because NICE asks about mood elevation to detect bipolar disorder, which needs different treatment [NICE-NG222].
- If the PHQ-9 is high but the interview finds few symptoms → trust the interview and explore the gap. Because using the PHQ-9 alone to diagnose is discouraged [VA-DoD-MDD-2022].

Example phrasing: "Many people who feel this low have thoughts of not wanting to be alive. Have you had thoughts like that in the last two weeks?"

## Common problems

- Skipping the history of highs because the client presents low.
- Recording "denies suicidal ideation" without the questions asked or the follow-up.
- Missing chronicity: always ask "When did you last feel well for two months?" (see `depression.persistent-depression`).
