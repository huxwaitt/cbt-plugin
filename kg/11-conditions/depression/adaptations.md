**Bottom line:** Keep the core of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for depression (activation, testing thoughts, relapse prevention) and change the delivery to fit the person. Severity shifts the balance toward behavioral work, combination treatment and closer monitoring; comorbidity, age, pregnancy and culture change format and content. Refer or step up when there are psychotic features, a bipolar history, risk beyond outpatient care, or no response after 4 to 6 weeks [NICE-NG222].

## The principle

The United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises matching treatment to need and preference, using the least intrusive option that fits or that has worked before [NICE-NG222]. Adapt the delivery (pace, format, language, who is involved) before changing the content. Culturally adapted psychological treatments for depression mostly changed language, context and who delivered them, and they were effective (standardized mean difference -0.72) [Chowdhary-etal-2014].

## Guidance

**Severity and risk**
- If depression is severe, with slowing and poor concentration → shorten tasks, write things down, and lead with behavioral activation (BA). Because BA held up in more severe depression where cognitive therapy lagged [Dimidjian-etal-2006].
- If the client wants CBT alone for severe depression → offer it, with close monitoring. Because severity did not moderate CBT versus antidepressant outcomes [Weitz-etal-2015].
- If suicide risk is raised → keep treating the depression, increase contact and consider specialist referral. Because NICE advises against withholding depression treatment on the basis of suicide risk [NICE-NG222]. Crisis contacts: United States (US) 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) or 911; UK 999, NHS 111 (National Health Service) or Samaritans 116 123.
- If there are psychotic symptoms → refer to specialist mental health services. Because psychotic depression needs combined specialist treatment and risk assessment [NICE-NG222].

**Comorbidity**
- If anxiety symptoms accompany depression → usually treat the depression first. Because NICE gives depression priority here [NICE-NG222].
- If a full anxiety disorder came first or dominates, with depression secondary → consider treating the anxiety disorder first using its own protocol. Because NICE advises this sequence [NICE-NG222].
- If a personality disorder is present → combine medication with a structured psychological treatment, track mood with a validated measure, and extend treatment up to a year if needed (see `personality-disorders.treatment`). Because NICE advises this and says not to withhold depression treatment for coexisting personality disorder [NICE-NG222].
- If substance use is significant → assess its role in mood and address it in the plan (`substance-use.treatment`). Because NICE lists drug and alcohol use among the factors to assess in depression [NICE-NG222].
- If a chronic physical illness is present → link activity goals to what the illness allows and liaise with medical teams. Because NICE points to its guideline on depression with chronic physical health problems and to collaborative care [NICE-NG222].

**Age and life stage**
- If the client is 12 to 18 with moderate to severe depression → offer individual CBT for at least 3 months; if there is no response after 4 to 6 sessions, arrange multidisciplinary review. Because NICE sets this course and review point; the specialist team then considers fluoxetine and family factors [NICE-NG134].
- If a 12 to 18 year old has mild depression that persists after about 2 weeks of watchful waiting → offer digital or group therapy for about 2 to 3 months, or individual CBT if those do not fit. Because NICE sets this sequence [NICE-NG134].
- If the client is an older adult → offer psychotherapy first for mild to moderate depression, consider group CBT, and screen cognition. Because the US Veterans Affairs and Defense (VA/DoD) guideline suggests first-line psychotherapy at 65 and over [VA-DoD-MDD-2022], the American Psychological Association (APA) guideline recommends group CBT or group life review [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019], and depression can mimic dementia [Coryell-2026].
- If the client is pregnant, postnatal or breastfeeding → offer CBT or another evidence-based psychotherapy first, and involve the prescriber if medication is considered. Because VA/DoD recommends psychotherapy first-line for mild to moderate depression in pregnancy [VA-DoD-MDD-2022], and NICE offers medication for moderate or severe perinatal depression only after an informed discussion of risks [NICE-CG192].

**Culture and context**
- If the client describes distress mainly through pain, fatigue or weakness, or through cultural idioms → use their terms in the formulation. Because the salience of symptoms varies across cultures [WHO-CDDR-2024].
- If stigma, shame or family roles shape the problem → adapt examples, language and who attends, with consent. Because adapted treatments mostly changed language, context and delivery, not core content [Chowdhary-etal-2014].

**Medication and stepping up**
- If the client is on an antidepressant with partial response → offer CBT alongside it. Because in primary care treatment-resistant depression, CBT plus usual care doubled response (46% versus 22%) [Wiles-etal-2013].
- If there is no response at all after 4 to 6 weeks of therapy → review adherence, stressors, comorbidity and diagnosis, then discuss switching therapy or adding or switching to an antidepressant (`04-treatment-planning.managing-non-response`). Because NICE sets this checkpoint [NICE-NG222].
- If the client is starting or changing medication → check agitation and suicidal thoughts at the next session. Because risk can rise early in treatment [NICE-NG222].

## Example phrasings

- "Depression makes concentrating hard, so let's keep today short and write down what we agree."
- "In your family, how do people talk about feeling low? What words fit for you?"
- Avoid: "We must fix the anxiety before touching the depression." Decide sequence together from the formulation.

## Evidence

Guideline-recommended for adults and young people [NICE-NG222; APA-Depression-Guideline-2019; NICE-NG134]. CBT works in children and adolescents (g = 0.41) [Cuijpers-etal-2023a]. Older adult, perinatal and comorbidity advice rests on fewer trials and partly on expert consensus.
