**Bottom line:** Major depressive disorder (MDD) means at least 2 weeks of five or more of nine depressive symptoms, one of them depressed mood or loss of interest, with distress or impairment and no history of mania or hypomania [APA-DSM5TR-2022; Coryell-2026]. Persistent depressive disorder (PDD) means depressed mood on most days for at least 2 years [Coryell-2026]. Depression often recurs and carries suicide risk, so screen for both, and for past highs, before treating. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is a guideline-recommended first-line treatment [NICE-NG222; Cuijpers-etal-2023a].

## Diagnostic essentials

Paraphrased from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR); see the manual for the criteria [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

**Major depressive episode and MDD** [Coryell-2026; VA-DoD-MDD-2022]
- Five or more of nine symptoms in the same 2-week period, nearly every day: depressed mood; markedly reduced interest or pleasure; appetite or weight change; insomnia or hypersomnia; psychomotor agitation or slowing that others can see; fatigue; worthlessness or excessive guilt; poor concentration or indecision; recurrent thoughts of death, suicidal ideation, a plan or an attempt.
- At least one of the five is depressed mood or loss of interest. In children and adolescents low mood can show as irritability [WHO-CDDR-2024].
- Significant distress or impairment; not caused by a substance, medication or medical condition; not better explained by a psychotic disorder; never a manic or hypomanic episode [Coryell-2026].
- Record single or recurrent episode, severity, psychotic features and remission. Other specifiers: anxious distress, mixed features, melancholic, atypical, catatonia, peripartum onset (in pregnancy or the 4 weeks after delivery) and seasonal pattern [Coryell-2026].
- Bereavement does not rule out MDD. Since DSM-5 (2013) the clinician judges whether symptoms exceed a normal grief response [Uher-etal-2014]. Grief comes in waves with self-esteem kept; MDD brings pervasive low mood and worthlessness [APA-WhatIsDepression-2024].

**PDD (dysthymia):** depressed mood most of the day, more days than not, for at least 2 years (1 year in children and adolescents), plus at least two associated symptoms from appetite, sleep, energy, self-esteem, concentration or decisions, and hopelessness [Coryell-2026; APA-WhatIsDepression-2024]. Symptoms do not lift for more than 2 months at a time [VA-DoD-MDD-2022]. PDD covers dysthymia with or without superimposed major depressive episodes, chronic MDD, and recurrent MDD without recovery between episodes [Schramm-etal-2020].

**International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) differences** [Reed-etal-2019; WHO-CDDR-2024]
- Categories: single episode depressive disorder (6A70), recurrent depressive disorder (6A71), dysthymic disorder (6A72), mixed depressive and anxiety disorder (6A73).
- A depressive episode needs at least five of ten symptoms over 2 weeks, with depressed mood or reduced interest required. Hopelessness is the added tenth symptom, and fatigue alone cannot be the entry symptom.
- Normal grief within 6 months of a death (longer if culturally normative) is not an episode; constant symptoms 1 month or more after the loss, worthlessness unrelated to the deceased, psychotic symptoms, suicidal ideation or psychomotor slowing suggest one.
- Dysthymic disorder requires that no depressive episode occurred in the first 2 years. DSM-5-TR PDD instead absorbs chronic major depression.

## Course and prevalence

- United States (US) adults: 12-month MDD prevalence 6.6% and lifetime 16.2% in the National Comorbidity Survey Replication (NCS-R) [Kessler-etal-2003]; 10.4% and 20.6% in the 2012 to 2013 national epidemiologic survey (NESARC-III) [Hasin-etal-2018].
- In 2021, 8.3% of US adults and 20.1% of adolescents aged 12 to 17 had a past-year major depressive episode [NIMH-MDD-Stats].
- PDD affects about 0.5% of US adults each year and often starts in childhood, adolescence or early adulthood [APA-WhatIsDepression-2024].
- Untreated episodes typically last 3 to 4 months and most remit within a year. About half recur within 5 years, risk rises with each episode, and residual symptoms speed relapse [WHO-CDDR-2024].

## Differential and comorbidity

- **Bipolar disorder:** ask about past high or irritable periods with more energy (`bipolar-and-psychosis.pitfall-missed-bipolarity`). Early onset, a family history of bipolar disorder and psychotic features raise the risk of later bipolar transition [WHO-CDDR-2024].
- **Medical and substance causes:** hypothyroidism, Parkinson disease, medications and substances [Coryell-2026] (`12-medical-pharmacology.medical-mimics`).
- **Grief, prolonged grief disorder, demoralization and, in older adults, dementia** [Coryell-2026].
- **Comorbidity is the rule:** 72.1% of lifetime MDD cases had another disorder [Kessler-etal-2003]. The strongest link was with generalized anxiety disorder (adjusted odds ratio 5.7) [Hasin-etal-2018]. With anxiety symptoms, usually treat the depression first; with a primary anxiety disorder, consider treating that first [NICE-NG222].

## CBT approach in brief

- Formulate (`depression.cbt-model`), treat with CBT or behavioral activation (`depression.treatment`), and after remission plan relapse prevention (`depression.relapse-prevention-mbct`).
- In a meta-analysis of 409 trials, CBT beat control conditions with a moderate to large effect (g = 0.79). It matched antidepressants at the end of treatment and did better at 6 to 12 months, with few follow-up trials [Cuijpers-etal-2023a]. Evidence strength: guideline-recommended, many randomized trials.

## Risk notes

- Ask every client with depression directly about suicidal ideation and intent [NICE-NG222]. Hopelessness predicted eventual suicide in hospitalized patients [Beck-etal-1985]. Ask about access to lethal means and help restrict it (`02-risk.safety-planning`).
- Agitation, anxiety and suicidal thoughts can rise in the early weeks of treatment. Warn the client, check at each contact, and make sure they know how to get help [NICE-NG222].
- Imminent risk → urgent referral to specialist mental health services or emergency services [NICE-NG222]. US: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) or 911. United Kingdom (UK): 999, NHS 111 (National Health Service) or Samaritans 116 123. See `02-risk.crisis-response`.
- Severe depression features include suicidal ideation with intent or plan, a suicide attempt, homicidal ideation, psychotic symptoms, dangerous weight loss, or inability to manage daily self-care, including catatonia [VA-DoD-MDD-2022]. Psychotic depression needs specialist referral [NICE-NG222].
