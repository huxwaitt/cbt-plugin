**Bottom line:** Depression often comes back: about half of people have a recurrence within 5 years, and risk rises with each episode [WHO-CDDR-2024]. After remission, offer relapse prevention to anyone at higher risk. Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT), an 8-session group, cut relapse compared with usual care (hazard ratio 0.69) [Kuyken-etal-2016b] and in one large trial did not differ from maintenance antidepressants [Kuyken-etal-2015]. Group or individual cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) focused on staying well is the main alternative [NICE-NG222].

## When to use

Higher relapse risk, per the United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE): recurrent episodes (especially frequent or within the last 2 years), incomplete response or residual symptoms, avoidance or rumination, a history of severe depression, other chronic health problems, and ongoing stressors such as poverty or isolation [NICE-NG222].

- Recovered after antidepressants alone and wants to stop them → group CBT or MBCT, with a supervised taper [NICE-NG222].
- Recovered after therapy → continue the same therapy for at least 4 more sessions with a relapse prevention focus [NICE-NG222].
- Three or more past episodes → MBCT has its clearest evidence here [Teasdale-etal-2000; Ma-Teasdale-2004].

## How it works

MBCT trains recovered clients to notice early mood dips and step out of the depressive thinking that low mood triggers, instead of getting pulled into rumination [Teasdale-etal-2000]. Its theme is relating to thoughts differently ("Thoughts are not facts") rather than disputing them one by one [Segal-etal-2013]. Preventive and continuation CBT instead rehearse cognitive and behavioral skills and plan for triggers [Bockting-etal-2005; Vittengl-etal-2007].

## Steps

**MBCT program** [Segal-etal-2013]: weekly group sessions with home practice using recordings between sessions [NICE-NG222].

| Session | Theme |
|---|---|
| 1 | Awareness and automatic pilot |
| 2 | Living in our heads |
| 3 | Gathering the scattered mind |
| 4 | Recognizing aversion |
| 5 | Allowing, letting be |
| 6 | Thoughts are not facts |
| 7 | How can I best take care of myself? |
| 8 | Maintaining and extending new learning |

NICE describes group CBT or MBCT for relapse prevention as usually 8 sessions over 2 to 3 months, with optional further sessions over the next 12 months [NICE-NG222].

**Relapse prevention content for any therapy** [NICE-NG222]:
1. Review what was learned and what helped.
2. Make concrete plans to keep practicing useful strategies.
3. List personal triggers, warning signs (such as poor sleep or anxiety) and unhelpful behaviors (such as avoidance or rumination), with a written plan for each.
4. Plan for hard events over the next 12 months, including anniversaries.
5. Reassess relapse risk at the end and decide on follow-up.

## Guidance

- If the client has had three or more episodes and is now well → offer MBCT or relapse-focused CBT. Because MBCT benefits were clearest in this group [Teasdale-etal-2000; Ma-Teasdale-2004].
- If residual symptoms remain → prioritize relapse prevention before discharge. Because higher residual symptoms predicted relapse [Breedvelt-etal-2021], and MBCT's effect was larger in those with more residual symptoms [Kuyken-etal-2016b].
- If the client wants to stop antidepressants → coordinate a gradual taper with the prescriber while therapy runs. Because therapy during tapering did about as well as staying on medication [Breedvelt-etal-2021; Kuyken-etal-2015].
- If home practice is not happening → explore barriers and shorten practices, but keep some regular practice. Because NICE names willingness to practice with recordings at home as a requirement [NICE-NG222].
- If mood dips during follow-up → act on the written plan early and offer a booster. Because NICE builds optional further sessions into the year after treatment [NICE-NG222].

Example phrasings:
- "What were the very first signs last time, before it became a full episode?"
- "When that thought shows up, can you notice it as a thought, the way you noticed the breath drifting?"
- Avoid: "You're better now, so you won't need this." It ignores the recurrence risk.

## Common problems

- **Group not available:** use individual relapse-focused CBT or continuation sessions.
- **Current moderate to severe episode:** treat the episode first. MBCT trials recruited people in remission [Kuyken-etal-2016b].
- **Mindfulness felt as one more thing to fail at:** frame practice as noticing, not achieving.
- **Plan filed and forgotten:** give the client a copy and review it at boosters.

## Evidence

- MBCT versus no MBCT across 9 trials (1,258 patients): relapse 38% versus 49% within 60 weeks (hazard ratio 0.69); versus active treatments, hazard ratio 0.79. In the pooled data, number of past episodes and age at onset did not change MBCT's effect [Kuyken-etal-2016b].
- In people with three or more episodes, MBCT reduced relapse from 78% to 36% in one trial [Ma-Teasdale-2004]; it did not help those with only two episodes [Teasdale-etal-2000].
- MBCT with antidepressant tapering did not differ from maintenance antidepressants over 24 months (hazard ratio 0.89) [Kuyken-etal-2015].
- After acute cognitive therapy, 29% relapsed within 1 year and 54% within 2 years. Continuation cognitive therapy cut relapse by 21% versus assessment only [Vittengl-etal-2007]. Cognitive therapy's protective effect outlasts treatment [Hollon-etal-2005].
- Preventive cognitive group therapy cut relapse from 72% to 46% in people with five or more episodes [Bockting-etal-2005].
- Guidelines: NICE recommends group CBT or MBCT [NICE-NG222]. The American Psychological Association (APA) suggests CBT, MBCT or interpersonal psychotherapy over medication or usual care (conditional) [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019]. The United States (US) Departments of Veterans Affairs and Defense (VA/DoD) guideline suggests CBT, interpersonal therapy or MBCT after remission for those at high risk, such as two or more prior episodes or unstable remission [VA-DoD-MDD-2022].
