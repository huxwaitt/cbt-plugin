**Bottom line:** Individual cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for depression is guideline-recommended first-line care. It usually runs about 8 sessions for less severe and 16 for more severe depression [NICE-NG222]. Start with behavioral activation, then identify and test automatic thoughts, address beliefs if needed, and finish with relapse prevention [Beck-etal-1979; Jacobson-etal-1996]. Measure every session, review progress with the client 2 to 4 weeks in, and change course if there is no response at all by 4 to 6 weeks [NICE-NG222].

## When to use

- Adults with major depressive disorder of any severity who choose psychological treatment, alone or with an antidepressant.
- The United Kingdom (UK) National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) defines less severe depression as a Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) score below 16 and more severe as 16 or more [NICE-NG222].
- Refer instead of, or alongside, outpatient CBT when there are psychotic features, a bipolar history, or risk that outpatient care cannot hold (`depression.adaptations`).

## How it works

CBT breaks the maintenance cycles in `depression.cbt-model`. Activity scheduling restores reward and mastery; testing automatic thoughts weakens biased appraisals; belief work reduces vulnerability; relapse prevention keeps skills in use. Behavioral activation (BA) alone matched the full package in a component trial [Jacobson-etal-1996].

## Steps

A 16-session outline for more severe depression. Compress to about 8 sessions for less severe depression [NICE-NG222; Gautam-etal-2020].

| Sessions | Focus | Key tasks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Assessment, engagement | Measures, risk review, socialize to the model, goals, first cycle diagram, start activity monitoring [Beck-2021] |
| 2 to 6 | Behavioral activation | Review monitoring; schedule pleasant, mastery and value-based activities; graded tasks; problem solving; hopelessness and risk check each time [Beck-2021] |
| 5 to 11 | Automatic thoughts | Catch hot thoughts, thought records, Socratic questioning, behavioral experiments, concrete processing for rumination |
| 10 to 14 | Beliefs | Identify rules and core beliefs; modify them with evidence, experiments and new experiences (if needed) |
| 14 to 16 | Relapse prevention | Write the blueprint, rehearse early-warning plans, space sessions out, plan boosters [Beck-2021] |

Each session follows the same structure: mood check with the PHQ-9, bridge from last session, agenda, homework review, agenda items, new homework, summary and feedback [Beck-2021] (see `05-session-structure.session-structure`).

## Guidance

- If the client is slowed, exhausted or can barely leave bed → lead with activation and small graded tasks, not thought records. Because among more severely depressed clients BA matched medication and both beat cognitive therapy in one trial [Dimidjian-etal-2006].
- If there is no improvement at all after 4 to 6 weeks → review adherence, life stressors, comorbidity and the diagnosis, then discuss switching therapy or adding a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI). Because NICE sets this checkpoint for psychological therapy [NICE-NG222].
- If the client prefers both therapy and medication for more severe depression → support combination treatment and liaise with the prescriber. Because NICE lists individual CBT plus an antidepressant first for more severe depression [NICE-NG222], and combined treatment beats medication alone [Cuijpers-etal-2023a].
- If scores fall but residual symptoms remain → keep going toward remission and add relapse prevention. Because residual symptoms predict relapse (`depression.pitfall-stopping-at-response`).
- If PHQ-9 item 9 rises or hopelessness deepens → stop the agenda and assess risk that session; if risk is imminent, use emergency routes: United States (US) 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) or 911, UK 999 or Samaritans 116 123 (`02-risk.crisis-response`). Because NICE asks for suicidal ideation monitoring, especially early in treatment [NICE-NG222].

Example phrasings:
- "This week, let's pick one small thing that used to matter to you and put it in the diary at a set time."
- "What went through your mind just before your mood dropped?"
- Avoid: "Just try to think more positively." It dismisses the client and tests nothing.

## Common problems

- **Homework not done:** shrink the task, plan time and place, and explore the thought behind not doing it ("What's the point?").
- **Agenda swallowed by crises:** put the crisis on the agenda and use problem solving.
- **Agreement without felt change:** shift to behavioral experiments.
- **Early dropout:** check the alliance and expectations every session with feedback.

## Evidence

- **Efficacy:** across 409 trials CBT beat control (g = 0.79), matched pharmacotherapy short term and beat it at 6 to 12 months (g = 0.34). Combined treatment beat medication alone (g = 0.51) but not CBT alone [Cuijpers-etal-2023a]. Evidence strength: supported by many randomized trials.
- **Severity:** in moderate to severe depression, cognitive therapy and medication each reached 58% response at 16 weeks [DeRubeis-etal-2005]. An individual patient data meta-analysis found baseline severity did not moderate CBT versus antidepressant outcomes [Weitz-etal-2015]. Results may depend on therapist expertise [DeRubeis-etal-2005].
- **Durability:** after stopping, relapse was 30.8% following cognitive therapy versus 76.2% following medication withdrawal [Hollon-etal-2005].
- **BA alternative:** BA delivered by junior workers was non-inferior to CBT and cost less [Richards-etal-2016].
- **Guidelines:** NICE lists individual CBT (usually 8 sessions for less severe, 16 for more severe depression) and individual BA (usually 8, or 12 to 16) [NICE-NG222]. The American Psychological Association (APA) guideline recommends psychotherapy or a second-generation antidepressant, found comparable effects across therapy models, and for combined treatment recommends CBT or interpersonal psychotherapy plus an antidepressant [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019]. It was set to expire in about 2024; check for a successor [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019]. The American College of Physicians (ACP) recommends CBT or a second-generation antidepressant alone as initial treatment for moderate to severe depression (strong), and suggests CBT alone for mild depression (conditional) [Qaseem-etal-2023]. The US Departments of Veterans Affairs and Defense (VA/DoD) guideline strongly recommends psychotherapy or medication as monotherapy by preference, and suggests combination for severe (PHQ-9 above 20), persistent or recurrent depression [VA-DoD-MDD-2022].
