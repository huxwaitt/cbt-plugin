**Bottom line:** Rumination is repetitive, passive focus on one's distress and its possible causes and consequences, without moving to action [Nolen-Hoeksema-1991]. It deepens low mood, biases thinking, impairs problem solving and erodes social support, and it is linked to depression, anxiety, binge eating, binge drinking and self-harm [Nolen-Hoeksema-etal-2008]. Treat it as a habit with triggers and functions, not as a set of thoughts to dispute.

## Components

- **Trigger.** Low mood, a setback, a criticism, an unresolved goal, a quiet moment (lying in bed, commuting).
- **The process.** Repeated abstract "why" questions: "Why am I like this?" "Why does this always happen?" Repetitive negative thinking (RNT) shares three features across disorders: it is repetitive, partly intrusive and hard to disengage from [Ehring-etal-2011].
- **Level of thinking.** Abstract, evaluative thinking ("What does this say about me?") tends to be unhelpful; concrete, specific thinking ("What exactly happened, and what is one next step?") tends to aid recovery [Watkins-2008].
- **Brooding versus reflection.** The Ruminative Responses Scale (RRS) has two subscales: brooding, a passive comparison of one's situation with an unmet standard, and reflection, a purposeful turning inward to solve problems. Treynor and colleagues proposed that brooding is the maladaptive part and reflection the adaptive one [Treynor-etal-2003]; later studies confirm brooding's link to depression more consistently than any benefit of reflection [Hasegawa-etal-2014].
- **Beliefs about rumination.** Many clients believe it helps them understand themselves or prevents future mistakes.

## How it keeps the problem going

| Link | Effect |
|---|---|
| Rumination to mood | Depressed mood is prolonged because rumination lets mood bias thinking [Nolen-Hoeksema-1991] |
| Rumination to action | Interferes with instrumental behavior and problem solving [Nolen-Hoeksema-etal-2008] |
| Rumination to relationships | Erodes social support [Nolen-Hoeksema-etal-2008] |
| Mood to rumination | Low mood cues more "why" questions, closing the loop |

Rumination more consistently predicts the onset of depression than its duration, and it interacts with negative cognitive styles to predict how long symptoms last [Nolen-Hoeksema-etal-2008]. In a meta-analysis of self-focused attention, ruminative self-focus showed stronger links with negative affect than nonruminative self-focus [Mor-Winquist-2002]. Across disorders, rumination had the largest association with symptoms of six emotion regulation strategies [Aldao-etal-2010].

## Drawing it with the client

Do a functional analysis of one recent episode: when it started, where the client was, what they were doing, what the first question was, how long it lasted, how they felt after, and what they did not do because of it.

- If the client believes rumination helps → ask for evidence from the last few episodes: "When you went over it for two hours on Sunday, what did you work out? How did you feel after?" Because rumination usually impairs problem solving rather than delivering answers [Nolen-Hoeksema-etal-2008].
- If the client brings a new ruminative topic each session → map the process (trigger, duration, effect) rather than evaluating each topic's content. Because RNT is defined by its process, which is shared across content [Ehring-etal-2011].
- If rumination is mostly "why" questions → practice switching to "how" questions about one concrete step. Because concrete processing is linked to constructive outcomes and abstract processing to unconstructive ones [Watkins-2008].
- If episodes cluster in inactive times → schedule activity at those times. Because positive distraction relieves depressed mood in experiments [Nolen-Hoeksema-etal-2008].
- If rumination centers on self-blame or worthlessness, or you notice hopelessness → ask directly about suicidal thoughts the same session and follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment` and `02-risk.safety-planning`. Because rumination is linked to self-harm [Nolen-Hoeksema-etal-2008]. In a crisis in the US, call or text 988 or call 911; in the UK, call 999, NHS 111 or Samaritans on 116 123.

Example phrasing: "It sounds like your mind keeps asking why. Let's look at what happens to your mood while it does that, rather than trying to answer the why."
Avoid: "Let's work out once and for all why this happened."

What to record: triggers, typical duration per episode, episodes per week, mood before and after (0 to 100), and the client's beliefs about what rumination does for them.

## Treatment targets it implies

- **Rumination-focused cognitive behavioral therapy (RFCBT).** Developed from behavioral activation principles, it targets rumination directly. Adding up to 12 sessions to usual care for 42 people with medication-refractory residual depression improved residual symptoms and remission, mediated by change in rumination [Watkins-etal-2011]. In 131 outpatients, 12 sessions of group RFCBT beat group standard cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) on observer-rated depression at post-treatment (d 0.38), with no difference in rumination or in depression at 6 months [Hvenegaard-etal-2020]. Evidence: supported by small randomized trials.
- **Behavioral activation** for the inactivity rumination fills (`behavioral.behavioral-activation`).
- **Concreteness training and alternative responses** (`cognitive.worry-and-rumination-techniques`).
- **Self-compassion work** where rumination is self-attacking (`15-transdiagnostic-processes.self-criticism-and-shame`).
