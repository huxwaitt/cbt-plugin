# 15-transdiagnostic-processes: Transdiagnostic Processes

## This stage
This domain covers the processes that keep problems going across diagnoses: avoidance, safety behaviors, worry, rumination, intolerance of uncertainty, attention biases, thought suppression, perfectionism, self-criticism and shame, emotion regulation difficulties and cognitive distortions. It feeds assessment and case formulation, where processes explain what maintains a client's problems, and treatment planning, where they become targets or point to a transdiagnostic protocol. Use it alongside the condition pages, not instead of them.

## Decisions in order
1. Decide whether a process-level view will help this client, especially with comorbidity or no clear diagnosis → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.transdiagnostic-overview`
2. Screen, analyze and measure the processes that may be maintaining the problems → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.assessing-transdiagnostic-processes`
3. If the client stays away from situations, tasks, feelings or memories, map the avoidance → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.avoidance`
4. If fear persists despite facing situations, look for safety behaviors → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.safety-behaviors`
5. If repetitive thinking is future-focused, formulate worry → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.worry`
6. If repetitive thinking dwells on causes, meanings and losses, formulate rumination → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.rumination`
7. If not knowing is intolerable and drives checking or reassurance seeking, formulate intolerance of uncertainty → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.intolerance-of-uncertainty`
8. If the client scans for threat or watches themselves, map attention → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.attention-processes`
9. If the client tries to push thoughts away, address suppression → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.thought-suppression`
10. If self-worth depends on demanding standards, formulate clinical perfectionism → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.perfectionism`
11. If the client attacks themselves or feels fundamentally flawed, formulate self-criticism and shame → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.self-criticism-and-shame`
12. If emotions feel overwhelming or are managed by avoidance, suppression or harmful behaviors, formulate emotion regulation → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.emotion-regulation`
13. Identify and tag biased thinking styles in automatic thoughts → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.cognitive-distortions`
14. If one process drives several disorders or comorbidity is high, consider a transdiagnostic protocol → `15-transdiagnostic-processes.transdiagnostic-treatments`

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Disputing Each Worry Instead of the Worry Process | Each session the therapist disputes a new worry or ruminative topic; the client feels briefly reassured, a fresh topic appears next week, and time spent worrying does not fall. | `15-transdiagnostic-processes.pitfall-disputing-repetitive-thought-content` |
| Coping Skills That Quietly Become Safety Behaviors | The client faithfully uses breathing, distraction, reassuring self-talk or a companion during exposures and homework, yet fear and threat beliefs barely change and setbacks follow whenever the strategy is unavailable. | `15-transdiagnostic-processes.pitfall-coping-that-becomes-avoidance` |
| Telling Clients to Stop or Push Away Unwanted Thoughts | The therapist advises "try not to think about it," thought stopping or distraction as escape; intrusions keep returning, distress rises, and the client concludes their mind is out of control. | `15-transdiagnostic-processes.pitfall-telling-clients-to-stop-thinking` |
