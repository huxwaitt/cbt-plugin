**Bottom line:** Self-criticism is a habitual, hostile way of relating to oneself after setbacks; shame is the painful sense that the whole self is flawed or exposed. Both are transdiagnostic: self-criticism is linked to depression, eating disorders, social anxiety, personality disorders and psychotic symptoms [Werner-etal-2019], and shame is more strongly linked to depressive symptoms than guilt is (r = 0.43 versus 0.28) [Kim-etal-2011]. Harsh forms resist logical challenge, so cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) adds work on a warmer inner stance.

## What it is

- **Self-criticism** is not one thing. In a study of 246 female students, Gilbert and colleagues separated an "inadequate self" form (dwelling on mistakes, feeling not good enough) from a "hated self" form (wanting to hurt the self, self-disgust). Its functions also differ: self-improving ("If I'm hard on myself I'll do better") versus self-persecuting (wanting to punish the self). The hated-self, self-persecuting form appeared most harmful [Gilbert-etal-2004].
- **Shame** focuses on the whole self ("I am bad"); **guilt** focuses on a specific act ("I did something bad") [Tangney-etal-2007]. External shame is about how one exists in others' eyes; internal shame is how one judges oneself. External shame showed a larger association with depressive symptoms (r = 0.56) than internal shame (r = 0.42) [Kim-etal-2011].
- Gilbert's model describes three emotion systems: threat, drive and soothing. In people high in shame and self-criticism the threat system dominates and the soothing system is hard to access [Gilbert-2009].

## Why it matters in CBT

- **It is common across disorders.** A review of 48 studies found self-criticism related to eating disorder, social anxiety, personality disorder and psychotic symptoms as well as depression [Werner-etal-2019].
- **Shame is linked to anxiety too.** Shame was generally more strongly associated with anxiety symptoms than guilt, and external shame especially with social anxiety [Candea-SzentagotaiTatar-2018].
- **It blocks standard techniques.** Clients may say they understand the logic of an alternative thought but it does not help them feel better; Gilbert's conclusion is that logic is not enough and alternatives must feel kind and supportive [Gilbert-2009].
- **It is harder to shift in its harshest form.** Compassion- and emotion-focused interventions reduced self-criticism, but harsh forms were more persistent [Werner-etal-2019].
- **It can be reduced.** Across 19 randomized trials (1,350 participants), self-compassion-related interventions produced a medium reduction in self-criticism compared with controls (g = 0.51), with larger effects for longer interventions and passive controls [Wakelin-etal-2022].

## Key details

| Sign | Ask |
|---|---|
| Name-calling the self ("idiot," "disgusting") | "What tone does that voice use? Whose voice does it sound like?" |
| Belief that criticism keeps them in line | "What do you fear would happen if you were less harsh?" |
| Hiding, avoiding eye contact, wanting to disappear | "What do you imagine people would see?" |
| "I deserve to suffer" | Ask about self-harm and suicidal thoughts directly |

## In the session

- If the client believes self-criticism motivates them → explore its function and cost before trying to change it. "If a coach spoke to a player like that after every mistake, how would the player do?" Because self-criticism has self-improving and self-persecuting functions that differ in harm [Gilbert-etal-2004].
- If the client says "I know it's not true but I still feel it" → shift from testing the thought to practicing a warmer tone toward it (`cognitive.self-compassion-techniques`). Because alternatives need to feel supportive, not just logical [Gilbert-2009].
- If self-attack includes hatred or wanting to hurt the self → assess suicide and self-harm risk the same session and follow `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`. In the US, call or text 988 or call 911 in an emergency; in the UK, call 999, NHS 111 or Samaritans on 116 123. Because the hated-self form is the most pathogenic [Gilbert-etal-2004].
- If the client feels ashamed in session (looks down, goes quiet, apologizes) → slow down, validate, and normalize before asking for detail. Because shame drives hiding, and pressing for disclosure can deepen it [Tangney-etal-2007].
- If the client resists compassion ("I don't deserve kindness") → treat this fear as a belief to explore gently, not a refusal. Because the soothing system can feel unfamiliar or threatening in people high in shame [Gilbert-2009].
- Avoid: "You shouldn't be so hard on yourself." It adds a new thing to fail at.

Example phrasing: "That sounded like a very harsh voice. If a close friend had made the same mistake, what would you say to them?"

What to record: typical self-critical phrases in the client's words, triggers, form (inadequate or hated self), beliefs about its function, shame cues, risk screening, and any measure such as the 22-item Forms of Self-Criticizing/Attacking and Self-Reassuring Scale (FSCRS) [Gilbert-etal-2004].
