**Bottom line:** Clinical perfectionism is the overdependence of self-evaluation on the determined pursuit and achievement of self-imposed, personally demanding standards in at least one valued area, despite adverse consequences [Shafran-etal-2002]. It is a risk and maintaining factor across eating disorders, anxiety disorders and depression [Egan-etal-2011]. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for perfectionism has medium to large effects on perfectionism and medium effects on eating disorder and depressive symptoms [Galloway-etal-2022].

## Components

The Shafran, Cooper and Fairburn model [Shafran-etal-2002]:

1. **Self-worth tied to striving.** "I am only as good as my last result."
2. **Demanding, rigid standards** in one or more domains (work, study, sport, eating and weight, appearance, tidiness, parenting).
3. **Biased evaluation of performance.** All-or-nothing judgments, attention to mistakes, discounting success.
4. **Failure to meet the standard brings self-criticism.**
5. **Meeting the standard leads to raising it**, judged as "too easy, so it doesn't count."
6. **Performance checking and avoidance.** Repeated checking, procrastination, avoiding tests of performance.

Perfectionism is often described in two dimensions: perfectionistic strivings (high personal standards) and perfectionistic concerns (fear of mistakes, doubts, discrepancy). In a meta-analysis of 284 studies, both dimensions were associated with psychopathology [Limburg-etal-2017]. Hewitt and colleagues dispute the narrow, self-oriented definition and argue for a multidimensional model that includes interpersonal forms, such as believing others demand perfection [Hewitt-etal-2003]. In practice, assess both self-imposed and perceived external pressure.

## How it keeps the problem going

| Cycle | Example |
|---|---|
| Failure, then self-criticism, then more effort | Gets a B, calls self stupid, studies until 2 a.m. |
| Success, then standards rise | Hits the weight target, sets a lower one |
| Checking keeps attention on flaws | Rereads a report ten times, finds new errors |
| Avoidance prevents disconfirmation | Does not submit, so never learns a "good enough" version is accepted |
| Narrowing of life | Time goes to one domain; other sources of worth fade |

In eating disorders, perfectionism often expresses itself directly through control over eating, shape and weight [Shafran-etal-2002], and it is one of four extra maintaining processes in the transdiagnostic eating disorder model [Fairburn-etal-2003].

## Drawing it with the client

Start from a recent episode of falling short or of succeeding, and trace what happened to the standard and to how the client judged themselves.

- If the client says high standards are why they succeed → separate healthy striving from the costs. Ask: "What has pursuing this cost you this month in sleep, relationships or enjoyment?" Because clinical perfectionism is defined by pursuit despite adverse consequences [Shafran-etal-2002].
- If success never feels like success → draw the "moving goalposts" loop. Because meeting a standard leads to raising it [Shafran-etal-2002].
- If the client fears that easing standards will make them lazy or a failure → set up a behavioral experiment: do one task to a "good enough" standard and record the actual outcome and others' reactions. Because avoidance and checking keep perfectionism untested [Shafran-etal-2002].
- If self-worth rests on one domain → draw a pie chart of what currently makes up self-evaluation and plan to build other slices. Because overdependence of self-worth on one domain is the core feature [Shafran-etal-2002].
- If you hear hopelessness, "I'd be better off dead if I fail," or self-harm → assess suicide risk directly the same session (`02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`). Because perfectionistic concerns and strivings both show small to moderate associations with suicide ideation, and socially prescribed perfectionism predicts increases over time [Smith-etal-2018b]. In the US give the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) or 911; in the UK, 999, NHS 111 or Samaritans on 116 123.

Example phrasing: "Your standards have got you a lot. I'm curious whether the way you judge yourself against them is also costing you something."
Avoid: "You just need to lower your standards."

What to record: domains, the rules in the client's words, checking and avoidance behaviors with frequency, the Clinical Perfectionism Questionnaire (CPQ) total (12 items, with strivings and concerns factors, validated in female adolescents) [Shu-etal-2020], and risk screening results.

## Treatment targets it implies

- Psychoeducation with the client's own maintenance diagram.
- Surveys and behavioral experiments testing feared consequences of lowered standards.
- Reducing performance checking and avoidance.
- Broadening the basis of self-evaluation.
- Self-compassion for the self-criticism after failure (`cognitive.self-compassion-techniques`).

Evidence: across 15 randomized trials (912 participants, mean age 23), CBT for perfectionism, self-help or face to face, reduced concern over mistakes (g 0.89) and clinical perfectionism (g 0.87), eating disorder symptoms (g 0.61), depression (g 0.60) and anxiety (g 0.42). Trials were few and lacked active comparisons [Galloway-etal-2022].
