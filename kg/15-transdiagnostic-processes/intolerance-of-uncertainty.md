**Bottom line:** Intolerance of uncertainty (IU) is the tendency to find the possibility of a negative event unacceptable, however unlikely it is [Carleton-etal-2007]. A meta-analysis of 181 studies found moderate associations between IU and symptoms of generalized anxiety, social anxiety, panic, agoraphobia, obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), depression and eating disorders (r = 0.51) [McEvoy-etal-2019]. Treatments, including cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), reduce IU, and greater reductions go with greater symptom change [Miller-McGuire-2023].

## What it is

IU is a dispositional trait: a stable way of reacting to not knowing. It was first developed to explain worry in generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), and the IU model is one of five contemporary GAD models reviewed by Behar and colleagues [Behar-etal-2009]. Carleton later argued that fear of the unknown may be a fundamental fear underlying anxiety, a transdiagnostic idea closely tied to IU [Carleton-2016].

The 27-item Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale (IUS) was shortened to a 12-item form (IUS-12), which has two factors: an anxious, forward-looking (prospective) component and an avoidance (inhibitory) component [Carleton-etal-2007].

## Why it matters in CBT

- **It is shared across disorders.** The association with symptoms held across seven disorder groups in 181 studies (52,402 participants), with high heterogeneity and small moderator effects [McEvoy-etal-2019].
- **It drives many safety behaviors.** Checking, reassurance seeking, over-preparing, list making, information searching and refusing to delegate are often attempts to make uncertainty go away. They work briefly and teach that uncertainty must be removed.
- **It is changeable.** Across 28 randomized controlled trials (RCTs), evidence-based treatments for anxiety-related disorders had a large effect on IU compared with control conditions (g = 0.89), and change in IU accounted for 36 percent of the variance in symptom improvement [Miller-McGuire-2023].
- **Targeting it directly may speed change.** In a meta-analysis of 26 GAD studies (1,199 participants), CBT that targeted IU throughout reduced IU and worry more than general CBT at post-treatment, but the advantage was not maintained at follow-up [Wilson-etal-2023].

## Key details

| Sign in session | Example |
|---|---|
| Needs certainty before acting | "I can't book it until I know the weather" |
| Reassurance seeking | Asks the therapist "But you're sure it's not serious?" |
| Repeated checking or searching | Rereads emails, checks symptoms online |
| Avoids ambiguous situations | Turns down new tasks without clear instructions |
| Indecision and procrastination | Weighs options for weeks |
| Over-preparation | Rehearses every possible question |

IU is about the reaction to uncertainty, not the probability estimate. Lowering the estimated chance of a bad outcome often leaves the distress in place, because the remaining uncertainty is still intolerable.

## In the session

- If the client asks for reassurance ("You're sure I'll be okay?") → reflect the need for certainty and decline to supply it, kindly. "I notice part of you wants me to promise it will be fine. I can't know that, and neither can you. What would it be like to carry on without knowing?" Because IU is the unacceptability of a possible negative event whatever its probability, so reassurance can never remove it for long [Carleton-etal-2007].
- If the client's goal is to "be sure" → reframe the goal as acting well without certainty. Because IU is unacceptability of a negative outcome regardless of its probability [Carleton-etal-2007].
- If you are tempted to argue the odds → stop and ask how they would feel if the odds were one in a million. Because a lower probability does not resolve intolerance of the possibility [Carleton-etal-2007].
- If IU is high → design behavioral experiments in tolerating uncertainty: sending an email without rereading it, choosing a restaurant without checking reviews, delegating a task. Record the prediction and what happened. Because treatments that reduce IU produce linked symptom change [Miller-McGuire-2023].
- If the client has worry and OCD or social anxiety together → name IU as a shared target in one formulation. Because IU is associated with symptoms across these disorders [McEvoy-etal-2019].

What to record: the IUS-12 score or idiographic ratings ("How uncomfortable is not knowing, 0 to 100?"), the main uncertainty-reducing behaviors with weekly frequency, and results of each uncertainty experiment.

Evidence strength: the transdiagnostic link is supported by a large meta-analysis of correlational studies; the treatment effect on IU is supported by meta-analyses of randomized trials; the benefit of IU-specific CBT over general CBT is emerging and not shown to last.
