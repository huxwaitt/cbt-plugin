**Bottom line:** Emotion regulation is how people influence which emotions they have, when, and how they experience and express them [Gross-1998]. Difficulties show up as low awareness or clarity about feelings, not accepting them, losing control of behavior when upset, and relying on strategies such as avoidance, suppression and rumination. These strategies are associated with symptoms across anxiety, depression, eating and substance-related disorders [Aldao-etal-2010], and dysregulation falls after effective treatment for many disorders [Sloan-etal-2017]. In cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), it is a formulation target in its own right.

## What it is

Gross's process model groups regulation strategies into five families, by when they act on an emotion [Gross-1998]:

| Family | Example (helpful use) | Example (unhelpful use) |
|---|---|---|
| Situation selection | Choosing to see a supportive friend | Avoiding all social events |
| Situation modification | Asking to move a meeting | Bringing a companion everywhere |
| Attentional deployment | Absorbing activity | Rumination, threat monitoring |
| Cognitive change | Reappraising what an event means | Catastrophizing |
| Response modulation | Slow breathing, expressing feelings | Suppression, drinking, self-harm |

Gratz and Roemer framed difficulties as multidimensional and measured six areas in the 36-item Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale (DERS): nonacceptance of emotional responses, difficulty with goal-directed behavior when upset, impulse control difficulties, limited emotional awareness, limited access to regulation strategies and lack of emotional clarity [Gratz-Roemer-2004; Herzog-etal-2026]. A 16-item version (DERS-16) keeps good reliability and validity [Bjureberg-etal-2016].

## Why it matters in CBT

- **Shared across disorders.** In a meta-analysis of 114 studies, rumination had a large association with psychopathology, avoidance, problem solving and suppression medium to large, and reappraisal and acceptance only small to medium [Aldao-etal-2010]. Rumination, avoidance and suppression went with more symptoms, problem solving with fewer, and reappraisal and acceptance were more weakly linked than their place in treatment models would suggest.
- **Changes with treatment.** In a systematic review of 67 studies, maladaptive strategy use and overall dysregulation decreased after treatment in all but two, whatever the disorder or intervention, alongside symptom change [Sloan-etal-2017].
- **Comorbidity.** People with both depression and an anxiety disorder showed more difficulty with nonacceptance, goal-directed behavior, impulse control and access to strategies than those with one diagnosis [Herzog-etal-2026].
- **Treatments can target it directly.** The Unified Protocol, an emotion-focused transdiagnostic treatment, produced large symptom reductions and moderate shifts toward adaptive strategies across 15 studies with 1,244 participants [Sakiris-Berle-2019].

## Key details

- Strategies are not good or bad in themselves. Distraction, for example, can be helpful briefly and harmful as a way of life. Ask what the strategy does for the client, how often and at what cost.
- Look for the sequence: emotion, judgment of the emotion ("I shouldn't feel this," "it will never stop"), urge, action, short-term relief, longer-term cost.
- Impulsive regulation (bingeing, substance use, self-harm, aggressive outbursts) needs a risk lens as well as a skills lens.

## In the session

- If a client cannot name what they feel → slow down and help label it with a feelings list and body cues, then rate intensity 0 to 100. Because limited awareness and clarity are core regulation difficulties [Gratz-Roemer-2004].
- If a client judges their emotions ("I'm pathetic for being upset") → validate the emotion as understandable and separate it from the judgment. "It makes sense to feel hurt after that. The 'pathetic' part is a judgment on top of the hurt." Because nonacceptance of emotion is one of the measured difficulties [Gratz-Roemer-2004].
- If the main strategies are avoidance, suppression or rumination → map their short- and long-term effects and pick one to replace, rather than adding many new skills at once. Because these strategies show the strongest associations with symptoms [Aldao-etal-2010].
- If a client manages emotion with self-harm, substance use or bingeing → ask about frequency, recency and suicidal intent, and follow `02-risk.self-harm`. In the US, call or text 988 or call 911 in an emergency; in the UK, call 999, NHS 111 or Samaritans on 116 123. Because impulse control difficulty is a dimension of dysregulation and some strategies carry immediate risk [Gratz-Roemer-2004].
- If many disorders share an emotion regulation core → consider an emotion-focused transdiagnostic protocol (`15-transdiagnostic-processes.transdiagnostic-treatments`). Because regulation improves across disorders with treatment [Sloan-etal-2017; Sakiris-Berle-2019].

Example phrasing: "When that wave of anger comes, what do you usually do next? And what happens an hour later?"
Avoid: "You need to calm down before we can work on this."

What to record: the emotions most often dysregulated, triggers, strategies used with their short- and long-term effects, DERS or DERS-16 score, and any risk behaviors with the risk plan.
