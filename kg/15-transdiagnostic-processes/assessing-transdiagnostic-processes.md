**Bottom line:** Assess processes the same way you assess symptoms: ask about them directly, analyze a recent example, and measure them before and during treatment. Use a content-independent measure such as the Perseverative Thinking Questionnaire (PTQ) when repetitive thinking spans several topics [Ehring-etal-2011], add a process-specific measure only where it will change the plan, and back every questionnaire with idiographic ratings of the client's own behaviors.

## When to use

- After the presenting problem and risk are assessed, and before or while building the case formulation.
- When a client has two or more disorders, or when a disorder-specific protocol has stalled.
- At planned reviews, to check whether the process you targeted is changing.

## Steps

1. **Screen with plain questions.** Ask one for each family:
   - Repetitive thinking: "Do you find yourself going over things again and again, past or future?"
   - Avoidance: "What do you stay away from, put off or try not to feel?"
   - Safety behaviors: "When you can't avoid it, what do you do to stop the worst from happening?"
   - Uncertainty: "How hard is it for you to not know how something will turn out?"
   - Standards: "Do you judge yourself mainly by how well you do at something?"
   - Self-criticism: "When things go wrong, how do you talk to yourself?"
   - Emotion: "When feelings get strong, what do you do with them?"
2. **Analyze a recent example of each positive screen.** Trigger, what the client did, the short-term payoff, the long-term cost (`03-formulation.functional-analysis`).
3. **Choose measures.** Pick at most two or three questionnaires from the table, matched to the processes that screened positive.
4. **Set idiographic ratings.** For example minutes per day of worry, number of checks, 0 to 100 belief in a feared outcome, or 0 to 100 self-criticism intensity (`08-measurement.idiographic-measures`).
5. **Link to the formulation.** Write which presenting problem each process maintains and predict what should change if it is targeted.
6. **Repeat measures** at agreed points and compare with symptom measures.

| Process | Measure | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Repetitive negative thinking (any content) | PTQ | 15 items rated 0 to 4; total 0 to 60; core features, unproductiveness and capturing mental capacity [Ehring-etal-2011] |
| Worry | Penn State Worry Questionnaire (PSWQ) | 16 items, trait worry [Meyer-etal-1990] |
| Rumination | Ruminative Responses Scale (RRS) | 22 items; brooding and reflection subscales [Treynor-etal-2003; Hasegawa-2013] |
| Intolerance of uncertainty | 12-item Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale (IUS-12) | 12 items; anxious (prospective) and avoidance (inhibitory) factors [Carleton-etal-2007] |
| Experiential avoidance | Acceptance and Action Questionnaire-II (AAQ-II) | 7 items; good reliability [Bond-etal-2011]; items overlap with general distress [Wolgast-2014] |
| Emotion regulation | 36-item Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale (DERS) or its 16-item form (DERS-16) | DERS-16 keeps validity of the full scale [Bjureberg-etal-2016] |
| Clinical perfectionism | Clinical Perfectionism Questionnaire (CPQ) | 12 items; strivings and concerns factors, confirmed in female adolescents [Shu-etal-2020] |
| Self-criticism | Forms of Self-Criticizing/Attacking and Self-Reassuring Scale (FSCRS) | 22 items; inadequate self, hated self, reassured self; developed in female students [Gilbert-etal-2004] |
| Cognitive distortions | Cognitive Distortions Questionnaire (CD-Quest) | 15 items; 9-item and 5-item short forms [Morrison-etal-2022] |

Check terms of use for each measure before adding it to a service or digital tool (`08-measurement.instrument-licensing`). Do not paste item wording into records or software unless the license allows it.

## What to record

- Each process screened, positive or negative.
- One example per positive process, in the client's words.
- Measure name, date and score, plus idiographic ratings.
- The presenting problem each process maintains and the prediction for change.

## Guidance

- If repetitive thinking appears in several disorders → use the PTQ rather than separate worry and rumination scales. Because it measures repetitive negative thinking independent of content and relates to both depression and anxiety diagnoses [Ehring-etal-2011].
- If a client scores high on the AAQ-II → confirm avoidance with a behavioral example before formulating it. Because AAQ-II items load more on distress than on acceptance [Wolgast-2014].
- If a client struggles with long forms → use the DERS-16 or a CD-Quest short form. Because the brief versions keep most of the validity of the full scales [Bjureberg-etal-2016; Morrison-etal-2022].
- If a client hears "process" as a fault in their character → describe processes as habits that once helped. "This is something your mind learned to do to protect you. Let's see what it costs now." Because avoidance-type habits are learned and kept going by short-term relief, which means they can be unlearned [Krypotos-etal-2015].

## Common problems

- **Measuring everything.** Long batteries tire clients and rarely change decisions. Measure what will guide this plan.
- **Questionnaire without example.** A high score with no concrete episode cannot be formulated or targeted.
- **No repeat measurement.** A process measured once cannot show whether treatment is working on it.
- **Treating scores as diagnoses.** These tools describe processes; they do not diagnose a disorder.
