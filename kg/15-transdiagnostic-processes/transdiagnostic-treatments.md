**Bottom line:** Transdiagnostic treatments apply the same treatment principles across disorders [Dalgleish-etal-2020]. They outperform control conditions for anxiety and depression [Newby-etal-2015], and the Unified Protocol matched single-disorder cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) protocols for four anxiety disorders with less dropout [Barlow-etal-2017]. Choose one when comorbidity is high or a shared process is the main driver, and keep disorder-specific protocols where they are the established first choice for a single clear problem.

## When to use

- The client has two or more emotional disorders (anxiety, depressive, obsessive-compulsive or trauma-related) and treating them in sequence would be long.
- A service needs one protocol that trained staff can deliver across mixed referrals.
- A single process (rumination, perfectionism, emotion dysregulation) runs through several problems.
- A disorder-specific protocol has stalled and a shared process is the likely obstacle (`04-treatment-planning.managing-non-response`).

## How it works

Dalgleish and colleagues describe two delivery styles [Dalgleish-etal-2020]:
- **Universal**: one fixed set of elements for all clients, such as the Unified Protocol for Transdiagnostic Treatment of Emotional Disorders (UP). Easy to train, but not tailored.
- **Modular**: self-contained modules chosen and ordered for each client.

A third route targets one process across disorders. Some protocols instead span one disorder family, such as eating disorders [Dalgleish-etal-2020]:

| Treatment | Main target | Example components |
|---|---|---|
| UP | Emotion regulation in emotional disorders | Skills for noticing, accepting and responding differently to strong emotion [Sakiris-Berle-2019] |
| Rumination-focused CBT (RFCBT) | Depressive rumination | Behavioral activation principles applied to rumination [Watkins-etal-2011] |
| CBT for perfectionism | Clinical perfectionism | Self-help or face-to-face CBT aimed at perfectionism itself [Galloway-etal-2022] |
| Enhanced cognitive behavioral therapy for eating disorders (CBT-E) | Shared eating disorder mechanisms plus perfectionism, low self-esteem, mood intolerance and interpersonal problems | Modules added as the formulation requires [Fairburn-etal-2003] |

## Steps

1. Complete assessment, risk screening and a formulation that names the shared processes (`15-transdiagnostic-processes.assessing-transdiagnostic-processes`).
2. Decide with the client between a single-disorder protocol and a transdiagnostic one, using the decision rules below.
3. Socialize the client to the shared process: "One habit seems to feed several of your problems. If we work on that, we may help more than one thing at once."
4. Deliver the protocol's modules in order or as the formulation dictates; keep exposure and behavior change in, not just discussion.
5. Measure symptoms of each disorder and the targeted process at baseline and at planned reviews.
6. If one disorder does not improve, add the disorder-specific elements it needs.

## Guidance

- If comorbid anxiety and depression are both moderate or worse → consider a transdiagnostic protocol. Because transdiagnostic treatments beat control conditions for both anxiety and depression [Newby-etal-2015].
- If there is one clear disorder with an established specific protocol → prefer that protocol, and use transdiagnostic modules only for a process it misses. Because only four comparisons with disorder-specific treatment were available, suggesting similar effects on anxiety and possibly larger effects on depression, which is too little to displace established protocols [Newby-etal-2015].
- If dropout is a concern → the UP is a reasonable option. Because patients were more likely to complete the UP than single-disorder protocols (odds ratio 3.11) [Barlow-etal-2017].
- If residual depression is driven by rumination → consider RFCBT. Because it improved residual symptoms and remission when added to usual care [Watkins-etal-2011].
- If perfectionism drives eating, anxiety and mood problems together → target it directly. Because CBT for perfectionism reduces perfectionism and comorbid symptoms [Galloway-etal-2022].

## Common problems

- **Drift into general talk.** A broad protocol can lose its active ingredients. Keep exposure, experiments and homework.
- **One disorder left behind.** Track each disorder's own measure, not only a global score.
- **Mismatch.** A universal protocol may underdose a severe specific problem such as obsessive-compulsive disorder needing intensive exposure and response prevention.

## Evidence

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Newby and colleagues, 50 studies | Pre to post effects g 0.85 (anxiety) and 0.91 (depression); versus controls in 24 trials g 0.65 and 0.80; trial quality generally poor, heterogeneity high [Newby-etal-2015] |
| Barlow and colleagues, 223 patients | Up to 16 sessions; UP equivalent to single-disorder protocols on clinician-rated severity at post and 6 months; both beat waitlist [Barlow-etal-2017] |
| Bullis and colleagues, 80 completers | Gains largely maintained at 36 months; some secondary outcomes favored single-disorder protocols at intermediate points, but no significant differences at 36 months [Bullis-etal-2023] |
| Sakiris and Berle, 15 studies | Large symptom reductions across several disorders; moderate improvements in regulation strategies [Sakiris-Berle-2019] |
| Hvenegaard and colleagues, 131 patients | Group RFCBT beat group CBT on observer-rated depression (d 0.38); no difference at 6 months [Hvenegaard-etal-2020] |

Evidence strength: supported by randomized trials and meta-analyses for anxiety and depressive disorders; trial quality varies, and long-term comparisons are few.
