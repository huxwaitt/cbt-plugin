# 07-interventions/cognitive: Cognitive Interventions

## This surface
Techniques that work with thoughts, images and beliefs, from catching a single automatic thought to rebuilding a core belief over months. These pages sit in the treatment stage, after a shared formulation, and are ordered roughly as skills are usually taught. Process methods for worry and rumination, imagery work and compassion-focused methods cover cases where testing thought content alone does not help.

## Pages in order
1. `cognitive.identifying-automatic-thoughts`: every cognitive method starts with one specific hot thought or image.
2. `cognitive.thought-records`: the written structure for catching and testing thoughts between sessions.
3. `cognitive.cognitive-restructuring`: how to test a hot thought through guided discovery.
4. `cognitive.identifying-cognitive-distortions`: a quick cue for spotting patterns, with its limits.
5. `cognitive.downward-arrow`: moving from recurring thoughts to the rules and beliefs beneath them.
6. `cognitive.modifying-intermediate-beliefs`: testing rules and if-then assumptions, mainly through experiments.
7. `cognitive.core-belief-work`: slow construction of new core beliefs once earlier skills are in place.
8. `cognitive.imagery-and-imagery-rescripting`: when distress is carried by images and early memories.
9. `cognitive.worry-and-rumination-techniques`: when the problem is the process of thinking, not one thought.
10. `cognitive.self-compassion-techniques`: when change is understood logically but not felt.

## Pages
| Page | Summary | Applies when | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cognitive.identifying-automatic-thoughts` | How to elicit automatic thoughts and images, catch the hot thought behind a mood shift in session, and rate belief and emotion before any evaluation. | A client reports a distressing reaction, or their emotion visibly shifts in session, and you need the specific thought or image driving it. | L1-L2 |
| `cognitive.thought-records` | How to teach and use thought records, building from three columns to the seven-column record, with column contents, rating rules and how to review them. | A client can name situations, moods and thoughts and is ready for a written, structured way to catch and test thoughts between sessions. | L1-L2 |
| `cognitive.cognitive-restructuring` | How to evaluate a hot thought through guided discovery, examining evidence, generating alternatives, decatastrophizing and checking usefulness, ending in a believable balanced response. | You and the client have one specific hot thought with a belief rating and want to test whether it is accurate and helpful. | L2 |
| `cognitive.identifying-cognitive-distortions` | How to teach clients to label common thinking traps as a prompt for testing thoughts, and why labels alone do not establish that a thought is false. | A client can already catch automatic thoughts and you want a quick way to spot recurring patterns in how they reason. | L1-L2 |
| `cognitive.downward-arrow` | How to move from a hot automatic thought to the assumptions and core beliefs beneath it by repeatedly asking what the thought would mean if it were true. | A hot thought recurs across situations, or the emotion seems out of proportion to the thought, and you suspect a deeper rule or belief is driving it. | L2 |
| `cognitive.modifying-intermediate-beliefs` | How to identify rules, attitudes and conditional assumptions, weigh their advantages and disadvantages, test them with behavioral experiments, and write flexible replacement rules. | The same automatic thoughts keep returning because the client lives by a rigid rule or if-then assumption, such as "If I'm not perfect, I'll be rejected." | L2 |
| `cognitive.core-belief-work` | How to weaken old core beliefs and build new ones using the positive data log, continuum methods, the historical test, core belief worksheets and experiential work. | A client has solid automatic-thought skills but a global belief such as "I'm worthless" or "People will hurt me" keeps driving chronic problems or relapse. | L2-L3 |
| `cognitive.imagery-and-imagery-rescripting` | How to assess intrusive images and the memories behind them, and how to run imagery rescripting so the image's meaning changes, with safety limits and evidence. | A client's distress is carried by recurring images, nightmares or early memories, or they say "I know it isn't true, but it feels true." | L2-L3 |
| `cognitive.worry-and-rumination-techniques` | How to reduce repetitive negative thinking with worry postponement, metacognitive techniques such as detached mindfulness, and rumination-focused methods that shift from abstract to concrete thinking. | The main problem is the process of thinking, such as long worry chains or brooding about why, rather than one hot thought, so testing content keeps feeding it. | L2 |
| `cognitive.self-compassion-techniques` | How to use compassion-focused methods within CBT for shame and harsh self-criticism, including compassionate imagery, a compassionate tone for alternative thoughts, and handling fears of compassion. | A client produces good alternative thoughts but says "I know it logically, but I don't feel it," or meets every setback with harsh self-attack and shame. | L2 |

## Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Symptom | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Debating the Client Instead of Guiding Discovery | The therapist builds a case against the client's thought, the client concedes "I suppose so," and the belief and mood are back by the next session. | `cognitive.pitfall-debating-the-client` |
| Working on Cold Thoughts | Restructuring goes smoothly but mood barely moves, because the thought chosen is a vague, low-emotion description or question rather than the hot thought. | `cognitive.pitfall-working-on-cold-thoughts` |
| Thought Challenging That Becomes Reassurance or Worry | The client challenges the same worry many times a day seeking certainty, anxiety drops briefly then rebounds, and the method has become another compulsion or worry loop. | `cognitive.pitfall-restructuring-as-reassurance` |
| Challenging Thoughts That Are Accurate | The therapist asks for evidence against a thought that reflects real loss, hardship or discrimination, and the client feels dismissed, argues back or becomes more hopeless. | `cognitive.pitfall-challenging-accurate-thoughts` |
