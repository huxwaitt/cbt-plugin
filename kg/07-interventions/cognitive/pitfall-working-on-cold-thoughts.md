**Bottom line:** Restructuring only shifts emotion when it targets the thought that carries the emotion. Working on a description ("He didn't reply"), a question ("Why me?") or a mild side-thought produces tidy worksheets and no relief [Greenberger-Padesky-2016]. Find the hot thought first, often by following the strongest feeling or asking about images [Padesky-1994; Holmes-Mathews-2010].

## What it looks like

- The "thought" chosen is a fact about the situation: "My boss didn't say hello."
- The thought is a question or a feeling: "Why does this happen?" or "I felt awful."
- The balanced thought is easy to reach, but the emotion rating drops only slightly.
- The client says "That makes sense, but it's not really what's bothering me."
- Thought records are complete but repetitive, always about surface details.

## Why it happens

- The first thought reported is often the least threatening one; hotter thoughts may feel shameful or frightening to say.
- The therapist moves to evaluation before asking what the event meant to the client.
- Images are not asked about, though they often carry stronger emotion than words [Holmes-Mathews-2010].
- The therapist picks the thought that looks easiest to challenge rather than the one that hurts most.

## The fix

- If the listed thought is a fact → ask, "And what did that mean to you?" or "What was the worst part?" Because meaning, not the event, drives the emotion [Beck-2021].
- If the thought is a question → ask, "What answer does your mind give?" Because the implied answer is the testable thought [Greenberger-Padesky-2016].
- If several thoughts are listed → ask, "Which one makes you feel worst?" and check that its belief and emotion ratings are high. Because the hot thought is the one most linked to the strongest emotion [Greenberger-Padesky-2016].
- If nothing hot appears → follow the affect: "What does this say about you? About other people? About your future?" Because high-emotion meanings lead toward the beliefs that matter [Padesky-1994].
- If the client hesitates → normalize: "Often the thought that hurts most is the hardest to say. Whatever it is will be useful." Because shame hides hot thoughts.
- If a hot image appears → work with the image directly. Because restructuring only the words may leave the image's meaning unchanged.

Example phrasings:
- "Of all these, which thought hits hardest?"
- "If that were true, what would be the worst part about it?"
- Avoid: "Let's start with the first thought you wrote."

## Sources note

Choosing the hot thought is standard teaching in the main self-help and therapist manuals [Greenberger-Padesky-2016; Beck-2021]. Research supports the reason for asking about images: imagery evokes stronger emotion than verbal processing [Holmes-Mathews-2010]. No trial has compared restructuring hot versus cold thoughts directly; the guidance rests on expert consensus.
