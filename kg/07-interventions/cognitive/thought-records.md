**Bottom line:** A thought record is a written worksheet that walks the client through one upsetting moment: situation, moods, thoughts, then evidence and a balanced alternative. Start with three columns to catch thoughts, then add columns as skills grow, ending with the seven-column record [Greenberger-Padesky-2016]. Its purpose is to teach the client to question their own thinking, so the record matters less than the skill it builds [Kazantzis-etal-2014].

## When to use

- After the client can link a situation, an emotion and a thought in session (see `cognitive.identifying-automatic-thoughts`).
- For recurring situational distress in depression, anxiety, anger, guilt or shame.
- As homework between sessions, and as a structure for in-session restructuring.
- Use with care, or choose another tool, when writing is hard (low literacy, intellectual disability, severe concentration problems) or when the client starts using records compulsively to seek certainty (see `cognitive.pitfall-restructuring-as-reassurance`).

## How it works

Writing slows thinking down and separates the event, the interpretation and the feeling. Each column carries a prompt that mirrors a Socratic question, so repeated use trains the client to ask the questions without the therapist [Kazantzis-etal-2014]. Padesky reports, as clinical observation rather than trial data, that after roughly 15 to 30 written records many clients begin to ask "What's the evidence?" automatically and may no longer need to write them out [Kazantzis-etal-2014].

## Steps

Build up in stages. Move on when the client completes the current version with little help.

| Version | Columns | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Three-column | Situation; Moods (each rated 0 to 100); Automatic thoughts and images | Learn to catch thoughts and link them to moods |
| Five or six column | Adds an adaptive or alternative response and an outcome column that re-rates belief and emotion | Practice answering thoughts in the format used in Beck's manual [Beck-2021] |
| Seven-column | Situation; Moods; Automatic thoughts with the hot thought circled; Evidence that supports the hot thought; Evidence that does not support it; Alternative or balanced thought (rated 0 to 100); Re-rate moods | Full evaluation of one hot thought [Greenberger-Padesky-2016] |

How to run a seven-column record:
1. Fill the first three columns for one recent, specific moment. Circle the hot thought.
2. Evidence for: list facts, not interpretations. Accept the client's reasons respectfully; they explain why the thought feels true.
3. Evidence against: use prompts such as "Have I had experiences that show this isn't completely true?", "What would I tell a friend?", "Am I ignoring strengths?" [Greenberger-Padesky-2016].
4. Write an alternative or balanced thought that takes account of both evidence columns. Rate how much the client believes it.
5. Re-rate the original moods. Discuss what changed and what did not.
6. Agree a homework plan: when the client will fill a record (ideally close to the event), and what to bring back.

What to record in notes: which version the client uses, ratings before and after, recurring hot thoughts and themes (themes across records hint at underlying beliefs), and barriers to completion.

## Guidance

- If the client brings a record with only the first columns filled → praise the catching and complete the rest together in session. Because catching thoughts is the hardest first skill and the review teaches the remaining steps [Greenberger-Padesky-2016].
- If the client rates the balanced thought as only weakly believed → ask what is missing, and look for evidence the client discounted. Because a balanced thought that is not believed will not shift mood [Greenberger-Padesky-2016].
- If moods barely shift after a careful record → check whether the hot thought was chosen, then consider a behavioral experiment. Because experiments tend to change belief faster and more broadly than written records [McManus-etal-2012a; Bennett-Levy-2003].
- If the client says "I know it's not true, but it still feels true" → note this as a sign to add experiential work (experiments, imagery, compassion-focused methods). Because, in the theory Bennett-Levy tested, written records act mainly on logical (propositional) meaning, while experiential methods reach felt (implicational) meaning, and trainees found experiments more compelling [Bennett-Levy-2003].
- If records are not done → ask what got in the way before re-assigning. Because nonadherence is information about the task, the rationale or the client's beliefs (see `05-session-structure.homework-review-and-adherence`).

Example phrasings:
- "Let's take Tuesday's moment and walk it across the page together."
- "Which of these thoughts would you circle as the hot one?"
- Avoid: "Your homework is to do a thought record every day." Without a specific trigger and plan, adherence drops.

## Common problems

- **Situations written as summaries.** Ask for one moment with a time and place.
- **Thoughts in the mood column.** "Worthless" belongs with thoughts, not moods.
- **Positive thinking instead of balance.** "Everything will be fine" is not supported by evidence and will not be believed.
- **Records done hours later from memory.** Fine at first; aim to record nearer the event as skills grow.
- **Ritualized use.** Writing dozens a day to get certainty is a safety behavior; reduce and switch strategy.

## Evidence

Component research is small. In a single-session study of 91 nonclinical participants, both thought records and behavioral experiments beat a control condition on belief, anxiety and behavior, with experiments changing belief sooner [McManus-etal-2012a]. Trainee therapists practicing both on themselves rated experiments as more powerful and compelling than thought records [Bennett-Levy-2003]. In 35 responders to cognitive therapy for depression, coping skills and independent use of cognitive therapy methods predicted lower relapse over the following year [Strunk-etal-2007]. The case for thought records rests on these findings plus expert consensus [Beck-2021; Greenberger-Padesky-2016].
