**Bottom line:** Behavioral activation (BA) is not a list of nice things to do. Its active ingredient is changing what depressed behavior does for the client, especially avoidance, and reconnecting with activities that matter to them [Martell-etal-2022] [Kanter-etal-2010]. If you schedule generic pleasant events without asking what function the client's current behavior serves, the diary fills up but mood does not move.

## What it looks like

- The schedule is a generic list (a walk, a bath, a coffee with a friend) chosen in the first session.
- The client completes the activities but reports "I was just going through the motions."
- A major avoided problem, such as an unopened pile of bills, a conflict with a partner or a job application, is never on the plan.
- The client ruminates through every scheduled activity.
- The plan is all duty or all pleasure, with nothing linked to the client's values.
- The therapist keeps adding activities when mood does not shift.

## Why it happens

Pleasant activity scheduling is easy to teach and looks like progress. Functional analysis takes more time and skill. BA manuals emphasize that the same activity can be approach in one context and avoidance in another, so the function matters more than the form [Martell-etal-2022]. Effective BA protocols include identifying depressed behaviors, analyzing their triggers and consequences, and developing alternative goal-directed behavior and responses to rumination, not just scheduling [Richards-etal-2016]. Values work is also part of modern protocols [Lejuez-etal-2011].

## The fix

1. **Do a functional analysis** of the main low-mood patterns: trigger, response, avoidance pattern, short-term relief and long-term cost [Martell-etal-2022].
2. **Ask what is being avoided:** "Is there something you've been putting off that weighs on you?" Put a first step toward it on the schedule.
3. **Link activities to values and life areas** the client names [Lejuez-etal-2011].
4. **Check engagement, not just completion.** "Where was your attention while you walked?" If on rumination, plan to attend to the senses and the task, and to notice when thinking is not helping [Martell-etal-2022].
5. **Balance the schedule** across routine, pleasure, mastery and necessary tasks.
6. **When mood does not shift, analyze before adding more.** Review a recent activity in detail: context, function and experience [Martell-etal-2022].
7. **Use problem solving** for practical barriers (see `behavioral.problem-solving-therapy`).

Example phrasing: "You did everything on the plan, which took real effort. I'm curious what it was like from the inside. Were you there, or somewhere else in your head?"

Avoid: "Let's add a few more pleasant things this week." This adds load without understanding why the current plan isn't working.

## Sources note

The emphasis on function, avoidance and rumination comes from the main BA clinician's guide [Martell-etal-2022] and a review of BA components [Kanter-etal-2010]. The protocol of the Cost and Outcome of Behavioral Activation (COBRA) trial shows the techniques used in a large trial of BA delivered by junior workers [Richards-etal-2016]. Values-based BA is described in the revised brief BA manual [Lejuez-etal-2011]. No trial has directly compared function-based BA with scheduling alone delivered in the same way; this guidance rests on manuals and expert consensus.
