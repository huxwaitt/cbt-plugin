**Bottom line:** Behavioral activation (BA) treats depression by helping clients act according to a plan and their values rather than their mood. You and the client work out what depressed behavior does for them (often avoidance), then schedule specific, graded activities that restore contact with reward. BA is supported by randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and meta-analyses, performs about as well as full cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and is a guideline-recommended option for both less and more severe depression in the United Kingdom (UK) [Uphoff-etal-2020] [Richards-etal-2016] [NICE-NG222].

## When to use

- Depression where withdrawal, inactivity, avoidance or rumination is prominent.
- Early in treatment, when concentration is too poor for detailed thought work, or when the client prefers a practical approach.
- More severe depression: in a trial of 241 adults, BA matched antidepressants among the more severely depressed, and both beat cognitive therapy [Dimidjian-etal-2006].

Screen for suicide risk before and during BA. The largest BA trial excluded acutely suicidal people [Richards-etal-2016], so risk management comes first.

## How it works

Losses, stress and disrupted routines cut a person off from positive reinforcement. Low mood then leads to withdrawal and avoidance, which bring short-term relief but remove reward and create new problems, so mood stays low [Martell-etal-2022]. BA works from the outside in: change what the person does in their context, and mood tends to follow. A review of BA protocols found these components: activity monitoring, life goals and values assessment, activity scheduling, skills training, relaxation, contingency management, and procedures targeting verbal behavior (such as rumination) and avoidance. Only monitoring and scheduling appeared in every protocol [Kanter-etal-2010].

## Steps

1. **Rationale.** Draw the withdrawal cycle with the client's own example. "When you feel low you stay in, which makes sense, but staying in cuts off what used to lift you."
2. **Monitor.** One week of activity and mood ratings (see `behavioral.activity-monitoring-and-scheduling`).
3. **Functional analysis.** For each low-mood pattern, map the trigger, the response and the avoidance pattern, and what it achieves in the short and long term. Martell's TRAP (Trigger, Response, Avoidance Pattern) frame helps [Martell-etal-2022].
4. **Values and life areas.** Ask what matters in relationships, work or study, health, leisure and daily responsibilities, then link activities to those values [Lejuez-etal-2011].
5. **Schedule.** Choose small, specific activities with a day, time and place. Mix routine, pleasant, mastery and necessary tasks.
6. **Alternative coping.** Replace the avoidance pattern with a planned approach response, the TRAC (Trigger, Response, Alternative Coping) step [Martell-etal-2022].
7. **Rumination.** Treat rumination as a behavior. Ask whether it solves a problem; if not, shift attention to the task or the senses [Martell-etal-2022].
8. **Review and grade.** Each session, review what happened, what helped mood and what got in the way, then build up.
9. **End.** Summarize which activities and alternative responses worked and write a relapse plan.

## Guidance

- If the client says "I'll do it when I feel like it" → say "Let's test whether doing comes first and feeling follows, starting with something small." Because waiting for motivation keeps the withdrawal cycle going [Martell-etal-2022].
- If a planned activity did not happen → ask what got in the way, without blame, then shrink or reschedule the task. Because BA treats non-completion as information about barriers, not as failure [Martell-etal-2022].
- If the schedule holds only chores → add one activity tied to a stated value or to enjoyment. Because the target is renewed contact with reward and values, not busyness [Kanter-etal-2010].
- If activity rises but mood does not → check function: is the client ruminating during the activity, or using it to avoid something more important? Because the same activity can serve approach or avoidance [Martell-etal-2022].
- If the client voices hopelessness or suicidal thoughts → pause the activation agenda, assess risk (see `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`), and give crisis contacts: in the United States (US), call or text 988 or call 911; in the UK, 999, NHS 111 or Samaritans 116 123. Because safety takes priority over the session plan.
- If you are a junior or non-specialist worker → follow a written manual with regular supervision. Because the equivalence trial used 5 days of training, manuals and fortnightly 60-minute supervision [Richards-etal-2016].

## Common problems

Clients often over-schedule the first week; start with two or three activities. Specify what, when and where. Practical barriers (money, caregiving, transport, unsafe neighborhoods) need problem solving, not more encouragement (see `behavioral.problem-solving-therapy`). Scheduling pleasant events without a functional analysis is a common way BA goes flat (see `behavioral.pitfall-activity-without-function`).

## Evidence

- A Cochrane review of 53 RCTs (5,495 participants) found BA more effective than treatment as usual in the short term (risk ratio (RR) 1.40, 7 RCTs, moderate certainty, not robust in sensitivity analyses) and no clear difference from CBT (RR 0.99, 5 RCTs, 601 participants, moderate certainty) [Uphoff-etal-2020].
- A meta-analysis of 26 RCTs (1,524 participants) found a standardized mean difference (SMD) of -0.74 against controls. The median course was 8 sessions, and session number was not linked to effect size [Ekers-etal-2014].
- In the Cost and Outcome of Behavioral Activation (COBRA) trial (440 adults), BA from junior mental health workers was non-inferior to CBT from qualified therapists at 12 months and cost about 21% less. Each arm allowed up to 20 sessions over 16 weeks [Richards-etal-2016].
- UK guidance (National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) NG222) describes individual BA as usually 8 sessions for less severe depression and 12 to 16 for more severe depression, and group BA (less severe only) as usually 8 sessions with 8 participants [NICE-NG222].
