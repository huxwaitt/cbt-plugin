**Bottom line:** For these presentations cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is the first psychological step for some (bulimia and binge eating, via CBT-based guided self-help; insomnia; adult self-harm), one of several equal options for others (adult anorexia), offered to everyone alongside medication in psychosis, and not the main treatment for borderline personality disorder or adolescent anorexia. Most protocols here are longer than for anxiety and need specialist training. Use the table to place CBT, then read the condition page.

## What it is

A reference table built from UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guidance and US guidance. The American Psychological Association has no guideline for these conditions, so the US column uses the American Psychiatric Association (written "Psychiatric Assn" below) and other US bodies. CBT for eating disorders is written CBT-ED, as NICE does. Other abbreviations: interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT), dialectical behavior therapy (DBT), posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD).

## Why it matters in CBT

Here a generic CBT course is most likely to be the wrong dose, format or first treatment, and medical or safety risks can change the order of care.

## Key details

| Condition | NICE (UK) | US guidance | Usual dose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anorexia nervosa, adults | Choice of individual CBT-ED, Maudsley Anorexia Nervosa Treatment for Adults (MANTRA) or specialist supportive clinical management [NICE-NG69] | Psychiatric Assn: eating-disorder-focused psychotherapy [APA-ED-Guideline-2023] | Up to 40 sessions over 40 weeks, twice weekly for the first 2 to 3 weeks [NICE-NG69] |
| Anorexia nervosa, children and young people | Family therapy for anorexia first; CBT-ED or adolescent-focused psychotherapy if family therapy is unacceptable, contraindicated or ineffective [NICE-NG69] | Psychiatric Assn: family-based treatment for adolescents [APA-ED-Guideline-2023] | See condition page |
| Bulimia nervosa, adults | CBT-based guided self-help; individual CBT-ED if that is unacceptable or ineffective after 4 weeks [NICE-NG69] | Psychiatric Assn: eating-disorder-focused psychotherapy [APA-ED-Guideline-2023] | Up to 20 sessions over 20 weeks [NICE-NG69] |
| Binge eating disorder | CBT-based guided self-help; then group CBT-ED; individual CBT-ED if group is unavailable or declined [NICE-NG69] | Psychiatric Assn: eating-disorder-focused psychotherapy [APA-ED-Guideline-2023] | Group: 16 weekly 90-minute sessions; individual: 16 to 20 sessions [NICE-NG69] |
| Psychosis and schizophrenia | Offer CBT to everyone with psychosis or schizophrenia; first episode: antipsychotic plus individual CBT and family intervention; increased risk of psychosis: CBT, no antipsychotic [NICE-CG178] | Psychiatric Assn: CBT for psychosis, recommendation 1B [Keepers-etal-2020] | At least 16 planned one-to-one sessions [NICE-CG178] |
| Bipolar depression | A bipolar-specific manualized psychological intervention, or CBT, IPT or behavioral couples therapy; monitor for hypomania; therapists need bipolar training [NICE-CG185] | No American Psychological Association guideline | As for more severe depression |
| Bipolar, relapse prevention | Structured, manualized individual, group or family intervention designed for bipolar disorder [NICE-CG185] | No American Psychological Association guideline | See condition page |
| Borderline personality disorder | No brief interventions (under 3 months) outside a structured service; consider comprehensive DBT for women when reducing recurrent self-harm is the priority; no drug treatment specifically for the disorder [NICE-CG78] | Psychiatric Assn: structured psychotherapy with research support (1B); any medication time-limited and adjunctive (2C) [APA-BPD-Guideline-2024] | Months, not weeks |
| Self-harm, adults | Offer a structured, CBT-informed intervention tailored to self-harm, for example CBT or problem solving [NICE-NG225] | No American Psychological Association guideline | Typically 4 to 10 sessions [NICE-NG225] |
| Self-harm, young people with emotional dysregulation | Consider DBT adapted for adolescents [NICE-NG225] | No American Psychological Association guideline | See condition page |
| Chronic insomnia | No NICE clinical guideline; the Sleepio digital CBT program is recommended in primary care for people who would otherwise get sleep hygiene advice or sleeping pills [NICE-HTG624] | CBT for insomnia (CBT-I) as initial treatment, strong recommendation [Qaseem-etal-2016]; American Academy of Sleep Medicine: multicomponent CBT-I (strong), sleep hygiene alone not advised [Edinger-etal-2021a] | See `insomnia.cbt-i-components` |
| ADHD, adults | Medication if impairment persists after environmental changes; a structured supportive psychological intervention, which may include CBT, if medication is declined, poorly tolerated or ineffective, or alongside medication for residual impairment [NICE-NG87] | No American Psychological Association guideline | See condition page |
| Harmful drinking with depression or anxiety | Treat the alcohol misuse first; reassess after 3 to 4 weeks of abstinence [NICE-CG115] | No American Psychological Association guideline | See condition page |
| PTSD with substance use disorder | See PTSD guidance | Strong: trauma-focused CBT plus usual substance use care [APA-PTSD-Guideline-2025] | As for PTSD |

## In the session

- If you are not trained in the specific protocol (CBT-ED, CBT for psychosis, DBT, bipolar-specific work) → refer or arrange specialist supervision before starting. Because these guidelines specify manualized treatment from trained practitioners [NICE-NG69; NICE-CG178; NICE-CG185].
- If a client with bipolar depression shows signs of hypomania or worsening depression → liaise with or refer to specialist care, urgently if mania or severe depression develops. Because NICE builds mood monitoring and escalation into psychological treatment for bipolar depression [NICE-CG185].
- If a client with borderline personality disorder asks for "a few sessions of CBT" for self-harm → explain that short courses are not recommended and discuss a structured program: "Short courses tend not to help with this pattern. I'd like to connect you with a longer program designed for it." Because NICE advises against brief interventions under 3 months outside a structured service [NICE-CG78].
- If an eating disorder client is underweight or medically unwell → arrange medical assessment before or alongside therapy (see `eating-disorders.medical-risk`). Because NICE builds monitoring of weight and physical health into all anorexia care [NICE-NG69].
- If a client who self-harms is at imminent risk → follow the crisis pathway (US: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, call or text 988, or 911; UK: 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111, Samaritans 116 123). Because a planned course of therapy does not replace urgent care (see `02-risk.self-harm`).
