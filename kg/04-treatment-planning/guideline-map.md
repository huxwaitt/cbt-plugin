**Bottom line:** For adult depression and anxiety disorders, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is a guideline-recommended first-line option almost everywhere, but guidelines differ on dose, format and whether medication or another therapy is an equal choice. The UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) gives the most detailed doses. In the US, the American Psychological Association (APA) updated its posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) guideline in 2025, its 2019 depression guideline is past its planned expiry, and the American College of Physicians (ACP) covers acute adult depression. Check the row, then confirm details in the source.

## What it is

A map of current recommendations for adults, each row checked against the guideline text. "First-line" means recommended as an initial treatment. Abbreviations: behavioral activation (BA), interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT), selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI), serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRI), tricyclic antidepressant (TCA), second-generation antidepressant (SGA), exposure and response prevention (ERP), eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR), generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9).

## Why it matters in CBT

Guidelines set the default treatment and dose that payers, services and supervisors expect, and name the alternatives when CBT is declined or fails. Depart from them knowingly, and record why.

## Key details

| Condition | NICE (UK) | US | Usual CBT dose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depression, less severe (PHQ-9 below 16) | Shared choice from a ranked list: guided self-help first, then group CBT, group BA, individual CBT, individual BA, group exercise, group mindfulness, IPT, SSRI, counseling, short-term psychodynamic therapy. No routine first-line antidepressant [NICE-NG222] | ACP: CBT alone for mild depression (conditional) [Qaseem-etal-2023]. APA: psychotherapy or SGA, with comparable effects across therapy models [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019] | Individual CBT usually 8 sessions; guided self-help 6 to 8 [NICE-NG222] |
| Depression, more severe (PHQ-9 16 or more) | Ranked list headed by CBT plus antidepressant, then individual CBT, individual BA, antidepressant, problem solving, counseling, psychodynamic therapy, IPT [NICE-NG222] | ACP: CBT or SGA alone (strong); CBT plus SGA (conditional) [Qaseem-etal-2023]. APA: if combining, CBT or IPT plus SGA [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019] | CBT usually 16 sessions; BA 12 to 16 [NICE-NG222] |
| Chronic depressive symptoms | CBT, SSRI, SNRI or TCA, or CBT plus an SSRI or TCA; CBT should cover avoidance, rumination and interpersonal difficulties [NICE-NG222] | APA: insufficient evidence on cognitive behavioral analysis system of psychotherapy [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019] | Not specified |
| Depression, relapse prevention | Remitted on an antidepressant: group CBT or mindfulness-based cognitive therapy, usually 8 sessions over 2 to 3 months. Remitted with therapy: at least 4 more sessions of the same therapy [NICE-NG222] | APA: CBT, mindfulness-based cognitive therapy or IPT rather than medication (conditional) [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019] | 4 to 8 sessions |
| GAD | Step 2 low intensity; step 3 CBT or applied relaxation, or medication, by preference. No benzodiazepine except short-term in crisis [NICE-CG113] | No APA guideline | 12 to 15 weekly 1-hour sessions [NICE-CG113] |
| Panic disorder | Self-help for mild to moderate; CBT, or an antidepressant if long-standing or psychological therapy declined. No benzodiazepines [NICE-CG113] | No APA guideline | 7 to 14 hours, weekly, finished within 4 months [NICE-CG113] |
| Social anxiety disorder | Individual CBT built for social anxiety (Clark and Wells or Heimberg model); group CBT not in preference; add CBT after partial response to an SSRI at 10 to 12 weeks [NICE-CG159] | No APA guideline | Up to 14 sessions of 90 minutes over about 4 months (Clark and Wells) [NICE-CG159] |
| OCD | Mild impairment: low-intensity CBT with ERP; moderate: SSRI or more intensive CBT with ERP; severe: both [NICE-CG31]. Replacement guideline expected February 2027 [NICE-GID-NG10435] | No APA guideline | Up to 10 therapist hours low intensity; more than 10 high intensity [NICE-CG31] |
| PTSD | Trauma-focused CBT (cognitive processing therapy, cognitive therapy for PTSD, narrative exposure, prolonged exposure) after 1 month; EMDR after 3 months for non-combat trauma; venlafaxine or an SSRI if the person prefers drugs [NICE-NG116] | First-line (strong): cognitive processing therapy, prolonged exposure, trauma-focused CBT. Second-line (conditional): cognitive therapy, EMDR, narrative exposure; fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline, venlafaxine [APA-PTSD-Guideline-2025] | 8 to 12 sessions, more for multiple traumas [NICE-NG116] |

**Age groups.** For adolescent depression APA recommends CBT or IPT adapted for adolescents, with fluoxetine as first-line medication if one is used; for older adults, group CBT or group life review, or IPT plus an SGA [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019].

**Currency.** The APA depression guideline was set to expire in about 2024 [APA-Depression-Guideline-2019] and an update is under way; check for a replacement. NICE anxiety guidance dates from 2004 to 2013 in parts, and the 2005 OCD guideline is being replaced [NICE-GID-NG10435].

**APA Division 12.** The Society of Clinical Psychology lists psychological treatments by research support: archived older ratings (strong, modest, controversial) and newer grades (very strong, strong, weak, or insufficient evidence) [SCP-Treatments-Site; Tolin-etal-2015a]. Check the live list for a treatment's rating.

## In the session

- If the client meets criteria for more than one row → start with the row the formulation says drives the others (see `04-treatment-planning.problem-list-and-goals`). Because NICE gives explicit ordering rules for some pairs, such as PTSD before depression unless depression blocks trauma work [NICE-NG116].
- If the client prefers another first-line option (for example IPT or EMDR) → offer it as a real choice and refer if you do not provide it. Because both NICE and APA build recommendations around shared decision-making [NICE-NG222; APA-Depression-Guideline-2019].
- If you plan to depart from the guideline dose or treatment → record the clinical reason and the client's informed agreement. Because NICE gives "usual" doses and asks that treatment manuals guide duration [NICE-NG222].
- Explain choices plainly: "For panic, the best-tested approach is CBT over a few months. Medication is another option."
