**Bottom line:** The written cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) treatment plan records what you and the client agreed: problems, measurable goals and objectives, interventions, dose, measures and review dates. Draft it together after the case formulation, record agreement, and update it at every review. In the United States (US), Medicare local coverage determinations (LCDs) expect type, amount, frequency and duration of services, diagnoses, goals and periodic updates [CMS-LCD-L34353]. In the United Kingdom (UK), the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) expects jointly developed care plans with a written copy [NICE-CG136].

## When to use

- After assessment and formulation, before active treatment. In case formulation work, pretreatment ends with a treatment plan the client consents to [Persons-2008].
- At each review, plan change, payer review and handover.

## Steps

1. **Header.** Client identifiers, date, clinician and credentials, and diagnoses with International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM) codes if your license allows diagnosis.
2. **Problem list.** Number each problem in behavioral terms with its effect on functioning. Risk goes first.
3. **Goals and objectives.** Per problem, one goal (the outcome) and a few objectives (observable steps, each with a number and a date). Many US planning guides use this problem, goal, objective, intervention layout [Jongsma-etal-2021]. Example objective: "by week 8, rides the bus to work alone on 4 of 5 days."
4. **Interventions.** Name each technique and the formulation link it targets.
5. **Modality, frequency and dose.** Individual, group, couple or family; in person or telehealth; session length, frequency, and estimated number of sessions, with the guideline dose it rests on [CMS-LCD-L34353; CMS-LCD-L34616].
6. **Measures and review dates.** Standardized and idiographic measures with baseline and target values. For depression, NICE asks for a review of how treatment is working 2 to 4 weeks after it starts [NICE-NG222].
7. **Coordination.** Prescriber, primary care, consented contacts, and where the safety plan is filed.
8. **Agreement.** Read it back, invite changes, record that the client took part and agrees, sign, and give them a copy.
9. **Update.** At each review, record progress on each objective, what changed and why, and the next review date.

## What to record

**US medical necessity.** Medicare excludes services that are not reasonable and necessary for diagnosing or treating illness [USC-42-1395y]. Contractor LCDs ask the record to show target symptoms, goals, how outcomes are monitored, the client's capacity to benefit from psychotherapy, and an expectation that treatment will improve health status or function [CMS-LCD-L34616]. They also ask for a periodic summary of goals, progress and an updated plan; for long-term conditions, maintaining function to avoid deterioration or hospitalization counts as expected improvement [CMS-LCD-L34353]. Other payers set their own rules.

**Signatures.** Medicare reviewers need the responsible clinician to be identifiable, and enforce signatures that statute, regulation or coverage policy requires [CMS-PIM-Ch3]. Client signature rules vary. In Wisconsin certified outpatient clinics, for example, the client or legal representative must approve and sign the plan, and a mental health professional reviews it with them at least every 90 days or 6 sessions, whichever covers the longer period [WI-DHS-35-19].

**UK equivalents.** NICE asks services to develop care plans jointly, give an up-to-date written copy, agree a review time, and include a crisis plan for people at risk of crisis [NICE-CG136]. In England the Care Programme Approach (CPA) is superseded for community mental health services; National Health Service (NHS) England wants brief co-produced plans listing actions for the person, carers and services, with flexible reviews, accessible language and links to outcome measurement [NHSE-CPA-Position-2022]. In Wales, people receiving secondary mental health services have a statutory written care and treatment plan that can cover psychological interventions [Wales-MH-Measure-2010].

## Guidance

- If the client reports suicidal thoughts, self-harm or risk to others → list safety as problem 1, reference the safety plan, and write crisis contacts into the plan (US: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, call or text 988, or 911; UK: 999, NHS 111, Samaritans 116 123); with intent or imminent danger, stop planning and follow your crisis protocol now (emergency assessment, 911 or 999). Because NICE asks for a crisis plan inside the care plan for people at risk [NICE-CG136; Lifeline-988; NHS-Urgent-Help; Samaritans-Site].
- If you are tempted to write the plan alone after the session → draft it with the client in the room. Because goal consensus correlates with outcome (r = .24) [Tryon-etal-2018], and in randomized trials in long-term health conditions (mostly diabetes), personalized care planning slightly reduced depression (standardized mean difference -0.36, 5 studies, moderate-quality evidence) [Coulter-etal-2015].
- If you need more sessions than a payer has approved → show each objective's baseline, current value and why more treatment should help. Because coverage rests on documented expected improvement [CMS-LCD-L34616].
- If the client declines part of the plan → record what they declined and what was agreed instead. Because some state rules require refusals to be noted [WI-DHS-35-19].
- If the plan reads like insurer jargon → add plain wording: "By week 8, you'll ride the bus to work most days." Because NHS England wants plans accessible in language [NHSE-CPA-Position-2022].

## Common problems

- **Boilerplate.** Prewritten goal libraries are convenient [Jongsma-etal-2021], but generic text ("client will reduce depression") is not the individualized plan LCDs require [CMS-LCD-L34353] or NHS England expects [NHSE-CPA-Position-2022].
- **Filed and forgotten.** Notes never mention the objectives; see `04-treatment-planning.pitfall-continuing-without-review`.
- **Plan kept in psychotherapy notes.** Under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), plan summaries cannot be psychotherapy notes, so keep the plan in the main record [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501].
