**Bottom line:** Ask every new client, directly and early, about suicidal thoughts, self-harm and thoughts of harming others, and check for anyone in their care who may be at risk. Asking has not been found to plant the idea. A negative screen is documented and revisited; a positive screen moves you into full risk assessment in the same session; imminent danger means emergency services now.

## When to use

- Every intake, whatever the referral says. The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) says to always ask people with depression directly about suicidal ideation and intent [NICE-NG222].
- Repeat whenever new information raises concern.

## Steps

1. **Frame it as routine.** "I ask everyone I see about safety, because it's important and people often don't bring it up."
2. **Ask about suicide directly.** Start broad (hopelessness, wishing to be dead), then ask plainly about thoughts of suicide, any plans or preparation, and past attempts. The Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) screener uses between 2 and 6 plain-language questions depending on answers, needs no mental health training to ask, and is free for community and healthcare use [C-SSRS-Site].
3. **Ask about self-harm** without suicidal intent, current and past.
4. **Ask about harm to others.** Current or past aggressive ideas or behavior, and anger or impulsivity concerns [APA-PsychEval-2016].
5. **Ask about dependants and safety at home.** Children or vulnerable adults in the client's care; whether the client is being harmed (for example, domestic abuse).
6. **For video or phone sessions,** confirm where the client is right now and a local emergency contact before asking, so you can send help if needed.
7. **Note urgent medical risk.** For example, very low food intake, possible alcohol or sedative withdrawal, or new neurological symptoms; route to medical care.
8. **Decide the level of response** (table below).
9. **Document** the questions asked, answers, your risk estimate and the factors behind it [APA-PsychEval-2016].

| Screen result | Response |
|---|---|
| No current thoughts of suicide or harm | Document, give crisis numbers routinely, revisit at review points |
| Passive or current thoughts, no plan or intent | Full suicide risk assessment now; begin a safety plan; check social support and sources of help [NICE-NG222] |
| Plan, intent, recent preparation or attempt | Same-day action: full assessment before the session ends, restrict access to lethal means, involve supports, consult a supervisor, refer urgently if risk is considerable [NICE-NG222] |
| Immediate danger to self or others | Emergency services now; do not leave the person alone if in person |

**Crisis contacts.** United States: call or text 988 (988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, 24/7; chat at chat.988lifeline.org) [Lifeline-988], or 911 for emergencies; veterans, service members and their supporters can dial 988 then press 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line [VCL-Site]. United Kingdom: 999 if life is at risk, National Health Service (NHS) 111 and choose the mental health option, or Samaritans on 116 123 [NHS-Urgent-Help].

## What to record

- Exact areas asked about and the client's answers in brief, including denials.
- Your estimate of risk (suicide and harm to others) with the factors influencing it [APA-PsychEval-2016].
- Actions taken: safety plan started, supports contacted, consultation, referral, emergency call.
- Crisis numbers given.
- When risk will next be reviewed.

## Guidance

- If you worry that asking will upset or prompt the client → ask anyway, calmly. Because a review found no significant rise in suicidal ideation from asking [Dazzi-etal-2014], and a randomized trial in high school students found no rise in distress or ideation [Gould-etal-2005].
- If the client says "Sometimes I wish I wouldn't wake up" → thank them, stay calm, and ask follow-up questions about frequency, plans and intent. "Thank you for telling me. Can I ask a few more questions so I understand?" Because any risk calls for assessment and help matched to need [NICE-NG222].
- If the client discloses risk to a child or vulnerable adult → follow your safeguarding or mandatory reporting procedure the same day. Because these duties do not wait for the next session (see `02-risk.safeguarding`).
- If the client is at risk of suicide → do not withhold treatment for that reason; increase support and contact. Because NICE advises against this [NICE-NG222].
- If the client reports thoughts of harming a specific person → assess intent, access to weapons including firearms, and the identity of any target, and consult the same day. Because these factors guide the response to aggression risk [Armstrong-2016], and duty-to-protect law varies by state (see `14-ethics-law.duty-to-warn-and-protect`).
- If you are tempted to sum up the screen with one label ("low risk") → write the specific answers, the factors and the plan. Because NICE advises against using risk tools or low, medium or high stratification to predict suicide or decide who gets treatment [NICE-NG225], and any risk estimate should name its factors [APA-PsychEval-2016].

Never ask for or record method details beyond what is needed to judge access and plan, and never describe methods to the client.

## Common problems

- **Relying on a form.** A blank or "no" on paper is not a screen; ask in person, and review any endorsed form item before the client leaves.
- **Screen at the end.** Leaves no time to act. Screen in the first half.
- **No follow-up of a positive screen.** See `01-intake.pitfall-risk-screen-skipped`.
