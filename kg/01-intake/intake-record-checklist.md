**Bottom line:** A complete intake record lets another clinician pick up the case safely: who the client is, what was consented to, what the problem is, what the risks are, what else is going on, what the client wants, and what was agreed. Record "not assessed" rather than leaving gaps, label informant reports as such, and keep the risk entry specific.

## When to use

- Completing notes after an evaluation session.
- Auditing records or supervising trainees.
- Designing intake forms, templates or artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted capture tools. Every item below should map to a field, and missing items should be visible, not silently empty.

## Steps

Work through the groups in order. Items marked (R) are named in the American Psychiatric Association adult evaluation guideline [APA-PsychEval-2016; Armstrong-2016].

| Group | Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Administrative | Identity, contact, emergency contact, referral source and question, primary care clinician (R) | Continuity, emergencies, coordination |
| Administrative | Insurer or payer and member ID, or self-pay; prior authorization status; payment method; signed fee agreement and cancellation policy acknowledgment; release-of-information forms; Good Faith Estimate (GFE) given to uninsured or self-pay clients [CFR-45-149-610] | Fees and billing agreed in writing before services [ACA-Ethics-2014]; the GFE is filed in the record [CFR-45-149-610] |
| Consent | What was explained (nature and course of therapy, fees, third parties, confidentiality limits), questions invited, consent or assent obtained and how [APA-Ethics-2017] | Consent must be documented [APA-Ethics-2017] |
| Consent | Authorizations for contact with others, with recipient, purpose and expiry [HHS-45CFR164] | Lawful collateral contact |
| Presenting problem | Problem list in client's words; onset, course, triggers, frequency, severity, impact; "why now" | Comprehensive assessment weighs severity, duration, course and impairment [NICE-NG222] |
| Presenting problem | One specific recent episode (situation, thoughts, emotions, body, behavior) | Basis of the initial descriptive formulation [Kuyken-etal-2008] |
| Risk | Suicidal ideation, plans, intent, past attempts, self-harm (R) | Direct inquiry is recommended [NICE-NG222] |
| Risk | Harm to others, access to firearms or other means (R) | Aggression and suicide risk estimates must be documented [Armstrong-2016] |
| Risk | Dependants at risk, abuse of the client | Safeguarding duties |
| Risk | Risk estimate, factors, actions, crisis numbers given, review date (R) | Defensible, actionable risk record |
| History | Psychiatric history, treatments and response, hospitalizations (R) | Guides treatment choice |
| History | Past mood elevation | Possible bipolar disorder [NICE-NG222] |
| History | Medical conditions, current medicines including nonprescribed, side effects (R) | Medical and medication contributors |
| History | Tobacco, alcohol, other drugs, misuse of medicines (R) | Maintaining factors, risk |
| History | Trauma exposure and current impact (R) | Need for trauma-focused care |
| History | Family history including suicide and violence (R) | Vulnerability and risk |
| History | Developmental history, relationships, living conditions, work, money, isolation [NICE-NG222] | Predisposing and maintaining factors |
| Culture | Language and interpreter need, cultural view of the problem (R) | Engagement and accurate assessment |
| Strengths | Personal strengths, supports, past coping [NICE-NG222] | Resources for change [Kuyken-etal-2008] |
| Measures | Baseline scores on validated measures of symptoms and functioning [NICE-NG222] (suggested in the guideline) | Baseline for measurement-based care |
| Mental status | Appearance, mood, thought content and process, perception, cognition (R) | Detects psychosis, cognitive problems, acute risk |
| Collateral | Informants, consent, what each reported | Labeled, traceable third-party data |
| Goals | Goals in client's words, hopes, expectations, preferences [NICE-NG222] | Shared goals and preferences shape engagement |
| Impression | Provisional diagnosis or problem description, differential, initial formulation [Persons-2008] | Links assessment to plan |
| Plan | Agreed treatment, rationale for the choice (R), first task, next appointment, referrals | Decision-making shared and documented [Armstrong-2016] |
| Feedback | Client's view of the session | Early alliance check |

## What to record

Record each row above, writing "denied" for a negative answer and "not assessed" for a gap, with a date for completing it. Keep informant reports and clinician inferences labeled.

## Guidance

- If time ran out before a group was covered → write "not assessed" and schedule it. Because an empty field reads as a negative finding.
- If the risk screen was positive → record the specific answers, your estimate with its factors, and actions, not just "risk assessed" or a low, medium or high label. Because guidelines ask for an estimate with the factors that influence it [APA-PsychEval-2016], and in the UK the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises against using such labels to predict suicide [NICE-NG225].
- If the client is uninsured or will not file a claim → record the GFE date and what it covered. Because the federal rule sets deadlines and requires the estimate to be kept in the record [CFR-45-149-610]. In the UK, National Health Service (NHS) care is free of charge, so billing fields apply only in private practice [NHS-Constitution-2023].
- If you are designing a digital intake form → make risk items mandatory for clinician review and route positive answers to a human the same day. Because a form cannot respond to a positive answer.
- If the client declines to answer an item → record "declined" and respect it. Because consent and autonomy apply to each question.
- If an AI tool drafts the intake note → check every item against what the client actually said before signing. Because the clinician owns the record.
- If notes include another person's information → label the source and keep it minimal. Because third-party information has its own privacy implications.

## Common problems

- **Templates that hide gaps.** Default values like "no risk" are unsafe; use explicit unanswered states.
- **Copy-forward of old risk entries.** Always re-ask and date the current answer.
- **No rationale for the plan.** The guideline asks for the reasons behind the treatment choice to be documented [Armstrong-2016].
