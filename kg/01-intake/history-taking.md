**Bottom line:** History tells you whether something other than the presenting problem must be treated or ruled out first (a medical cause, a substance, a past manic episode, current danger), and it supplies the predisposing and protective factors for the formulation. Cover every domain briefly at intake, go deeper only where the answer changes a decision, and always ask about strengths.

## When to use

- Every new client, after the presenting problem and risk screen.
- Use intake forms for the factual parts (medications, medical conditions, family history) and spend interview time on meaning and gaps.

## Steps

Work through the domains below. Depth depends on the answer.

| Domain | Ask about | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Psychiatric | Past episodes, diagnoses, treatments, adherence and response, hospitalizations and emergency visits [APA-PsychEval-2016] | Predicts course; past response guides choice |
| Mood elevation | Any past period of unusually high or irritable mood with less need for sleep | Depression may be part of bipolar disorder [NICE-NG222] |
| Medical | Ongoing primary care relationship, past illness, head injury, sleep including sleep apnea, pain [APA-PsychEval-2016] | Medical conditions can cause or worsen symptoms |
| Medication | All current and recent medicines, including nonprescribed and over-the-counter products, and side effects [APA-PsychEval-2016] | Side effects and interactions mimic or mask symptoms |
| Substances | Tobacco, alcohol, other drugs, misuse of prescribed medicines [APA-PsychEval-2016] | Substance use maintains anxiety, low mood and poor sleep |
| Family | Mental illness, suicidal behavior and violence in biological relatives [Armstrong-2016] | Risk and vulnerability |
| Developmental | Early home life, school, key relationships, losses | Predisposing and protective factors for the longitudinal formulation [Kuyken-etal-2008] |
| Social | Relationships, living conditions, debt, employment, loneliness and isolation, diet, activity, sleep [NICE-NG222] | Maintaining factors and practical barriers |
| Trauma | Exposure to potentially traumatic events | Trauma-related problems need specific treatment |
| Culture and language | Need for an interpreter, cultural explanations of the problem [Armstrong-2016] | Shapes engagement and meaning |
| Strengths | Skills, values, supportive relationships, past coping [NICE-NG222] | Resources for change and resilience [Padesky-Mooney-2012] |

**Screening questions you can use.**
- Alcohol: the single question validated in primary care asks how many times in the past year the person has had 5 or more drinks in a day (4 for women); one or more is positive. It was 81.8% sensitive and 79.3% specific for unhealthy alcohol use [Smith-etal-2009].
- Drugs: how many times in the past year the person has used an illegal drug or a prescription medicine for nonmedical reasons; one or more is positive. It was 100% sensitive and 73.5% specific for a drug use disorder [Smith-etal-2010].
- Trauma exposure: the Life Events Checklist for the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5), known as the LEC-5, lists 16 event types plus "other", lets clients mark them without describing them, and gives no total score [LEC-5-Site].
- Trauma symptoms: the Primary Care Posttraumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) Screen for DSM-5 (PC-PTSD-5) asks about trauma exposure, then 5 yes/no symptom items. The National Center for PTSD suggests a cut-point of 4, lower for women when resources allow [PC-PTSD-5-Site]; the validation study found 3 maximized sensitivity [Prins-etal-2016]. Both instruments are public domain.
- Culture: the Cultural Formulation Interview (CFI) has 16 core questions and an informant version [APA-CFI-Site].

## What to record

- Each domain covered, with "denied" or "not assessed" stated explicitly rather than left blank.
- Medication list with doses and prescriber; primary care clinician's name.
- Screen results with the exact question asked.
- Strengths in the client's words.
- What still needs assessment and when.

## Guidance

- If the client reports a past period of high energy and little need for sleep → assess for bipolar disorder before starting cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for depression and consider psychiatric review. Because the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) asks clinicians to check mood elevation history [NICE-NG222].
- If the client has no primary care clinician or new physical symptoms → advise a medical review. Because the American Psychiatric Association recommends establishing a primary care relationship [APA-PsychEval-2016].
- If a substance screen is positive → explore quantity, pattern and function, and consider a substance use assessment. Because substances can drive or maintain the presenting problem.
- If the client discloses trauma → acknowledge it, ask about current impact, and do not ask for a detailed narrative at intake. "You don't need to go into details today. Can you tell me how it affects you now?" Because a full account belongs in planned trauma-focused work with time to process it (expert consensus).
- If the client struggles to name any strengths → ask what others would say, or how they got through a past hard time. Because a structured search brings hidden strengths into awareness [Padesky-Mooney-2012].

## Common problems

- **History crowds out engagement.** Use forms and spread history over two sessions if needed.
- **Blank fields.** An unrecorded domain looks the same as a negative one. Write "not yet asked."
- **Only deficits recorded.** Strengths are part of the formulation [Kuyken-etal-2008].
