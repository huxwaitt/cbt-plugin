**Bottom line:** Structured and semi-structured interviews make diagnosis more reliable and catch comorbidity that routine interviews miss. Use the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview (MINI) for a fast broad screen, the Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-5, Clinician Version (SCID-5-CV) for a thorough general diagnosis, the Anxiety and Related Disorders Interview Schedule for DSM-5 (ADIS-5) when anxiety and mood differential diagnosis matters, and the Diagnostic Interview for ADHD in Adults (DIVA-5) for adult attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). None replaces clinical judgment, and diagnosis is restricted by license in many places.

## What it measures

All four map client reports onto the criteria of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5). Check how the version you use handles changes made in the text revision (DSM-5-TR).

- **SCID-5-CV:** the major disorders seen in clinical practice. Sister versions cover research (SCID-5-RV), clinical trials (SCID-5-CT) and personality disorders (SCID-5-PD) [McGill-SCID-Page].
- **MINI 7.0.2:** 17 disorders or subtypes, including depression, bipolar, psychotic, substance use and anxiety disorders and suicidality; adults over 18, with a MINI Kid for ages 6 to 17 [MINI-Site].
- **ADIS-5 (Adult and Lifetime versions):** anxiety, mood, obsessive-compulsive, trauma and related disorders such as somatic symptom and substance use disorders, built for differential diagnosis among them [Brown-Barlow-2014].
- **DIVA-5:** the 18 ADHD symptom criteria in childhood and adulthood, age of onset, and impairment in five areas of everyday life [DIVA-Site]. It covers ADHD only, so other conditions need separate assessment.

## Format and scoring

| Interview | Format | Typical time | Training and access |
|---|---|---|---|
| SCID-5-CV | Semi-structured, clinician-rated | 30 to 120 minutes; longer with complex comorbidity [McGill-SCID-Page] | Needs clinical training and experience of open diagnostic interviewing; purchased from American Psychiatric Association Publishing [McGill-SCID-Page] |
| MINI 7.0.2 | Structured, clinician-rated, branching yes/no questions | Median 15, mean 18.7 minutes [MINI-Site]; about 15 minutes in the original validation [Sheehan-etal-1998] | License required, 15 US dollars per administration including nonprofits; web training with certificate [MINI-Site] |
| ADIS-5 | Semi-structured, clinician-rated, with 0 to 8 severity ratings per diagnosis | No time given in the publisher material reviewed; plan an extended appointment | Published by Oxford University Press with a clinician manual [Brown-Barlow-2014] |
| DIVA-5 | Semi-structured, with everyday examples for each criterion | About 1 to 1.5 hours [DIVA-Site] | 10 euros per PDF; clinic option of 10 euros per clinician with unlimited use; online training from the developers [DIVA-Site] |

The Lifetime version of the ADIS for DSM-IV (ADIS-IV-L), predecessor of the ADIS-5, showed good to excellent interrater reliability for most anxiety and mood diagnoses, and its 0 to 8 dimensional ratings showed favorable agreement [Brown-etal-2001a].

## Interpreting scores

- **Diagnoses, not totals.** These interviews output present or absent diagnoses (and, for the ADIS-5, severity ratings), not a single score.
- **Reliability.** The SCID-5-CV showed 73% to 97% positive agreement with clinical diagnoses and kappas above 0.70 for most diagnoses in joint interviews, and the authors judged telephone administration adequate [Osorio-etal-2019]. The Korean DIVA-5 showed 92% accuracy, 91.3% sensitivity and 93.6% specificity [Hong-etal-2020].
- **Why bother.** In one practice, clients assessed with the SCID for DSM-IV, the predecessor of the SCID-5, received significantly more diagnoses; over a third had three or more, versus fewer than 10% with routine interviews [Zimmerman-Mattia-1999]. Across 38 studies, agreement between routine clinical and standardized diagnoses averaged kappa 0.27 [Rettew-etal-2009]. Anxiety disorders were among those most often missed [Zimmerman-Mattia-1999].
- **Limits.** A structured interview is only as good as the answers and the interviewer's follow-up. The SCID-5-CV validation team advised careful use by clinicians with little psychiatric experience [Osorio-etal-2019].

## Licensing and access

- SCID-5: purchased from the American Psychiatric Association; some uses need permission [McGill-SCID-Page].
- MINI: signed license agreement before any use; buying a download is not a license [MINI-Site].
- ADIS-5: purchased client interview schedules and clinician manual [Brown-Barlow-2014].
- DIVA-5: a password-protected PDF from the DIVA Foundation, or a clinic template charged per clinician, in many languages; Young DIVA-5 covers ages 5 to 17, DIVA-5 ID covers intellectual disability, and a revised DIVA-5-R has been announced [DIVA-Site]. Some secondary sources still describe it as free; the developer's site charges a fee.

Do not reproduce item wording from any of these in notes, forms or software.

## Guidance

- If you need a quick broad check for missed disorders at intake → use the MINI. Because it is short and covers the common disorders [MINI-Site].
- If diagnosis drives the treatment protocol or the picture is complex → use the SCID-5-CV, possibly across two appointments. Because routine interviews under-detect comorbidity [Zimmerman-Mattia-1999].
- If the main question is which anxiety or mood disorder is primary → use the ADIS-5. Because it is built for differential diagnosis within these disorders [Brown-Barlow-2014].
- If an adult asks to be assessed for ADHD → use the DIVA-5 and seek an informant who knew them as a child. Because the DIVA-5 asks for collateral information where possible [DIVA-Site].
- If you are not licensed to diagnose → use interview results as structured information for the diagnosing clinician. Because scope of practice varies by license and jurisdiction.
- If the client finds the interview tedious → explain the purpose: "These questions make sure we don't miss anything that would change the plan." Because routine interviews miss comorbid disorders that change treatment, anxiety disorders among them [Zimmerman-Mattia-1999].
