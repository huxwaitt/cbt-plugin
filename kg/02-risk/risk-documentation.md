**Bottom line:** A good risk note lets a later reader see what you knew, how you thought about it, what you decided and why, and what happens next. The Joint Commission, a US accreditor, requires the suicide risk assessment to cover ideation, plan, intent, suicidal or self-harm behavior, risk factors and protective factors, and requires documented risk stratification and a mitigation plan [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024]. The reasoning matters more than the label: the note should hold up to the review that often follows a suicide or attempt [Wortzel-etal-2013].

## When to use

- After every suicide, self-harm, violence or safeguarding assessment, including brief checks that find no current risk.
- After any risk decision: admission or not, increased contact, disclosure to a third party, a welfare check, or a report.
- After a crisis contact, missed appointment by an at-risk client, or a change in risk.

## Steps

1. **Write the same day**, as close to the event as possible.
2. **Sources**: who you spoke with (client, family, other clinicians), records reviewed, instruments used.
3. **Current presentation**: ideation, plan, intent, preparatory behavior, recent self-harm or attempts, in the client's own words where useful. Do not record method detail beyond what care requires.
4. **Instruments**: name and result (for example "Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) screener: yes to items 1 and 2, no to 3 to 6"). Instruments add structure and record a baseline, but they supplement the clinical interview [Homaifar-etal-2013].
5. **Risk and protective factors and warning signs** relevant to this person.
6. **Formulation**: two or three sentences on what is driving risk now and what is reducing it.
7. **Risk level, if your service uses one**: acute and chronic separately, each with its reasons [Wortzel-etal-2014]. In the UK, the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises against global low, medium or high labels to predict risk, so record a formulation of needs instead [NICE-NG225] [Mughal-etal-2023].
8. **Decision and rationale**: what you decided, the options you considered and rejected, and why.
9. **Plan**: safety plan (attached or summarized), lethal means steps agreed and who will carry them out [Simonetti-etal-2021], contact frequency, next appointment, crisis contacts given, and any referrals.
10. **Consultation and communication**: who you consulted, what was shared with whom, on what legal basis.
11. **Client's view**: their agreement or disagreement with the plan.

## What to record

Use this checklist line by line: sources; ideation, plan, intent, behavior; instruments; factors; formulation; level with reasons (if used); decision and alternatives; plan with means safety; consultation and disclosures; client's view; next review date.

Example of a decision line: "Acute risk judged intermediate: daily ideation, no intent, able to use safety plan, reasons for living (children). Hospitalization considered and not chosen because client is engaged and lives with partner who agreed to secure means. Plan: safety plan revised, partner call today with consent, review in 48 hours, 988 and after-hours line given."

## Guidance

- If you decide not to hospitalize or not to disclose → record the alternatives considered and why the chosen plan fits the formulation. Because the record should show a process that withstands later scrutiny [Wortzel-etal-2013].
- If you request a welfare check → record the information that prompted it, who you called, and the outcome. Because welfare-check decisions need documented reasoning [Wortzel-etal-2019].
- If you discuss lethal means → record what was agreed, by whom and by when. Because documentation of means safety counseling is part of firearm and lethal means safety practice [Simonetti-etal-2021].
- If a client "contracted for safety" in the past → do not record it as a safety measure. Because no-suicide contracts lack evidence of effectiveness [Rudd-etal-2006a].
- If the note uses a risk label → put the drivers and actions next to it. Because a label alone does not explain the decision [Wortzel-etal-2014].

## Common problems

- "Denies suicidal ideation (SI) and homicidal ideation (HI)" as the whole assessment. Record what was actually asked and answered.
- Copy-forward risk text that no longer matches the session.
- Recording a level with no plan, or a plan with no reasons.
- Pejorative language ("manipulative", "attention-seeking"). Describe behavior and function instead.
- Too much detail about methods or injuries; record only what care needs.
