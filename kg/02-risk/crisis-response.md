**Bottom line:** When a client has intent to die and cannot keep themselves safe without outside help, the job shifts from therapy to securing safety: stay with them, reduce access to means, get emergency evaluation, and hand over directly. Such high acute risk typically requires psychiatric hospitalization, voluntary or involuntary, to maintain safety [MIRECC-TRM-2024]. Afterward, the days and weeks following discharge are a period of elevated risk, so follow up actively [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].

## When to use

- Intent to act, a worked-out plan with intent, or suicidal behavior in the past three months on screening [C-SSRS-Site].
- The client cannot agree to a safety plan or says they will act when they leave.
- Severe agitation, intoxication, psychosis with commands to harm, or a credible threat to another person.
- A family member or other source reports an imminent threat.

## Steps

1. **Stay with the client.** Ask them to stay and keep someone with them; do not physically restrain them yourself. Ask a colleague to help with calls if you are in person.
2. **Reduce immediate access to means** in the room or at their location (for telehealth, ask them to move away from means or have a support person remove them).
3. **Decide the route** with your supervisor or on-call lead if available:
   - Imminent danger, medical injury, or the client is leaving: US call 911; UK call 999 [NHS-Urgent-Help].
   - High acute risk but cooperative: arrange same-day emergency psychiatric evaluation and safe transport. Do not let the client drive themselves [NHS-Urgent-Help].
   - Intermediate acute risk: consider hospitalization if the drivers would respond to inpatient care; otherwise frequent contact, a revised safety plan and lethal means counseling [MIRECC-TRM-2024].
4. **Use crisis services** as a bridge, ideally together in session: US 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, call or text 988 or chat at 988lifeline.org [Lifeline-988] (veterans and service members: 988 then press 1, or text 838255 [VCL-Site]); UK National Health Service (NHS) 111, choosing the mental health option, or Samaritans on 116 123, free 24 hours [NHS-Urgent-Help] [Samaritans-Site].
5. **Involve supports** the client agrees to, and share only what is needed for safety. Under the US Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), disclosure is permitted when the clinician believes in good faith it is needed to prevent or lessen a serious and imminent threat, to someone able to help, including the target [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-512]. State law may be stricter.
6. **Hand over directly** (warm handoff) to emergency or crisis staff with your assessment, the plan and your contact details.
7. **If the client leaves or disconnects**, call them back, then their emergency contact, then emergency services for a welfare check if danger remains. Request a welfare check for a clear safety reason, not defensively, because a poorly judged one can harm the relationship and the client's rights; document the reasoning [Wortzel-etal-2019].
8. **Document** the same day (see `02-risk.risk-documentation`) and debrief with a supervisor.

## What to record

Time line, what the client said, risk drivers, actions taken and why, who was contacted and what was shared, the outcome, the follow-up plan, and consultation.

## Guidance

- If the client is on video or phone → confirm their location and an emergency contact at the start of every session. Because you cannot send help without an address (expert consensus).
- If the client asks you to keep it secret → say plainly that you will keep as much private as you can, and that safety comes first. Because disclosure to prevent serious and imminent harm is legally permitted [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-512].
- If the client agrees to go to the emergency department → arrange accompanied transport and call ahead. Because handovers fail at transitions (expert consensus).
- If the client is discharged from hospital or the emergency department → contact them within days and keep contact going. Because follow-up calls after an emergency department visit were associated with fewer suicidal behaviors and better attendance [Stanley-etal-2018].
- If the client was hospitalized for suicide risk → consider periodic caring messages (letters or texts) for 12 months. Because these are suggested to reduce attempts after hospitalization [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].

Example phrasings: "I'm not going to leave you on your own with this. Let's call together now." "Right now my job is to keep you safe. Here is what I'm going to do, and I'll tell you each step."

## Common problems

- Ending the session on time with an unresolved high-risk disclosure.
- Giving a crisis number instead of calling it with the client.
- Assuming involuntary admission criteria; they are set by US state law and, in England and Wales, the Mental Health Act. Know the local route before you need it.
- No follow-up after the crisis episode.
