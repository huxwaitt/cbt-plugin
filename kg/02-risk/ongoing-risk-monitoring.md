**Bottom line:** Risk changes, so check it briefly every session for anyone with past suicidal thoughts or self-harm, and act on any positive answer the same day. On the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9), item 9 answered "nearly every day" carried about ten times the one-year risk of a suicide attempt compared with "not at all" [Simon-etal-2013], yet most later suicides in a large Veterans Health Administration (VHA) cohort followed a "not at all" answer [Louzon-etal-2016]. So a positive item 9 always needs a conversation, and a negative one never ends the inquiry.

## When to use

Every session for clients with any history of suicidal ideation, attempts or self-harm; whenever the PHQ-9 or another measure with a suicide item is completed; and at the reassessment triggers below.

## Steps

1. **Brief check in the mood check.** "Since we last met, have you had any thoughts of suicide or of hurting yourself?" Add "How strong, how often, and did you use your safety plan?"
2. **Review PHQ-9 item 9 before the client leaves.** Any answer above "not at all" → ask about the content (passive wish or active thoughts), frequency, intent, plan, behavior since last contact and means access. Use a C-SSRS (Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale) Since Last Visit or Since Last Contact version if you want structure [C-SSRS-Site].
3. **Compare with baseline.** Is this the client's usual chronic ideation, or new, more frequent, more intense, or linked to intent or preparation?
4. **Review the safety plan.** Did they use it, what worked, what needs changing [Stanley-Brown-SPI-Site].
5. **Re-check lethal means steps** agreed earlier.
6. **Decide**: no change, increased contact, full reassessment, or crisis response.
7. **Document** the check and the decision, even when negative.

**Reassessment triggers** (do a full assessment, not a brief check): new or intensifying ideation; intent, planning or preparatory behavior; a new attempt or self-harm; acute warning signs such as hopelessness, agitation, withdrawal or rising substance use; major loss, conflict, legal or financial crisis; hospital or emergency department discharge; medication start, stop or change; treatment transitions, therapist absence and ending. The period after acute care discharge is documented as high risk [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024]; the rest of the list is expert consensus.

## What to record

Each session: the answer to the risk question, PHQ-9 item 9 score and follow-up, safety plan use, changes from baseline, and the decision. At reassessment: the full note in `02-risk.risk-documentation`.

## Guidance

- If item 9 is positive → discuss it in that session, not at the next one. Because excess risk after a positive answer emerged within days and kept growing over months [Simon-etal-2013].
- If item 9 is "not at all" but other signs worry you → ask anyway. Because 71.6% of suicides in the VHA cohort had answered "not at all" on their most recent PHQ-9 [Louzon-etal-2016].
- If a client has chronic ideation at a stable level → track it against the personal baseline and focus on spikes, intent and preparation. Because chronic risk calls for routine follow-up, a well-developed safety plan and ongoing skills work, with action when acute features appear [MIRECC-TRM-2024].
- If the client says "the same as always" → still ask about intent and preparation. Because chronic ideation can mask an acute change (expert consensus).
- If scores improve quickly after a period of high risk → keep monitoring. Because risk is dynamic and improvement in mood does not remove past risk factors (expert consensus).
- If your service relies on PHQ-9 item 9 as its only suicide screen → use the full PHQ-9 and add a direct question or a validated screener such as the C-SSRS. Because the 2024 VA/DoD (Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense) guideline dropped its 2019 item 9 recommendation, lists the full PHQ-9 among several validated tools, and notes its evidence does not cover item 9 asked on its own [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].
- If a tool or app collects item 9 between sessions → route any positive answer to a clinician promptly and show crisis contacts (US 988 [Lifeline-988] or 911; UK 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123 [Samaritans-Site]). Because a screen without follow-up offers no protection (expert consensus).

Example phrasing: "You marked the last question as 'several days'. Can you tell me about those thoughts this week?"

## Common problems

- Item 9 scored and filed with no conversation (see `02-risk.pitfall-unaddressed-positive-screen`).
- Monitoring that stops when mood improves or near ending; build risk into the relapse prevention plan.
- Asking only "any thoughts of harming yourself?", which blurs suicide and self-harm and invites a quick no.
- Over-relying on screening scores: individual prediction from any single factor is near chance [Franklin-etal-2017].
