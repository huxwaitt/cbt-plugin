**Bottom line:** A screen only helps if someone acts on it. Any positive answer on Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) item 9 or on a suicide screener needs a same-day clinical conversation, because excess risk after a positive answer emerges over several days [Simon-etal-2013]. Build the workflow so positive items are seen before the client leaves and so a "no" is never treated as clearance [Louzon-etal-2016].

## What it looks like

- The PHQ-9 total is recorded for measurement-based care, but no one reads item 9.
- An app or portal collects questionnaires between sessions and nobody reviews them until the next appointment.
- A clinician sees "several days" on item 9 and assumes it is the client's usual pattern without asking.
- A positive screen is noted, a crisis number is printed, and no assessment follows.

## Why it happens

Questionnaires are often added for outcome tracking, not risk, and the workflow for positive items is never designed. Busy sessions push the conversation to "next time". Clinicians may also assume that passive or infrequent ideation is low risk. Yet item 9 answered "nearly every day" carried about a 4% one-year attempt risk versus 0.4% for "not at all", and the excess risk appeared within days and grew over months [Simon-etal-2013]. Screening programs on their own have not been shown to reduce suicide [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024], which is why the follow-up step carries the value.

## The fix

- If a questionnaire with a suicide item is used → read that item before the session ends, every time. Because excess risk after a positive answer emerges over several days and keeps growing for months [Simon-etal-2013].
- If item 9 or a screener item is positive → ask about frequency, intent, plan, behavior and means, then decide on action using the screener triage. Because intent, plan with intent, or recent behavior triggers safety precautions, while other positives trigger evaluation [C-SSRS-Site].
- If the screen is positive in a medical or remote setting → arrange a brief suicide safety assessment by a trained clinician. Because a positive ASQ (Ask Suicide-Screening Questions) screen is followed by a brief suicide safety assessment [NIMH-ASQ].
- If measures are collected remotely → set a response standard (who sees positives, how fast, what happens out of hours), show crisis contacts on screen (US 988 or 911; UK 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123), and tell clients the form is not monitored live. Because a screen without a response path gives false reassurance (expert consensus).
- If item 9 is negative → keep asking directly when other signs appear. Because 71.6% of suicides in one large cohort had answered "not at all" on their most recent PHQ-9 [Louzon-etal-2016].

Example phrasing: "Before we finish, I noticed you marked the question about thoughts of being better off dead. Can we talk about that for a few minutes?"

## Sources note

The time course and risk gradient come from a cohort of 84,418 outpatients [Simon-etal-2013]; the proportion of suicides after a "not at all" answer comes from a Veterans Health Administration cohort [Louzon-etal-2016]. Triage rules come from the official C-SSRS (Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale) primary care screener [C-SSRS-Site]; the response-standard advice for remote collection is expert consensus.
