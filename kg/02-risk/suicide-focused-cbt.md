**Bottom line:** Treat suicidality as a target in its own right, not only as a symptom that will fade when depression lifts. Brief cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) protocols focused on suicide cut repeat attempts by about half or more in two randomized trials [Brown-etal-2005] [Rudd-etal-2015], and the 2024 US guideline suggests CBT-based therapy focused on suicide prevention after recent suicidal behavior (weak recommendation) [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].

## When to use

- Suicide attempt or other suicidal behavior within the past six months: CBT-based therapy focused on suicide prevention is suggested to reduce attempts [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].
- History of self-directed violence: CBT focused on suicide prevention, including problem-solving based therapy, is suggested to reduce suicidal ideation [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].
- Adults who self-harm: the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) recommends considering a CBT-informed intervention of about 4 to 10 sessions [NICE-NG225] [Mughal-etal-2023].

## How it works

These protocols treat the suicidal crisis as a learned, understandable sequence of triggers, thoughts, emotions and actions. Hopelessness, perceived burdensomeness and entrapment are treated as cognitive targets, and the move from thought to action is treated as a skills and environment problem, consistent with models that separate the emergence of ideation (defeat, entrapment) from the transition to behavior (capability, means, planning) [OConnor-Kirtley-2018].

**The suicidal mode.** Rudd's suicidal mode extends Beck's theory of modes to suicidal crises [Rudd-2000]. Stable predispositions, such as prior attempts, early adversity and past mental disorder, set a baseline of risk. A trigger, external or internal, then activates four systems at once [Bryan-etal-2020]:
- **Cognitive:** the suicidal belief system, such as hopelessness, burdensomeness and the sense that the pain is unbearable.
- **Affective:** emotional pain such as despair, shame, guilt or anger.
- **Physiological:** arousal, agitation and disturbed sleep.
- **Behavioral:** withdrawal, substance use, preparation or an attempt.

The systems feed each other, so a crisis can escalate fast and then ease; risk rises and falls around the baseline [Bryan-etal-2020]. Short-term work deactivates the mode with the crisis plan and skills; longer-term work weakens the suicidal belief system (expert consensus). Map one recent crisis onto the four systems with the client, without method details.

## Steps

Common elements across protocols (described in general terms; follow a manual for delivery):
1. **Engage and assess**: collaborative risk assessment and the client's story of the recent crisis.
2. **Safety plan in session one** and lethal means safety, reviewed every session.
3. **Chain or narrative analysis** of the crisis: map triggers, thoughts, emotions and behaviors that led to it to find intervention points [Borges-etal-2019].
4. **Case formulation of suicidality**: what made suicide seem like a solution.
5. **Skills phase**: emotion regulation, problem solving, distress tolerance, and building reasons for living (for example a hope kit of reminders).
6. **Cognitive phase**: restructure hopelessness, burdensomeness and "unbearable" beliefs; work on self-criticism.
7. **Relapse prevention**: rehearse applying skills to a likely future crisis, and plan follow-up.

## Guidance

- If depression treatment is underway but suicidal thoughts persist → add suicide-specific targets rather than waiting. Because the US guideline says treatment should directly target suicidal thoughts and behaviors [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].
- If the client describes suicide as the only way out → formulate the entrapment and generate alternatives with problem solving. Because entrapment is a core driver of ideation [OConnor-Kirtley-2018].
- If the client keeps returning to crisis → run a chain analysis of the latest episode in the next session. Because chain analysis identifies the links that maintain ideation and behavior [Borges-etal-2019].
- If repeated self-harm with emotion dysregulation dominates → consider dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) or DBT-informed skills. Because DBT reduced self-directed violence in a meta-analysis [DeCou-etal-2019].
- If intent, a plan or preparatory behavior appears during treatment → pause protocol work, follow `02-risk.crisis-response`, and give crisis contacts (US 988 or 911; UK 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123). Because intent with inability to stay safe is high acute risk, which typically needs hospital-level care [MIRECC-TRM-2024].
- If a client is between sessions and wants support → offer self-guided digital CBT content as an adjunct, not a replacement. Because such tools are suggested only for short-term reduction in ideation [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].

## Common problems

- Dropping the suicide focus once mood improves. Keep checking ideation and the safety plan until relapse prevention ends.
- Skipping the crisis narrative because it is distressing. It grounds the formulation and plan; pace it and validate.
- Expecting ideation to fall as fast as behavior. In the trials below, attempts fell while ideation differences were small or absent.

## Evidence

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Cognitive therapy for suicide prevention [Brown-etal-2005] | 120 adults after an attempt, 10 sessions vs enhanced usual care, 18 months | Reattempts 24.1% vs 41.6%; hazard ratio 0.51; no difference in ideation |
| Brief CBT for suicide prevention [Rudd-etal-2015] | 152 active-duty soldiers with a recent attempt or ideation with intent, brief CBT plus usual care vs usual care, 24 months | Attempts 13.8% vs 40.2%; hazard ratio 0.38; no difference in psychiatric symptoms |
| DBT meta-analysis [DeCou-etal-2019] | 18 controlled trials | Self-directed violence d = -0.32; ideation not significant |
| Cochrane, adults who self-harm [Witt-etal-2021a] | 76 trials | CBT-based therapy may reduce repetition (low certainty) |

Sources disagree on DBT: the 2024 US Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense (VA/DoD) guideline found insufficient evidence for or against DBT to reduce suicidal ideation or attempts [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024], while the meta-analysis found a small effect on self-directed violence [DeCou-etal-2019]. The same guideline found insufficient evidence for or against the Collaborative Assessment and Management of Suicidality (CAMS) for ideation [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].
