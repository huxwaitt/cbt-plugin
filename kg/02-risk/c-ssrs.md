**Bottom line:** The Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) rates the severity and intensity of suicidal ideation and the types of suicidal behavior, in versions from a two-to-six question screener to a full clinician scale. On the primary care screener, intent, a plan with intent, or suicidal behavior in the past three months triggers behavioral health consultation and safety precautions; other yes answers trigger referral [C-SSRS-Site]. It structures and documents risk; it does not predict who will die by suicide.

## What it measures

Four constructs [Posner-etal-2011]:
- **Severity of ideation**: five ordered levels, from a wish to be dead, through non-specific active thoughts, active thoughts with method but no intent, active thoughts with some intent, up to active thoughts with a specific plan and intent.
- **Intensity of ideation**: five items (frequency, duration, controllability, deterrents, reasons for ideation), each on a five-point scale.
- **Suicidal behavior**: actual, interrupted and aborted attempts, preparatory behavior, and non-suicidal self-injurious behavior, recorded separately.
- **Lethality** of actual attempts, with potential lethality rated when actual lethality is zero.

## Format and scoring

Versions on the official site include the full Lifetime/Recent scale, Since Last Visit and Since Last Contact versions for repeated use, screeners with triage points (Recent, Since Last Contact, primary care, community), a screener for children aged 6 to 11, full-scale versions for very young children (about ages 4 to 5) or cognitively impaired people, and a risk and protective factors page [C-SSRS-Site]. The screener asks two to six questions depending on answers [C-SSRS-Site]. Ideation questions cover the past month; the behavior question asks about lifetime and then the past three months. If the active-thoughts question is no, the screener skips to the behavior question [C-SSRS-Site]. The full scale combines interview, family and record information, and time frames are set to need: lifetime worst point, recent, or since last visit [Posner-etal-2011].

## Interpreting scores

| Screener answer | Primary care triage response [C-SSRS-Site] |
|---|---|
| Wish to be dead; active thoughts; method thinking | Behavioral health referral |
| Intent; worked-out plan with intent | Behavioral health consultation and patient safety precautions |
| Suicidal behavior, lifetime only | Behavioral health referral |
| Suicidal behavior within past 3 months | Behavioral health consultation and patient safety precautions |

The basic screener uses low, moderate and high risk bands, and the site says each setting can set its own triage next steps [C-SSRS-Site]. In validation studies the scale showed high sensitivity and specificity for classifying behavior, for example 100% sensitivity and specificity for lifetime actual attempts in an emergency department sample. In adolescent suicide attempters, a lifetime history of the two highest ideation levels (intent, or intent with plan) raised the odds of an attempt during follow-up (odds ratio 3.26, wide confidence interval) [Posner-etal-2011]. Treat these as group associations. Individual prediction of suicidal behavior remains near chance [Franklin-etal-2017], so a low score never closes the question.

## Licensing and access

Free for community and healthcare settings and for federally funded or nonprofit research; free training; more than 150 country-specific language versions [C-SSRS-Site]. Download from the official site rather than copying items into local forms. The full scale states it is intended for trained administrators, while the screener questions can be asked by anyone [C-SSRS-Site]. The US VA/DoD (Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense) guideline lists the C-SSRS screener among validated tools for both general and higher-risk populations (weak recommendation) [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024]. For medical settings, the free Ask Suicide-Screening Questions (ASQ) is an alternative for patients aged 8 and older, with a positive screen followed by a brief suicide safety assessment by a trained clinician [NIMH-ASQ]. The VA/DoD guideline also lists the full Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) for general populations, but notes its evidence came from administering all nine items, not item 9 alone [VA-DoD-Suicide-CPG-2024].

## Guidance

- If the client answers yes to intent or plan with intent, or reports behavior in the past three months → do not end the session; move to `02-risk.crisis-response` and consult. Because these answers trigger safety precautions [C-SSRS-Site].
- If only passive or active thoughts without method or intent are endorsed → complete a full assessment and safety plan within the session where possible. Because the screener flags a need for evaluation, not a risk level on its own [C-SSRS-Site].
- If a client was screened before → use a Since Last Visit or Since Last Contact version at follow-up. Because it captures new events between contacts [C-SSRS-Site].
- If you use the scale in the record → keep the completed form plus your narrative formulation. Because structured instruments document baseline ideation and behavior but do not replace clinical reasoning [Homaifar-etal-2013].
- If a tool or app administers the screener → route any trigger answer to a human clinician immediately and show crisis contacts (US 988 or 911; UK 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123). Because the triage depends on clinical follow-through, not the score [C-SSRS-Site].
