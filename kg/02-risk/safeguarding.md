**Bottom line:** In the US, about 38 states name counselors, therapists and other mental health professionals as mandated reporters of child abuse and neglect, and four more require every person to report without listing professions (laws as of May 2023), so assume you must report; the usual threshold is suspicion or reason to believe, not proof [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023]. Duties for elder and dependent adult abuse vary by state [NAPSA-Site]. In England, anyone with concerns about a child should consider a referral to local authority children's social care, and make it immediately if the child is suffering or likely to suffer significant harm [DfE-Working-Together-2026]. Explain these limits to confidentiality at intake, and act the same day.

## When to use

- A child discloses abuse, or an adult client discloses abuse of a child (including their own children), or you observe signs of abuse or neglect.
- An older or dependent adult client describes abuse, neglect, exploitation or self-neglect.
- A client describes intimate partner violence (IPV), including coercive control.

## Steps

1. **Listen and stay calm.** Let the person tell it in their words. Do not interrogate or ask leading questions; that is the investigators' job.
2. **Check immediate safety.** If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).
3. **Be honest about limits.** Do not promise secrecy. Explain what you must do and why.
4. **Decide which duty applies.**
   - US child abuse: report to the state child protective services hotline or law enforcement, as your state law specifies [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023]. Childhelp (800-422-4453) offers crisis counseling and a state directory of reporting lines but does not take reports [Childhelp-Site].
   - US older or dependent adult abuse: report to the local Adult Protective Services (APS) office; reporting routes differ by state [NAPSA-Site].
   - England, child: refer to local authority children's social care, immediately where significant harm is suspected [DfE-Working-Together-2026].
   - England, adult with care and support needs who is experiencing or at risk of abuse or neglect and cannot protect themselves: refer to the local authority, which must make enquiries under section 42 of the Care Act 2014 [Care-Act-2014-s42].
   - Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own safeguarding laws and guidance; follow the local route.
   - UK female genital mutilation (FGM) in a girl under 18: regulated health and social care professionals in England and Wales must report known cases to the police [HomeOffice-FGM-Duty-2015].
5. **Consult** a supervisor or safeguarding lead, but do not let consultation delay a required report.
6. **Report** factually: what was said or seen, by whom, when.
7. **Continue care.** Repair the alliance, support the client through the process, and update the safety plan.

## What to record

The disclosure in the client's words, observations, date and time, who you consulted, the report (agency, reference number, person spoken to, time), what you told the client, and the follow-up plan.

## Guidance

- If a child or teen asks you to keep abuse secret → say kindly that you cannot, explain who you will tell and why, and stay with them. Because most state reporting laws restrict privilege for mandated reporters, so the duty to report overrides confidentiality [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
- If you are unsure whether the threshold is met → consult the hotline or safeguarding lead the same day rather than gathering more evidence yourself. Because mandated reporters report the facts behind their suspicion and do not carry the burden of proof [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
- If you worry about breaching privacy in the UK → share the information needed. Because guidance states that fears about sharing must not stand in the way of safeguarding, and consent is not required where another lawful basis exists [DfE-Working-Together-2026].
- If a competent adult discloses IPV and does not want it reported → check whether a state reporting rule applies (children or dependent adults in the home change this); otherwise respect their choice and safety-plan with them. Because the benefit of IPV screening comes from offering or referring to interventions with ongoing support [USPSTF-IPV-2025], and respecting a competent adult's choice keeps them engaged (expert consensus).
- If you screen for IPV → do it privately, without the partner present. Because the US Preventive Services Task Force recommends IPV screening for women of reproductive age (grade B, 2025) and notes it works best in safe, private settings [USPSTF-IPV-2025].
- If a client needs IPV support → offer the National Domestic Violence Hotline: 800-799-7233, or text START to 88788. Because it is free and open 24/7 [NDVH-Site].
- If disclosure about an adult is needed for safety in the US → limit it to what prevents serious and imminent harm. Because the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) permits such disclosures [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-512] and the American Psychological Association (APA) Ethics Code allows disclosure as permitted by law [APA-Ethics-2017].

Example phrasing: "Thank you for telling me. What you've described means I need to talk to child protective services so that you and your brother can be kept safe. I'll tell you what I'll say."

## Common problems

- Delaying a report to "build the case" or to protect the alliance.
- Promising confidentiality before hearing the disclosure.
- Assuming the US rules apply in the UK, or that one state's elder abuse rules apply in another.
- For older adults, the evidence for routine screening for caregiver abuse is insufficient (grade I), so act on concerns rather than a screen [USPSTF-IPV-2025].
