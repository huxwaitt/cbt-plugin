**Bottom line:** Many common side effects look like the problems cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) treats: akathisia looks like anxiety, sedation looks like depression or low motivation, sexual side effects look like relationship problems, and low sodium or lithium-related thyroid changes look like cognitive slowing or relapse. Ask about medication changes whenever symptoms shift, and pass the observation to the prescriber.

## What it looks like

| What the therapist sees | Possible medication explanation |
|---|---|
| New inner restlessness, pacing, "can't sit still," labeled as anxiety | Akathisia: about 25% versus 4% on placebo when aripiprazole is added for depression [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label] |
| Agitation, panic, insomnia or new suicidal thoughts soon after starting an antidepressant | Listed in the patient guide as reasons to call the prescriber right away [FDA-Sertraline-Label] |
| Low drive, oversleeping, poor homework completion | Sleepiness on mirtazapine (54% versus 18%) [FDA-Mirtazapine-Label]; next-day impairment after a z-drug [FDA-Zolpidem-Label] |
| "Lost interest" in sex, relationship strain | Sexual side effects of selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), which clients rarely volunteer [FDA-Sertraline-Label] |
| Confusion, headache, unsteadiness in an older adult | Low sodium on an SSRI [FDA-Sertraline-Label] |
| Slowed thinking and low mood on lithium | Lithium-related hypothyroidism [FDA-Lithium-Label] |
| New anxiety or insomnia in someone treated for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) | Common stimulant adverse reactions [FDA-Methylphenidate-Label] |
| New gambling, spending or binge eating | Compulsive behaviors reported on aripiprazole [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label] |

## Why it happens

- The therapist's model explains everything psychologically, so new symptoms are absorbed into the case formulation.
- Clients do not link symptoms to a medicine started weeks ago.
- Medication lists are taken at intake and never updated.
- Therapists are unsure of their role and say nothing.

## The fix

- If a symptom is new or does not fit the formulation → ask "Has anything changed with your medicines, doses or timing in the last month?" Because many side effects mimic psychological symptoms [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label; FDA-Sertraline-Label].
- If restlessness appears after starting or raising an antipsychotic → describe it to the prescriber as possible akathisia, not anxiety. Because it is common and treatable [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].
- If agitation or suicidal thoughts appear after starting an antidepressant → assess risk now, follow `02-risk.crisis-response` if it is imminent, and otherwise contact the prescriber the same day. Because the label lists these for immediate contact [FDA-Sertraline-Label].
- If sedation is undermining activation work → plan activity at times of better alertness and tell the prescriber. Because sleepiness is a frequent effect [FDA-Mirtazapine-Label].
- If a client on lithium seems to be relapsing into depression → ask whether thyroid tests are up to date. Because lithium can lower thyroid function [FDA-Lithium-Label].
- If you are unsure → record the observation and ask; do not diagnose the side effect yourself (scope of practice).

Example phrasing: "You've been restless since the new tablet started. That can be a side effect, not just anxiety. Would it be OK for me to let Dr. Ruiz know what we've noticed?"

## Sources note

Side-effect frequencies and warnings come from current United States (US) Food and Drug Administration (FDA) labels [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label; FDA-Sertraline-Label; FDA-Mirtazapine-Label; FDA-Methylphenidate-Label; FDA-Lithium-Label; FDA-Zolpidem-Label]. Label rates come from short-term trials and can differ in practice. The pattern of side effects being absorbed into psychological formulations is expert consensus.
