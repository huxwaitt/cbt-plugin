**Bottom line:** Lithium, valproate, lamotrigine and antipsychotics treat bipolar disorder and psychosis, and some are added for treatment-resistant depression. Each needs specific monitoring, and each has warning signs a therapist may be the first to see: lithium toxicity, a rash on lamotrigine, restlessness from akathisia, weight gain, or new compulsive behaviors. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) works alongside these medicines, and the therapist's role is to notice, support adherence conversations and alert the prescriber.

## What it is

| Medicine | Main uses | Monitoring and key risks |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium | Bipolar disorder | Serum levels about 0.8 to 1.2 mEq/L in acute mania and 0.8 to 1.0 mEq/L for maintenance; toxicity from about 1.5 mEq/L but sometimes within range. Thyroid tests before starting, at 3 months, then every 6 to 12 months; kidney function before and during [FDA-Lithium-Label] |
| Valproate (divalproex) | Bipolar disorder, epilepsy | Liver failure (mostly first 6 months), pancreatitis, birth defects and lower intelligence scores after prenatal exposure [FDA-Divalproex-Label] |
| Lamotrigine | Bipolar maintenance, epilepsy | Serious rash in about 0.8 to 3 per 1,000 adults (about 1 per 1,000 in bipolar trials), nearly all in weeks 2 to 8; higher risk with valproate or fast dose increases [FDA-Lamotrigine-Label] |
| Antipsychotics | Psychosis, mania, add-on in depression | Weight, glucose and lipid changes; movement effects; neuroleptic malignant syndrome; higher death rate in older adults with dementia [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label] |
| Clozapine | Treatment-resistant schizophrenia | Severe neutropenia (boxed warning): absolute neutrophil count (ANC) checked, when normal, weekly for 6 months, every 2 weeks to month 12, then monthly [FDA-Clozapine-Label]. In February 2025 the United States (US) Food and Drug Administration (FDA) ended the expectation to take part in its Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) program, while still recommending ANC monitoring [FDA-Clozapine-REMS-2025] |

The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) lists adding a second-generation antipsychotic or lithium among options for depression that has not responded to recommended treatments [Kendrick-etal-2022].

## Why it matters in CBT

- **Lithium toxicity is easy to trigger.** Levels rise with dehydration, low salt intake, and common medicines: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen, angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers for blood pressure, and diuretics. Early signs are diarrhea, vomiting, tremor, drowsiness, muscle weakness and unsteadiness [FDA-Lithium-Label]. Illness with vomiting, a heat wave or a new painkiller are moments to check in.
- **Lithium can lower thyroid function** [FDA-Lithium-Label], which can look like returning depression.
- **Akathisia looks like anxiety.** On aripiprazole added to an antidepressant for depression, akathisia (an inner restlessness with an urge to move) occurred in about 25% versus 4% on placebo [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].
- **Compulsive urges.** Gambling, sexual urges, compulsive shopping and binge eating have been reported; patients may not see them as abnormal, so prescribers are told to ask [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].
- **Metabolic monitoring is often missed.** The 2004 US consensus schedule checks weight, waist, blood pressure, glucose and lipids at baseline, weight again at 4, 8 and 12 weeks, blood pressure, glucose and lipids again at 12 weeks, then at regular intervals (yearly for most). In practice, a meta-analysis of about 219,000 patients found weight, glucose and cholesterol checked in fewer than half [Bui-etal-2025].
- **Valproate in pregnancy.** In the United Kingdom (UK), valproate must not be prescribed to women or girls able to have children unless a Pregnancy Prevention Programme is followed; around 11% of babies exposed in pregnancy have a birth defect and 30% to 40% may have developmental problems [MHRA-Valproate-2025].

## Key details

- **Tardive dyskinesia** (involuntary movements) can be irreversible; risk rises with duration and dose [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].
- **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome:** high fever, rigidity, altered mental state, unstable pulse and blood pressure; an emergency [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].
- **Falls and dizziness** on standing are more likely in older adults [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].

## In the session

- If a client on lithium has vomiting, diarrhea, a new tremor or unsteadiness → help them contact the prescriber the same day. Because these are early toxicity signs [FDA-Lithium-Label].
- If a client on lithium is prescribed a new painkiller or blood pressure medicine → suggest they check with the lithium prescriber. Because NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors and diuretics raise lithium levels [FDA-Lithium-Label].
- If a client recently started lamotrigine mentions any rash → urge same-day contact with the prescriber. Because rash should prompt stopping unless clearly unrelated [FDA-Lamotrigine-Label].
- If a client on an antipsychotic becomes restless and cannot sit still → describe it to the prescriber as possible akathisia. Because it is common and treatable [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].
- If a client reports new gambling or spending urges → ask directly and inform the prescriber. Because these are labeled effects [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].
- If weight gain is undermining adherence or self-esteem → include it in the case formulation and share it with the prescriber. Because metabolic effects need monitoring that is often missed [Bui-etal-2025].
- If a client of childbearing potential takes valproate and mentions plans for pregnancy → encourage an early specialist conversation. Because of the fetal risks [MHRA-Valproate-2025; FDA-Divalproex-Label].

Example phrasing: "You mentioned feeling jittery and unable to sit still since the new tablet. That can be a side effect called akathisia. Shall we let your psychiatrist know today?"
