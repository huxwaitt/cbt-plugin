**Bottom line:** Refer for medical assessment when symptoms are new, sudden, atypical, accompanied by physical or neurological signs, linked to a medicine or substance, or not responding to good cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT). Sort each concern by urgency: emergency (call emergency services now), same day (contact the prescriber or urgent care today), or routine (primary care within weeks). Tell the client why, send specifics, and follow up.

## When to use

- At intake, after the medical, medication and substance history.
- Any time a client reports new physical symptoms or a reaction after starting, stopping or changing a medicine.
- When a client looks confused, intoxicated or in withdrawal in session.
- When progress stalls and the formulation does not explain why.

## Steps

1. **Check for an emergency first.** Call 911 in the United States (US) or 999 in the United Kingdom (UK), or arrange immediate emergency department care, for:
   - Sudden confusion, fluctuating alertness or disorientation (possible delirium, an acute change in attention, awareness and cognition) [Wilson-etal-2020].
   - High fever, muscle rigidity and altered mental state in someone on an antipsychotic (possible neuroleptic malignant syndrome) [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].
   - Agitation, confusion, sweating, fever, tremor or muscle stiffness after combining serotonergic medicines (possible serotonin syndrome) [FDA-Sertraline-Label; FDA-Lithium-Label].
   - A seizure, or tremor with hallucinations or confusion after abruptly stopping alcohol or a benzodiazepine [ASAM-Alcohol-Withdrawal-2020; FDA-Benzodiazepine-DSC-2020].
   - Trouble breathing or extreme drowsiness when benzodiazepines are combined with opioids or alcohol [FDA-Benzodiazepine-DSC-2020].
   - Imminent suicide risk: follow `02-risk.crisis-response`. US: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) or 911 [Lifeline-988]. UK: 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111, or Samaritans on 116 123 [Samaritans-Site].
2. **Then check for same-day concerns.** Help the client contact the prescriber, on-call service or urgent care today for:
   - Diarrhea, vomiting, coarse tremor, drowsiness, muscle weakness or unsteadiness on lithium (possible toxicity) [FDA-Lithium-Label].
   - Any rash in the first weeks of lamotrigine [FDA-Lamotrigine-Label].
   - Abdominal pain with nausea or vomiting on valproate (possible pancreatitis) [FDA-Divalproex-Label].
   - Fever, sore throat or other signs of infection on clozapine (possible severe neutropenia, a dangerously low white cell count) [FDA-Clozapine-Label].
   - New or worse agitation, restlessness, panic, insomnia or suicidal thoughts after starting or changing an antidepressant [FDA-Sertraline-Label].
   - New psychotic or manic symptoms on a stimulant or atomoxetine, jaundice on atomoxetine, or an erection lasting over 4 hours [FDA-Methylphenidate-Label; FDA-Atomoxetine-Label].
   - Mood or behavior change on montelukast [FDA-Montelukast-DSC-2020].
3. **Otherwise arrange a routine primary care review** for: fatigue with low mood, possible thyroid or anemia features, snoring with daytime sleepiness [Povitz-etal-2014], first onset of psychological symptoms in mid or late life, and persistent non-response.
4. **Explain to the client** what you noticed, why it matters, and what the doctor may check. Get consent to share information.
5. **Communicate** by phone for same-day concerns and in writing for routine ones (see `12-medical-pharmacology.working-with-prescribers`).
6. **Follow up** at the next session: did they attend, what was found, and does the case formulation or plan need to change?

## What to record

- The observation in the client's words and your own (onset, timing, medicines involved).
- The urgency level you chose and why.
- Who you contacted, when, what you shared, and the reply.
- The client's consent, or the reason you acted without it in an emergency.
- The outcome and any change to the treatment plan.

## Guidance

- If you are unsure how urgent a physical symptom is → treat it as the higher level and ask a medical professional. Because conditions such as lithium toxicity can occur close to therapeutic levels [FDA-Lithium-Label].
- If a client wants to stop a benzodiazepine abruptly → advise them not to and to contact the prescriber. Because abrupt stopping can cause seizures [FDA-Benzodiazepine-DSC-2020].
- If a client on an antipsychotic seems newly restless and anxious → describe it to the prescriber as possible akathisia rather than labeling it anxiety. Because akathisia is common on some antipsychotics [FDA-Aripiprazole-Label].
- If the client refuses medical review → record the advice and their reasons, keep the door open, and revisit. Because an adult with capacity may decline, and a documented, repeated offer keeps the option open (expert consensus). If the concern is an emergency, Step 1 still applies: call emergency services and record your reasoning.

Example phrasing: "Some of what you're describing sounds physical, and I'd be letting you down if we didn't get it checked. Can we call your doctor's office together before you leave today?"

## Common problems

- **Psychologizing physical symptoms.** Explaining new confusion or a rash as stress. Fix: use the urgency sort above every time.
- **Referring without content.** "Please review" letters get filed. Fix: state the observation, the timeline, the medicine and your question.
- **No follow-up.** The referral is made and never checked. Fix: put it on the next agenda.
- **Acting outside scope.** Advising dose changes instead of referring; see `12-medical-pharmacology.pitfall-advising-on-medication-changes`.
