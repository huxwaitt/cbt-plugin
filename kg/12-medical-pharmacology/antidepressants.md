**Bottom line:** All 21 antidepressants compared in the largest network meta-analysis beat placebo for adult major depression, with modest differences between them [Cipriani-etal-2018]. For a cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) therapist the practical points are: benefit is judged over about 4 weeks at a therapeutic dose, early weeks bring side effects and a warning about suicidal thinking in young people, stopping needs a taper, and every change is the prescriber's decision.

## What it is

| Class | Common examples | Points for the therapist |
|---|---|---|
| Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) | Sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine, citalopram, paroxetine | Usual first choice; the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) names an SSRI among first options [Kendrick-etal-2022]. Nausea, sexual dysfunction, bleeding risk with anti-inflammatories or blood thinners, low sodium in older adults [FDA-Sertraline-Label]. Citalopram is capped at 40 mg a day (20 mg over age 60) because of heart rhythm risk [FDA-Citalopram-Label] |
| Serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) | Venlafaxine, duloxetine | Similar uses; short half-life drugs such as venlafaxine need slower tapering [Kendrick-etal-2022] |
| Bupropion | Bupropion | Dose-related seizure risk; not for people with bulimia or anorexia, or who are abruptly stopping alcohol or benzodiazepines [FDA-Bupropion-Label] |
| Mirtazapine | Mirtazapine | Sleepiness in 54% versus 18% on placebo, weight gain in 12% versus 2%; rarely a dangerously low white cell count, so fever or sore throat needs prompt review [FDA-Mirtazapine-Label] |
| Older drugs | Tricyclics such as amitriptyline; monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) | Amitriptyline had the largest effect versus placebo (odds ratio 2.13) but more side effects [Cipriani-etal-2018]; MAOIs combined with other serotonergic drugs raise serotonin syndrome risk [FDA-Sertraline-Label], so prescribers manage them closely |

Herbal St John's wort is not a safe add-on: it weakens many medicines, including birth control pills, and can add serotonin effects to antidepressants [NCCIH-St-Johns-Wort].

## Why it matters in CBT

- **Guidelines.** The NICE 2022 depression guideline lists individual CBT plus an antidepressant as a first option for more severe depression, and advises against routine antidepressants as first line for less severe depression unless the person prefers them [Kendrick-etal-2022].
- **Timing.** NICE advises reviewing adherence and options (dose change, switch or combination) if there is no or limited response after 4 weeks at a therapeutic dose, and continuing for at least 6 months after remission [Kendrick-etal-2022; RCPsych-Stopping-Antidepressants-2024]. A client's mood scores in weeks 1 to 4 are useful data for the prescriber.
- **Early risk.** The United States (US) Food and Drug Administration (FDA) boxed warning covers suicidal thoughts and behaviors in children and young adults. In pooled trials there were 14 extra cases per 1,000 under age 18 and 5 per 1,000 at ages 18 to 24, with fewer cases than placebo from age 25 [FDA-Sertraline-Label]. Monitoring matters most in the first months and after dose changes [FDA-Sertraline-Label].
- **Relapse.** In primary care patients who felt well enough to stop, 56% of those tapered to placebo relapsed within a year versus 39% who stayed on medication [Lewis-etal-2021]. CBT has an enduring effect: relapse after stopping cognitive therapy was 31% versus 76% when medication was withdrawn to placebo [Hollon-etal-2005].

## Key details

- **Activation and mixed states.** Treating depression in bipolar disorder can trigger mania or a mixed episode; screen for past highs [FDA-Sertraline-Label].
- **Serotonin syndrome.** Mental status change, sweating, fever, tremor and stiffness, more likely when serotonergic drugs are combined [FDA-Sertraline-Label]. It is an emergency.
- **Sexual side effects.** Clients rarely volunteer them, so the label advises asking before and during treatment [FDA-Sertraline-Label]. They often explain "lost interest" or relationship strain.
- **Do not stop suddenly.** The patient guide says not to stop without talking to a prescriber [FDA-Sertraline-Label].

## In the session

- If the client starts an antidepressant → agree to track mood and side effects weekly and ask about new restlessness, agitation, panic, insomnia or suicidal thoughts. Because the patient guide lists these as reasons to call the prescriber right away [FDA-Sertraline-Label].
- If the client is 24 or younger and has just started or changed dose → reassess suicide risk at each session in the first months, and follow `02-risk.crisis-response` if risk is imminent. Because the boxed warning risk is concentrated in young people [FDA-Sertraline-Label].
- If the client reports sexual side effects, weight gain or sleepiness → normalize them and encourage a conversation with the prescriber. Because these effects are common and expected [FDA-Mirtazapine-Label; FDA-Sertraline-Label], and the prescriber has options the client may not know about.
- If the client takes St John's wort or plans to → ask them to tell the prescriber and pharmacist. Because it interacts with antidepressants and contraception [NCCIH-St-Johns-Wort].
- If a client with depression describes past periods of high energy and little sleep → share this with the prescriber before or soon after an antidepressant starts. Because antidepressants can trigger mania [FDA-Sertraline-Label].

Example phrasing: "Medication usually takes a few weeks to show what it can do. Let's keep your weekly scores so you and Dr. Patel can see the trend at your review."
