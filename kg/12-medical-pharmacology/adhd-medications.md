**Bottom line:** Stimulants (methylphenidate and amphetamines) are the most effective short-term medicines for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD); a large network meta-analysis favored methylphenidate first for children and adolescents and amphetamines for adults [Cortese-etal-2018]. Non-stimulants such as atomoxetine and guanfacine are alternatives. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) adds benefit for adults whose symptoms persist on medication [Safren-etal-2010]. The therapist tracks sleep, appetite, mood, misuse risk and timing, and reports concerns to the prescriber.

## What it is

| Group | Examples | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Stimulants | Methylphenidate; amphetamines including lisdexamfetamine | Work within the day; short- and long-acting forms. Boxed warning for abuse, misuse and addiction, with risk assessed before and during treatment [FDA-Lisdexamfetamine-Label; FDA-Methylphenidate-Label] |
| Norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor | Atomoxetine (also viloxazine) | Non-controlled; boxed warning for suicidal ideation in children (0.4% versus 0% on placebo, all in the first month) [FDA-Atomoxetine-Label] |
| Alpha-2 agonists | Extended-release guanfacine (also clonidine) | Used alone or with stimulants in ages 6 to 17; sleepiness, low blood pressure; must be tapered, as stopping suddenly can cause rebound high blood pressure [FDA-Guanfacine-Label] |

## Why it matters in CBT

- **Medication helps, but rarely finishes the job.** In medicated adults with persistent symptoms, CBT produced more responders than relaxation with educational support (53% versus 23% on clinician global ratings), and gains held at 12 months [Safren-etal-2010].
- **Tolerability varies.** Dropouts due to side effects were higher than placebo for amphetamines in both children and adults, for guanfacine in children, and for methylphenidate and atomoxetine in adults [Cortese-etal-2018]. Side effects are common reasons clients stop.
- **Evidence gaps.** Long-term data were too thin for conclusions beyond 12 weeks [Cortese-etal-2018].

## Key details

- **Common stimulant effects:** insomnia, decreased appetite, weight loss, anxiety, headache, fast heartbeat [FDA-Methylphenidate-Label; FDA-Lisdexamfetamine-Label].
- **Heart and blood pressure:** stimulants raise average blood pressure and heart rate and should be avoided in serious heart disease [FDA-Lisdexamfetamine-Label]. With atomoxetine, rises of 20 or more beats per minute, or 15 to 20 mm Hg in blood pressure, occurred in roughly 5% to 10% of children in trials [FDA-Atomoxetine-Label].
- **Psychiatric effects:** stimulants can worsen psychosis and trigger mania; new psychotic or manic symptoms occurred in about 0.1% versus 0% on placebo [FDA-Methylphenidate-Label].
- **Growth:** children's height and weight are monitored [FDA-Lisdexamfetamine-Label; FDA-Atomoxetine-Label].
- **Rare urgent effects:** prolonged painful erections on methylphenidate or atomoxetine, and liver injury on atomoxetine [FDA-Methylphenidate-Label; FDA-Atomoxetine-Label].
- **Scope:** controlled-substance prescribing rules and preferred first choices vary by country; the prescriber decides.

## In the session

- If the client's attention varies across the day → schedule sessions and homework for times when medication is active, and note it in the plan. Because short- and long-acting forms work within the day (expert consensus on scheduling).
- If the client reports poor sleep or low appetite after starting a stimulant → add it to monitoring and tell the prescriber before working on sleep habits alone. Because insomnia and decreased appetite are common stimulant effects [FDA-Methylphenidate-Label].
- If new anxiety, irritability or panic appears after a dose increase → ask the prescriber to review before treating it as an anxiety disorder. Because anxiety is a listed stimulant adverse reaction [FDA-Methylphenidate-Label].
- If the client gives away, sells or takes extra doses, or has a substance use history → raise it with the prescriber. Because the boxed warning requires ongoing reassessment of misuse risk [FDA-Lisdexamfetamine-Label].
- If a child on atomoxetine shows new suicidal thoughts or behavior → assess risk now, follow `02-risk.crisis-response` if it is imminent, and tell the prescriber. Because this is the boxed warning, concentrated in the first month [FDA-Atomoxetine-Label].
- If a family plans to stop guanfacine suddenly, for example over the holidays → advise talking to the prescriber first. Because a taper of no more than 1 mg every 3 to 7 days is recommended [FDA-Guanfacine-Label].
- If the client credits the medicine for all progress → also review the skills they used. Because CBT adds gains that persist [Safren-etal-2010].

Example phrasing: "When do you usually take your medicine, and when does it wear off? Let's book our sessions when your focus is at its best."
