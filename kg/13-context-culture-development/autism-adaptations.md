**Bottom line:** Adapted cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) helps autistic people with anxiety, most clearly on clinician and parent ratings. Keep the CBT model but change the delivery: predictable structure, concrete language, visual material, emotion-recognition work, behavior first, supporter involvement and sensory adjustments. Screen for suicide risk, which is markedly raised.

## The principle

Treat the co-occurring problem, not autism. The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) adult guideline asks for psychosocial treatments from the relevant condition guideline, delivered by staff who understand autism, with adapted delivery: concrete structure, more written and visual information, behavior as the starting point, explicit rules with their context, plain English without excess metaphor or hypotheticals, a supporter for practice if the person agrees, and regular breaks with special interests built in [NICE-CG142]. For autistic young people with anxiety who can engage verbally and cognitively, NICE suggests autism-adjusted group CBT, or individual CBT if groups are hard, with emotion recognition training, simplified activities such as multiple-choice worksheets, and parent involvement [NICE-CG170].

In a survey of 50 therapists, the commonest adaptations were behavioral strategies (74%), plain English and a structured, concrete approach (70% each), emotion psychoeducation (68%) and more written and visual information (60%). The commonest challenge was rigid or black-and-white thinking (40%), followed by pacing the work (10 therapists) and communication such as literal use of language (9); sensory issues were named by only 2 [Cooper-etal-2018]. An expert Delphi panel favored adapting structure and process and choosing techniques from an individual formulation [Spain-Happe-2020].

Ask how the client wants autism described. Many autistic adults prefer "autistic" to "person with autism"; many professionals do the reverse [Kenny-etal-2016]. Avoid language that frames autism as a defect to fix [Bottema-Beutel-etal-2021].

## Guidance

- If you are preparing for the first session → ask about sensory needs (lighting, noise, personal space, seating, video versus in person) and send a written outline of what will happen. Because NICE asks services to adapt lighting, noise, space and visual cues for autistic adults, or to vary the length of sessions and add breaks where the room cannot be changed [NICE-CG142].
- If you set the agenda → write it down, keep the same order each session, and explain why each task is done. Because NICE recommends structure and explicit rules with their context [NICE-CG142].
- If the client takes words literally or misses metaphors → use plain, literal language, one question at a time, and check meaning by asking the client to say it back. Because literal interpretation is a common challenge [Cooper-etal-2018].
- If the client struggles to name feelings → teach emotion words with lists, body maps and 0 to 10 scales before thought work. Because NICE recommends emotion recognition training for autistic young people [NICE-CG170].
- If verbal disputation stalls on rigid thinking → switch to behavioral experiments and graded exposure. Because NICE advises using behavior as the starting point [NICE-CG142].
- If a routine or repetitive behavior appears in the formulation → ask what it does for the client before targeting it. Comforting routines that cause no impairment are not safety behaviors to remove. Because an individual formulation should guide which techniques are used [Spain-Happe-2020].
- If engagement or practice is low → use special interests for examples and rewards, offer breaks, and, if the client agrees, ask a partner, parent or support worker to prompt practice. Because NICE lists all three among the delivery adaptations [NICE-CG142].
- If sessions feel rushed or the client loses track → slow the pace, cover fewer tasks per session and allow more sessions if your service can. Because pacing was the second most common challenge therapists reported [Cooper-etal-2018].
- If you are assessing risk → ask directly and concretely about suicidal thoughts, plans and past attempts, and write the safety plan in a clear visual format. Because in a United Kingdom clinic cohort of adults newly diagnosed with Asperger syndrome, 66% reported lifetime suicidal ideation and 35% plans or attempts [Cassidy-etal-2014], and premature mortality is raised (odds ratio 2.56) [Hirvikoski-etal-2016]. If there is a plan, intent or recent preparation, arrange same-day emergency assessment (see `02-risk.crisis-response`). Crisis contacts: 988 (call or text) or 911 in the United States; 999, NHS 111 (the National Health Service helpline) or Samaritans on 116 123 in the United Kingdom.

**What to record:** preferred identity language, communication and sensory needs, agreed adaptations, who supports practice, and ratings from more than one informant where possible.

## Example phrasings

- "Here's today's plan, written down. We'll do it in this order. Tell me if you'd like to change anything."
- "When I ask how something felt, I mean: what did you notice in your body, and which word on this list fits best?"
- "On a scale from 0 to 10, where 0 is completely calm and 10 is the most anxious you've ever been, what number was it?"
- Avoid: "Just put yourself in their shoes" (an unexplained metaphor).
- Avoid: "You don't seem autistic."

## Evidence

Across 19 randomized trials in autistic youth, CBT reduced anxiety on clinician ratings (g = 0.88), parent ratings (g = 0.40) and child ratings (g = 0.25), with larger effects for younger children and individual therapy, but few trials reported follow-up and benefits were not shown there [Sharma-etal-2021]. Across 48 studies including adults, CBT for affective disorders had a nonsignificant self-report effect (g = 0.24) but significant informant (g = 0.66) and clinician (g = 0.73) effects [Weston-etal-2016]. Evidence strength: randomized trials for youth anxiety; guideline-recommended for adults, with thinner trials; specific adaptations rest on guidelines and consensus.
