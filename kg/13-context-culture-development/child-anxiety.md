**Bottom line:** About 6.5% of children and adolescents have an anxiety disorder [Polanczyk-etal-2015]. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) built around graded exposure is the guideline-recommended psychological treatment, supported by randomized trials [Walter-etal-2020; James-etal-2020], and can be delivered to the child, through parents as coaches, or by changing parents' accommodation alone. Use child and parent forms; scores are screens, not diagnoses.

## What it is

- **Separation anxiety disorder:** persistent, developmentally inappropriate fear of separation from attachment figures, often showing as school refusal, headaches or stomachaches, and refusing to sleep alone; in children it must last at least 4 weeks. Separation distress is expected between about 8 and 24 months [Merck-Separation-Anxiety].
- **Selective mutism:** a child who can speak, and does at home, consistently does not speak in settings such as school. It typically starts between 3 and 6 years and must last at least a month, not counting the first month of school. Prevalence was 0.71% in one school sample [Bergman-etal-2002].

## Why it matters in CBT

Parents are part of the maintenance cycle: among 75 parents of anxious children, 97.3% reported some family accommodation, reassurance was the most common daily form (45.3%), and accommodation correlated with anxiety severity (r = .45) [Lebowitz-etal-2013].

## Key details

**Treatment formats**

| Format | What it involves | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Child-focused CBT (Coping Cat style) | For ages 7 to 13, 16 weekly sessions: eight of psychoeducation and coping skills (the FEAR plan: body signals, anxious thoughts, coping actions, results and rewards), then eight of graded exposure; two parent sessions [NES-Coping-Cat] | First trial: 47 children aged 9 to 13 [Kendall-1994]. In ages 7 to 17, 14 Coping Cat sessions gave 59.7% much improved, versus 80.7% with sertraline added and 23.7% on placebo [Walkup-etal-2008] |
| Guided parent-led CBT | Parents learn to coach the child through CBT with brief therapist support | Eight sessions (four by phone): 50% recovered from the primary diagnosis versus 25% on waitlist [Thirlwall-etal-2013]. Ages 5 to 12: up to 8 sessions, about 5 hours, matched a comparison therapy at lower cost [Creswell-etal-2017] |
| SPACE (Supportive Parenting for Anxious Childhood Emotions) | Parent-only, 12 weekly sessions: supportive statements (acknowledge the fear, express confidence the child can cope) and planned reduction of one accommodation at a time | In 124 children aged 7 to 14, noninferior to child CBT; responders 87.5% versus 75.5% [Lebowitz-etal-2020] |
| Selective mutism programs | Graded speaking steps at home and school | Pilot trial of Integrated Behavior Therapy (21 children aged 4 to 8, 24 weeks): 75% responders [Bergman-etal-2013]. A 3-month home and school program (24 children aged 3 to 9) increased school speech versus waitlist; younger children improved more [Oerbeck-etal-2014] |

Across 87 trials, remission of the primary anxiety disorder was 49.4% after CBT versus 17.8% on waitlist [James-etal-2020]. The American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry (AACAP) states that CBT and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) have considerable support [Walter-etal-2020].

**Measures**

| Measure | Format | Interpreting | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spence Children's Anxiety Scale (SCAS) | 44 items (38 scored, 6 filler), 0 to 3, total 0 to 114, six subscales; parent and preschool versions | T-score 60 or more is elevated but not necessarily clinical | Free for researchers and individual practitioners; services and agencies need a license or permission [SCAS-Site] |
| Revised Child Anxiety and Depression Scale (RCADS) | 47-item and 25-item youth and caregiver versions, anxiety and depression | T-scores by grade and gender; ages 8 to 18 | Free under a limited license [RCADS-Site] |
| Screen for Child Anxiety Related Emotional Disorders (SCARED) | 41 items, five factors including separation anxiety and school avoidance [Birmaher-etal-1999] | Total 25 or more may indicate an anxiety disorder; above 30 is more specific [SCARED-Pitt-Form] | Form and scoring key posted by the University of Pittsburgh [SCARED-Pitt-Form] |

Collect both reports: in the original SCARED study, parent and child scores correlated only 0.20 to 0.47 [Birmaher-etal-1997].

## In the session

- If the child is young, will not attend, or the parent accommodates heavily → start with guided parent-led CBT or SPACE. Because both did as well as their comparison treatments in trials [Creswell-etal-2017; Lebowitz-etal-2020].
- If the parent reassures, answers for the child or changes routines → map the accommodations, pick one target, plan a gradual reduction, and announce it to the child with a supportive statement. Because that is the SPACE method, and accommodation fell more after SPACE than after child CBT [Lebowitz-etal-2020].
- If the child does not speak to you → do not press for speech; accept pointing, writing or whispering to the parent, and build graded speaking steps with the school. Because treatment should involve family and school [MedlinePlus-Selective-Mutism], and younger children improve more [Oerbeck-etal-2014].
- If the child is new to the school language or has a trauma history → assess these before diagnosing selective mutism. Because language uncertainty is not selective mutism and trauma can produce similar symptoms [MedlinePlus-Selective-Mutism].
- If anxiety is severe or not improving → refer for a medication review with a child psychiatrist or pediatrician. Because adding sertraline improved response over CBT alone [Walkup-etal-2008]. In the UK, the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advises against routinely offering medication for social anxiety disorder in under-18s [NICE-CG159].
- If the child reports self-harm, suicidal thoughts or abuse → assess the same day, follow `02-risk.safeguarding`, and share crisis contacts: 988 or 911 in the United States; 999, NHS 111 or Samaritans on 116 123 in the United Kingdom. Because safety duties do not wait [Lifeline-988; NHS-Urgent-Help; Samaritans-Site].

To a parent: "Try saying: 'This is hard, and I know you can handle it.'" Avoid: "Don't worry, I'll do it for you."
