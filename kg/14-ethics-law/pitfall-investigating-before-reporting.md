**Bottom line:** Mandated reporters report reasonable suspicion; they do not need proof, and the investigation belongs to the agency [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023]. Questioning a child in detail, confronting a parent, or waiting a week can contaminate evidence and leave a child at risk. In many states, telling your manager does not discharge your own duty [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].

## What it looks like

- "I'll see what she says next week before I call."
- Asking a child leading questions to "be sure".
- Phoning the parent to hear their side first.
- Emailing a supervisor and assuming they made the report.
- Recording a concern in the notes without any report.

## Why it happens

- Fear of being wrong, of damaging the alliance, or of the family's reaction.
- Believing that proof is required.
- Unclear organizational procedures.

## The fix

- If you suspect abuse but are unsure → consult the hotline or safeguarding lead the same day and report if the threshold is met. Because the standard is suspicion or reason to believe, without a burden of proof [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
- If a child begins to disclose → listen, ask only open questions needed for immediate safety, and avoid leading questions. Because reporters must report the facts behind the suspicion and do not have to prove abuse [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
- If you told a supervisor → make or confirm the report yourself. Because in many states institutional policy does not relieve the individual reporter [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
- If you worry about confidentiality → share what the report needs. Because the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) permits disclosure to the authority that receives child abuse reports [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-512].
- If you are a regulated professional in England or Wales and a girl under 18 discloses female genital mutilation (FGM) → notify police promptly rather than treating the deadline as a waiting period. Because the law sets one month as the outer limit [FGM-Act-2003-s5B], and the 2026 child sexual abuse duty, once in force, requires notification as soon as practicable [UK-Crime-Policing-Act-2026].
- If a child is in immediate danger → call 911 (US) or 999 (UK) before anything else, then make the formal report. Because immediate safety comes first, and proving the abuse is not the reporter's job [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].

## Sources note

The reporting standard and individual duty come from the Child Welfare Information Gateway summary of state statutes, current through May 2023. Check your own state statute and agency guidance for the exact standard and timing.
