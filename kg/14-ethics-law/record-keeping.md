**Bottom line:** Keep one clinical record that a client, a covering clinician or a court could read: dates, diagnosis, plan, progress, measures, risk decisions and disclosures. Psychotherapy notes, if you keep them at all, are a narrow, separately stored category under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and get extra protection [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501]. Where no law or contract sets a period, the American Psychological Association (APA) 2007 guidelines suggest keeping full records for 7 years after the last service for adults, or 3 years after a minor reaches majority, whichever is later [APA-RecordKeeping-2007]. State law, employer policy and payer contracts override this, and the guidelines may have been superseded.

## When to use

- Setting up a practice or a new record system.
- Deciding whether content belongs in the clinical record or in psychotherapy notes.
- Receiving a request for records from a client, parent, lawyer, insurer or court.
- Correcting an entry, or deciding when records can be destroyed.

## Steps

1. **Build the clinical record.** Include intake, consent, assessment, diagnosis, formulation summary, treatment plan, session dates and modality, measure scores, risk assessments, disclosures made, correspondence and discharge. United Kingdom (UK) professional bodies expect records to include all communications and other information held about the client [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026] and to be completed promptly [BABCP-Standards-2022]; regulated practitioner psychologists must keep records of their work [HCPC-SCPE-2024].
2. **Decide on psychotherapy notes.** They are a mental health professional's notes analyzing session conversation, kept separate from the record. They cannot contain medication, session times, modality, test results, or summaries of diagnosis, functional status, plan, symptoms, prognosis or progress [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501]. Anything clinically necessary belongs in the main record.
3. **Protect psychotherapy notes.** Most uses or disclosures need the client's specific authorization [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-508]. They are excluded from the client's HIPAA right of access [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-524] and from electronic health information under the information blocking rules [CFR-45-171-102].
4. **Store securely.** Use access controls, encryption and audited systems. In the UK, records must meet data protection law where they are stored [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].
5. **Correct without erasing.** Add a dated amendment; keep the original entry readable [BABCP-Standards-2022].
6. **Answer access requests on time.** Under HIPAA, act within 30 days, with one 30-day extension if you give written reasons. You may deny access only on narrow grounds; a denial because access is reasonably likely to endanger life or physical safety is reviewable by another licensed professional [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-524]. Providers who unreasonably interfere with access to electronic health information may also be engaging in information blocking [ONC-Information-Blocking].
7. **Set a retention period.** Follow the longest of state law, employer policy, payer contract and the ethical default above [APA-RecordKeeping-2007]. Note that HIPAA's 6-year rule covers privacy policies and compliance documentation, not the clinical record itself [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-530]. In the UK, follow your employer's retention schedule (for National Health Service (NHS) services, the NHS Records Management Code of Practice); this page does not yet give UK periods.
8. **Plan for your absence.** Name a records custodian in a professional (clinical) will so clients can still obtain records and be contacted. Members of the British Association for Counselling and Psychotherapy (BACP) must have a clinical will and an executor bound by confidentiality [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].
9. **Destroy securely** at the end of retention and keep a log of what was destroyed and when.

## What to record

- Every disclosure: to whom, what, when, and the legal basis or authorization.
- Each records request, the date received, the response date, and any denial with its reason.
- Amendments with date, author and reason.

## Guidance

- If you are tempted to put sensitive detail only in psychotherapy notes → put the clinically necessary summary in the main record and keep process notes minimal. Because summaries of symptoms, plan and progress cannot be psychotherapy notes [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501].
- If a client requests their full file → release the clinical record promptly and explain that psychotherapy notes are handled separately. Because psychotherapy notes are excluded from the right of access [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-524].
- If you fear reading the record will harm the client → offer to go through it together and document your reasoning; deny only if the endangerment test is met. Because that ground is narrow and reviewable [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-524].
- If an artificial intelligence (AI) tool drafts your notes → read and correct every note before signing. Because you remain responsible for accuracy, and Nevada, for example, requires independent review of AI-generated session notes [NV-AB406-2025].
- If you write about a third party (for example a partner) → record only what treatment needs. Because the client has a right of access to the record [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-524].

## Common problems

- Treating psychotherapy notes as a private diary that can hold the real clinical reasoning.
- Destroying records early, or keeping them forever with no policy.
- Relying on the 2007 guidance without checking for an update. It was scheduled to expire 10 years after adoption, so confirm the current American Psychological Association position [APA-RecordKeeping-2007].
