**Bottom line:** In the United States (US), every state requires therapists to report suspected child abuse or neglect, either by naming mental health professionals (38 states and the District of Columbia) or by requiring everyone to report; the usual standard is suspicion or reason to believe, and you do not need proof [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023]. Report yourself, promptly, to the agency your state names, even if you also tell your employer. Adult abuse duties vary by state [NAPSA-Site]. In England and Wales, regulated health professionals and teachers must report female genital mutilation (FGM) in a girl under 18 to police [FGM-Act-2003-s5B]; a 2026 child sexual abuse reporting duty for England is not yet in force [UK-Crime-Policing-Act-2026].

## When to use

- A child or teen discloses abuse, or you observe signs of it.
- An adult client discloses harming or neglecting a child.
- An older or dependent adult client discloses abuse, neglect or exploitation.
- In England or Wales, a girl under 18 tells you she has had FGM, or you see signs of it.
- A child is in immediate danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK) first, then make the report.

## Steps

1. **Stay calm and listen.** Ask open questions only to understand immediate safety. Do not interview for detail; investigation belongs to the agency.
2. **Apply the reporting standard.** Mandated reporters typically must report when they suspect or have reason to believe a child has been abused or neglected, and they do not carry the burden of proof [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
3. **Check who must report in your state.** About 46 states name required professions; about 17 states and Puerto Rico require any person to report [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
4. **Report personally.** In many states, telling a supervisor or institution does not relieve you of the duty; 17 states, the District of Columbia and the Virgin Islands say so explicitly [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
5. **Do not rely on privilege.** Most states restrict privilege as grounds for not reporting; physician-patient and spousal privileges are the ones most often denied, so check how your statute treats the psychotherapist privilege [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
6. **Report to the right agency.** Child protective services or police, as your statute names. For adults, contact Adult Protective Services in the state where the adult lives [NAPSA-Site].
7. **Share what the report needs.** Ethics codes allow disclosure without consent when law mandates it [APA-Ethics-2017]. The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) permits disclosure to the authority authorized to receive child abuse reports [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-512]. For adult victims, disclosure is permitted when law requires it, the person agrees, or a statute authorizes it and you judge it necessary to prevent serious harm; you must usually tell the adult unless that would put them at risk [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-512].
8. **Tell the client** what you are doing, unless that would endanger someone.
9. **Keep supporting the client** and consider immediate safety needs (see `02-risk.safeguarding`).

**England, Wales and the wider United Kingdom (UK):**
- FGM: in England and Wales, statutorily regulated healthcare professionals (including Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) registered psychologists), teachers, and in Wales social care workers must notify police when a girl under 18 discloses FGM or they observe physical signs, within one month of discovery; the notification does not breach confidentiality [FGM-Act-2003-s5B]. Counselors who are not statutorily regulated fall outside it but still follow local safeguarding procedures.
- Child sexual abuse: section 85 of the Crime and Policing Act 2026 (Royal Assent 29 April 2026) requires adults in regulated activity with children in England who are given reason to suspect a child sex offense to notify police or the local authority as soon as practicable; it was not in force as of November 2026 [UK-Crime-Policing-Act-2026]. Failing to report can lead to referral to the Disclosure and Barring Service [NSPCC-Mandatory-Reporting-2026]; preventing or deterring a report is an offense punishable by up to 7 years [UK-Crime-Policing-Act-2026]. Check commencement and guidance before relying on it.
- Otherwise, follow local safeguarding procedures under the current statutory guidance, Working Together to Safeguard Children 2026 [DfE-Working-Together-2026], and know your duties in both your country and the client's country of residence [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].

## What to record

Date and time of the disclosure, the client's words, observations, who you consulted, the agency, the time of the report, the reference number, the name of the person who took it, what you told the client, and the safety plan.

## Guidance

- If a teen says "promise you won't tell" before disclosing → say you cannot promise that, explain who you would tell and why, and let them decide what to share. Because the duty overrides confidentiality [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
- If you are unsure whether the threshold is met → call the hotline to consult, and report if they advise. Because the standard is suspicion, not proof [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
- If an adult client discloses abuse of themselves → check your state's adult protective law and the client's wishes before reporting, unless law mandates it. Because adult disclosures without consent are limited to defined situations [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-512].
- If your organization wants to "look into it first" → make the report yourself anyway. Because an internal policy does not relieve the reporter in many states [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].

Example phrasing: "Thank you for telling me. What you've said means I need to involve people whose job is to keep children safe. I'll tell you what I'm going to do, and I'll keep supporting you."
