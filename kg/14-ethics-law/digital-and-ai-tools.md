**Bottom line:** Guided internet-delivered cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) works about as well as face-to-face CBT across the trials that compared them directly [Carlbring-etal-2018]. Artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots show early, short-term benefits but no proof of safety, and the American Psychological Association (APA) advises against using them in place of a qualified clinician [APA-AI-Advisory-2025]. Since 2025, several United States (US) states have banned or restricted AI that delivers therapy, so check the client's state before recommending or building such a tool.

## What it is

- **Digital CBT:** structured CBT programs delivered online or by app, with or without guidance from a person.
- **Rule-based chatbots:** scripted conversational programs.
- **Generative AI chatbots:** large language model systems that produce open-ended replies, including general assistants that people use for emotional support.

## Why it matters in CBT

Many clients already talk to chatbots between sessions. These tools can extend practice of skills, but they can also give harmful advice, miss risk, foster dependence and expose sensitive data. Clinicians need to ask about use, advise on safety, and know what the law allows.

## Key details

**Evidence**

| Tool | Finding | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Guided internet CBT vs face-to-face | 20 trials, 1,418 participants, pooled difference Hedges g = 0.05, equivalent overall [Carlbring-etal-2018] | Meta-analysis of randomized trials |
| AI conversational agents | 15 randomized trials: depression g = 0.64, distress g = 0.70, no significant effect on well-being [Li-etal-2023] | Meta-analysis, short follow-up, mixed quality |
| Generative AI chatbot (Therabot) | 210 adults vs waitlist; average symptom reductions of about 51% depression, 31% anxiety, 19% eating concerns; staff monitored conversations and authors say no generative AI is ready to work autonomously [Heinz-etal-2025] | One randomized trial, inactive control |

The APA's November 2025 advisory states that even well-designed generative AI tools lack enough evidence of effectiveness or safety, that their crisis handling is limited and unpredictable, and that clinicians should ask clients about chatbot and app use [APA-AI-Advisory-2025]. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) opened an inquiry in September 2025 into companion chatbots' effects on children and teens [FTC-AI-Companions-2025].

**State law (check current text; this field changes monthly)**

| State | Law | Core rule |
|---|---|---|
| Illinois | Wellness and Oversight for Psychological Resources Act (2025) | Only licensed professionals may provide therapy; AI may not make therapeutic decisions, communicate therapeutically with clients, or detect emotions; fines up to $10,000 per violation [BakerDonelson-2025] |
| Nevada | AB 406, effective July 1, 2025 | AI may not be offered or represented as professional mental health care (civil penalty up to $15,000 per violation); providers may not use AI to deliver care directly but may use it for administrative tasks [NV-AB406-2025] |
| Utah | HB 452 (2025) | Mental health chatbots must disclose they are AI before access, after 7 days without use, and when asked; limits on ads and data sales; fines up to $2,500 per violation; the chapter is scheduled for repeal on July 1, 2027 unless extended [UT-HB452-2025] |
| California | SB 243, from January 1, 2026 | Companion chatbots must disclose they are not human, keep and publish suicide and self-harm protocols with crisis referral, and remind known minors at least every 3 hours [Skadden-2025] |
| Colorado | HB26-1195, effective August 12, 2026 | AI may take part in therapeutic communication only in real time with the professional present; AI treatment plans need professional review and approval; clients are told of these limits at first contact; licensing boards discipline violations [CO-HB26-1195] |

A September 2026 tracker also lists 2026 laws in Maine, Rhode Island, Vermont, Tennessee, Hawaii and others, and New York safeguards in force since November 2025; it warns it is not exhaustive [PsychologyCom-AI-Laws-2026]. In the United Kingdom (UK), members of the British Association for Counselling and Psychotherapy (BACP) must assess an AI tool's risks, get informed consent before entering client data, and not defer decisions to AI [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].

## In the session

- If you start therapy with any client → ask whether they use apps or AI chatbots for emotional support, and what for. Because the APA advises clinicians to ask proactively [APA-AI-Advisory-2025].
- If a client relies on a chatbot between sessions → explore what helps, check advice against the formulation, and agree that crisis needs go to people (988, 911, or UK 999 and Samaritans 116 123), not the bot. Because chatbot crisis responses are unpredictable [APA-AI-Advisory-2025].
- If a chatbot gave unsafe advice (for example, encouraging restriction or reassurance seeking) → review it openly, without shaming, and reconnect to the treatment plan. Because chatbots behave unpredictably and clinicians are advised to discuss their use [APA-AI-Advisory-2025].
- If you want to recommend a digital program → prefer guided, evidence-based CBT programs with clear privacy terms. Because guided internet CBT has trial support [Carlbring-etal-2018].
- If you are designing a client-facing AI tool → check every target state's law, disclose that it is AI, build human escalation for risk, and never market it as a therapist. Because several states restrict AI therapy and require disclosure and crisis protocols [NV-AB406-2025] [UT-HB452-2025] [Skadden-2025].
