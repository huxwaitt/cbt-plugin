**Bottom line:** Recording or transcribing a session needs the client's permission before you start [APA-Ethics-2017], and in several states an artificial intelligence (AI) recording or transcription tool needs advance, written, informed consent that names the tool and its purpose [BakerDonelson-2025] [CO-HB26-1195]. Use only vendors that sign a business associate agreement, read and correct every AI-drafted note, and let clients decline without losing care. You, not the tool, remain responsible for the record.

## When to use

- Before buying or switching on an AI scribe, ambient listening tool or note generator.
- Before recording sessions for supervision, training or your own review.
- When a client asks whether sessions are recorded or how notes are written.

## Steps

1. **Check the law where the client is.** Illinois requires written informed consent describing the AI tool and its purpose when a session is recorded or transcribed [BakerDonelson-2025]. Colorado (from August 12, 2026) requires professionals to explain its AI limits at initial contact, and to disclose an AI recording or transcription tool and its purpose in advance and obtain written, informed consent [CO-HB26-1195]. Nevada allows AI for administrative tasks, including organizing notes, but requires the provider to independently review the accuracy of AI-generated billing information and session notes and to comply with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) [NV-AB406-2025]. A 2026 tracker reports that Maine requires written disclosures on purpose, storage, retention, training use and deletion, and bars denying therapy to clients who decline, and that Texas requires disclosure of AI use in care [PsychologyCom-AI-Laws-2026].
2. **Vet the vendor.** Get a signed business associate agreement before any protected health information reaches the tool [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-502]. Ask where audio and text are stored, how long they are kept, whether they train the vendor's models, and how deletion is confirmed.
3. **Write a separate consent.** State the tool's name, what it records, what it produces, who can access it, retention and deletion, model training, the right to say no or to stop at any time, and that care does not depend on agreeing. In the United Kingdom (UK), members of the British Association for Counselling and Psychotherapy (BACP) must be transparent about AI use and have informed consent before entering a client's data [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].
4. **In session,** say when recording starts and stops, and switch it off on request (for example during a disclosure the client wants unrecorded).
5. **Review every note.** Correct errors, remove invented content, and make sure the clinical judgment in the note is yours. UK guidance says not to defer decisions to AI and to evaluate its output critically [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].
6. **Delete source audio** as soon as the note is final unless your policy says otherwise, and confirm the vendor deleted it too.
7. **Keep psychotherapy notes out of shared tools** if you keep them; they are a separate, specially protected category [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501].

## What to record

Signed AI or recording consent (or refusal), the tool and version, any session where recording was paused, your review of AI drafts, and deletion dates.

## Guidance

- If a client hesitates when you mention the scribe → say it is optional, turn it off, and write notes yourself. Because consent must be free, and some states bar denying care for declining [PsychologyCom-AI-Laws-2026].
- If a client asks "Does the AI listen and decide anything?" → explain it only drafts notes, that you check and own every note, and that it does not make treatment decisions. Because clinicians remain responsible for AI output [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].
- If you see an AI note that states something the client never said → correct it and report the error pattern to the vendor. Because accuracy review is your duty [NV-AB406-2025].
- If you treat minors → get the parent's written consent and the young person's agreement before recording. Because permission must come from the person or their legal representative [APA-Ethics-2017].
- If you want to record for supervision → get permission from everyone recorded, before recording. Because the code requires prior permission [APA-Ethics-2017].

Example phrasing: "I'd like to use a note-taking tool that records our session and drafts my notes. You can say no, and it won't change your care. If you agree, I'll check every note myself, and the audio is deleted after that. Would you like to see the details first?"

## Common problems

- Treating clicked terms of use as consent; Illinois law treats them as insufficient, per the same tracker [PsychologyCom-AI-Laws-2026].
- Using a consumer AI assistant with no business associate agreement (see `14-ethics-law.pitfall-unvetted-ai-and-apps`).
- Signing AI drafts unread.
