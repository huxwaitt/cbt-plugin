**Bottom line:** A boundary crossing (accepting a small gift, attending a graduation) is not automatically wrong; it is wrong when it risks impairing your objectivity or exploiting or harming the client [APA-Ethics-2017]. Sexual or romantic contact with a current client is always prohibited, and with former clients psychologists must wait at least 2 years, counselors 5 years, and social workers should not at all [APA-Ethics-2017] [ACA-Ethics-2014] [NASW-Code-2021]. When you extend a boundary, consult and document beforehand.

## The principle

A multiple relationship exists when you hold a professional role with someone and also another role with them or someone close to them, or promise one in future [APA-Ethics-2017]. It is unethical when it could reasonably be expected to impair your objectivity, competence or effectiveness, or risks exploitation or harm; relationships that carry no such risk are not unethical [APA-Ethics-2017]. Boundary issues span role, time, place, money, gifts, clothing, language, self-disclosure and physical contact, and the effect of any crossing depends on context [Gutheil-Gabbard-1993].

Fixed rules:
- No sexual intimacy with current clients [APA-Ethics-2017], and for counselors no sexual or romantic relationship with clients, their partners or their family members, in person or electronically [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- Former clients: psychologists at least 2 years after therapy ends, and even then only in the most unusual circumstances [APA-Ethics-2017]; counselors 5 years, with documented reasoning that no exploitation is possible [ACA-Ethics-2014]; social workers should not, and bear the full burden of justifying any exception [NASW-Code-2021].
- No personal virtual relationships, such as social media friendships, with current clients [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- In the United Kingdom (UK), the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) prohibits registrants from abusing their position to pursue personal, sexual, emotional or financial relationships [HCPC-SCPE-2024], and the British Association for Counselling and Psychotherapy (BACP) bars sexual relationships with clients and requires a clear boundary between personal and professional social media [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].

## Guidance

- If a client offers a gift → consider the therapeutic meaning, its value, the client's motive and your own, and cultural norms. Because some cultures use small gifts to express respect [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- If a client invites you to a wedding or graduation → weigh risks and benefits, consult, and document the rationale before attending. Because extending boundaries calls for consultation and prior documentation [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- If a client sends a friend or follow request → decline without shaming and explain your policy, ideally already stated at intake. Because personal online relationships with clients are prohibited [ACA-Ethics-2014] and personal technology contact should be avoided [NASW-Code-2021].
- If a client offers a service or barter in place of fees → agree only if it is not clinically contraindicated and not exploitative. Because barter is limited to those conditions [APA-Ethics-2017].
- If you meet a client by chance in a small community → let the client lead on acknowledging you, keep it brief, and discuss it next session. Because unavoidable overlaps need clear boundaries set by the clinician [NASW-Code-2021], and UK cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) standards call for a clear line between personal and professional relationships even in social settings [BABCP-Standards-2022].
- If you notice attraction, secrecy, extended sessions, or special treatment of one client → take it to supervision now. Because the clinician is responsible for preventing exploitation, and a harmful relationship must be resolved with the client's interests first [APA-Ethics-2017].
- If an unforeseen dual role arises (a new client turns out to be your neighbor's partner) → discuss options openly and refer if objectivity is at risk. Because reasonable steps to resolve the conflict are required [APA-Ethics-2017].
- If physical comfort seems called for → avoid touch that could cause psychological harm and keep any contact culturally sensitive and clearly bounded. Because social work standards limit physical contact on that basis [NASW-Code-2021].

## Example phrasings

- Friend request: "I saw your request, and I'm glad you feel connected. I don't connect with clients on social media, to protect your privacy and our work. That isn't a rejection of you."
- Gift: "Thank you, this is thoughtful. Can we talk for a moment about what it means to you?"
- Chance meeting, next session: "We saw each other at the store. How was that for you? My practice is to let you decide whether to say hello."
- Avoid: "I can't accept that, it's against the rules." (It shames the client and skips the clinical meaning.)

## Evidence

Rules here are ethics code requirements and expert consensus [Gutheil-Gabbard-1993], not trial findings. Sexual boundary rules are absolute; other crossings are judgment calls that should be consulted and documented.
