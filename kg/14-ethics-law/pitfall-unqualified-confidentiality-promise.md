**Bottom line:** "Everything you say here is confidential" is untrue, because law and ethics codes require or permit disclosure for serious harm, abuse and legal process [APA-Ethics-2017]. Clients who disclose on that promise and then learn you must report feel betrayed, and the alliance can break. Explain the limits at the outset, in plain words, before the client tells their story.

## What it looks like

- Intake paperwork or a welcome script that says sessions are "completely confidential".
- The clinician skips limits because the client seems low risk or the session is short.
- A teen asks "you won't tell my parents, right?" and hears "of course not".
- A digital intake flow or chatbot says conversations are private without stating exceptions.
- A disclosure of child abuse arrives in the first 10 minutes, before limits were covered.

## Why it happens

- Wanting to build trust quickly and fearing that listing exceptions will frighten the client.
- Rushing the first session or treating the consent form as enough.
- Not knowing local law well enough to describe the limits confidently.

## The fix

- If this is a first contact → explain confidentiality and its limits before history-taking, and repeat when circumstances change. Because the discussion belongs at the outset and thereafter as needed [APA-Ethics-2017].
- If a client asks for a promise of secrecy → decline kindly and name the specific limits that could apply. Because clients have the right to an explanation of the limits [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- If a disclosure arrives before limits were explained → pause, explain the limits now, and still meet any reporting duty. Because most states restrict privilege as grounds for not reporting child abuse, and an earlier promise does not cancel the duty [CWIG-Mandatory-Reporting-2023].
- If you are writing scripts for an intake tool → include the harm, abuse, legal and supervision limits, and the jurisdiction's duty-to-warn rule. Because duties differ by state [NCSL-Duty-to-Warn-2022], and in the United Kingdom (UK) members of the British Association for Counselling and Psychotherapy (BACP) must make foreseeable limits clear in their privacy notice [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].

Replacement phrasing: "What you say here is private, with a few exceptions I want you to know before we start."

Avoid: "Nothing you say leaves this room."

## Sources note

The duty to explain limits is explicit in the American Psychological Association (APA) and American Counseling Association (ACA) codes and in UK professional frameworks. The harm to the alliance is clinical consensus rather than trial evidence.
