**Bottom line:** Telehealth is treated as happening where the client is, so you typically need a license, or a compact privilege, in the client's state at the time of each session [CCHP-Cross-State-2026]. Use a platform whose vendor signs a business associate agreement, add telehealth-specific items to consent, and set an emergency plan for the client's location before the first session. Federal flexibilities change often: as of November 2026, Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) telemedicine prescribing flexibilities run through December 31, 2026 [DEA-Telemedicine-Extension-2025], and Medicare telehealth flexibilities through December 31, 2027 [CMS-Telehealth-FAQ-2026].

## When to use

- Before offering any video or phone sessions.
- When a client moves, travels, attends college or works from another state or country.
- When choosing or changing a telehealth platform.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the client's location** at every session and record it. Some states allow temporary practice, exemptions or contiguous-state allowances, so check the client's state rule [CCHP-Cross-State-2026].
2. **Check licensure or a compact.** The Center for Connected Health Policy (CCHP) lists more than 40 member jurisdictions for the Psychology Interjurisdictional Compact (PSYPACT) and more than 30 for the Social Work Licensure Compact [CCHP-Compacts-2026]; the Counseling Compact lists 39 states plus the District of Columbia [Counseling-Compact-Site]. Membership is not the same as operation: in November 2026, Counseling Compact privileges were live only for licensees in Arizona, Minnesota and Ohio [Counseling-Compact-Site], and the social work compact was not yet issuing multistate licenses [SW-Compact-Site]. Check each compact's own map before relying on a count.
3. **Choose a compliant platform.** If a vendor handles protected health information (PHI), you need a written business associate agreement under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-502].
4. **Add telehealth items to consent.** Cover your location and contact details, how technology is used, what happens if the connection fails, when you are unavailable, alternative service options, and privacy risks of electronic communication [ACA-Ethics-2014] [APA-Ethics-2017]. Tell clients which laws apply across state lines or borders [ACA-Ethics-2014].
5. **Build the emergency plan.** Record the client's address for the session, a local emergency contact, the nearest emergency department, and local crisis numbers (United States (US) 988 or 911; United Kingdom (UK) 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans 116 123).
6. **Check privacy at the client's end.** Ask who else is in the room and agree a signal for interruptions.
7. **Prescribers:** check DEA status, state rules and DEA registration in the client's state. The fourth temporary extension of the pandemic-era flexibilities runs through December 31, 2026 [DEA-Telemedicine-Extension-2025]; separate January 2025 final rules cover buprenorphine by telemedicine and Veterans Affairs continuity of care [CCHP-DEA-2026]; the special registration framework had been published only as a proposed rule (January 2025) [DEA-Special-Registration-NPRM-2025].
8. **Medicare billing:** through December 31, 2027, beneficiaries can receive telehealth anywhere, including at home, and audio-only care at home is allowed. After that date, home mental health telehealth for a new patient needs an in-person visit within 6 months before the first telehealth service and then at least every 12 months, with limited exceptions [CMS-Telehealth-FAQ-2026].
9. **International clients:** confirm the law in both your country and the client's, and that insurance covers cross-border work [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].

## What to record

Client location and phone number each session, modality (video or audio-only), platform, any technical failure, the emergency plan, and the license or privilege that authorized the session.

## Guidance

- If a client says they are calling from a vacation in another state → confirm whether you are authorized there before continuing clinical work; if not, provide only what that state permits and plan next steps. Because licensure follows the client's location [CCHP-Cross-State-2026].
- If the video drops during a risk discussion → call the client's phone at once and follow the emergency plan if unreachable. Because consent should cover what happens when technology fails [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- If a client wants to use a consumer chat app for between-session messages → explain the privacy risks and offer the secure channel. Because clients must be told the risks to privacy of electronic services [APA-Ethics-2017].
- If you are a social worker practicing remotely → follow the laws where you are regulated and where the client is. Because the code requires both [NASW-Code-2021].
- If billing Medicare or prescribing controlled substances → recheck federal status before each policy year, and plan in-person visits for Medicare mental health clients before January 2028. Because flexibilities have been extended in short increments [CCHP-MedPAC-2026] [DEA-Telemedicine-Extension-2025], and the in-person requirement applies after December 31, 2027 [CMS-Telehealth-FAQ-2026].

Example phrasing: "Before we start, can you confirm where you are right now and the best number to reach you if we get cut off?"

## Common problems

- Treating licensure as tied to where the clinician sits. See `14-ethics-law.pitfall-practicing-where-not-licensed`.
- Using a platform without a business associate agreement.
- No emergency plan for the client's actual location.
