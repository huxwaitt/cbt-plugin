**Bottom line:** For telehealth, the practice is treated as happening where the client is, so a provider typically needs a license or privilege in the client's state [CCHP-Cross-State-2026]. Unlicensed sessions risk board discipline, uninsured practice and unclear duties if a crisis occurs. Check the client's location every session and plan for moves before they happen.

## What it looks like

- A college student starts sessions from a dorm in another state and nobody checks.
- A client "working remotely for a few months" abroad continues weekly therapy unchanged.
- The clinician assumes a license in their own state covers any client they see.
- Crisis planning uses the clinician's local emergency numbers, not the client's.

## Why it happens

- Licensure is mistakenly linked to the clinician's location.
- Compact membership is confused with compact implementation; a state may be a member before privileges are issued [CCHP-Compacts-2026].
- Fear of abandoning a client mid-treatment.

## The fix

- If a client says they will be away → check the destination's rules before the next session and agree a plan in advance. Because states vary, with some offering temporary practice or other exceptions [CCHP-Cross-State-2026].
- If you hold a compact privilege → confirm the destination state is an active member for your profession. Because membership and implementation differ [CCHP-Compacts-2026].
- If the client relocates where you cannot practice → arrange a warm handover to a local clinician and limit contact to what that state permits. Because counselors are subject to laws in both their location and the client's [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- If you are a social worker → apply the laws where you are regulated and where the client is located. Because the code requires both [NASW-Code-2021].
- If the client is in another country → check that country's law and your insurance cover. Because cross-border work must be insured and lawful in both places [BACP-Ethical-Framework-2026].

Replacement phrasing: "Before we start, where are you today? If you're traveling, let me check what I'm allowed to do there."

## Sources note

Rules here come from statutes, compacts and ethics codes. Exceptions and compact status change often, so check the client's state board and the compact's own site before relying on any list.
