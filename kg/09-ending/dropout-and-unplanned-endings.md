**Bottom line:** About one in five adults drops out of psychotherapy (19.7%), and in cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) about one in four starters (26.2%) stops during treatment [Swift-Greenberg-2012; Fernandez-etal-2015]. Lower it by agreeing expectations, accommodating preferences, watching the alliance and using progress feedback. When a client stops coming, reach out more than once, check risk first if risk is known, and close the episode in writing with a route back.

## When to use

- A client misses a session without notice, or two in a row with notice.
- The client signals stopping: "I'm not sure this is for me," vague rescheduling, homework stops, alliance ratings drop.
- The client announces they are ending today.
- You are setting a service policy for missed appointments.

## Steps

**Prevent**
1. **Agree expectations at intake:** what CBT involves, typical length, and that ups and downs are expected (see `01-intake.goals-and-expectations`).
2. **Accommodate preferences** for format and approach where you can: clients whose preferences were accommodated were less likely to drop out (odds ratio 1.79) [Swift-etal-2018].
3. **Monitor the alliance and outcome every session.** Weaker alliance is linked to dropout (d = .55) [Sharf-etal-2010]; progress feedback slightly reduces dropout (odds ratio 1.19) [DeJong-etal-2021].
4. **Name early warning signs out loud** and repair (see `alliance-and-stance.rupture-and-repair`).

**Respond to a missed session**
5. **Check the risk record first.** If the client has known suicide or safeguarding risk, act the same day.
6. **Contact the client,** with repeated attempts if necessary. The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) recommends this when people with less severe depression miss follow-up [NICE-NG222]; the same logic applies more widely. Use the agreed channel; keep messages brief and warm.
7. **For known risk with no response,** escalate: try other numbers and, where available, a mobile crisis team, then contacts named on the safety plan. Request an emergency welfare check when information points to acute, possibly imminent risk [ActionAlliance-2019]. Follow your service's written protocol; for example, the US Veterans Health Administration requires at least four separate contact attempts after a missed mental health appointment [ActionAlliance-2019].
8. **If you reach the client,** ask what got in the way, offer a choice (continue, pause, change format, end with a review session, or refer), and give crisis contacts.
9. **If there is no reply,** send a closing letter after your service's set period: you are sorry not to have heard, the episode will close on a date, how to return, and crisis numbers.
10. **Write the closing summary** (see `09-ending.discharge-summary`).

## What to record

- Each contact attempt: date, time, channel, outcome.
- Risk review and any escalation, with reasons.
- The client's stated reason for stopping, in their words.
- The closing letter sent and the date the episode closed.

## Guidance

- If the client has a history of suicidal behavior and misses a session → call that day and do not wait for the next appointment. Because immediate, repeated outreach after a missed appointment is best practice for people with suicide risk, written for the period after hospital discharge [ActionAlliance-2019].
- If you cannot reach a high-risk client → follow the escalation steps, document them, and put crisis contacts in every message. US: call or text 988, or call 911 [Lifeline-988]. UK: call 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 mental health option, or Samaritans on 116 123 [NHS-Urgent-Help; Samaritans-Site]. Because outreach should move from first-line to second-line resources and then to welfare checks as risk indicates [ActionAlliance-2019].
- If a client says they want to stop today → ask for one more session to review and plan. "Can we use one session to look at what helped, what didn't, and what you'd want if you come back?" Because pre-ending counseling and suggestions of other providers are expected unless the client prevents it [APA-Ethics-2017].
- If the client stopped because they feel better → treat it as a possible good outcome, check scores if you can, and send the relapse plan. Because routine-care data suggest many clients end once improvement is good enough [Barkham-etal-2006].
- If dropout clusters in a group (younger clients, particular diagnoses, trainee caseloads) → review the service pathway. Because dropout was higher in younger clients, personality and eating disorders and with trainee clinicians [Swift-Greenberg-2012].
- If treatment is online or for depression → monitor engagement closely. Because CBT dropout was highest for depression and e-therapy [Fernandez-etal-2015].
- If the client is angry → avoid blame. Avoid: "You didn't engage." Say: "It sounds like therapy wasn't giving you what you needed. That's important for me to hear." Because weaker alliances are linked to dropout, and repair keeps the door open [Sharf-etal-2010].

## Common problems

- **One call, then discharge.** See `09-ending.pitfall-silent-dropout`.
- **Dropout labeled as poor motivation.** Fix: ask, and review your own part (fit, pace, alliance).
- **Inconsistent definitions.** Rates vary widely depending on how dropout is defined [Swift-Greenberg-2012]; agree one local definition.
- **Therapist absence ends care.** When you leave or fall ill, plan for continuation of care [APA-Ethics-2017].
