**Bottom line:** Start ending when agreed goals are met and measures show reliable improvement to a non-clinical range that has held for a few weeks (expert consensus). Residual symptoms and other relapse risk factors are reasons to extend for relapse prevention, not to stop [Paykel-etal-1995; NICE-NG222]. If therapy is not helping after an adequate trial and a review, end and refer: ethics codes require it [APA-Ethics-2017].

## When to use

- At each planned review and when the planned number of sessions is close.
- When the client asks to stop, or a payer limit approaches.
- When progress has stalled despite a non-response review.

## Steps

1. **Check the data.** Look at the session-by-session scores, the goal ratings and the client's own view. Use reliable and clinically significant change, not impression (see `08-measurement.reliable-and-clinically-significant-change`).
2. **Check stability.** A useful research standard counts remission only after 3 consecutive weeks of minimal symptoms, and recovery only after at least 4 months of remission [Rush-etal-2006]. One good week is not remission.
3. **Check residual symptoms.** List what is left: sleep, low energy, avoidance, worry, rumination. In one cohort, 76% of people with residual depressive symptoms relapsed, against 25% without [Paykel-etal-1995].
4. **Check relapse risk factors.** For depression the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) lists recurrent episodes (especially frequent ones or any in the last 2 years), incomplete response or residual symptoms, avoidance or rumination, past severe depression, other chronic physical or mental health problems, and personal or social stressors that are still present [NICE-NG222].
5. **Apply the decision table below** and agree the decision with the client.
6. **Record the decision and the reasons**, including the client's view.

| Situation | Decision |
|---|---|
| Goals met, reliable improvement, stable, few residual symptoms, low risk | Begin the ending phase (see `09-ending.planning-the-ending`) |
| Improved but residual symptoms or higher relapse risk | Extend with a relapse prevention focus; for depression at least 4 more sessions [NICE-NG222] |
| Client wants to stop early but has improved | Respect it; agree a short ending phase and a route back [Barkham-etal-2006] |
| No benefit after an adequate trial and a non-response review | End and refer [APA-Ethics-2017] |
| Therapy is causing harm | End or change course; refer [APA-Ethics-2017] |
| Therapist threatened or endangered | May end; arrange alternative care [APA-Ethics-2017] |
| Payer or service limit reached | Pre-ending counseling and alternative providers unless prevented [APA-Ethics-2017] |

## What to record

- Scores at start and now, and whether change is reliable.
- Goals met, partly met and not met.
- Residual symptoms and named relapse risk factors.
- The decision, the reasons, the client's view and who else was consulted.
- For any ending without full benefit: the referral offered.

## Guidance

- If scores have only just dropped below the clinical range → keep going for a few more weeks before opening the ending phase. Because remission is defined by sustained, not single-week, improvement [Rush-etal-2006].
- If the client has responded but still has residual symptoms → name them and target them before ending. "Your mood is much better. Your sleep and the evening brooding are still there, and those are the things most linked to depression coming back." Because residual symptoms strongly predicted relapse [Paykel-etal-1995].
- If a client with depression is at higher relapse risk → discuss continuing therapy for relapse prevention and reassess risk when it ends. Because NICE recommends discussing this choice, at least 4 more sessions if therapy continues, and a reassessment at the end of relapse prevention therapy [NICE-NG222].
- If the client has improved quickly → do not extend to fill the planned number. Because in data from over 100,000 NHS Talking Therapies patients, 95% of those who reached reliable and clinically significant improvement did so within 7 low-intensity or 14 high-intensity sessions [Robinson-etal-2020b].
- If the client wants to stop and you think they need more → share the data and your view once, then respect the choice and leave the door open. Because outcome data from routine care suggest many clients end at a "good enough" level [Barkham-etal-2006].
- If there is no progress after an adequate trial and a review → end and refer rather than continue indefinitely. Because continuing when the client is not likely to benefit breaches ethical standards [APA-Ethics-2017].

## Common problems

- **Ending on the calendar.** Sessions run out and the client is discharged regardless of scores. Fix: decide on data and document it.
- **Ending at response, not remission.** See `09-ending.pitfall-ending-with-residual-symptoms`.
- **Endless therapy.** No ending criteria were ever set. Fix: agree criteria in the first review (see `04-treatment-planning.problem-list-and-goals`).
- **Diagnostic definitions vary.** The remission and recovery durations come from depression research and rest largely on expert logic [Rush-etal-2006]; apply them flexibly to anxiety problems.
