**Bottom line:** Write a closing summary for every episode, planned or not. It states why the client came, what was done, what changed (with scores), the risk status and relapse plan at the end, why therapy ended, and how to get help again. Records should support continuity of care and document the nature, delivery, progress and results of services [APA-RecordKeeping-2007]. Write it so the client could read it, because they usually have a legal right to [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-524].

## When to use

- At the end of every course of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), including dropout and transfer.
- When a client is handed over to another provider (send a copy with consent; see `09-ending.referral-and-handover`).
- In US psychiatric hospitals, the Medicare conditions of participation require a discharge summary: it must recap the stay, give follow-up or aftercare recommendations and summarize the condition at discharge [CFR-42-482-61]. Outpatient practice has no single federal template; the same three elements make a sound core.

## Steps

1. **Pull the data:** dates of first and last sessions, sessions attended and missed, start and end scores, goal ratings, risk notes.
2. **Write each section** of the template below in plain language.
3. **Agree the content with the client** in the final session where possible, especially the relapse plan and recommendations.
4. **Check consent before sending.** Decide who receives it and confirm the legal basis (see Guidance).
5. **Send promptly.** After an inpatient stay, best practice is to forward the discharge summary preferably within 24 hours [ActionAlliance-2019]; for outpatient endings, send soon after the last contact.
6. **Close the record** and file it under your retention policy.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Identifiers | Name, date of birth, record number, therapist, service |
| Episode | Referral source and date, first and last session, sessions attended and missed |
| Presenting problems | Main problems and any diagnosis, with who made it |
| Formulation | Two or three sentences on what maintained the problem |
| Treatment | Model and main interventions, format, any medication or other care |
| Outcome | Start and end scores per measure, whether change is reliable and clinically significant, goals met or not |
| Risk at ending | Current risk summary, safety plan status, crisis contacts given |
| Reason for ending | Planned completion, client choice, dropout, referral, or other |
| Relapse prevention | Early warning signs and setback plan in brief; boosters scheduled |
| Recommendations | Follow-up, referrals made, what other clinicians should watch for |
| Route back | How to self-refer or re-access, and how quickly |

## What to record

- The summary itself, the date sent, the recipients and the consent or legal basis for each.
- Any client disagreement with the content, in their words.
- For depression relapse prevention work: the reassessment of relapse risk and whether more follow-up is needed [NICE-NG222].

## Guidance

- If you are writing the outcome section → give numbers, not adjectives. "PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9) 19 at start, 6 at end: reliable and clinically significant improvement." Because records should document the progress and results of services [APA-RecordKeeping-2007].
- If you are describing the client → avoid pathologizing or blaming words. Avoid: "non-compliant," "poorly motivated." Write: "attended 6 of 12 sessions; reported that evening work hours made attendance hard." Because psychologists are asked to stay sensitive to how derogatory or pathologizing language in the record affects the client [APA-RecordKeeping-2007].
- If the summary goes to another treating provider in the US → tell the client, check state law, and send only what helps their care. Because the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) permits disclosure for another provider's treatment without the client's authorization [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-506].
- If you keep separate psychotherapy notes → do not include or forward them. Because disclosing psychotherapy notes generally needs the client's written authorization [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-508].
- If the record comes from a federally assisted substance use disorder program → check consent under 42 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Part 2 before sending. Because those records carry extra consent and redisclosure rules [CFR-42-2-33].
- If the client asks for a copy → provide it. Because clients have a right of access, and HIPAA requires covered entities to act within 30 days (one 30-day extension allowed); psychotherapy notes are excluded [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-524]. A shorter deadline has been proposed, so check the current rule.
- If you are deciding how long to keep the record → follow state law first; without a stricter rule, consider 7 years after the last service for adults, or 3 years after a minor reaches majority, whichever is later. Because these are the American Psychological Association's suggested periods [APA-RecordKeeping-2007]. These 2007 guidelines carried a 10-year expiry, so confirm their status with the association.

In the UK, summaries usually go to the client's general practitioner, and access requests fall under UK data protection law (see `14-ethics-law.hipaa-and-gdpr`).

## Common problems

- **No summary after dropout.** The record ends mid-treatment. Fix: write a short closing summary for every unplanned ending.
- **Narrative without numbers.** Fix: always include start and end scores.
- **Over-sharing.** Trauma detail sent to an employer or school. Fix: send the minimum needed for the recipient's purpose, with consent.
- **Risk not updated.** The last risk note is from intake. Fix: state risk at ending explicitly.
