**Bottom line:** Treating non-attendance as the client's decision and closing the file quietly misses risk, loses clients who could be re-engaged, and leaves no record of what happened. Make repeated contact, check risk first for anyone with known risk, offer a choice, and close in writing with a route back [ActionAlliance-2019; NICE-NG222].

## What it looks like

- One unanswered call, then nothing until an administrator closes the file.
- No one looked at the risk record before deciding how hard to chase.
- The client receives no letter, or a letter saying only "you have been discharged."
- The record stops mid-treatment with no closing summary.
- The service counts the case as "did not attend" without knowing whether the client got better, got worse, or found therapy unhelpful.

## Why it happens

- Waiting lists put pressure on clinicians to free up slots.
- Dropout is read as low motivation rather than as information about fit, pacing or the alliance.
- Therapists fear that chasing a client is intrusive.
- There is no written missed-appointment protocol, so each clinician improvises.

## The fix

- If a client misses a session → contact them, with repeated attempts if necessary. Because NICE (the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence) recommends repeated contact attempts when people with less severe depression miss follow-up, and the logic applies more widely [NICE-NG222].
- If the client has known suicide risk → act the same day (call during the missed appointment time if you can), escalate through safety plan contacts, and request a welfare check if information points to acute risk. Because assertive, persistent outreach is best practice for clients with suicide risk [ActionAlliance-2019].
- If you send any message → include crisis contacts: US 988 (call or text) or 911 [Lifeline-988]; UK 999, NHS 111 (mental health option) or Samaritans 116 123 [NHS-Urgent-Help; Samaritans-Site]. Because the client may be in crisis and unable to reply.
- If you reach the client → ask what got in the way and offer options: "We can carry on, take a break, change how we meet, or have one session to wrap up." Because a pre-ending discussion is expected unless the client prevents it [APA-Ethics-2017].
- If the client has stopped because they feel better → send the relapse plan and a route back. Because many clients end once improvement is good enough [Barkham-etal-2006].
- If dropout is common in your caseload → review it by client group and clinician. Because dropout is higher among younger clients and with trainee clinicians [Swift-Greenberg-2012].
- If there is no response after the agreed period → send a warm closing letter naming other providers and how to return, and write a closing summary. Because suggesting alternative providers at termination is an ethical expectation [APA-Ethics-2017].

Brief, caring messages that ask nothing of the client are a recognized practice after care transitions for people with suicide risk [ActionAlliance-2019].

## Sources note

The outreach steps come from care-transition best practice for people with suicide risk [ActionAlliance-2019] and from a NICE recommendation written for less severe depression [NICE-NG222]; they are not trial-tested for routine outpatient cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) dropout. Dropout rates and predictors come from a meta-analysis of 669 studies [Swift-Greenberg-2012].
