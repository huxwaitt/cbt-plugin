| Urge surfing | Observing a craving or urge rise, peak and fall like a wave, without acting on it. | `cs.urge-surfing` |
| Expressive writing | Putting thoughts and feelings about stressful events into words, in writing or speech, to make sense of them. | `cs.expressive-writing` |
| Spiritual or religious coping | Drawing on religious or spiritual beliefs, practices or community for comfort, meaning or guidance. | `cs.spiritual-coping` |

#### Maintenance processes (what keeps problems going)

| Label | Definition | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| Avoidance cycle | Avoiding or escaping feared situations brings short-term relief that negatively reinforces avoidance and prevents learning that the feared outcome is unlikely or manageable. | `mp.avoidance-cycle` |
| Procrastination cycle | Putting off difficult or boring tasks brings brief relief, but tasks pile up, setbacks confirm negative beliefs, and avoidance grows. | `mp.procrastination-cycle` |
| Safety behavior cycle | Actions taken in a feared situation to prevent a feared outcome; the non-occurrence is credited to the behavior, so threat beliefs are never disconfirmed. | `mp.safety-behavior-cycle` |
| Reassurance-seeking cycle | Repeatedly seeking reassurance from others, online searches, or doctors lowers anxiety briefly, then doubt returns stronger, maintaining preoccupation and straining relationships. | `mp.reassurance-cycle` |
| Experiential avoidance | Unwillingness to stay in contact with unwanted thoughts, feelings, sensations, or memories, with efforts to change or escape them even when this causes harm. | `mp.experiential-avoidance` |
| Rumination | Repetitive, passive dwelling on one's distress, its causes, meanings, and consequences, without moving to problem solving; deepens and prolongs low mood. | `mp.rumination` |
| Worry | A chain of negative, relatively uncontrollable thoughts about possible future threats, an attempt at mental problem solving that rarely reaches a resolution. | `mp.worry` |
| Worry about worry (meta-worry) | Negative appraisal of one's own worrying as uncontrollable or dangerous, which raises anxiety and drives unhelpful attempts to control or avoid worry. | `mp.worry-about-worry` |
| Anticipatory processing | Detailed rehearsal before a feared social event of likely failures and negative self-images, which raises anxiety, primes self-focus, and often prompts avoidance. | `mp.anticipatory-processing` |
| Post-event processing | Detailed, self-focused review after a social event that dwells on perceived failures and negative self-impressions, strengthening beliefs of social inadequacy. | `mp.post-event-processing` |
| Self-focused attention | Attention shifts inward to anxious sensations, behavior, and a distorted self-image, used as evidence of how one appears while external cues are missed. | `mp.self-focused-attention` |
| Attentional bias and threat monitoring | Preferential, often automatic, attention to threat-related cues in the environment and repeated scanning for danger, which inflates perceived threat and sustains anxiety. | `mp.threat-monitoring` |
| Body-focused monitoring | Repeated attention to and checking of bodily sensations, which makes normal sensations more noticeable and supplies apparent evidence of illness or danger. | `mp.body-focused-monitoring` |
| Interpretation bias | A habitual tendency to resolve ambiguous situations, sensations, or social cues in a negative or threatening direction. | `mp.interpretation-bias` |
| Memory bias | Selective recall of negative or threat-related information, and overgeneral rather than specific memories, which keep negative views of self and world feeling true. | `mp.memory-bias` |
| Belief-confirming processing | Information fitting a core belief is noticed and remembered, while contradicting information is ignored, discounted, or distorted, so the belief survives contrary evidence. | `mp.belief-confirming-processing` |
| Hasty data-gathering bias | Reaching firm conclusions on little evidence and gathering less information before deciding; linked to the formation and persistence of delusional beliefs. | `mp.hasty-data-gathering` |
| Thought suppression | Deliberately trying to push unwanted thoughts out of mind, which often produces rebound, more frequent intrusions, and a sense that thoughts are out of control. | `mp.thought-suppression` |
| Catastrophic misinterpretation of intrusive thoughts | Normal unwanted thoughts, images, or urges are read as significant, revealing, or dangerous, which turns ordinary intrusions into persistent, distressing obsessions. | `mp.misinterpretation-of-intrusions` |
| Intolerance of uncertainty | A tendency to find uncertainty unacceptable and threatening regardless of likely outcome, driving worry, checking, reassurance seeking, and avoidance of ambiguous situations. | `mp.intolerance-of-uncertainty` |
| Clinical perfectionism | Self-worth depends heavily on pursuing demanding self-imposed standards despite adverse consequences; performance is judged all-or-nothing and standards rise when met. | `mp.clinical-perfectionism` |
| Catastrophic misinterpretation of bodily sensations | Normal or anxiety-related bodily sensations are read as signs of imminent disaster (panic) or serious illness (health anxiety), increasing anxiety and the sensations themselves. | `mp.catastrophic-misinterpretation-of-bodily-sensations` |
| Negative appraisal of trauma and its aftermath | Excessively negative meanings given to the trauma or its consequences (symptoms, others' reactions, life changes), producing a sense of serious current threat. | `mp.negative-appraisal-of-trauma` |
| Disjointed trauma memory | Trauma memory is poorly elaborated and contextualized, with strong perceptual priming, so reminders trigger intrusive reexperiencing with a here-and-now quality. | `mp.disjointed-trauma-memory` |
| Dietary restraint and binge cycle | Rigid dietary rules and restriction lead to rule breaks and binge eating; compensatory purging or further restriction follows, which in turn invites more binges. | `mp.dietary-restraint-cycle` |
| Mood intolerance | Difficulty tolerating intense mood states, managed with behaviors such as bingeing, vomiting, exercising, or self-harm that relieve emotion briefly and maintain the eating disorder. | `mp.mood-intolerance` |
| Sleep effort and sleep-related worry | Worry about sleep and its daytime effects, monitoring for signs of poor sleep, and deliberate effort to sleep raise arousal and inhibit automatic sleep onset. | `mp.sleep-effort` |
| Conditioned arousal to bed and bedtime | Repeated wakefulness, frustration, and activity in bed turn the bed and bedtime into cues for arousal rather than sleep. | `mp.conditioned-arousal` |
| Sleep compensation | Coping with poor sleep by spending more time in bed, napping, sleeping in, or keeping irregular schedules, which weakens sleep drive and fragments sleep. | `mp.sleep-compensation` |
| Withdrawal and inactivity loop | Low mood leads to withdrawal from activities and people; reduced reward and mounting problems deepen low mood, prompting further inactivity. | `mp.withdrawal-inactivity-loop` |
| Self-criticism | A habitual hostile, condemning stance toward oneself after setbacks, which activates threat and shame and blocks self-soothing and recovery. | `mp.self-criticism` |
| Emotion dysregulation | Difficulty noticing, accepting, and modulating emotions, with high sensitivity, intense reactions, slow return to baseline, and impulsive or avoidant regulation attempts. | `mp.emotion-dysregulation` |
| Cognitive-interpersonal cycle | Expectations about others prompt behavior that elicits responses confirming those expectations, so beliefs and relationship problems maintain each other. | `mp.interpersonal-cycle` |
| Family accommodation | Family members change their behavior to reduce a relative's distress, by joining rituals, giving reassurance, or enabling avoidance, which maintains anxiety or obsessive-compulsive symptoms. | `mp.family-accommodation` |
| Substance relief cycle | Using a substance relieves distress, craving, or withdrawal quickly, negatively reinforcing use, while consequences and withdrawal raise distress that cues further use. | `mp.substance-relief-cycle` |
| Abstinence violation effect | After a lapse, self-blame, guilt, and a sense of lost control lead the person to abandon the goal, turning a single lapse into full relapse. | `mp.abstinence-violation-effect` |
| Extreme appraisals of mood and energy changes | Changes in energy, mood, or arousal are given extreme personal meanings (success, catastrophe), prompting ascent or descent behaviors that escalate mood swings. | `mp.extreme-appraisals-of-internal-states` |
| Pain catastrophizing | An exaggerated negative mental set toward actual or expected pain: ruminating on it, magnifying its threat and feeling helpless to manage it. | `mp.pain-catastrophizing` |
| Fear-avoidance cycle (pain) | Pain read as threatening breeds pain-related fear, avoidance and hypervigilance, leading to disuse, low mood and disability that maintain pain. | `mp.fear-avoidance-cycle` |
| Overactivity and underactivity cycle | Overdoing activity when pain is lower, then resting for long periods after the resulting flare, so activity and pain swing widely. | `mp.activity-cycling` |
| Poor integration of the loss | The loss is not integrated into autobiographical memory and self-knowledge, so it feels unreal and reminders trigger intense, intrusive grief. | `mp.poor-loss-integration` |
| Anxious avoidance of the loss | Avoiding reminders, thoughts and feelings about the loss, often from fear of being overwhelmed, which blocks integration and keeps grief intense. | `mp.anxious-avoidance-of-loss` |
| Depressive avoidance after loss | Withdrawing from activities, relationships and plans that could foster adjustment, often from beliefs that life is meaningless without the deceased. | `mp.depressive-avoidance-after-loss` |

#### CBT interventions and techniques

| Label | Definition | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| Identifying automatic thoughts | Helping the client notice and record the thoughts and images present when their emotion shifts, then select the hot thought to work on. | `iv.identifying-automatic-thoughts` |
| Cognitive restructuring | Collaboratively examining an automatic thought's accuracy and usefulness through guided questions, then building a more balanced, believable alternative response. | `iv.cognitive-restructuring` |
| Thought record | Structured worksheet linking situation, moods, automatic thoughts, evidence for and against the hot thought, and a balanced alternative, with ratings before and after. | `iv.thought-record` |
| Decatastrophizing | Examining a feared outcome by estimating its realistic likelihood, the worst, best and most likely outcomes, and how the client would cope. | `iv.decatastrophizing` |
| Double-standard technique | Asking how the client would view or advise a friend with the same thought or situation, then applying that kinder standard to themselves. | `iv.double-standard-technique` |
| Responsibility pie chart | Dividing a pie chart among all contributing factors to an event, rating own share last, to correct inflated personal responsibility or blame. | `iv.responsibility-pie-chart` |
| Advantages-disadvantages analysis | Listing and weighing the short- and long-term advantages and disadvantages of holding a belief, keeping a rule, or making a change. | `iv.advantages-disadvantages-analysis` |
| Identifying cognitive distortions | Teaching the client to recognize recurring biased thinking patterns, such as catastrophizing or mind reading, and label them in their own thoughts. | `iv.identifying-cognitive-distortions` |
| Downward arrow | Repeatedly asking what a thought would mean if it were true, to move from an automatic thought to underlying assumptions and core beliefs. | `iv.downward-arrow` |
| Modifying rules and assumptions | Identifying conditional assumptions and rigid rules, examining their costs and origins, testing them with behavioral experiments, and building more flexible versions. | `iv.modifying-rules-and-assumptions` |
| Core belief work | Identifying an unhelpful core belief, then constructing and strengthening an alternative belief over time with evidence logs, historical review, continua and experiential methods. | `iv.core-belief-work` |
| Positive data log | Daily written record of everyday experiences consistent with a new, more adaptive core belief, countering biased attention to belief-confirming information. | `iv.positive-data-log` |
| Continuum technique | Rating self or others on a 0 to 100 scale with explicitly defined anchors, to replace all-or-nothing judgments with graded ones. | `iv.continuum-technique` |
| Imagery rescripting | Recalling a distressing memory or intrusive image in imagination, then changing its course to meet unmet needs and update its meaning. | `iv.imagery-rescripting` |
| Worry postponement | Noticing worry, deferring it to a set daily worry period, then using that period briefly, to weaken worry and test beliefs about uncontrollability. | `iv.worry-postponement` |
| Rumination-focused techniques | Functional analysis of rumination, then shifting from abstract, evaluative thinking to concrete, specific processing, absorption in experience, and self-compassion. | `iv.rumination-focused-techniques` |
| Detached mindfulness | Metacognitive therapy stance of noticing thoughts as passing mental events without engaging, analyzing, suppressing or acting on them. | `iv.detached-mindfulness` |
| Behavioral activation | Structured approach that increases contact with rewarding, valued activity and targets avoidance, using activity monitoring, scheduling and functional analysis of behavior. | `iv.behavioral-activation` |
| Activity monitoring | Hour-by-hour record of what the client does, with ratings of mood, mastery and pleasure, to reveal links between activity and mood. | `iv.activity-monitoring` |
| Activity scheduling | Planning specific valued, pleasurable or mastery activities at set times in advance, then reviewing what happened and how mood changed. | `iv.activity-scheduling` |
| Graded task assignment | Breaking an overwhelming task or goal into small, sequential steps that are attempted in order, starting with one the client is likely to complete. | `iv.graded-task-assignment` |
| Behavioral experiment | Planned activity that tests a specific prediction or belief through direct experience, with a written prediction, belief rating, outcome and conclusion. | `iv.behavioral-experiment` |
| Problem-solving therapy | Structured steps: define the problem, generate options, weigh pros and cons, choose, plan, act and review, alongside work on negative problem orientation. | `iv.problem-solving-therapy` |
| Relaxation training | Teaching skills such as progressive muscle relaxation, slow breathing or applied (cue-controlled) relaxation to lower physiological arousal and use as a coping skill. | `iv.relaxation-training` |
| Safety behavior reduction | Identifying safety behaviors and reassurance seeking, then dropping or fading them, often within experiments, so feared outcomes can be disconfirmed. | `iv.safety-behavior-reduction` |
| Self-monitoring | Client records a target behavior, symptom, thought or urge as it happens, with context, to establish a baseline, reveal patterns and track change. | `iv.self-monitoring` |
| Interpersonal effectiveness and assertiveness training | Teaching and rehearsing clear, respectful requests, refusals and opinions through instruction, modeling, role play and feedback, balancing goals, relationship and self-respect. | `iv.interpersonal-effectiveness-training` |
| Behavior chain analysis | Detailed step-by-step review of vulnerabilities, prompting event, links and consequences of a specific problem behavior, to identify points where change is possible. | `iv.chain-analysis` |
| Exposure | Repeated, planned contact with feared but objectively safe stimuli, without avoidance or safety behaviors, so new non-threat learning can occur. | `iv.exposure-therapy` |
| Exposure hierarchy | Collaboratively listed feared situations with predicted distress ratings, used to plan the order, variety and progression of exposure tasks. | `iv.exposure-hierarchy` |
| In vivo exposure | Exposure to real feared or avoided situations, objects or activities, in session or in daily life. | `iv.in-vivo-exposure` |
| Imaginal exposure | Repeatedly and vividly imagining or narrating a feared scenario or traumatic memory, usually in the present tense, without avoidance, to process it. | `iv.imaginal-exposure` |
| Interoceptive exposure | Deliberately inducing feared bodily sensations, such as dizziness, breathlessness or a racing heart, through exercises, so the client learns they are safe. | `iv.interoceptive-exposure` |
| Inhibitory learning strategies | Designing exposure to maximize violation of specific feared predictions, vary stimuli and contexts, combine cues, remove safety signals and label affect. | `iv.inhibitory-learning-strategies` |
| Emotion awareness training | Teaching the client to identify, label and rate emotions and their components (thoughts, sensations, urges) with present-focused, nonjudgmental awareness. | `iv.emotion-awareness-training` |
| Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) | Eight-week group program of guided mindfulness practices with cognitive elements, helping people disengage from ruminative patterns to prevent depressive relapse. | `iv.mindfulness-based-cognitive-therapy` |
| Compassionate mind training | Compassion-focused exercises, such as soothing rhythm breathing, compassionate self imagery and compassionate letters, building capacity to meet distress and self-criticism with warmth. | `iv.compassionate-mind-training` |
| Distress tolerance skills | Dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) crisis survival skills, such as TIPP, distraction, self-soothing and pros and cons, for getting through intense urges without worsening things. | `iv.distress-tolerance-skills` |
| Emotion regulation skills training | DBT skills for understanding emotions, checking the facts, opposite action, problem solving, and reducing vulnerability through sleep, eating, exercise and positive events. | `iv.emotion-regulation-skills` |
| Socialization to CBT | Orienting the client to the cognitive model, the structured collaborative format, homework and their active role, using examples from their own life. | `iv.socialization-to-cbt` |
| Sharing the formulation | Co-constructing and presenting the case formulation with the client, inviting correction, to explain the problem and guide choice of interventions. | `iv.sharing-the-formulation` |
| Action plan (homework) | Collaboratively designed between-session task that applies session learning, with a clear rationale, specific plan, anticipated obstacles and a review next session. | `iv.action-plan` |
| Routine outcome monitoring with feedback | Regularly administering standardized measures and reviewing scores and trends with the client to detect non-response and guide treatment decisions. | `iv.routine-outcome-monitoring` |
| Motivational interviewing | Collaborative, person-centered method for strengthening a person's own motivation and commitment to change by evoking and exploring their reasons. | `iv.motivational-interviewing` |
| Relapse prevention blueprint | End-of-treatment written plan summarizing what was learned, helpful skills, early warning signs, likely high-risk situations, and steps to take after setbacks. | `iv.relapse-prevention-blueprint` |
| Booster sessions | Scheduled sessions after the main course of therapy to review progress, consolidate skills and address early signs of relapse. | `iv.booster-sessions` |
| Exposure and response prevention (ERP) | Graded exposure to obsessional triggers while refraining from compulsions, neutralizing and reassurance seeking, so fear and urges reduce without rituals. | `iv.exposure-and-response-prevention` |
| Video feedback (social anxiety) | Client predicts how they came across, then watches video of their social performance as an observer would, to correct a distorted self-image. | `iv.video-feedback` |
| Cognitive processing therapy (CPT) | Twelve-session trauma-focused therapy that identifies and challenges stuck points about the trauma and its implications using Socratic dialogue and structured worksheets. | `iv.cognitive-processing-therapy` |
| Stuck point challenging | In CPT, identifying stuck points (beliefs that keep recovery stuck) and examining them with exploring questions, thinking patterns and alternative thoughts worksheets. | `iv.stuck-point-challenging` |
| Prolonged exposure (PE) | Trauma-focused protocol combining psychoeducation, breathing retraining, in vivo exposure to avoided safe cues, and repeated imaginal revisiting of the memory with processing. | `iv.prolonged-exposure` |
| Cognitive therapy for PTSD (CT-PTSD) | Modifies excessively negative appraisals, updates trauma memory hot spots, discriminates triggers (then versus now), and drops maintaining behaviors and cognitive strategies. | `iv.cognitive-therapy-for-ptsd` |
| CBT for insomnia (CBT-I) | Multicomponent insomnia treatment combining sleep restriction, stimulus control, cognitive therapy for sleep-related beliefs, sleep hygiene education and often relaxation. | `iv.cbt-for-insomnia` |
| Sleep restriction | Limiting time in bed to average actual sleep time from diaries, then adjusting it weekly by sleep efficiency, to strengthen sleep drive. | `iv.sleep-restriction` |
| Stimulus control (insomnia) | Re-associating bed with sleep: bed only for sleep and sex, leave bed when unable to sleep, fixed rise time, and no daytime napping. | `iv.stimulus-control` |
| Sleep hygiene education | Education about lifestyle and environmental factors affecting sleep, such as caffeine, alcohol, light and exercise; not recommended as a stand-alone insomnia treatment. | `iv.sleep-hygiene-education` |
| Enhanced CBT for eating disorders (CBT-E) | Fairburn's transdiagnostic, personalized eating disorder treatment in four stages targeting maintaining processes; usually 20 sessions, about 40 if underweight. | `iv.cbt-e` |
| Collaborative weighing | Therapist and client weigh the client once weekly in session, plotting and interpreting the trend, replacing frequent self-weighing or weight avoidance. | `iv.collaborative-weighing` |
| Regular eating | Establishing three planned meals and two or three planned snacks daily, without long gaps, to displace binge eating and dietary restriction. | `iv.regular-eating` |
| Addressing shape concern and body checking | Mapping overevaluation of shape and weight, developing other life domains, and reducing body checking, comparison and avoidance that maintain shape concern. | `iv.addressing-shape-concern` |
| Behavioral parent training | Teaching caregivers to use specific praise, positive attention, clear instructions, consistent consequences and reward systems to improve child behavior. | `iv.behavioral-parent-training` |
| Organization, planning and distractibility skills (adult ADHD) | Adult ADHD modules: one calendar and task list, prioritizing, breaking tasks into steps, matching work chunks to attention span, and distractibility delay. | `iv.organization-and-planning-skills` |
| Safety planning | Collaboratively writing a prioritized plan: warning signs, internal coping, social distraction, people to ask for help, professional and crisis contacts, and making the environment safer. | `iv.safety-planning` |
| Habit reversal training | Awareness training, a competing response physically incompatible with the habit, and social support, used for tics and body-focused repetitive behaviors. | `iv.habit-reversal-training` |
| Contingency management | Providing tangible incentives, such as vouchers or prize draws, contingent on objectively verified target behaviors such as negative drug tests or attendance. | `iv.contingency-management` |
| High-risk situation coping (relapse prevention) | Identifying personal high-risk situations for substance use and rehearsing coping responses, including handling lapses to limit the abstinence violation effect. | `iv.high-risk-situation-coping` |
| Normalizing rationale (CBT for psychosis) | Presenting unusual experiences as understandable and on a continuum with common experience, for example under stress or sleep loss, to reduce distress and stigma. | `iv.normalizing-rationale` |
| Early warning signs plan (bipolar relapse) | Identifying the client's personal early signs of mania or depression and agreeing specific early action steps, often with family and prescriber. | `iv.early-warning-signs-plan` |
| TRAP analysis (Trigger, Response, Avoidance Pattern) | Mapping one recent episode as trigger, emotional response and avoidance pattern, including avoidant thoughts, to show how short-term relief maintains avoidance. | `iv.trap-analysis` |
| TRAC plan (Trigger, Response, Alternative Coping) | Planning an active coping step for the same trigger and response that usually lead to avoidance, then trying it and reviewing the result. | `iv.trac-plan` |
| Implementation intentions (if-then plans) | Linking a specific cue to a planned action in an if-then statement, so the action starts when the cue occurs instead of relying on willpower. | `iv.implementation-intentions` |
| Solution analysis (DBT) | After a chain analysis, choosing a skill or change for each link, vulnerability and prompting event, plus ways to repair consequences. | `iv.solution-analysis` |
| Crisis response plan | A brief, collaboratively written card listing personal warning signs, self-management steps, reasons for living, social supports and crisis contacts for suicidal crises. | `iv.crisis-response-plan` |
| Activity pacing (chronic pain) | Breaking activities into planned, time- or quota-limited portions with breaks, regardless of pain level, to avoid overactivity and underactivity cycles. | `iv.activity-pacing` |
| Graded exposure to feared movements (chronic pain) | Exposure to a hierarchy of activities the client fears will cause pain or harm, to test those predictions and reduce pain-related fear. | `iv.graded-exposure-to-movement` |
| Grief exposure (revisiting the loss) | Guided, repeated confrontation with memories of the death and avoided loss reminders, to integrate the loss and reduce avoidance in prolonged grief. | `iv.grief-exposure` |

#### Therapist skills (in-session response codes)

| Label | Definition | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| Open question | A question that invites an elaborated answer in the client's own words, rather than yes, no, or a single fact. | `ts.open-question` |
| Closed question | A question that limits the answer to yes or no, a choice between options, or a specific fact such as a number or rating. | `ts.closed-question` |
| Guided discovery | The overall CBT process in which questions, summaries and experiments help the client reach new perspectives through their own observation and reasoning. | `ts.guided-discovery` |
| Socratic questioning | Padesky's four-stage dialogue of informational questions, empathic listening, summary and synthesizing questions, aimed at guiding discovery rather than changing the client's mind. | `ts.socratic-questioning` |
| Socratic informational question | Stage 1 of Socratic dialogue: concrete questions, within the client's knowledge, that bring overlooked or unnoticed information into awareness. | `ts.socratic-informational-question` |
| Empathic listening (Socratic stage) | Stage 2 of Socratic dialogue: listening closely for the client's meanings, emotional reactions and missing information, and using them to choose the next question. | `ts.socratic-empathic-listening` |
| Socratic summary | Stage 3 of Socratic dialogue: summarizing, often in writing and in the client's words, the information just gathered, to prepare for synthesis. | `ts.socratic-summary` |
| Synthesizing or analytical question | Stage 4 of Socratic dialogue: a question asking the client to apply the summarized information to their original belief or concern. | `ts.socratic-synthesizing-question` |
| Minimal encourager | A brief sound, word or gesture that signals listening and invites the client to keep going, without adding content. | `ts.minimal-encourager` |
| Simple reflection | A statement that gives back what the client said with little or no added meaning or emphasis. | `ts.simple-reflection` |
| Complex reflection | A statement that reflects the client's words with added meaning or emphasis, such as an inferred feeling, meaning or the next unspoken step. | `ts.complex-reflection` |
| Double-sided reflection | A complex reflection capturing both sides of the client's ambivalence, usually joining them with "and" rather than "but". | `ts.double-sided-reflection` |
| Amplified reflection | A complex reflection that slightly overstates the client's sustain talk, without sarcasm, so the client can back away from the extreme position. | `ts.amplified-reflection` |
| Reflection of feeling | A reflection that names the client's emotion, stated or implied, often with its context, to show empathic understanding. | `ts.reflection-of-feeling` |
| Reflection of meaning | A complex reflection naming the values, beliefs or personal significance beneath the client's account, beyond its facts and feelings. | `ts.reflection-of-meaning` |
| Collecting summary | A summary that gathers several related client statements, often change talk, and offers them back before inviting the client to continue. | `ts.collecting-summary` |
| Linking summary | A summary connecting what the client just said with something said earlier or in a past session, to show a pattern or ambivalence. | `ts.linking-summary` |
| Transitional summary | A summary that draws together a topic or session segment and signals a shift to a new task or topic. | `ts.transitional-summary` |
| Capsule summary | A brief CBT summary made at intervals in a session, condensing the main points or conclusion of an agenda item, ideally in the client's words. | `ts.capsule-summary` |
| Affirmation | A genuine statement recognizing the client's strengths, efforts, intentions or worth, focused on the client rather than on the therapist's approval. | `ts.affirmation` |
| Empathic statement | A compassionate or understanding comment conveying that the therapist grasps and cares how hard something is for the client. | `ts.empathic-statement` |
| Normalizing | Conveying, with accurate information, that an experience or symptom is common and understandable rather than a sign of being abnormal or broken. | `ts.normalizing` |
| Validation | Communicating that the client's emotions, thoughts or actions are understandable and make sense, given their history, situation or nature. | `ts.validation` |
| Validation level 1: attending | Listening and observing with full, nonjudgmental attention and interest, so the client sees they are taken seriously. | `ts.validation-level-1` |
| Validation level 2: accurate reflection | Accurately reflecting back the client's own stated feelings, thoughts and assumptions, without judgment, to communicate understanding. | `ts.validation-level-2` |
| Validation level 3: articulating the unverbalized | Putting into words feelings, thoughts or meanings the client has not stated, inferred from context or behavior, offered tentatively for correction. | `ts.validation-level-3` |
| Validation level 4: past learning or biology | Conveying that a response makes sense given the client's history, past learning or biology, even if it is not effective now. | `ts.validation-level-4` |
| Validation level 5: reasonable in the present context | Conveying that the response is reasonable, normal or effective given the current situation, one most people would share. | `ts.validation-level-5` |
| Validation level 6: radical genuineness | Treating the client as a capable equal, not as fragile or merely a patient, and responding genuinely as one person to another. | `ts.validation-level-6` |
| Psychoeducation | Providing information about a condition, the CBT model or how a problem works, tailored to the client and checked for understanding. | `ts.psychoeducation` |
| Giving a rationale | Explaining why a proposed task, technique or homework should help, linked to the client's formulation and goals, before asking them to try it. | `ts.giving-rationale` |
| Asking permission | Asking the client's consent before offering information, advice, feedback or a sensitive topic, leaving the client free to decline. | `ts.asking-permission` |
| Elicit-provide-elicit | An information exchange sequence: ask what the client knows or wants to know, offer information briefly, then ask what they make of it. | `ts.elicit-provide-elicit` |
| Advice with permission | Offering a suggestion or recommendation after the client asks or agrees, framed so the client remains free to decide. | `ts.advice-with-permission` |
| Emphasizing autonomy | A statement highlighting the client's choice, control and right to decide about change, without blame. | `ts.emphasizing-autonomy` |
| Agenda setting | Collaboratively deciding near the start of a session which topics or problems to cover, in what order, and for roughly how long. | `ts.agenda-setting` |
| Eliciting feedback | Asking the client for reactions to the session, the therapist or a task, including what was unhelpful, and acting on the answer. | `ts.eliciting-feedback` |
| Checking understanding | Confirming shared understanding: asking the client to restate a key point or plan, or asking whether the therapist's understanding is accurate. | `ts.checking-understanding` |
| Collaborative goal setting | Working with the client to turn problems into specific, client-owned, measurable goals and agreeing on the tasks to reach them. | `ts.collaborative-goal-setting` |
| Reframing | Offering a different, plausible meaning for something the client described, which changes its emotional valence, for example from weakness to care. | `ts.reframing` |
| Acknowledging the rupture | Noticing and openly naming a strain in the relationship, such as withdrawal, appeasing or a complaint, instead of ignoring it. | `ts.repair-acknowledge-rupture` |
| Taking responsibility for the rupture | Acknowledging, without defensiveness, the therapist's own part in a rupture, such as a misunderstanding or pushing too fast. | `ts.repair-take-responsibility` |
| Metacommunication | Talking about what is happening between client and therapist in the moment, including disclosing the therapist's own experience of the interaction. | `ts.repair-metacommunication` |
| Exploring the rupture | Inviting the client to express negative thoughts and feelings about the therapist or therapy, and staying with them nondefensively. | `ts.repair-explore-rupture` |
| Changing the task or goal | Responding to a rupture by changing, pausing or adjusting the current task or goal so that it better fits the client. | `ts.repair-change-task` |
| Purposeful silence | Deliberately pausing without speaking to give the client time to think, feel or continue, while remaining visibly attentive. | `ts.purposeful-silence` |
| Self-disclosure | Revealing something personal, such as a similar experience or feeling, briefly and for the client's benefit, then returning the focus to the client. | `ts.self-disclosure` |
| Humor | Light, well-timed humor attuned to the client and the alliance, used to ease tension or offer perspective, never at the client's expense. | `ts.humor` |
| Righting reflex | The urge to fix the client's problem by persuading, advising or arguing for change, which tends to provoke the client's counterarguments. | `ts.righting-reflex` |
| Lecturing | Extended one-way explanation or information giving without checking the client's knowledge, interest or reaction. | `ts.lecturing` |
| Premature reassurance | Reassuring before understanding the client's concern, or in reply to anxious reassurance seeking, which dismisses feelings and can maintain anxiety. | `ts.premature-reassurance` |
| Arguing | Directly disagreeing, correcting, criticizing or debating with the client in a way that sets the therapist against the client's view. | `ts.arguing` |
| Interrupting | Cutting into the client's speech or changing topic before they finish, outside brief purposeful redirection, signaling that the therapist's agenda matters more. | `ts.interrupting` |
| Labeling the client | Applying a fixed, judgmental label or diagnosis to the client as a person, such as "resistant" or "in denial", instead of describing behavior. | `ts.labeling-client` |
| Premature advice | Telling the client what to do before understanding the problem or getting permission, often solving the wrong problem. | `ts.premature-advice` |
| Tentative hypothesis | Offering an inference about a thought, belief or pattern as a possibility the client can confirm or correct, never stated as fact. | `ts.tentative-hypothesis` |
| Instilling hope | Building realistic expectation of benefit through a credible, personalized rationale, honest answers about likely progress, and visible evidence of change, without promising outcomes. | `ts.instilling-hope` |
| Highlighting progress | Drawing the client's attention to specific, measured improvement and crediting their own actions for it, so early gains build hope and engagement. | `ts.highlighting-progress` |
| Nonverbal attunement | Using posture, facial expression, eye contact, pace and tone of voice that fit the client's state and match the therapist's words. | `ts.nonverbal-attunement` |
| Using the client's words | Repeating the client's own key words, images and metaphors in reflections and summaries instead of translating them into technical terms. | `ts.using-client-words` |
| Plain language | Explaining ideas in everyday words and short sentences, adding a technical term only after the plain version and only if it helps. | `ts.plain-language` |
| Redirecting to the agenda | Politely interrupting a peripheral or circular discussion with a brief summary and a link back to the agreed agenda item. | `ts.redirecting-to-agenda` |
| Grounding in session | Bringing a dissociating or overwhelmed client back to the present by orienting to time and place and directing attention to concrete sensations. | `ts.grounding` |
| Verbal de-escalation | Responding to anger or agitation with calm, concise speech, respectful distance, honest agreement where possible, clear limits and choices. | `ts.de-escalation` |
| Asking directly about suicide | Asking plainly and calmly whether the client is thinking about suicide when hopelessness or distress appears, then assessing risk if indicated. | `ts.direct-suicide-question` |

#### Risk indicators (suicide, self-harm, harm to others, safeguarding)

| Label | Definition | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| Previous suicide attempt | Any past self-initiated, potentially injurious act with at least some intent to die, including interrupted and aborted attempts; a key historical risk factor. | `rk.prior-suicide-attempt` |
| History of self-harm | Past deliberate self-injury without intent to die; it raises later risk of suicide and repeat self-harm and is recorded separately from attempts. | `rk.history-of-self-harm` |
| Family history of or exposure to suicide | Suicide or suicide attempts by a family member, close friend or peer, or living in a community with a recent suicide cluster. | `rk.family-history-or-exposure-to-suicide` |
| Childhood adversity | Abuse, neglect, household violence or other serious adversity in childhood, recorded as an enduring background risk factor for suicide. | `rk.childhood-adversity` |
| History of mental disorder | Past or lifetime depression, bipolar disorder, psychosis, substance use, eating, personality or other mental disorder, or past mental health treatment. | `rk.history-of-mental-disorder` |
| Impulsive or aggressive traits | A longstanding pattern of acting quickly on urges or reacting aggressively, which can shorten the step from suicidal thought to action. | `rk.impulsive-or-aggressive-traits` |
| Thwarted belongingness | An unmet need to belong: feeling lonely and lacking reciprocally caring relationships, even when other people are around. | `rk.thwarted-belongingness` |
| Defeat or humiliation | A loss, rejection or failure experienced as a crushing defeat or humiliation, often with intense shame; a common trigger for entrapment. | `rk.defeat-or-humiliation` |
| Substance use problem | Ongoing harmful or dependent use of alcohol or drugs, which raises risk through disinhibition, low mood and its life consequences. | `rk.substance-use-problem` |
| Recent major loss or crisis | A recent loss or crisis, such as relationship breakdown, bereavement, job or financial loss, or legal trouble, that precipitates or worsens risk. | `rk.acute-life-stressor` |
| Access to lethal means | Ready access to means that could be used in a suicide attempt; a modifiable factor addressed by restricting access to lethal means. | `rk.access-to-lethal-means` |
| Active psychotic symptoms | Current hallucinations or delusions, especially commands to harm or beliefs of being threatened, that may drive harm to self or others. | `rk.active-psychotic-symptoms` |
| Suicidal communication | Talking, writing or posting about wanting to die or kill oneself; a direct warning sign that calls for prompt risk assessment. | `rk.suicidal-communication` |
| Passive suicidal ideation | Thoughts of wanting to be dead or not wake up, without thoughts of acting to end one's own life. | `rk.passive-suicidal-ideation` |
| Active suicidal ideation | Thoughts of killing oneself, from nonspecific thoughts to thinking about how, without stated intent to act on them. | `rk.active-suicidal-ideation` |
| Suicidal intent | Some intention to act on suicidal thoughts, as distinct from having the thoughts while clearly not intending to act. | `rk.suicidal-intent` |
| Suicide plan | Suicidal intent with some details of a plan worked out; document that a plan exists and its timing, not method specifics. | `rk.suicide-plan` |
