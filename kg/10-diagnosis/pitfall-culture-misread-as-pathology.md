**Bottom line:** Diagnosis is shaped by the clinician's cultural lens. Black clients in the United States have about 2.4 times the odds of a schizophrenia diagnosis that White clients have, and structured interviews do not reliably close the gap. Before labeling unusual beliefs, intense distress or mistrust as pathology, find out how the client's own community would see them, and actively assess mood symptoms.

## What it looks like

- A religious or spiritual experience normal in the client's community is recorded as a hallucination or delusion.
- Fear or suspicion that matches the client's real experience of discrimination, policing or immigration enforcement is recorded as paranoia.
- Somatic idioms of distress ("my heart is heavy," "thinking too much") are missed as depression or anxiety, or read as health anxiety.
- Mood symptoms in a client with psychotic features are underweighted, and schizophrenia is chosen over a mood disorder with psychotic features.
- The client disengages after the assessment, feeling misunderstood.

## Why it happens

- **Clinician bias and unfamiliarity.** A review of 24 years of research found Black clients were diagnosed with psychotic disorders at three to four times the rate of White clients, and Latino clients at about three times, with clinician bias among the explanations [Schwartz-Blankenship-2014].
- **Structured tools do not fix it.** Across 55 US studies, Black individuals had 2.42 times the odds of a schizophrenia diagnosis. In the 14 studies using structured interviews the odds ratio was 1.77, still significant and not statistically lower than in unstructured studies [Olbert-etal-2018].
- **Mood symptoms underweighted.** In one outpatient service (1,657 patients), African American patients diagnosed with schizophrenia were more likely than non-Latino White patients with the same diagnosis to screen positive for major depression [Gara-etal-2019].
- **Language and context.** Questions translated literally, or asked without knowing the client's norms, produce answers that sound abnormal.

## The fix

- If a belief or experience sounds unusual → ask "How would people in your family or community understand this?" and "Do others you trust share this belief?" before calling it a symptom. Because counselors must consider culture and recognize historical prejudice in diagnosis [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- If you are unfamiliar with the client's culture, unsure the criteria fit, or the client disagrees with your view → use the Cultural Formulation Interview. Because these are the situations its developers named as most needing cultural assessment [Lewis-Fernandez-etal-2014].
- If you are considering a psychotic disorder in a racialized client → assess depressive and manic symptoms carefully and document why a mood disorder with psychotic features was ruled out. Because underweighting mood symptoms may contribute to the schizophrenia disparity [Gara-etal-2019].
- If you rely on a structured interview → keep checking for bias anyway. Because structured assessment did not significantly reduce the disparity [Olbert-etal-2018].
- If mistrust is part of the presentation → name it as understandable first: "Given what you've been through, it makes sense to be careful about who you trust. Can we look at where that helps you and where it costs you?" Because DSM-5-TR (the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision) explicitly attends to racism and discrimination as context [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025].
- Use professional interpreters, not family, and seek consultation from a clinician who knows the culture when in doubt.
- Avoid: "That's a delusion" or "You're being paranoid" before you understand the context.

## Sources note

The disparity data are mostly from US samples and observational designs, so they show that bias contributes but cannot measure how much [Olbert-etal-2018; Schwartz-Blankenship-2014]. The clinic screening study is a single site and its authors call for replication [Gara-etal-2019]. UK studies of psychosis in Black and minority ethnic groups raise similar questions, but were not reviewed for this page.
