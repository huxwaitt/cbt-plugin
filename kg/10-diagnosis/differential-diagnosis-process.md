**Bottom line:** Work in a fixed order: make sure the client is safe, rule out substance and medical causes, then find the disorder that best explains the picture, then decide between adjustment disorder, a residual category or no disorder. Check the high-cost distinctions every time, especially past hypomania in anyone presenting with depression. Hold the result as a working hypothesis and reassess it.

## When to use

- At every intake, before choosing a cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) protocol.
- When symptoms do not fit the working diagnosis, treatment is not working, or new information arrives (collateral history, medical results).

## Steps

The six steps follow First's handbook for the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR), which pairs them with 30 symptom-based decision trees and 67 differential tables [First-2024]. Step 0 is added for therapy settings.

0. **Safety first.** Screen suicide, self-harm and harm to others before diagnostic detail (see `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment`); if risk is imminent, follow `02-risk.crisis-response`. In a US emergency, call 911 or the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988); in the UK call 999, National Health Service (NHS) 111 or Samaritans on 116 123.
1. **Consider feigning.** Ask whether symptoms are feigned for an external incentive (malingering) or without one (factitious disorder) [First-2024]. Rare in routine CBT; move on unless records conflict.
2. **Substance or medication.** Ask about alcohol, drugs, prescribed and over-the-counter medicines, and timing. Symptoms that began before use, or persist for about a month after acute withdrawal or severe intoxication ends, point to an independent disorder [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
3. **Medical condition.** Ask about physical illness, new or atypical symptoms, and recent medical review. A mental disorder due to another medical condition must be excluded before a primary diagnosis [First-2024]. Refer for medical assessment when onset is late, sudden or atypical, or physical signs accompany the symptoms (see `12-medical-pharmacology.medical-mimics`).
4. **The best-fitting disorder.** Compare the presentation against neighboring diagnoses (table below). Ask about each criterion directly; a questionnaire score is a screen, not a diagnosis [Levis-etal-2019].
5. **Adjustment versus residual category.** Clinically significant symptoms that do not meet full criteria are an adjustment disorder if they are a maladaptive response to an identifiable stressor; otherwise use "other specified" (record the reason) or "unspecified" [First-2024; APA-DSM5TR-2022].
6. **Boundary with no disorder.** Without clinically significant distress or impairment, there is no disorder. Problems such as bereavement can still be a focus of care and coded with Z codes [First-2024].

**Distinctions CBT therapists most often need:**

| Presenting picture | Check against | Deciding question |
|---|---|---|
| Depression | Bipolar disorder | Any past period of elevated or irritable mood with more energy and less need for sleep? |
| Depression | Prolonged grief, adjustment disorder | Is distress centered on a loss or stressor, and how long since? |
| Chronic worry | Depressive rumination, obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) | Future-oriented "what if" worry, past-focused "why" brooding, or intrusive unwanted thoughts with neutralizing? |
| Panic attacks | Medical causes, substances, other anxiety disorders | Is the fear of the attacks themselves, or of a situation, object or evaluation? |
| Social avoidance | Autism, avoidant personality, depression | Fear of negative evaluation, or difficulty with social communication? |
| Trauma symptoms | Adjustment disorder, depression, psychosis | Is the event a qualifying trauma, and are there intrusions and avoidance? |
| Poor concentration | Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), anxiety, depression, sleep loss | Present since childhood across settings, or only during episodes? |

## What to record

- Working diagnosis with code, specifiers, and whether it is provisional.
- Differentials considered and the evidence for ruling each in or out.
- Substance and medical screen results, and any referral made.
- Information still needed (records, collateral, medical results) and a date to reassess.

## Guidance

- If a client presents with depression → ask about past hypomania or mania and family history of bipolar disorder before planning treatment. Because in a survey of 600 support-group members with bipolar disorder, 69% had first been misdiagnosed, most often with unipolar depression [Hirschfeld-etal-2003].
- If symptoms began or worsened with a new medication, substance or illness → say "Before we treat this as anxiety, I'd like your doctor to check a few physical causes," and refer. Because steps 2 and 3 come before any primary diagnosis [First-2024].
- If the picture is unclear after intake → record the diagnosis as provisional (or use an unspecified category), set a date to reassess, and start the lowest-risk part of treatment, such as psychoeducation and monitoring, while you gather information. Because DSM-5-TR provides the provisional label for this situation [APA-DSM5TR-2022], and early diagnoses often change on re-evaluation [Bradford-etal-2024].
- If you are relying on unstructured interviewing in a complex case → add a structured interview. Because in one randomized study, adding a nurse-administered structured interview changed the diagnosis within 90 days in 73% of new outpatients, against 16% under usual care [Bradford-etal-2024].
- If you are building a tool that suggests diagnoses → present them as hypotheses for a qualified clinician to verify. Because the criteria are meant for clinically trained users, not mechanical application [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025].

## Common problems

- Stopping at the first diagnosis that fits (see `10-diagnosis.pitfall-premature-diagnostic-closure`).
- Diagnosing from a questionnaire total.
- Skipping steps 2 and 3 because the client "looks anxious."
- Never revisiting the diagnosis once treatment starts.
