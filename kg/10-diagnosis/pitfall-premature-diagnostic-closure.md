**Bottom line:** Premature closure means accepting the first diagnosis that fits and stopping the search. It is common in mental health care and costly: bipolar disorder treated as depression for years, anxiety hidden behind a depression label, a medical cause missed. The fix is a fixed differential routine, a structured check for the high-cost alternatives, and scheduled reassessment.

## What it looks like

- The referral letter says "depression" and the cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) plan follows it without asking about past hypomania, substances or physical health.
- The diagnosis in the record never changes, even after months of poor response or a new symptom.
- A second disorder (social anxiety, attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder or an eating disorder) only surfaces late, when the client mentions it in passing.
- Notes record one diagnosis with no differentials considered.

## Why it happens

- **Cognitive bias.** Time pressure and cognitive biases interfere with gathering and weighing data, and content effects matter: in vignette studies clinicians were less likely to suspect obsessive-compulsive disorder when obsessions had taboo content [Bradford-etal-2024].
- **Screens read as diagnoses.** Rating scales interpreted without a diagnostic interview give inaccurate impressions [Bradford-etal-2024].
- **The client's own label.** A self-reported earlier diagnosis of "depression" had a 62% false-positive rate in a study of over 5,000 US adults [Bradford-etal-2024].
- **Hidden history.** People rarely volunteer past elevated mood. In a UK sample of 441 people diagnosed with major depression in the past 5 years, 15% did not meet criteria for any mood disorder and 30% had undetected bipolar disorder; in a study of 924 people with mood disorders, the correct bipolar diagnosis came on average 5.4 years after the first depression diagnosis [Bradford-etal-2024]. In a US survey, 69% of people with bipolar disorder were first misdiagnosed, and over a third waited 10 years or more [Hirschfeld-etal-2003].

## The fix

- If you are accepting a diagnosis from a referral or a previous clinician → treat it as a hypothesis and run the differential steps yourself: substances, medical causes, then neighboring disorders. Because substance and medical causes must be excluded before a primary diagnosis [First-2024].
- If the presenting problem is depression → always ask about past periods of unusually high or irritable mood with more energy and less need for sleep, and about family history. Because bipolar disorder is most often misdiagnosed as unipolar depression [Hirschfeld-etal-2003].
- If the case is complex or the diagnosis is uncertain → add a structured interview. Because in one randomized study a nurse-administered structured interview changed the diagnosis within 90 days in 73% of new outpatients, against 16% under usual care [Bradford-etal-2024].
- If treatment response is poor after a fair trial → reopen the diagnosis before changing technique. Because a significant share of depression diagnoses convert to bipolar disorder when re-evaluated, and anxiety disorders are often the better fit [Bradford-etal-2024].
- Write "differentials considered" with one line on each, and set a review date for the diagnosis in the treatment plan.

**Example phrasing:** "Before we settle on a plan, I'd like to ask about a few things that can look like depression but need a different approach. Have you ever had a stretch of days when you felt unusually high, energized or irritable, and needed much less sleep?"

## Sources note

Rates of missed diagnosis come from mixed designs (surveys, record reviews, structured interview comparisons), summarized in a 2024 review that notes the field lacks shared definitions of diagnostic error [Bradford-etal-2024]. The bipolar survey figures are from support-group members and may overstate rates in the general population [Hirschfeld-etal-2003].
