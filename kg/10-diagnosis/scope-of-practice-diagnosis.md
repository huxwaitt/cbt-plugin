**Bottom line:** In the United States, state law decides which licenses may diagnose; physicians, psychologists and psychiatric nurse practitioners generally can, and clinical social workers, counselors and marriage and family therapists can where their state practice act allows. In the UK, regulation is mainly by protected title and employer role, and some diagnoses, such as attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) at any age, are reserved for specialists. Everyone else, including trainees acting alone, coaches, peer workers and artificial intelligence (AI) tools, describes and screens but does not diagnose.

## The principle

Diagnosis has legal and financial consequences: it goes into records, to insurers and sometimes to courts. The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) itself warns that its criteria are for people with clinical training and should not be applied mechanically [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025]. Diagnose only when your license allows it and you are competent in that area [ACA-Ethics-2014].

**United States, by role.** Check your state practice act; this is a starting point.

| Role | Diagnosis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Psychiatrist or other physician | Yes | Can also prescribe |
| Licensed psychologist | Yes | Psychological testing is usually within this scope |
| Psychiatric-mental health nurse practitioner | Yes, within state nursing scope | Prescribing authority varies by state |
| Licensed clinical social worker | Where state law allows | Medicare covers their diagnosis and treatment of mental illness as far as state law authorizes [CFR-42-410-73] |
| Licensed professional or mental health counselor; marriage and family therapist | Where state law allows | Medicare has covered their diagnosis and treatment services since January 1, 2024 [CMS-MFT-MHC-Page] |
| Pre-licensed associate or trainee | Only under a licensed supervisor, as state rules allow | The supervisor reviews and co-signs; rules for associates vary by state |
| Coach, peer worker, unlicensed staff, AI tool | No | May screen, describe symptoms and refer |

**United Kingdom.** The Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) protects titles such as clinical psychologist and counseling psychologist (UK spelling "counselling"), but not "psychotherapist," "counselor" or "cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) therapist" [HCPC-Professions]. Accreditation by the British Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapies (BABCP) requires a core mental health profession or equivalent plus CBT training [BABCP-Accreditation]. In the National Health Service (NHS), who records a diagnosis is set by role; for example, ADHD should be diagnosed only by a specialist psychiatrist, pediatrician or other qualified clinician with training and expertise in ADHD [NICE-NG87].

## Guidance

- If your license does not allow diagnosis → record a description ("symptoms consistent with panic attacks, not assessed further") and refer to a qualified clinician. Because practicing outside the boundaries of competence and credentials is an ethics breach [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- If you are a trainee → write "provisional, discussed with supervisor" and get the diagnosis co-signed. Because you practice under your supervisor's oversight and your own competence is still developing (expert consensus; check your board's rules on who signs) [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- If an insurer needs a code you cannot assign → have a qualified clinician assess the client; never borrow another clinician's name or guess. Because reports to insurers must be accurate and honest [ACA-Ethics-2014].
- If a client asks a non-diagnosing worker "Do I have bipolar disorder?" → explain the limit and offer a route: see the phrasings below. Because a label from an unqualified source can mislead treatment.
- If you are designing a tool → let it screen, summarize and flag "consider assessment for", never output a diagnosis, and route positives and risk signals to a human clinician. Because the criteria are meant for trained clinicians [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025].
- If you practice across state or national lines by telehealth → check licensure and scope in the client's location, not only yours (see `14-ethics-law.telehealth-law`). Because Medicare judges authority by the law of the state where the service is performed [CFR-42-410-73], and licensing boards generally treat that as the client's location.
- If a diagnosis would harm the client and is not required → discuss with the client and consider not recording it. Because counselors may refrain from making or reporting a diagnosis that would cause harm [ACA-Ethics-2014].

## Example phrasings

- Non-diagnosing worker: "I'm not able to give a diagnosis. What you've described is worth a proper assessment, and I can help you arrange one."
- Trainee: "My impression is that this fits social anxiety disorder. I'll confirm it with my supervisor and we'll go over it together next week."
- Tool or chatbot: "Some of what you describe can be part of depression, but only a clinician can tell. Would you like information on how to get assessed?"
- Licensed clinician to a client: "Based on what we've covered, you meet criteria for generalized anxiety disorder. Here is what that means and what it doesn't."
- Avoid: "You definitely have ADHD" without a completed ADHD assessment.

## Evidence

This page rests on regulation, regulator listings and professional codes, not trials. Medicare rules tie diagnosis by social workers, marriage and family therapists and counselors to state authorization [CFR-42-410-73; CMS-MFT-MHC-Page]. The American Counseling Association code requires proper diagnosis, competence and accurate third-party reports [ACA-Ethics-2014]. State law changes; verify your board's current rules. Risk never waits for diagnosis: anyone in any role who hears suicide risk follows `02-risk.crisis-response` (US: call or text 988, or 911; UK: 999, NHS 111, Samaritans 116 123).
