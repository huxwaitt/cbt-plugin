**Bottom line:** In the United States, the code on a claim comes from the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM), and the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) lists the matching code for each diagnosis. Codes change every October 1, sometimes April 1, so a cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) practice must code from the set in force on the date of service. The UK and most other countries use the World Health Organization (WHO) ICD instead, and ICD-11 has been in effect since January 2022 with different diagnostic concepts in several areas.

## What it is

- **ICD-10-CM** is the US clinical modification of WHO ICD-10, maintained by the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) [CDC-ICD-10-CM-Page]. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) state that ICD-10 applies to all parties covered by the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) [CMS-ICD-10-Page].
- **DSM-5-TR** does not have its own codes; it prints ICD-10-CM codes, and the American Psychiatric Association (APA) posts code changes each September [APA-DSM5TR-Code-Updates].
- **ICD-11** was adopted by the World Health Assembly in May 2019 and came into effect on January 1, 2022 [WHO-ICD11-2022]. Its clinical manual, the Clinical Descriptions and Diagnostic Requirements (CDDR), was published on March 8, 2024 as a free download [WHO-CDDR-2024].

## Why it matters in CBT

A wrong or expired code gets a claim rejected, and a code follows the client in their record and to insurers. Codes also carry clinical meaning: "Depression, unspecified" (F32.A) says less than a coded major depressive episode with severity [CDC-ICD-10-CM-2027]. Outside the US, colleagues and letters may use ICD-11 names, such as complex posttraumatic stress disorder (complex PTSD), that have no DSM-5-TR equivalent.

## Key details

**The US update cycle.** The fiscal year 2027 code set covers encounters from October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027; April 1 update sets also occur [CMS-ICD-10-Page]. The APA reports no ICD-10-CM changes to DSM-5-TR diagnoses planned for 2026 [APA-DSM-Page]. Example: prolonged grief disorder was printed as F43.8 in 2022 and has coded as F43.81 since October 1, 2022 [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025; CDC-ICD-10-CM-2027]. Another: current suicidal behavior, printed as T14.91A, must be coded T14.91XA (initial encounter) [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025].

**Z codes.** Psychosocial problems can be coded alongside a disorder, for example homelessness (Z59.00 to Z59.02), food insecurity (Z59.41), acculturation difficulty (Z60.3), and moral, religious or spiritual problem (Z65.8) [CDC-ICD-10-CM-2027; APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025]. They record context that shapes the formulation.

**How ICD-11 differs** [Reed-etal-2019]:
- It describes essential features rather than fixed symptom counts and durations, except where evidence supports a threshold.
- New categories include complex PTSD, prolonged grief disorder, gaming disorder, compulsive sexual behavior disorder, bodily distress disorder and olfactory reference disorder.
- Personality disorder is rated by severity (mild, moderate, severe), with optional trait domains (negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition, anankastia) and an optional borderline pattern.
- PTSD is defined more narrowly around re-experiencing in the present, avoidance and a sense of current threat.
- Mixed depressive and anxiety disorder is a diagnosis; hypochondriasis sits with obsessive-compulsive and related disorders; gender incongruence moved out of the mental disorders chapter.
- Prolonged grief disorder can be diagnosed 6 months after a loss, versus 12 months for adults in DSM-5-TR [Prigerson-etal-2021].

**Adoption.** As of May 2026, WHO reports 132 countries at various stages of implementing ICD-11 [WHO-ICD11-Factsheet-2026]. CMS still publishes ICD-10-CM code sets through fiscal year 2027 [CMS-ICD-10-Page]. ICD-10-CM is a US modification [CDC-ICD-10-CM-Page]; UK and other services use WHO editions of the ICD, so check which edition a letter or record uses before comparing codes.

## In the session

- Code only what your documented assessment supports, at the highest specificity the record justifies.
- Recheck codes each October and at any April update; billing software can lag.
- Tell clients that a diagnosis code goes to their insurer and stays in their record before you submit it.
- When a report from abroad uses an ICD-11 diagnosis, map it to the nearest DSM-5-TR diagnosis only after your own assessment; complex PTSD, for example, has no direct DSM-5-TR code.
