**Bottom line:** The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR) is the United States reference for naming mental disorders. Each diagnosis is a set of criteria (symptoms, duration, distress or impairment, exclusions) applied with clinical judgment, never mechanically. For a cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) therapist it supplies shared language, a link to disorder-specific protocols and the codes that payers require. It is updated in place each year, so check the current supplement.

## What it is

DSM-5-TR was published by the American Psychiatric Association (APA) on March 18, 2022, nine years after DSM-5 [Singer-2022]. It has three components: the diagnostic classification with codes, the criteria sets, and the descriptive text for each disorder [APA-About-DSM]. The book is laid out in three sections:

- **Section I** explains how to use the manual, including the definition of a mental disorder and how to record diagnoses [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- **Section II** holds the criteria sets by chapter, plus "other conditions that may be a focus of clinical attention" (psychosocial problems coded with Z codes, which are not disorders) [APA-DSM5TR-2022].
- **Section III** holds emerging measures and models: cross-cutting symptom measures, the Cultural Formulation Interview, an alternative dimensional model of personality disorders, and conditions for further study [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

The APA states the criteria are guidelines informed by clinical judgment, "not meant to be used in a rigid cookbook fashion," and should not be applied by people without clinical training [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025].

## Why it matters in CBT

- Most CBT protocols and guidelines are written and tested for a named disorder, so the diagnosis points you to the protocol with the best evidence.
- Diagnosis tells you what else to check (typical comorbidity, risk, medical causes) and what course to expect.
- United States insurers require a coded diagnosis to pay for treatment.
- It does not tell you why this person's problem continues. That is the job of the formulation (see `10-diagnosis.diagnosis-vs-formulation`).

## Key details

**How criteria work.** A typical criteria set combines a symptom count (for example, a minimum number from a list), a minimum duration, a clinical significance requirement (distress or impairment), and exclusions (not better explained by another disorder, a substance or a medical condition) [APA-DSM5TR-2022]. Expectable, culturally sanctioned reactions to common stressors, such as ordinary grief, are not disorders [APA-DSM5TR-2022].

| Term | What it means | Example use |
|---|---|---|
| Subtype | Mutually exclusive subgroups; pick one | Type of specific phobia |
| Specifier | Not mutually exclusive; add any that apply | "With anxious distress", "with panic attacks" |
| Severity and course specifiers | Current intensity or remission status | Mild, moderate, severe; in partial or full remission |
| Other specified | Criteria not fully met, and you record the reason | A short-duration depressive-like episode |
| Unspecified | Criteria not met and you do not or cannot give the reason | Emergency setting, too little information |
| Provisional | Strong presumption, information still incomplete | Collateral history pending |

[APA-DSM5TR-2022]

**What changed from DSM-5 (2013) to DSM-5-TR (2022)** (paraphrased):
- One new disorder, prolonged grief disorder: in adults at least 12 months after a death (6 months for children and adolescents), with yearning or preoccupation plus at least 3 of 8 further symptoms, nearly every day for at least the last month [Prigerson-etal-2021].
- New symptom codes for current and past suicidal behavior and nonsuicidal self-injury; these are codes, not disorders [Singer-2022; APA-About-DSM].
- Criteria clarified for a number of disorders, including autism spectrum disorder (all three social communication deficits now explicitly required) and bipolar I and II disorder; a new "unspecified mood disorder" category was added [Singer-2022; APA-About-DSM].
- Fully revised text, with review for racism, discrimination and non-stigmatizing language; "intellectual developmental disorder" replaces older terms [Singer-2022; APA-About-DSM].

**Updates since 2022.** The APA issues a supplement each September. The September 2025 supplement relabeled several "other specified" examples with "-like" wording (for example, "cyclothymia" became "cyclothymic-like disorder") and widened the Z65.8 category to "Moral, Religious, or Spiritual Problem" [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025]. Earlier supplements corrected codes: for example, current suicidal behavior, printed in the manual as T14.91A, is coded T14.91XA [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025]. The APA reports no code changes for 2026 and plans criteria and text changes in early 2027 [APA-DSM-Page].

**Where it is heading.** In early 2026 the APA published strategy papers for a future manual in the American Journal of Psychiatry. Its subcommittees aim to add contextual factors (socioeconomic, cultural, environmental), transdiagnostic symptoms, candidate biomarkers, and functioning and quality of life [APA-Future-DSM-2026]. A new name, "Diagnostic and Scientific Manual," has been floated [Alpert-2026]. These are proposals; DSM-5-TR remains the manual in use.

**Dimensional tools.** Section III measures, such as the Level 1 Cross-Cutting Symptom Measure (13 domains for adults) and the 36-item World Health Organization Disability Assessment Schedule 2.0 (WHODAS 2.0), support but do not make a diagnosis [APA-Assessment-Measures].

## In the session

- Ask about every criterion yourself; never infer one from a questionnaire total.
- Record the criteria met, duration, specifiers, exclusions considered and whether the diagnosis is provisional.
- Explain it in plain words: "The name describes a pattern that many people share. Our formulation will describe how it works for you."
- Never paste criteria text into notes, client handouts or software; paraphrase (DSM-5-TR is copyrighted) [APA-DSM5TR-Update-2025].
