**Bottom line:** Each cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) progress note should let another clinician, or you next week, see what was worked on, measure scores, what was done, how the client responded, the risk picture and what happens next. SOAP (subjective, objective, assessment, plan) and DAP (data, assessment, plan) are two common layouts for the same content. In the United States (US), process notes kept separately can qualify as "psychotherapy notes" under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), but the core session facts always belong in the main record [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501].

## When to use

- After every session, including brief phone contacts, missed appointments and between-session risk calls.
- When designing a note template or an artificial intelligence (AI) documentation tool.

## Steps

1. **Pick a format and use it consistently.**
   - **SOAP:** Lawrence Weed developed this structure as part of the problem-oriented medical record [Podder-etal-2023]. S holds the client's report, O holds observations and measure scores, A holds your clinical interpretation of progress, and P holds the plan.
   - **DAP:** Data (client report plus observations and scores together), Assessment, Plan. It suits psychotherapy, where "objective" findings are mostly observations and questionnaire scores.
2. **Fill it from the session structure** (see the table below).
3. **Separate observation from interpretation.** Write what the client said and did, then your assessment, and mark uncertainty [Monaci-etal-2025].
4. **Write for a reader who may be the client.** Use plain, respectful language and avoid stigmatizing shorthand [Monaci-etal-2025].
5. **Reread before the next session.** Reviewing last session's note is the simplest way to remember the action plan review [Beck-2021].

| Session element | Where it goes (SOAP) | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| Mood check and measures | S and O | Client's rating in their words; scores and change from last time |
| Bridge and action plan review | S and A | What was done, what was learned, barriers |
| Agenda items and interventions | O and A | Problems addressed, techniques used, key thoughts and beliefs worked on |
| Client response | A | Shift in belief or distress ratings, engagement, new learning |
| Risk | O and A | Screen result, assessment if done, actions taken |
| New action plan and next steps | P | The exact task, next appointment, referrals, coordination |
| Feedback | A or P | Client feedback and any change agreed |

## What to record

Every progress note, whatever the format:
- Date, start and stop times, modality (in person, video or phone) and who attended.
- Measure scores, such as Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) totals. Recording and using scores is the core of measurement-based care, which fewer than 20% of practitioners do routinely [Lewis-etal-2019].
- Interventions used and the client's response, tied to treatment goals.
- Risk status and actions, or "no risk indicators identified on screen and inquiry".
- The plan and the action plan.

The HIPAA definition of psychotherapy notes excludes medication prescription and monitoring, session start and stop times, modalities and frequencies of treatment, results of clinical tests, and any summary of diagnosis, functional status, treatment plan, symptoms, prognosis and progress to date [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501]. Those items therefore belong in the progress note in the main record, not in separately kept process notes.

## Guidance

- If you want to keep detailed process reflections (hypotheses, your reactions) → keep them as separate psychotherapy notes, not in the shared record. Because only notes kept separate qualify, and they then need the client's authorization for most disclosures and fall outside the HIPAA right of access, though state law may differ [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501; HIPAA-45-CFR-164-508; HIPAA-45-CFR-164-524].
- If the client may read the note through a patient portal → describe risk in context and avoid judgmental adjectives. Because patients report feeling judged by wording, and trust erodes when notes read as dismissive [Monaci-etal-2025].
- If you notice yourself writing vaguer notes because clients can read them → keep the clinical facts and change only the tone. Because euphemistic notes lose clinical value [Monaci-etal-2025].
- If a session departed from the plan (crisis, risk, rupture) → record what happened, why you changed course and what you did. Because the reason for a deviation is what a later reader most needs.
- If you are in the United Kingdom (UK) → the same content applies; records fall under the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018, not HIPAA, so do not assume private process notes are shielded from a client's access request. See `14-ethics-law.hipaa-and-gdpr`. Because HIPAA's psychotherapy-notes category is United States law only [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501].

Example of a brief DAP entry:
- "D: PHQ-9 14 (was 18). Completed 3 of 5 planned walks; noticed mood 'a bit lighter' after each. Worked on thought 'I'm letting everyone down' (belief 80% to 50%) with a thought record. A: Engaging well; activation helping; self-critical thinking is the main barrier. No risk indicators on screen or inquiry. P: Continue walks 5 times; one thought record when mood drops; next session in 1 week."

## Common problems

- **Generic notes.** "Discussed stressors, provided CBT" tells no one anything. Name the problem, technique and response.
- **Copied notes.** Carrying forward last week's text creates inaccurate records (expert consensus).
- **Missing scores and plan.** Without them, progress cannot be judged at review or discharge. See `09-ending.discharge-summary`.
