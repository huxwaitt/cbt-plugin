**Bottom line:** A cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) note that names no problem, technique, score, response, risk status or plan fails every reader: the next clinician, an auditor, the client, and you at the next session. Build the note from the session structure, record scores and the action plan every time, keep private process reflections out of the main record, and write in language the client could read without feeling judged [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501; Monaci-etal-2025].

## What it looks like

- "Client discussed stressors. Provided CBT. Client tolerated session well. Continue plan."
- The same paragraph appears in every note with only the date changed.
- No measure scores, so no one can see whether the client is improving.
- The homework task is not recorded, so it is never reviewed [Beck-2021].
- Risk is never mentioned, or only "denies suicidal ideation (SI)" with no context of what was asked.
- The shared record contains speculation about the client's motives or the therapist's personal reactions.
- Labels such as "manipulative" or "noncompliant" appear without description.

## Why it happens

- **Time pressure.** Notes are written late and from memory, so detail is lost.
- **Template habits.** Electronic templates and copy-forward features make cloning easy.
- **Uncertainty about privacy.** Clinicians unsure what clients or payers can see either write too little or put private reflections in the wrong place.
- **Defensive vagueness.** Knowing clients may read notes, some clinicians write vague or euphemistic text, which reduces clinical value [Monaci-etal-2025].
- **No measurement routine.** Without session-by-session measures, there are no numbers to record; measurement-based care is used by fewer than 20% of practitioners [Lewis-etal-2019].

## The fix

- If notes are generic → use a fixed template that mirrors the session: scores, action plan review, agenda items and interventions, client response, risk, new action plan. Because the session structure already generates the content (see `05-session-structure.session-notes`).
- If you want to record hypotheses or your own reactions → keep them in separate psychotherapy notes. Because under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), only notes kept apart from the rest of the record qualify as psychotherapy notes, and summaries of symptoms, progress, treatment plan and test results never do [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501].
- If you are tempted to copy last week's note → start from a blank template and write only what happened today. Because cloned notes are inaccurate records.
- If a description could read as judgmental → describe the behavior instead of labeling it ("arrived 20 minutes late for the third time" rather than "noncompliant"). Because clients report feeling judged by wording, and observation separated from interpretation is more useful clinically [Monaci-etal-2025].
- If risk was screened → record what was asked, what the client said and what you did. Because a bare "denies" tells a later reader nothing. See `02-risk.risk-documentation`.

Example rewrite:
- Before: "Discussed anxiety. CBT. Continue."
- After: "Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) 13 (was 15). Reviewed exposure log: rode bus 3 of 4 planned days; peak anxiety fell from 80 to 50. Planned next step on hierarchy. No suicidal thoughts on direct inquiry. Plan: bus daily plus one stop further; review log next week."

## Sources note

The psychotherapy notes definition is the federal regulation itself [HIPAA-45-CFR-164-501]. The guidance on wording comes from a 2025 scoping review of 22 studies of open notes in mental health, half of them from the United States [Monaci-etal-2025]. State law, payer rules and United Kingdom (UK) record rules add requirements; see `14-ethics-law.record-keeping`. The harms of cloned notes are expert consensus rather than trial evidence.
