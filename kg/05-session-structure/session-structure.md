**Bottom line:** A cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) session follows a predictable arc: mood check, agenda, brief update and action plan review, work on one or two agenda items with new action plans set along the way, then a summary and feedback [Beck-2021]. The structure protects time for change work; it is not an end in itself. Deviate on purpose when risk, acute distress or a strained relationship makes the agreed agenda the wrong priority [Beck-2021].

## When to use

- Every treatment session after the evaluation session; group, youth and protocol-specific treatments adapt the content, not the skeleton.
- When reviewing a recording or supervising: agenda, feedback, pacing and homework are scored items on the Cognitive Therapy Rating Scale (CTRS) [Young-Beck-1980].

## Steps

| Phase | What happens | Timing rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Mood check | Brief rating and measure review, compared with last session | A few minutes |
| 2. Agenda | Client and therapist name and prioritize items | Usually within five minutes [Young-Beck-1980] |
| 3. Update and action plan review | The week in two or three sentences; what the client did and learned from the action plan | Brief; detail only if it serves today's agenda [Beck-2021] |
| 4. First agenda item | Specify the problem, elicit key thoughts and behaviors, intervene, set an action plan step | Largest block |
| 5. Second agenda item | Same pattern | Two items, possibly a third [Beck-2021] |
| 6. Ending | Client summary, review of the new action plan, feedback on the session | Flag the time before wrap-up starts, for example "10 minutes left before we start finishing up" [Beck-2021] |

- Socialize the client to this structure in the first therapy session: say why you structure sessions, describe each element and give the rationale [Beck-2021].
- Set action plan steps while discussing each agenda item, then confirm the whole plan at the end, so each task links to what was just learned (expert consensus).

## What to record

- Date, start and stop times, attendance and format (in person, video or phone).
- Measure scores and the client's own mood rating.
- Agenda items agreed, and any deviation with its reason (for example "agenda set aside to assess risk").
- Action plan review, interventions used, the client's response, the new action plan, risk screen result and feedback. See `05-session-structure.session-notes`.

## Guidance

- If the client gives a long narrative during the mood check or update → gently interrupt and ask for the big picture, then offer to put the detail on the agenda. Because unfocused discussion usually comes from missing interruptions and summaries [Beck-2021].
- If the client discloses risk to self or others → set the agenda aside and assess the risk now with `02-risk.suicide-risk-assessment` and `02-risk.crisis-response`. If danger is imminent, do not end the contact with the client alone: call 911 or the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) in the United States, or 999 in the United Kingdom (UK). Because risk to life, health or safety overrides the agreed agenda [Beck-2021].
- If the client is too distressed by another problem to focus on the agreed item → switch to the distressing problem and say why. Because a switch made without explanation can read to the client as "this problem is hopeless" [Young-Beck-1980].
- If pushing the structure seems likely to cost the relationship → negotiate, for example split the time between free talk and agenda work. Because the first goal is a client who returns next week [Beck-2021].
- If time is running short → name the time and offer a choice between continuing the current item and moving on. Because tracking time together is collaborative [Beck-2021].
- If the client is still upset near the end → acknowledge it, agree to put the issue first on the next agenda, and close with a lighter topic. Because clients should not leave in peak distress when time has run out [Beck-2021].

Example phrasings:
- "Can I pause you there? I want the big picture first: was this week mostly better, worse or about the same?"
- "We have about 10 minutes before we start wrapping up. Do you want to stay with this, or move to the second thing on our list?"
- Avoid: "We have to stick to the agenda." It frames structure as a rule rather than a shared tool.

## Common problems

- **Therapist reluctance to interrupt.** Thoughts such as "I shouldn't interrupt her" are common in novices and in therapists from less directive models. Practice in role plays, then run one fully structured session as a behavioral experiment [Beck-2021].
- **Talking without intervening.** Describing a problem rarely helps by itself. Keep asking "How can I help the client feel better by the end of the session?" and end each item with a response, a partial solution or an action plan [Beck-2021].
- **Evidence context.** In a large depression trial, the competence component most related to outcome was structuring skill, though overall competence effects were "not as strong or consistent as expected" (secondary analysis of a randomized trial) [Shaw-etal-1999]. In first sessions of CBT for severe depression, active action planning predicted more improvement, while time spent on the mechanics of completing measures predicted less (one study, emerging evidence) [Malins-etal-2026].
